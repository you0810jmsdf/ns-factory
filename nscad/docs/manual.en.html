Drawing and coordinates

Coordinates are x right, y down, in mm. Arcs and rotations are clockwise-positive. Chamfer trims both edges by the given distance; fillet creates a tangent arc. Positive offset is outward on closed shapes/circles, left of travel on open paths; arc offset increases radius.

Arcs: choose Arc method in Drawing options. "Center and radius": drag from the center to the start, then click the end direction. Enter a value in the radius box (mm) to fix the radius; then click the center, the start direction and the end direction. "3 points": click the start, a point on the arc, then the end to make the arc through them (collinear points cannot make an arc; Esc cancels).

End and center magnet: while drawing, the cursor sticks to other shapes' endpoints and to circle/arc centers regardless of the Snap checkbox (centers show as faint crosses only while a drawing tool is active). Set the distance with "Magnet distance to ends/centers (px)" in Drawing options (default 10; 0 turns it off; saved). Midpoints, intersections and the grid still follow the Snap checkbox. The midpoint and both ends of a line, of each side of a polyline and of each span of a Bezier or pen curve (the midpoint of a curve is the middle of its length), the midpoint (middle of the curve) and both ends of an arc, the 8 points of a circle (top, bottom, left, right and the four 45° points), and the center of each visible stitching hole and mark also attract the cursor at the same distance (so the Ruler can measure the pitch between holes), regardless of the Snap checkbox; with a drawing tool, these points appear as small diamonds when the pointer comes within about 40 px.

Fillet (R) and Chamfer (C) work on two lines sharing an endpoint, or on a polyline (e.g. a rectangle) with one click: click near a corner to round just that corner, or along an edge to round every corner with the radius (C distance) you enter. The result is a chain of lines and arcs, and Undo restores it in one step. It cannot be made when the radius would overlap a neighboring corner, or at the ends of an open polyline. Remove stitching holes from the shape first.

Rectangle: with the Rectangle tool, drag between two opposite corners (or click, then click) to draw a closed 4-point polyline. Hold Shift for a square. In the entry box, type "x,y" for the first corner and "@width,height" for the second. Nothing is made when the width or height is 0.

Offset: first set the Offset distance and Side (outside / inside) in Drawing options. Click a shape to select the part between intersections with other shapes (shown in orange); double-click to select the whole connected shape (a closed shape if it is closed). Press Enter or the Offset button to run it. Connected lines are treated as one, passing through their joints. Closed shapes go outside or inside; open lines go to the left (outside) or right (inside) of travel. The result is a yellow dashed line; change color and line style in the Info card.

Stitching and editing

Click a shape with Stitching to place holes along a connected path. Branches split paths. Choose fixed, variable, equal margins, or manual placement; set margins and an s section measured from the chosen start direction. Replacing only a section allows mixed tools. Manual advance is (teeth−1) × pitch; a single-tooth tool advances one pitch. Alt/⌥+click places one hole at the clicked position. The gold square marks the next first tooth. Closed paths never duplicate the seam hole; backstitching reuses 2–3 holes.

Use Select to drag holes freely or constrained to the path. Shift selects multiple holes. Angle, slant and deletion affect selected holes or the selected shape’s whole path. Shape moves and copies include the entire stitch path. Locked layers are protected.

Dots default to 0.5mm diameter. Mark precedence is hole, path, document, tool. Round tools use holeD; an explicit circle mark uses holeW. Tool updates change dimensions but keep existing hole pitch and angle; replace holes to apply these settings. Tools persist in browser storage and document JSON. Matched stitching is STEP 6; SVG/PDF/DXF exports are later steps.

Matching the real iron: in the Stitch card set hole width, height (blade length) and slant to measured values and press Update. "Print test strip" prints 10 holes with a 50 mm scale at actual size to compare with the iron. If an imported drawing (.lcc etc.) uses a small drawing-only tool, reassign its holes to a registered tool so the hole size matches the real iron.

Mark tool: click on a line or curve to drop a dot mark at that spot, one at a time (you choose the start and can mark anywhere along the line). Connected lines are treated as one path and their joints are passed through. Marks are points on the same path as stitch holes; the size is "Dot diameter" in the Stitching card. You can select, move or delete them.

Marking by distance: enter 1 or more in "From the base point along the line (mm)" in the Stitching card to drop a mark that far along the line from a base point. Click the base point (a line end or corner), then click the line in the direction you want (small circles show the possible spots). For the end of an open line the direction is fixed (into the line), so one click is enough. Distance is measured through joints of connected lines, and wraps around on a closed line. Nothing is placed past the end of the line. Set it back to 0 to drop marks where you click.

Diamond angle and diamond / round choice: in Stitching > "Tool editor", set the kind (diamond, European, round), the blade slant (angle in degrees), the diamond width and height, the round diameter, the number of teeth and the pitch, then press Add (or Update). Registered tools appear in "Tool to use". The direction of holes already placed can be set numerically with "Hole angle" in "Hole editing".

Settings sets: in the "Settings sets (save / recall)" card on the right, save the punching, patchwork, offset and other settings together with your registered tools under a name (up to 30 in this browser, newest first). "Recall the selected settings" restores them (tool changes can be undone), and "Export to a settings file" / "Import a settings file" carry them to another computer as a JSON file.

Forward and reverse slant: newly placed diamond holes default to forward slant ("/" on a line drawn left to right). Tick "Reverse slant of new holes" for reverse ("\\" direction). Set the size of the slant as a number in the tool editor Angle (forward uses the opposite sign). On vertical or diagonal lines the holes rotate with the tangent.

Pen (continuous Bézier)

Bezier tool: click the start, click midpoints (any number), then double-click the end (Enter also finishes). A smooth curve passes through every clicked point, and you can edit nodes and handles afterwards. Right-click also edits via points (on the curve: add one there; on a via point: remove it).

Click for corner nodes, drag for smooth nodes with handles, as many as you like. Enter or double-click finishes; clicking the first node closes the path. Esc removes the last node.

Select a single pen shape with the Select tool to show its nodes. Drag nodes or handles (Alt/⌥ breaks one side of a handle). Double-click on the curve to add a node, Delete removes the chosen node. Use “Smooth / corner” in the Info card to switch. Lines, Béziers and polylines can be converted with “To pen path”.

Text and dimensions

The Text tool asks for the text after you click its position. Size and angle are edited in the Info card.

The Dimension tool takes two clicks and draws a dimension line with the value in mm. The offset is set in Drawing options and can be edited later. Text and dimensions are ignored by stitching and offsetting.

The Ruler tool shows the distance, horizontal, vertical and angle between a start and an end point you click (it snaps to endpoints and centers; Shift = horizontal / vertical). Nothing is added to the drawing.

Numeric entry

Type in the field at the bottom and press Enter. Formats: “x,y” (absolute), “@dx,dy” (relative to the last point), “len<deg” (from the last point, clockwise positive), or a plain number for radii.

Line: start point, then end points (keeps chaining). Circle: center, then radius. Arc: center, radius, start angle, end angle. Polyline: points, then Enter on an empty field. Dimension: two points.

Layers and the Info card

Choose the drawing layer, visibility, lock and name in the Layers card. Defaults are Pattern, Marks and Guide. Shapes on locked layers cannot be selected.

The Info card shows coordinates, length, radius and angles of the selected shape; editing a value updates the shape, and stitching holes follow.

The Info card also lets you pick a shape color (blue, green, red, white or yellow) and line style (solid or dashed); not available for text, dimensions or fold lines. These are reflected in SVG, DXF and PDF export. Importing a .lcc file maps its original colors (Aqua=outline to blue, Lime=patch lines to green, Orange=dimensions and Yellow=marks to yellow) onto these five colors automatically.

Changing several shapes at once: Shift+click, or drag over empty space to box-select (shapes that fit entirely inside the box are selected; Ctrl/⌘+A selects all). With two or more selected, the Info card shows only Layer, Color and Line style; changing one applies to every selected shape in a single undo step. Setting Line style to Dashed moves them all to the Guide layer. A blank field means the selected shapes have different values.

Trim and mirror copy

Trim removes the clicked span of a shape between its intersections (and endpoints), including overhanging ends. Shift+click keeps the clicked span instead. Shapes with stitching prompt before removing holes and seam settings on connected paths. Undo restores everything together.

Mirror copies the selection across an axis: select shapes, then click an axis line or two points. Holes are copied too; by default their slant is mirrored (reverse). Set 'Slant of mirrored holes' in Drawing options to 'Same direction' to keep the slant. Positions coincide either way. Flip horizontally/vertically follows the same setting.

Output and printing

The Output card exports SVG (mm units, one group per layer, for laser software and Inkscape) and DXF R12 (Y flipped up, holes on the Stitch_Holes layer). Dots can be written as DXF POINTs.

Choose the paper (A4/A3, portrait/landscape) and press Print. Patterns larger than the page are split with a 10 mm overlap, cross marks and page numbers. In the print dialog always choose “Actual size / 100%”.

Calibration: place the L-shaped 100 mm scale, print it, measure X and Y on paper, enter and save. Later prints, SVG and DXF are corrected per axis. Print the scale again to confirm 100 mm.

Thickness, folds, box stitch, patchwork

Parts: select shapes and press Make part from selection. A part has a thickness and a stack order, editable in the Info card.

Fold lines: click two points. Angle, inner side and the neutral factor K are in the Design card. Select a fold and press Add fold allowance to create a copy extended by dL = theta x (r + K x t).

Stack correction: the part with the highest order is the outer one; its closed shapes are offset outward by the sum of inner thicknesses (folded: theta x sum t, box: sum t).

Box stitch: click stitch line A (holes through the face) then B (holes through the edge). Hole counts are matched per span (corners, curve joints) and a warning appears when the pitch differs beyond tolerance. Paired holes are connected by thin lines. Make stitch line A offsets a selected edge inward by half of B's thickness.

Patchwork: seams (pairs of neighboring edges) are created automatically by "Make grid" and "Fill enclosed shape with pattern" (there is no manual tool for pairing edges). Optimize equalizes hole counts on shared edges, keeps a gap at junctions and keeps pitches within tolerance. Suggest sizes lists three near-square cut sizes in pitch multiples; Make grid creates the pieces with seams.

Patterns (Argyle, Ichimatsu): in the Patchwork section of the Design card, choose a pattern and cell size (mm), press "Fill enclosed shape with pattern", then click inside a shape closed by solid lines (connected lines, a closed polyline or a circle). The shape is cut by the cells and the pieces are drawn as closed polylines. Dashed shapes are not used as boundaries. Tiny scraps (under 5% of a cell) are skipped. If "Also add seams and stitch holes" is checked (default), for cross-stitching each piece gets a stitch line (yellow dashed, Marks layer) shrunk inward from its edge by the "Stitch line distance from edge" (default 2.5 mm), and the stitch holes go on it. Holes are never placed on the boundary; holes facing each other across the boundary are paired (neighboring stitch lines are registered as seams, and the pitch is matched with the same calculation as Optimize). If "No diamond holes within 2.5 mm of an edge" is checked (default), nothing is made when a distance under 2.5 mm is given for a diamond punch. Uncheck it to allow; it does not apply to round or other non-diamond punches.

File formats (import and export)

Open or drag-and-drop: .json (this CAD's document), .svg (path, line, rect, circle, ellipse, polyline, polygon, text, transforms, mm/cm/in/px units), .dxf (LINE, CIRCLE, ARC, LWPOLYLINE/POLYLINE with bulge, SPLINE, TEXT/MTEXT, INSERT blocks, $INSUNITS). Imported shapes are added to the current drawing; broken elements are dropped and counted. SVG <g id> becomes a layer.

Export: SVG (mm, per-layer groups, optional fill), DXF R12, PDF (A4/A3, tiling, calibration, ASCII text only), PNG (dpi, optional transparency, up to 50 M pixels), JSON.

Leathercraft CAD and Beam Studio

Target “Leathercraft CAD” writes DXF with layers LineType_01 (pattern), LineType_02 (marks), LineType_03 (guide), holes on Stitch_Holes, origin at the lower-left corner. Opening a Leathercraft CAD DXF maps LineType_NN back to layers, restores Stitch_Holes circles/diamonds as stitching holes and recovers arcs from polyline approximations (toggle in the Output card).

Target “Beam Studio” fixes SVG colors: cut lines black (closed shapes offset outward by half the kerf), holes red circles, engraving (marks layer, text) blue, guides omitted. In Beam Studio assign black = cut, red = holes, blue = engrave. PNG gives the same drawing in black and white for engraving.

3D folding and three views

Toggle with the 3D button. Closed shapes of parts become panels with thickness, split by fold lines, and rotate by the fold angle as the slider goes from 0% (open) to 100% (closed). Play loops the motion. The fold direction follows the fold line's inner-side setting (grain faces meet). Parts with a higher stack order sit on top of the lower ones.

Left drag rotates, right drag (or Shift) pans, wheel zooms. Quad view shows front, top and right orthographic views plus the free view.

Panels overlapping when closed are drawn red and counted (neighbors joined by a fold are excluded). Views SVG exports third-angle front/top/right views in mm; Print views prints them. Without WebGL a simple painter renderer is used.

From three views to a solid (front / top / right)

Draw the front, top and right views, then check the result as a solid. Follow the steps in order.

[Setup] Tick “3 views (front / top / right)” at the top. Three boxes appear: the front view at the lower left, the top view right above it, and the right view to its right.

[Choosing the boxes yourself] Draw a straight rectangle, select it and press “Use selected rectangle as box”. A rectangle in the front area becomes the front box (depth from the “Depth” field); one in the top area sets the width and depth; one in the right area sets the height and depth. The other boxes are made from it, and the choice is saved with the file. “Reset boxes” returns to the standard size.

[Step 1 Draw the front] Draw the front shape inside the Front view box (a closed shape such as a rectangle, circle or polyline). Dotted lines run from its corners and ends: vertical ones into the top box and horizontal ones into the right box. They mark where the other views line up with the front view.

[Step 2 Make the top and right outlines] Type the depth (front to back, in mm) into “Depth” and press “Make top & right views”. From the box enclosing everything in the front view and that depth, the outlines (rectangles) of the top and right views are drawn. Nothing is made if the same rectangles already exist. Undo removes them.

[Step 3 Add the details] Draw the details inside the top and right boxes, following the dotted lines. Clicking near a dotted line pulls the point exactly onto it, sideways or up-and-down (this works even when Snap is off). In the top and right boxes the depth in mm is shown next to the cursor. The depth you draw in the top view travels along the 45° line at the upper right into the right view (the bottom edge of the top box and the left edge of the right box are the near side).

[Solid at the upper right] Once the front view has a closed shape, a small solid is always shown in the empty upper-right area (where the 45° line is) and changes as you draw. Drag inside it to turn it.

[Step 4 Check it as a solid] Press 3D at the top and a solid built from your three views appears. Left-drag rotates, right-drag (or Shift+drag) pans, the wheel zooms. In the “3D (folding)” card on the right, choose Quad view to see it next to the front, top and right views. “Views SVG” and “Print views” use the same solid.

[How it works] The height of the front shape is cut into thin slices; each slice is trimmed by the top shape and the right shape, then they are stacked. If the top or right box has no closed shape, a rectangle is assumed.

[Keep in mind] The boxes are only guides, not shapes (they are not saved or exported). They grow automatically to fit what you draw. The near-side lines (bottom edge of the top box, left edge of the right box) stay put unless the front view grows far upward or to the right. Small hollows, holes and multiple parts cannot be turned into a solid yet. This feature is still being built.

Three views (4 panes): separate sheets

Draw the front, top and right views on separate sheets (tabs) and see all of them at once in four panes. There are no boxes or 45° line; the sheets are linked only by dotted lines.

[Turning it on] Tick “3 views (4 panes)” at the top. The current drawing becomes the Front sheet, and Top and Right sheets (tabs) are created for you. The screen splits into four panes: top view at the upper left, front view at the lower left, right view at the lower right, and the solid at the upper right.

[Drawing] Click the pane you want to draw in; that sheet becomes the active one (its border turns gold and the tab switches). Then draw as usual. Each pane can be zoomed and panned on its own.

[Sheet directions] The Top sheet has a horizontal “near side line”; above it is further back (the distance from the line is the depth). The Right sheet has a vertical near side line; to its right is further back. The Front sheet is a normal drawing.

[Dotted lines] Lines coming from the other sheets: the x of front corners appear as vertical lines on the Top sheet, front heights as horizontal lines on the Right sheet, Top-sheet depths as vertical lines on the Right sheet, and Right-sheet depths as horizontal lines on the Top sheet. Clicking near a dotted line pulls the point onto it. Light-blue lines on the other sheets follow your cursor.

[Solid] The upper-right pane always shows the solid built from the three sheets and changes as you draw. Drag inside it to turn it. The 3D button, Views SVG and Print views use the same solid.

[Saving] Saving from any of the sheet tabs puts all three sheets into one file. Loading it brings the three tabs back.

[Keep in mind] “3 views (4 panes)” and “3 views (front / top / right)” (the box method) cannot be on at the same time. Turning 4 panes off keeps the sheets (tabs). Small hollows, holes and multiple parts cannot be turned into a solid yet.

Hardware library and binder mechanisms

Choose a category and an item in the Hardware card, adjust the size parameters (typical values; verify the ones marked) and the angle, press Place and click on the drawing. The outline symbol goes to the Marks layer; mounting holes (rivets, snap posts, screws) go to the Hardware holes layer. Placed hardware also appears in 3D as a box or cylinder on its panel, follows folding and is included in collision checks.

For binder mechanisms pick a standard (Micro5, Mini6, A6, Bible, A5, A5 Slim, A4, Franklin...) and a ring diameter. Hole pitches, first-to-last span and refill sizes come from N's factory's standards table (data/binder/*.json); plate and rivet sizes are estimates you can overwrite. Plan/side/front drawings are generated from the JSON. The required spine width = ring diameter + plate thickness + 2 × leather thickness + 2 mm play.

Spine simulation: in the binder section, enter ring ID, wire, plate thickness, cover leather, supporter thickness, clearance and bend radius; the closed section (ring OD + clearance = inner width, 90° fold allowances both ends) gives the spine width with a section drawing. Enter a spine you measured by wrapping real leather to derive the clearance, then reuse it for other sizes. "Apply to recipe spine" feeds the notebook recipe.

Part library, tabs and reuse

Part library: built-in parts (card pocket, pen holder, ring base, D-ring tab) and your own parts can be placed with adjustable variables (W, H...). Select shapes, name them and press Register to store them with holes, paths and part info (localStorage). Export/Import moves the library between PCs as .nscad-lib.json.

Tabs: open several drawings and switch with tabs (undo, selection and view per tab). Copy or move the selection to another tab.

Clipboard: Ctrl/⌘+C copies the selection as text; Ctrl/⌘+V pastes in another tab, window or PC. IDs are reassigned and missing layers are created. Scale lets you resize while pasting. Sources are kept in the document's provenance.

Ask AI

Send a question or request from the AI card; Claude replies with text and proposed actions. Nothing changes until you press Apply (undo available). “Attach the current drawing” sends a summary (mm, hole counts, thickness, selected ids; no images).

Enter the server URL and passphrase in AI settings (the server is the Cloudflare Worker in worker/; the API key stays on the server). The card shows an estimate before sending, the real token usage and cost after, and the monthly total.

Preset buttons: review the pattern, stitching margin, card-case draft, how-to. The last 20 exchanges are kept on this PC.

Auto design from a recipe

Fill the Recipe card (product, cards or refill and ring size, thickness, stitch style, pitch, paper) and press Generate. A new tab gets the full pattern (cut lines, stitch lines with holes, folds, labels, part thickness) and is checked immediately.

Rules: card 85.60×53.98×0.76 mm; pocket = card + 3 mm play, width + cards×0.76. Edge stitch: stitch line 3 mm from the edge, no allowance; turned: 6 mm allowance. Notebook: height = refill + 10, spine = ring + plate + 2×leather + 2, width = 2×(refill width + 3) + spine + fold allowance.

Check lists unconnected lines, parts outside the paper, seam hole mismatches, collisions when closed and zero-length lines; selecting one jumps to it. Thread estimate (length×4+100 mm) is in the stitching card. Products without rules can be drafted by the AI and checked the same way. The final decision is always yours.

Underlay images and tracing

Import a scanned pattern or sketch (PNG/JPEG) from the Underlay card or drop it on the canvas. It sits on the guide layer and is stored in this PC's IndexedDB (the document keeps only an id).

Scale: use the scan dpi, or click two points on the image and enter their real distance. Opacity and angle are adjustable; locking makes it unselectable.

Manual tracing: draw lines, pen paths or polylines over it (no snapping to the image).

Auto trace: set threshold (empty = auto), simplify tolerance and minimum area, press Preview, check the red contours and Apply to add them to the pattern layer.

Drafts from photos, sketches and 3D

From a photo or sketch: import it as an underlay, tick “Attach the underlay photo” in the AI card, enter the reference width and press the photo preset. The AI proposes parts and shapes (setRecipe / addShapes); after Apply you can run the recipe pipeline and the check. Estimated sizes are marked as estimates. Photos are not stored on the server.

From 3D: panels made with fold lines can be flattened with “Unfold to a new tab” in the 3D card; edges not on a fold get the given allowance (arbitrary stacked solids are out of scope).

Color simulation

In the Colors card choose a leather color per part (16 swatches or #RRGGBB) and a thread color per stitched path (92 Vinymo MBT colors, searchable by number and Japanese name, pin favorites). In 2D closed part shapes are tinted and holes take the thread color; 3D uses the part color on panels.

Color sets can be saved by name in the document and switched. “Colored SVG” exports fills and thread colors; “3D as PNG” saves the current 3D view. No leather textures are used (flat color with shading).

Troubleshooting

Common problems and fixes.

Nothing works from file://: make sure all files sit next to index.html; otherwise serve over http (e.g. py -m http.server).

Prints are not actual size: set the print dialog to Actual size / 100%, print the calibration scale, measure X and Y and save the correction.

Safari does not save: allow downloads when asked, then try again.

3D is black: WebGL is disabled; the simple renderer takes over.

AI unavailable: set the server URL and passphrase; deploy the Worker as in worker/README.md.

Cannot place holes: text, dimensions, folds and underlays cannot carry holes; closed shapes must really close.

Video tutorials (Show me)

Press “Show me” in Help and the app performs the steps on screen (fake cursor, highlighted buttons, captions). Change the speed, pause, or switch to “Do it myself” to get only prompts. Scripts live in tutorials/*.json; four are included: drawing, stitching, pen, output.

MP4 files are produced by the Playwright recording script (C:\dev\leather-cad-tools\record_tutorials.mjs) and embedded in the manual when present in docs/videos/.

Voice control

Hold the microphone button and speak to switch tools or edit. Typing the same words in the box below does the same.

Tools: line, circle, arc, bezier/curve, pen, polyline, text, dimension, fillet, chamfer, offset, trim, mirror, fold, koma, seam, stitch/holes

With numbers: "line 100 mm angle 30", "circle radius 15", "offset 3 inside/outside", "chamfer 5", "stitch 4 mm variable", "zoom 200", "grid 2 mm"

Editing: undo, redo, delete, copy, flip horizontal/vertical, select all, save, SVG/DXF/PDF, print

View: 3D, 2D, help, zoom in, zoom out, fit

When unsure, 2–3 candidates appear; tap one. Delete, save and print ask first: say "yes" to run or "no" to cancel.

Sentences outside the grammar ("check the allowance of this card case") go straight to the AI card.

Settings: hold vs tap mode, read-back on/off (off by default), mic level meter (warns on prolonged silence). Speech is processed by the browser's recognition (Chrome/Edge/Safari use cloud recognition). Browsers without it can use the text box only.

My designs (importing Leathercraft CAD)

Open a .lcc file (Leathercraft CAD save) and it becomes an N's CAD drawing with lines, arcs, Béziers, text, dimensions and stitch holes. Holes are re-attached to the nearest line and the tools (diamond/round, pitch, hole size) are recreated.

The library card's "My designs" lists past designs converted by tools/import_lcc.js (filter with the search box). "Open in new tab" loads the whole drawing; "Place into this drawing" pastes it at the center (scaled by the paste scale). The list is available only over http.

The tendencies of past designs (pitch, hole size, common corner radii, text size) are summarized in data/style.json and used as the recipe's default pitch and as context for the AI panel. A summary appears under the list.

Unreadable elements (broken files, elliptical arcs) are counted; broken files are listed in designs/_failed.json.

Stitching: closed seams and reverse direction

Circles and closed polylines do not duplicate the first and last hole. The seam has an arc-length position of 0 mm in either direction. The bisector corner option also applies at the seam, with the tool slant added to its angle.

Manual placement starts at the clicked point and places the selected number of teeth. Each following click shares the previous last hole, advancing by (teeth - 1) * pitch. Placement stops at the end of one lap. Reset the advance to restart elsewhere; Alt/Option-click places one hole. Switching direction and adding holes does not duplicate the seam hole.

Node editing and numeric drawing

Click the selected pen path to insert a node. Removing an inserted node without editing it restores the original curve. Drag nodes or handles; hold Alt (Option) to edit one handle independently. For lines, circles and arcs, click a starting point and complete the geometry in the bottom input. Use x,y for absolute coordinates, @dx,dy for relative coordinates and 50<30 for length and angle. Enter a radius for circles, or radius, start angle and end angle for arcs. F1 opens shortcuts; ? opens help.
