The Persecution by the Eighty Deities and the Land 根の堅州国*

八十神の迫害と根の堅州国

大穴牟遲神*, chosen by Yakami-hime, is killed twice by his brothers, the Eighty Deities (八十神), and comes back to life, and he flees to 根堅州國*, where Susanoo-no-Mikoto is. There, with the help of Suseri-bime, he gets through the ordeals set by Susanoo-no-Mikoto, is told "Become Ōkuninushi-no-Kami (大國主神)", returns to the earth, and begins making the land.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textAs 大穴牟遲神* fled from 根堅州國*, Susanoo-no-Mikoto called out to him from 黄泉比良坂*: "Drive away your brothers with the living sword (生大刀) and the living bow and arrows (生弓矢), become Ōkuninushi-no-Kami and 宇都志國主神*, make Suseri-bime (須世理毗賣) your principal wife, build a palace at the foot of 宇迦能山*, and live there." With that sword and bow 大穴牟遲神 drove away the Eighty Deities and for the first time made the land.
  2. Main textSusanoo-no-Mikoto set 大穴牟遲神* one ordeal after another: the chamber of snakes, the chamber of centipedes and bees, the field set on fire, and the picking of centipedes from his head. With the help of Suseri-bime's scarves (比礼), the tree nuts and red earth, and a mouse, 大穴牟遲神 got through them all.
  3. Main textThe Eighty Deities (八十神) killed 大穴牟遲神* twice, with a heated great rock and with a great tree into whose split a wedge had been driven. The first time, Kisakai-hime and Umukai-hime, sent by Kamumusuhi-no-Kami (神産巢日之命), brought 大穴牟遲神 back to life; the second time, 御祖命 * (his parent) did so.
  4. Main text大穴牟遲神* carried Suseri-bime on his back away from Susanoo-no-Mikoto's dwelling and fled, taking with him the living sword, the living bow and arrows, and the zither 天詔琴*. The zither touched a tree and the earth rang and roared, so the great deity woke, but because his hair was tied to the rafters he could not catch up with them.
  5. Compared with the Nihon ShokiIn the main text of section 8 of Book 1 of the Nihon Shoki, Ōanamuchi (大己貴神) is regarded as the child of Susanoo-no-Mikoto (素戔嗚尊) and 奇稻田姫*, but in the Kojiki Ōkuninushi-no-Kami is a descendant of Susanoo-no-Mikoto in the sixth generation (K115), and in this story he becomes Susanoo-no-Mikoto's son-in-law. Passages corresponding to the persecution by the Eighty Deities and the ordeals in the land 根の国* could not be confirmed in Books 1 and 2 of the Nihon Shoki.
  6. CommentaryAccording to the Kokugakuin University database of deity names, there are views that connect the two deaths and revivals and the ordeals in 根の堅州国* with coming-of-age rites, or that see them as a story of ordeals and growth leading to his becoming Ōkuninushi-no-Kami. The database also introduces a view that understands the change of his name from 大穴牟遲神* to Ōkuninushi-no-Kami as a story of growth into a king. [source, Japanese] [source, Japanese]

The story

The Eighty Deities (八十神) were angry and plotted to kill 大穴牟遲神*. At the foot of the mountain 手間の山 in 伯岐國 (readings unconfirmed) they said, "We will drive a red boar down from the mountain, so wait below and catch it. If you do not catch it, we will kill you," and they heated in fire a great rock resembling a boar and rolled it down. 大穴牟遲神 caught it, was burned fast to the rock, and died. His parent, 御祖命*, wept and grieved, went up to heaven and appealed to Kamumusuhi-no-Kami (神産巢日之命). Then Kisakai-hime and Umukai-hime were sent, and when Umukai-hime applied mother's milk, 大穴牟遲神 came back to life as a fine young man (麗壯夫) and went about once more.

Seeing this, the Eighty Deities again deceived 大穴牟遲神* and led him into the mountains. They cut down a great tree, drove a wedge into it, had him go into the split, and then knocked out the wedge and crushed him to death. 御祖命* searched for him weeping, and on finding him split the tree, took him out and brought him back to life, saying, "If you stay here, you will in the end be destroyed by the Eighty Deities," and hurriedly sent him away to Ōyabiko-no-Kami in 木國*. When the Eighty Deities came in pursuit, fitted arrows to their bows and demanded that he be handed over, 大穴牟遲神 was let slip away through the fork of a tree and told, "Go to 根堅州國*, where Susanoo-no-Mikoto (written 須佐能男命) is. That great deity will surely arrange things well for you."

When he went to Susanoo-no-Mikoto's place as he had been told, Susanoo-no-Mikoto's daughter Suseri-bime came out, and they exchanged glances and became husband and wife. Suseri-bime told her father, "A very splendid deity has come," and the great deity came out, looked, and said, "This is the one called 葦原色許男*." The great deity called him in and made him sleep in the chamber of snakes, but Suseri-bime gave him a snake scarf (比礼, a cloth) and taught him, "If the snakes try to bite you, wave this three times and drive them off," so the snakes grew quiet, and he slept safely and came out. The next night he was put in the chamber of centipedes and bees, but in the same way he was given a scarf and came out safely.

Next the great deity shot a whistling arrow (鳴鏑, an arrow that makes a sound) into a wide field and had him fetch the arrow. When 大穴牟遲神* went into the field, the great deity set fire to it all around and burned the field. While he could not find a way to escape, a mouse came and said, "Inside is 富良富良, outside is 須夫須夫," so he stamped on the spot, fell into a hole and was able to hide, and meanwhile the fire burned past. The mouse brought the whistling arrow in its mouth, but the arrow's feathers had been eaten by the mouse's young. Suseri-bime came weeping, carrying funeral implements, and the great deity too, thinking him dead, went out and stood in the field, but 大穴牟遲神 held out the arrow to him.

The great deity took 大穴牟遲神* back to his house, called him into a wide great chamber, and had him pick the lice from his head. When he looked, there were many centipedes on the head. His wife gave him nuts of the 牟久* tree and red earth, and when he chewed up the nuts, held the red earth in his mouth and spat it out, the great deity thought he was chewing up the centipedes and spitting them out, felt fondness for him in his heart, and fell asleep. Then 大穴牟遲神 tied the great deity's hair to each of the rafters of the chamber, blocked the door with a great rock that would take five hundred men to pull, took Suseri-bime on his back, and fled carrying the great deity's living sword (生大刀), living bow and arrows (生弓矢) and the zither 天詔琴*. At that moment the zither touched a tree and the earth rang and roared; the great deity woke in alarm and pulled down the chamber, but while he was untying his hair from the rafters, the two fled far away.

The great deity pursued them as far as 黄泉比良坂*, gazed at them from afar, and called out to 大穴牟遲神*: "With the living sword and the living bow and arrows that you carry, chase your half-brothers down to the foot of the slopes and drive them into the shallows of the rivers. Then you shall become Ōkuninushi-no-Kami and become 宇都志國主神*, make my daughter Suseri-bime (須世理毗賣) your principal wife, raise thick palace pillars at the foot of 宇迦能山*, make the crossed roof beams (千木) soar high enough to reach the High Plain of Heaven (高天原), and live there. You rascal!" With that sword and bow 大穴牟遲神 drove away the Eighty Deities, chasing them down at every slope and every river shallow, and for the first time made the land.

Yakami-hime, as she had promised before, was united with 大穴牟遲神* and was brought to him, but, in awe of his principal wife Suseri-bime (須世理毗賣), she placed the child she had borne in the fork of a tree and went back home. Therefore that child was named Kimata-no-Kami, also called Mii-no-Kami. After this the story continues with 八千矛神 * (another name of 大穴牟遲神) setting out to seek the hand of Nunakawa-hime of 高志國*, and the scene in which he sings songs at her house.

A plain retelling of what the original text of the Kojiki says (no published translation has been used).

In the original text

可參向須佐能男命所坐之根堅州國，必其大神議也

The words: Go to 根堅州國*, where Susanoo-no-Mikoto (written 須佐能男命) is. That great deity will surely arrange things well for you.

内者富良富良，外（者）須夫須夫。

The words the mouse taught 大穴牟遲神* in the burning field. A note in the original says that 富良富良 and 須夫須夫 are to be read by the sounds of the characters.

意礼為大國主神，亦為宇都志國主神

Susanoo-no-Mikoto's call: "You shall become Ōkuninushi-no-Kami, and also become 宇都志國主神*."

每坂御尾追伏，每河瀨追撥而始作國也

Chasing them down at the foot of every slope and driving them away at every river shallow, he for the first time made the land.

In the Nihon Shoki

Nihon Shoki, Book 1, section 8, main text

The main text says that Susanoo-no-Mikoto (素戔嗚尊) and 奇稻田姫* united and Ōanamuchi (大己貴神) was born, after which Susanoo-no-Mikoto went to 根國*. It does not have the plot of the Kojiki, in which Ōkuninushi-no-Kami is a descendant of Susanoo-no-Mikoto and becomes his son-in-law in the land 根の国*.

乃相與遘合而生兒大己貴神
Nihon Shoki, Book 1, section 8, variant account 2

Variant account 2 gives Ōanamuchi (大己貴命) as the grandson in the sixth generation of the child born of 奇稻田媛*. In making him a descendant many generations after Susanoo-no-Mikoto, it is close to the genealogy of the Kojiki (K115).

所生兒之六世孫。是曰大己貴命
Nihon Shoki, Book 1, section 8, variant account 6

Variant account 6 lists 葦原醜男, 八千戈神 and 顯國玉神 (readings unconfirmed), among others, as other names of Ōkuninushi-no-Kami, but passages corresponding to the persecution by the Eighty Deities and the ordeals in the land 根の国* could not be confirmed in the main text or the variant accounts of Books 1 and 2.
