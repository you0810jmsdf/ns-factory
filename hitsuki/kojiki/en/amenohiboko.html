Ame-no-Hiboko (天之日矛) and the Brothers of Autumn Mountain and Spring Mountain

天之日矛と秋山・春山の兄弟

Long ago, Ame-no-Hiboko (天之日矛), son of the ruler of Silla, crossed over to Japan in pursuit of his wife, who had been born from a red jewel; he settled in 多遅摩*, and from his descendants came the mother of 息長帯比売命*. The eight treasures Ame-no-Hiboko brought are regarded as Izushi-Yamae-no-Ōkami (伊豆志之八前大神), and over that deity's daughter, Izushi-otome-no-Kami (伊豆志袁登売神), the brothers Akiyama-no-Shitahi-otoko (秋山之下氷壮夫) and Haruyama-no-Kasumi-otoko (春山之霞壮夫) made a wager; the elder brother, who broke his promise, suffered for eight years under his mother's curse.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textAme-no-Hiboko (天之日矛) was the son of the ruler of Silla. He crossed over in pursuit of his wife, who had been born from a red jewel borne by a woman touched by the light of the sun, and who had fled to Naniwa. But he was blocked by the deity of the crossing and could not enter Naniwa, so he stayed in the province of 多遅摩*.
  2. Main textBetween 多遅摩比多訶 and 由良度美*, descendants of Ame-no-Hiboko (天之日矛), was born 葛城之高額比売命*, and her child is 息長帯比売命* (Empress Jingū). In the Kojiki, 息長帯比売命, who crossed over to Silla, is regarded as a descendant of Ame-no-Hiboko.
  3. Main textA note states that the eight treasures brought by Ame-no-Hiboko (天之日矛) (two strings of beads, four kinds of scarves (比礼) and the 奥津鏡 and 辺津鏡 mirrors*) are Izushi-Yamae-no-Ōkami (伊豆志之八前大神).
  4. Main textHaruyama-no-Kasumi-otoko (春山之霞壮夫) was united with Izushi-otome-no-Kami (伊豆志袁登売神) by means of the wisteria clothes and the bow and arrows his mother made; his elder brother Akiyama-no-Shitahi-otoko (秋山之下氷壮夫), who did not pay the stakes of the wager, fell ill for eight years under his mother's curse, and only after weeping and apologizing was he restored.
  5. Compared with the Nihon ShokiIn the Nihon Shoki (Book 6), the arrival of Ame-no-Hiboko (天日槍) takes place in the 3rd month of the 3rd year of Emperor Suinin, and the treasures are seven kinds, 羽太玉, 足高玉, 鵜鹿鹿赤石玉, 出石小刀, 出石桙, 日鏡 and 熊神籬*, which were deposited in Tajima Province. In the genealogy of an alternative account (一云) in a small-script note, 清彦* is the father of 田道間守*, which differs from the Kojiki, where 清日子 and 多遅麻毛理* are brothers.
  6. CommentaryAccording to the Kokugakuin University deity-name database, the story of the autumn-mountain and spring-mountain brothers is found only in the Kojiki. It has many points in common with the story of 海幸 and 山幸* at the end of Part 1, such as the younger brother defeating the elder, the use of bamboo work, and submission brought about by the ebb and flow of the tide, and the theory that the two were deliberately placed at the ends of Part 1 and Part 2 is regarded as influential. The story

Long ago, a man named Ame-no-Hiboko (天之日矛), son of the ruler of Silla, crossed over to Japan. The reason was this. Beside a marsh called 阿具奴摩* in Silla, a lowly woman was taking a midday nap when the light of the sun shone like a rainbow on her private parts. A lowly man who found this strange kept constant watch over the woman. From the time of that nap the woman became pregnant and gave birth to a red jewel. The man obtained the jewel and always kept it wrapped and tied at his waist.

Because the man had fields in a valley, he loaded an ox with food for the people working in the fields and went into the valley, where he met Ame-no-Hiboko (天之日矛). Ame-no-Hiboko said, "You mean to kill this ox and eat it," and seized the man, intending to put him in prison. The man answered that he was only carrying food for the people in the fields, but he was not released; he offered Ame-no-Hiboko the jewel from his waist and was released. When Ame-no-Hiboko placed the jewel beside his bed, it became a beautiful maiden, so he made her his chief wife.

His wife always prepared all kinds of fine dishes for her husband to eat, but Ame-no-Hiboko (天之日矛) grew arrogant and reviled her. His wife said, "I am not a woman who should be your wife. I will go to the land of my ancestors," and secretly boarded a small boat, fled across, and stayed at Naniwa (a note says she is Akaru-hime-no-Kami (阿加流比売神), who dwells at the 比売碁曽社* shrine in Naniwa). When Ame-no-Hiboko heard that his wife had fled, he crossed over in pursuit, but as he was about to reach Naniwa, the deity of the crossing blocked the way and would not let him in. So he turned back, anchored in the province of 多遅摩*, and stayed there.

Ame-no-Hiboko (天之日矛) married 前津見*, daughter of 多遅摩之俣尾*, and his descendants continued through 多遅摩母呂須玖, 多遅摩斐泥 and 多遅摩比那良岐*, whose children were the three 多遅麻毛理, 多遅摩比多訶 and 清日子*. 葛城之高額比売命*, born when 多遅摩比多訶 married his niece 由良度美*, was the mother of 息長帯比売命*. The treasures Ame-no-Hiboko brought were called the jewel treasures (玉津宝): two strings of beads; the 振浪比礼, 切浪比礼, 振風比礼 and 切風比礼 scarves; and the 奥津鏡 and 辺津鏡 mirrors*, eight kinds in all; a note says that these are Izushi-Yamae-no-Ōkami (伊豆志之八前大神).

This deity had a daughter, Izushi-otome-no-Kami (伊豆志袁登売神). Many deities wished to marry her, but none could. The elder brother, Akiyama-no-Shitahi-otoko (秋山之下氷壮夫), asked his younger brother, Haruyama-no-Kasumi-otoko (春山之霞壮夫), "I sought her hand and failed. Can you win this maiden?" When the younger brother answered, "Easily," the elder brother said, "If you win her, I will take off my upper and lower garments and give them to you, brew sake in a jar as tall as my own height, and prepare everything from the mountains and rivers," and made a 宇礼豆玖* (wager). When the younger brother told his mother, his mother took 布遅葛* vine and in a single night wove and sewed a robe, trousers, socks and shoes, and also made a bow and arrows; she had the younger brother put them on and sent him to the maiden's house. Then the clothes and the bow and arrows all turned into wisteria flowers.

Haruyama-no-Kasumi-otoko (春山之霞壮夫) hung the bow and arrows at the maiden's privy, and Izushi-otome-no-Kami (伊豆志袁登売神), finding the flowers strange, was about to take them home. The younger brother followed behind her into the house and married her, and one child was born. But the elder brother, jealous of his younger brother's marriage, did not pay the stakes of the wager. Their mother, resentful of her elder son, had a coarse basket with eight meshes (八目荒籠) made from the bamboo of an island in the Izushi river (伊豆志河), mixed stones from the river with salt, wrapped them in bamboo leaves, had a curse laid saying, "As these bamboo leaves grow green and wither, so grow green and wither. As this salt ebbs and flows, so ebb and flow. As these stones sink, so sink and lie prostrate," and placed it above the smoke. Because of this the elder brother withered, sickened and wasted away for eight years; but when he wept and pleaded with his mother, she had the cursing objects returned, and the elder brother's body became well again as before. A note says this is the origin of the expression "神宇礼豆玖"*.

Also, 若野毛二俣王*, son of Emperor 品陀*, married his mother's younger sister 百師木伊呂弁* (also named 弟日売真若比売命), and seven children were born, including 大郎子 (also named 意富富杼王) and 忍坂之大中津比売命*. 意富富杼王 is regarded as the ancestor of the 三国君, the 息長君* and others. Emperor 品陀 (Emperor Ōjin) died at the age of 130, and his tomb is at the 裳伏岡 of 恵賀 in 川内*.

A plain retelling of what the original text of the Kojiki says (no published translation has been used).

In the original text

日耀如虹指其陰上

The light of the sun shone like a rainbow on the woman's private parts (what led to the birth of the red jewel).

置於床邊。即化美麗孃子

When he placed the jewel beside his bed, it changed into a beautiful maiden.

其衣服及弓矢。悉成藤花

The clothes and the bow and arrows that the mother made all turned into wisteria flowers.

如此竹葉青。如此竹葉萎而。青萎

As these bamboo leaves grow green and wither, so grow green and wither (part of the words of the curse the mother had laid on the elder brother).

In the Nihon Shoki

Nihon Shoki, Book 6 — the 3rd year of Emperor Suinin, spring, 3rd month

In the Nihon Shoki, Ame-no-Hiboko (天日槍) is said to have come in the 3rd year of Emperor Suinin, and the treasures he brought were seven kinds, which were deposited in Tajima Province as the property of the deity. An alternative account (一云) in a small-script note says that he first arrived in Harima and came after yielding his land to his younger brother on hearing that there was a sage ruler (聖皇) in Japan, and gives the treasures as eight kinds. It does not say, as the Kojiki does, that he came in pursuit of his wife.

新羅王子天日槍來歸焉
Nihon Shoki, Book 6 — "this year" (是歳) of the 2nd year of Emperor Suinin (alternative account (一云) in a small-script note)

In the Nihon Shoki, a story in which a white stone turns into a beautiful maiden, who leaves and becomes the deity of the 比売語曾社* shrine in Naniwa, appears in an alternative account (一云) in a small-script note, not as a story of Ame-no-Hiboko but as a story of 都怒我阿羅斯等*, son of the king of the land of 意富加羅*. Its plot resembles the Kojiki story of Akaru-hime-no-Kami (阿加流比売神), in which a red jewel becomes a maiden who dwells at the 比売碁曽社 shrine in Naniwa.

所求童女者。詣于難波爲比賣語曾社神
Nihon Shoki, Book 6 — the 88th year of Emperor Suinin, autumn, 7th month (己酉朔戊午)

In the Nihon Shoki, the emperor says he wishes to see the divine treasures of Tajima and has 清彦*, great-grandson of Ame-no-Hiboko, present them. Only the small sword of 出石* vanished and appeared on Awaji Island, where the islanders enshrined it. This story of the presentation is not in the Kojiki, and in the Kojiki 清日子* is a descendant of Ame-no-Hiboko four generations later.

詔天日槍之曾孫清彦而令獻
