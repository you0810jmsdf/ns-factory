Hitokotonushi-no-Kami of Katsuragi and the Songs of Emperor Yūryaku

葛城の一言主大神と雄略天皇の歌

On Mount Katsuragi, Emperor Yūryaku met a procession exactly like his own; learning that its master was Hitokotonushi-no-Kami of Katsuragi (葛城之一言主大神), he was filled with awe and presented swords, bows and arrows, and the clothing of his officials. This section also contains the emperor’s song when chased by a boar, the story of 袁杼比売* and the 金鉏岡 hill*, and the 天語歌* of the palace attendant from Mie, and it records that the emperor died at the age of one hundred and twenty-four.

Read in the Nihon Shoki: The accession of Emperor Yūryaku and Hitokotonushi-no-Kami (一事主神) of 葛城山* ›

Key points to remember

  1. Main textOn Mount Katsuragi he met a company with the same attire and the same number of people as the emperor’s, and when he asked its name, the other named himself “Hitokotonushi-no-Kami of Katsuragi (葛城之一言主大神)”. The emperor, filled with awe, presented swords, bows and arrows and the clothing of his officials, and the Great Deity escorted the emperor as far as the entrance to the mountains of 長谷*.
  2. Compared with the Nihon ShokiIn the Nihon Shoki (the 4th year of Emperor Yūryaku, spring, 2nd month), the deity 一事主神* appears as a “tall man” (長人) resembling the emperor, hunts side by side with the emperor and escorts him as far as 来目水*, and the people are said to have called him “an emperor of virtue” (有徳天皇).
  3. Main textThe palace attendant (婇) from Mie offered sake with a zelkova (槻) leaf still floating in the cup and was nearly beheaded, but she was pardoned for offering a song and was even given rewards. Her song and the songs of the chief consort and the emperor, three in all, are called 天語歌*.
  4. CommentaryThe reading and meaning of “言離之神”, the words with which Hitokotonushi describes himself, are disputed, with views such as kotosaka, ihihanatsu and kotohanatsu, and this is regarded as bearing heavily on the interpretation of the deity’s name. [source, Japanese]
  5. CommentaryKokugakuin University’s database of deity names introduces the observation that, because in the Nihon Shoki the emperor refers to himself as “朕” and the deity refers to himself as “僕”, the Nihon Shoki portrays the emperor as the superior. [source, Japanese]
  6. Main textIt is recorded that the emperor lived to the age of one hundred and twenty-four and that his tomb is at 多治比高鸇* in Kawachi.

The story

Once, when the emperor climbed to the top of a mountain in Katsuragi, a great boar came out. When the emperor shot it with a whistling arrow (鳴鏑), the boar came at him in anger, “宇多岐”*; fearing it, the emperor climbed up into a hazel (榛) tree and composed a song.

Another time, when the emperor climbed Mount Katsuragi, all his officials wore blue-printed (青摺) robes with red cords. At that moment a company exactly like the emperor’s procession, in both attire and number, was climbing the ridge of the mountain opposite. When the emperor had them asked, “In this land of Yamato (倭) there is no king but me, so who goes along like this?”, the other side answered in the same words as the emperor. The emperor was greatly enraged and fitted an arrow to his bow, and when all his officials did the same, the people on the other side all fitted arrows too.

When the emperor said they should give their names, and each side was about to name itself and loose its arrows, the other answered that, since he had been asked first, he would name himself first: he was the deity who pronounces bad things with one word and good things with one word, Hitokotonushi-no-Kami of Katsuragi (葛城之一言主大神). The emperor was filled with awe and said that he had not recognised him because he was “宇都志意美”*, and he presented swords and bows and arrows and had his officials take off their clothing and offered it as well. Hitokotonushi clapped his hands and accepted the offerings, and when the emperor returned, escorted him as far as the entrance to the mountains of 長谷*. It is recorded that this Hitokotonushi appeared at this time.

The emperor went to Kasuga (春日) intending to marry 袁杼比売*, daughter of 佐都紀臣 of 丸邇 (readings unconfirmed). When 袁杼比売 met the procession on the road, she ran off and hid beside a hill, so the emperor composed a song. For that reason the hill is called 金鉏岡*.

When the emperor held a drinking party under the 百枝槻 zelkova* at 長谷*, the palace attendant (婇) from Mie in Ise Province held up the great sake cup, but offered the sake without noticing that a zelkova leaf had fallen and was floating in the cup. Seeing this, the emperor struck the attendant down, put his blade to her neck and was about to behead her, but she said, “Do not kill me. I have something to say,” and composed a song. Because she offered this song, her offence was pardoned, and then the chief consort and the emperor also composed songs. These three songs are called 天語歌*, and the emperor praised the attendant from Mie and gave her many gifts.

On the day of the same drinking party, when 袁杼比売* of Kasuga offered the great sake, the emperor sang (宇岐歌)*, and 袁杼比売 also offered a song (志都歌)*. It is recorded that the emperor lived to the age of one hundred and twenty-four and that his tomb is at 多治比高鸇* in Kawachi.

A plain retelling of what the original text of the Kojiki says (no published translation has been used).

In the original text

於茲倭國。除吾亦無王。

The emperor’s words: In this land of Yamato (倭) there is no king but me.

雖惡事而一言。雖善事而一言。言離之神。葛城之一言主大神者也。

Hitokotonushi’s self-naming: I am the deity who pronounces bad things with one word and good things with one word, Hitokotonushi-no-Kami of Katsuragi (葛城之一言主大神).

故獻此歌者。赦其罪也。

Because the palace attendant from Mie offered this song, her offence was pardoned.

天皇。御年。壹佰貳拾肆歲。

The emperor lived to the age of one hundred and twenty-four.

Deities and people, and the shrines that enshrine them

  • Hitokotonushi-no-Kami一言主神Enshrined at: Tosa Jinja
  • The emperor (大長谷若建命*, Emperor Yūryaku)On Mount Katsuragi he meets a boar and Hitokotonushi, and composes many songs.
  • Great boar (大猪)Shot by the emperor on Mount Katsuragi, it comes at him in anger. The emperor escapes by climbing a hazel tree.
  • Whistling arrow (鳴鏑)The sounding arrow with which the emperor shot the boar.
  • The officials (百官人等)They attend the emperor in blue-printed robes with red cords, and are later made to present their clothing to Hitokotonushi.
  • 袁杼比賣*Daughter of 佐都紀臣 of 丸邇 (readings unconfirmed). She hides beside a hill on seeing the procession, and later offers a song at a drinking party.
  • 金鉏岡*The hill where 袁杼比売* hid. It is said to have been named after the emperor’s song.
  • The palace attendant from Mie (三重婇)A palace attendant from Mie in Ise Province. Failing to notice a zelkova leaf floating in the cup, she is nearly beheaded, but is pardoned for her song.
  • 百枝槻*The zelkova tree at the site of the drinking party at 長谷*. Its leaf falls into the cup.
  • The chief consort (大后)She composes one of the 天語歌* songs at the drinking party.

In the Nihon Shoki

Nihon Shoki, Book 14, the 4th year of Emperor Yūryaku, spring, 2nd month

In the Nihon Shoki, while the emperor is hunting on Mount Katsuragi, a tall man resembling the emperor appears, names himself “一事主神”*, hunts together with the emperor, and at dusk escorts him as far as 来目水*. There is no scene, as in the Kojiki, in which the emperor is filled with awe and presents clothing.

僕是一事主神也。
Nihon Shoki, Book 14, the 5th year of Emperor Yūryaku, spring, 2nd month

In the Nihon Shoki an enraged boar also appears on Mount Katsuragi, but the emperor stops it with his bow and tramples it to death, and it is an attendant (舎人) who climbs a tree to escape. A song closely resembling the one the emperor composes in the Kojiki is attributed to the attendant when he is about to be executed, and the attendant is pardoned at the empress’s remonstrance.

舍人臨刑而作歌曰。
Nihon Shoki, Book 15, the 1st year of Emperor Seinei, winter, 10th month (癸巳朔辛丑)

In the Nihon Shoki, Emperor Yūryaku dies in the great hall in the 8th month of the 23rd year and is buried in the 丹比高鷲原 tomb* in the 10th month, winter, of the 1st year of Emperor Seinei. The place name of the tomb corresponds to the Kojiki’s “多治比高鸇”*, but no figure for his age like the Kojiki’s “one hundred and twenty-four” appears in the Nihon Shoki’s entry on his death.

葬大泊瀬天皇于丹比高鷲原陵。
