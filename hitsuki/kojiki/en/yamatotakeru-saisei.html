The Western Campaign of 小碓命* (熊曾建* and Izumo Takeru)

小碓命の西征（熊曾建と出雲建）

小碓命*, son of Emperor Keikō (大帶日子淤斯呂和氣天皇*), came to be feared by his father after violently killing his elder brother 大碓命*, and was sent to strike down the 熊曾建* brothers in the west. Disguised as a young girl, he slipped into their feast and killed the brothers, received the name "倭建" (Yamato Takeru) offered to him by 熊曾建, and on the way back also killed Izumo Takeru (出雲建) by deceit.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textOrdered to "泥疑"* his elder brother 大碓命*, 小碓命* seized his brother in the privy, tore off his limbs, wrapped him in a straw mat (薦) and threw him away. The emperor feared his violent heart and sent him to subdue the 熊曾建* in the west.
  2. Main textDisguising himself as a young girl by putting on the robe and skirt (御衣御裳) of his aunt 倭比賣命*, 小碓命 slipped into the 熊曾建* brothers' "御室樂"* feast and stabbed the brothers with his sword.
  3. Main textAs he was dying, 熊曾建* offered him the name "倭建御子" (Prince Yamato Takeru), and from then on 小碓命 (倭男具那王)* was called Yamato Takeru (倭建命).
  4. Main textIn Izumo on the way back, he became friends with Izumo Takeru (出雲建), made him exchange his own sword for a fake sword of 赤檮 wood (詐刀), and struck down Izumo Takeru, who could not draw the fake sword.
  5. Compared with the Nihon ShokiIn the Nihon Shoki, Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto (日本武尊) goes to attack the Kumaso in the 10th month of the 27th year of Emperor Keikō, at the age of sixteen, and the one he strikes down is 川上梟帥* alone. The story of Izumo Takeru is not in the Keikō chapter; a similar killing by deceit using a wooden sword appears in the 60th year of Emperor Sujin as the story of 出雲振根*.
  6. CommentaryConcerning the "Anato-no-Kami" (穴戸神) whom he subdued (言向) on the way back, the Kokugakuin University deity-name database introduces the view that it is 穴門* in Nagato Province, the view that it is 穴海* in Kibi Province, and the view that it is a word for straits in general and not a proper noun. The Nihon Shoki says that on this return journey he killed the evil deities of 穴濟 in Kibi and of 柏濟 in Naniwa*. [source, Japanese]

The story

Emperor Keikō ruled the realm from the 日代宮 of 纒向*. He married 針間之伊那毘能大郎女*, and 櫛角別王, 大碓命, 小碓命 (also named 倭男具那命), 倭根子命 and 神櫛王* were born. His children numbered eighty in all: twenty-one recorded princes and princesses and fifty-nine not recorded. Three of them, 若帶日子命, Yamato Takeru (倭建命) and 五百木之入日子命*, bore the title of crown prince, and the remaining seventy-seven were distributed among the provinces as governors (國造), 和氣, 稻置 and district chiefs (縣主). The original text first sums up that 若帶日子命 ruled the realm and that 小碓命 subdued the unruly deities and the unsubmissive people of east and west.

The emperor heard that the two daughters of 神大根王*, ancestor of the governors of 三野國*, 兄比賣 and 弟比賣*, were beautiful, and sent 大碓命* to bring them to court. But 大碓命 did not bring the two to court; he took them as his own wives, found other women, and presented them falsely under their names. The emperor knew they were other women and did not marry them (original text: "恒令經長眼。亦勿婚而惚也"). In this reign the 田部, the 淡水門 of the east, the 膳之大伴部 and the 倭屯家* were established, and the 坂手 Pond* was made.

The emperor asked 小碓命* why his elder brother did not come out to the morning and evening imperial meals, and ordered him to "泥疑"* him and teach him. When five days had passed and his brother still did not come out, the emperor asked, and 小碓命 answered that he had already "泥疑"-ed him. Asked how he had done it, he answered that at dawn, when his brother went into the privy, he had lain in wait, seized him and crushed him, torn off his limbs, wrapped him in a straw mat and thrown him away. The emperor feared his son's fierce and violent heart and sent him to strike down the two 熊曾建* in the west, who were unsubmissive and lacking in respect. At this time 小碓命's hair was still tied up on his forehead.

小碓命* received a robe and a skirt from his aunt 倭比賣命*, put a small sword in the fold of his garment, and set out. When he reached the house of the 熊曾建* and looked, an army surrounded the house in three rings, and the 熊曾建 had built a chamber (室) and were in it, clamoring that they would hold a "御室樂"* feast and preparing food. On the day of the feast, 小碓命 combed down his tied-up hair like a young girl's, put on his aunt's robe and skirt, took on the appearance of a young girl, and entered the chamber mixed among the women. The 熊曾建 brothers were charmed by this girl, seated her between the two of them, and made merry.

When the feast was at its height, 小碓命* drew the sword from the fold of his garment, seized 熊曾建* by the collar of his clothes and stabbed him through the chest. The younger 建 fled in fear, but 小碓命 chased him to the "椅本"* of the chamber, seized him by the back, and stabbed the sword through him from the buttocks. 熊曾建 said, "Do not move the sword; I have something to say," and asked his name. When 小碓命 declared that he was 倭男具那王*, son of the emperor who rules the Great Land of Eight Islands (大八嶋國) from the 日代宮 of 纒向*, 熊曾建 said that in the west there was no one stronger than the two of them, but that in the land of Great Yamato (大倭國) there was a strong man who surpassed them, and he offered the name "倭建御子" (Prince Yamato Takeru). When he had finished speaking, 小碓命 cut him down as one splits a ripe melon, and from this time he was called Yamato Takeru (倭建命).

On the way back to the capital, Yamato Takeru subdued and pacified (言向和) all the mountain deities, the river deities and Anato-no-Kami (穴戸神). On entering the province of Izumo, intending to kill Izumo Takeru (出雲建), he first became his friend, and secretly made a fake sword (詐刀) of 赤檮* wood and wore it. When the two bathed in the 肥河*, Yamato Takeru came up out of the river first, took and put on the sword that Izumo Takeru had untied and laid aside, and said, "Let us exchange swords." When Izumo Takeru, coming up afterwards, put on Yamato Takeru's fake sword, Yamato Takeru challenged him to cross swords; Izumo Takeru could not draw the fake sword and was struck down by Yamato Takeru. Yamato Takeru composed a song at this time, and having thus pacified the land, he returned to the capital and reported.

In the original text

朝署入廁之時。持捕搤㧗而。引闕其枝。裹薦投棄。

The answer of 小碓命*: at dawn, when his brother went into the privy, he seized him and crushed him, tore off his limbs, wrapped him in a straw mat and threw him away.

服其姨之御衣御裳。既成童女之姿。

He put on his aunt's robe and skirt and took on the full appearance of a young girl.

吾獻御名。自今以後。應稱倭建御子。

The words of 熊曾建*: "I will offer you a name. From now on it is fitting that you be called 倭建御子 (Prince Yamato Takeru)."

In the Nihon Shoki

Nihon Shoki, Book 7 — the 27th year of Emperor Keikō, winter, 10th month (丁酉朔己酉)

In the Nihon Shoki, because the Kumaso rebelled again, Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto (日本武尊) was sent in the 10th month of the 27th year of Emperor Keikō, and he was sixteen at the time. Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto takes with him 弟彦公*, a master archer of Mino, and others. The Kojiki's account, in which the killing of his elder brother 大碓命* was what led to his dispatch, does not appear in this entry.

遣日本武尊令撃熊襲。時年十六。
Nihon Shoki, Book 7 — the 27th year of Emperor Keikō, 12th month

In the Nihon Shoki, the one struck down is the Kumaso chief 取石鹿文 (川上梟帥)* alone, and Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto untied his hair and entered the feasting chamber in the guise of a young girl. As he was dying, 川上梟帥 offered him the title "日本武皇子" (Prince Yamato Takeru).

自今以後號皇子。應稱日本武皇子。
Nihon Shoki, Book 5 — the 60th year of Emperor Sujin, autumn, 7th month (丙申朔己酉)

There is no story of Izumo Takeru in the Keikō chapter. In the Sujin chapter there is a story in which 出雲振根* makes a wooden sword, bathes with his younger brother 飯入根 at the 止屋淵*, exchanges it for a real sword and kills his brother; the song of the people of the time also begins with words closely resembling Yamato Takeru's song in the Kojiki.

兄竊作木刀。形似眞刀。
