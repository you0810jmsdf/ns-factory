The Jealousy of 石之日売命* and 黒日売*

石之日売命の嫉妬と黒日売

Fearing the jealousy of the chief consort 石之日売命*, 黒日売*, who had been summoned from Kibi, fled back to her home, and the chief consort had her put off the boat and made her walk home. The emperor, falsely saying he was going to view 淡道嶋*, went all the way to Kibi to visit 黒日売, and the two exchanged songs.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textThe chief consort 石之日売命* was extremely jealous, and 黒日売*, daughter of the 海部直* of Kibi, who had been summoned by the emperor, fled back to her home in fear of the chief consort. Still angry, the chief consort had 黒日売 driven off the boat at 大浦* and made her walk home.
  2. Main textThe emperor deceived the chief consort, saying, "I wish to view 淡道嶋"*, and set out; after gazing far out from 淡道嶋 and singing, he went from island to island to the province of Kibi and met 黒日売*.
  3. Main textIn Kibi, 黒日売* welcomed the emperor at a place in the 山方* and offered him the imperial meal, and the emperor sang at the place where the 菘菜* greens were being picked. When the emperor returned, 黒日売 presented two songs.
  4. CommentaryAccording to the Kokugakuin University deity-name database, the song the emperor sang on 淡道嶋* is thought to be a land-viewing (国見) song set against the background of the myth of the birth of the islands, and there is a theory that Onogoroshima (淤能碁呂島) and Awashima (淡島) were included in it as mythical islands representing the land ruled by the royal power. There is also a theory that takes "佐気都志摩"* ("detached island") in the song to be 吉備児島*, showing the bond between the royal power and Kibi. [source, Japanese] [source, Japanese]
  5. CommentaryThe entry for 須勢理毘売* in the same database cites the story of 石之日売命* as a tradition of a principal wife who is jealous of other wives, and introduces a theory that sees it not as a mere love story but as telling of a struggle for leadership of rituals among women who, like shrine maidens (巫女), were in charge of worship. [source, Japanese]
  6. Compared with the Nihon ShokiThe story of 黒日売* could not be confirmed in Book 11 (Emperor Nintoku) of the Nihon Shoki. However, in an entry for the 7th month of the 16th year, the emperor says that because of the empress's jealousy he has been unable to be united with the palace lady 桑田玖賀媛*, and gives her to 速待*, ancestor of the governors of Harima (播磨国造).

The story

The chief consort 石之日売命* was extremely jealous, and the concubines the emperor kept in his service could not even peep into the palace. The text records that whenever there was anything to be said, the chief consort was so jealous that she stamped her feet.

The emperor heard of 黒日売*, daughter of the 海部直* of Kibi, who was beautiful in appearance, summoned her, and kept her in his service. But 黒日売, fearing the chief consort's jealousy, fled down to her home province. The emperor, from a high terrace, watched 黒日売's boat floating out onto the sea, and sang a song.

Hearing this song, the chief consort was furious; she sent people to 大浦* to drive 黒日売* off the boat and made her walk home. The emperor, longing for 黒日売, deceived the chief consort, saying, "I wish to view 淡道嶋"*, and set out. On reaching 淡道嶋, he gazed far out and sang a song. That song includes the names of islands such as Awashima (阿波志摩, 淡島) and Onogoroshima (淤能碁呂志摩).

From that island the emperor went from island to island to the province of Kibi. 黒日売* welcomed the emperor at a place in the 山方* of that province and offered him the imperial meal. When 黒日売 was picking the 菘菜* greens of that land to cook the imperial soup (大御羹), the emperor came to the place where she was picking and sang a song.

When the emperor returned to the capital, 黒日売* presented a song, and then sang one more. Both songs begin with the words "夜麻登幣邇"* ("toward Yamato").

In the Nihon Shoki

Nihon Shoki, Book 11 — the 16th year of Emperor Nintoku, autumn, 7th month (戊寅朔)

The story of 黒日売* could not be confirmed in Book 11 of the Nihon Shoki. Instead there is a story in which, because of the empress's jealousy, the emperor has been unable to be united with the palace lady 桑田玖賀媛*; 玖賀媛 was given to 速待*, but died of illness on the way while being sent to 桑田.

苦皇后之妬不能合
Nihon Shoki, Book 11 — the 2nd year of Emperor Nintoku, spring, 3rd month (辛未朔戊寅)

The Nihon Shoki writes the chief consort's name as "磐之媛命"* and records the year and month, stating that she was made empress in the 3rd month of the 2nd year after his accession.
