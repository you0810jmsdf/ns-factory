Emperor Sujin and Ōmononushi-no-Ōkami of Miwa

崇神天皇と三輪の大物主大神

When a pestilence spread in the time of Emperor Sujin, Ōmononushi-no-Ōkami (大物主大神) appeared in a dream and declared that if he were worshipped by 意富多多泥古*, the land would be at peace; when he was worshipped accordingly, the pestilence ceased. As the reason 意富多多泥古 was known to be a descendant of the deity, the story is told that when the man who visited 活玉依毘賣* was traced by a hemp thread, it led to the shrine on Mount Miwa (美和山).

Key points to remember

  1. Main textIn the time of Emperor Sujin, when people were on the verge of dying out from pestilence, Ōmononushi-no-Ōkami declared in a dream, “This comes from my will,” and asked to be worshipped by 意富多多泥古*. When the emperor sought out 意富多多泥古 from the village of 美努* in Kawachi and had him worship as the officiating priest at Mount 御諸*, the pestilence ceased.
  2. Main textA hemp thread was stuck into the garment of the man who visited 活玉依毘賣* night after night, and when the thread was followed, it led to the shrine of the deity of Mount Miwa (美和山). It is recorded that because three loops (三勾) of hemp were left over, that place is called “Miwa” (美和).
  3. Main textTo quell the pestilence, shrines for the heavenly and earthly deities (天神地祇) were established besides Ōmononushi-no-Ōkami; red shields and spears were offered to Sumisaka-no-Kami (墨坂神) of Uda and black shields and spears to Ōsaka-no-Kami (大坂神), and offerings (幣帛) were also made to the deities of the slopes and of the river shallows.
  4. Compared with the Nihon ShokiIn the Nihon Shoki, Ōmononushi-no-Kami first possesses Yamato-totohi-momoso-hime-no-Mikoto (倭迹迹日百襲姫命) and demands worship, and 大田田根子 * (意富多多泥古) is found at 陶邑* in 茅渟県* and gives his name as the child of Ōmononushi-no-Ōkami and 活玉依媛*. In the Kojiki, he is found in the village of 美努* in Kawachi and is a descendant in the fourth generation from Ōmononushi-no-Ōkami.
  5. CommentaryThe Kokugakuin University database of deity names introduces a view that the Kojiki's depiction of Ōmononushi-no-Kami going out through the keyhole of the door hints that his form is that of a snake, and states that in the record of the 9th month of the 10th year of Emperor Sujin in the Nihon Shoki it is revealed as a “small snake” (小蛇). [source, Japanese]
  6. CommentaryThe Kokugakuin University database of deity names introduces a view that the worship of the deities of 墨坂 (Sumisaka; an important route on the east of Yamato) and 大坂 (Ōsaka; an important route on the west of Yamato) was the worship of boundary deities to prevent the pestilence from entering. [source, Japanese] [source, Japanese]

The story

御眞木入日子印惠命 * (Emperor Sujin) ruled the realm from the 水垣宮* at 師木*. With three wives he had twelve children (seven sons and five daughters), and 伊久米伊理毘古伊佐知命* succeeded him. Toyokiirihiko-no-Mikoto (豊木入日子命) is regarded as the ancestor of the 上毛野君, the 下毛野君 (readings unconfirmed) and others; 豊鉏比賣命* worshipped at the shrine of the Great Deity of Ise (伊勢大神); and 大入杵命* is regarded as the ancestor of the 能登臣*. It is recorded that in the time of 倭日子命* a human fence (人垣) was first set up at a tomb.

In the time of this emperor much pestilence arose, and the people were on the verge of dying out. On a night when the emperor, grieving, was in the sacred bed (神牀), Ōmononushi-no-Ōkami appeared in a dream and declared, “This comes from my will. If you have me worshipped by 意富多多泥古*, the deity's influence will not arise and the land will be at peace.” So swift messengers on horseback were sent out in all directions to search, and that person was found in the village of 美努* in Kawachi and brought forward.

When the emperor asked whose child he was, 意富多多泥古* answered that he was the child of 建甕槌命*, son of 飯肩巣見命*, son of 櫛御方命*, who had been born when Ōmononushi-no-Ōkami took as his wife 活玉依毘賣*, daughter of 陶津耳命*. The emperor was greatly pleased and said that the realm would be at peace and the people would prosper. He then made 意富多多泥古 the officiating priest and had him worship 意富美和之大神* at Mount 御諸*.

Further, he commanded 伊迦賀色許男命* to make the 天之八十毘羅訶* and established shrines for the heavenly and earthly deities (天神地祇). He offered red shields and spears to Sumisaka-no-Kami (墨坂神) of Uda and black shields and spears to Ōsaka-no-Kami (大坂神), and made offerings (幣帛), leaving none out, to the deities of the ridges of the slopes and to the deities of the river shallows. By this the pestilence ceased entirely and the land was at peace.

The reason 意富多多泥古* was known to be the child of a deity is as follows. 活玉依毘賣* was beautiful, and a splendid man beyond compare came visiting her in the night, and soon she conceived. When her parents, finding this strange, asked her about it, she answered that a man whose name she did not know came every night, so her parents told her to scatter red earth in front of the bed and to stick a needle threaded with hemp thread into the hem of the man's garment. When they looked in the morning, the hemp thread had gone out through the keyhole of the door, and only three loops (三勾) of hemp were left in her hands. When they followed the thread, it led to the shrine of the deity of Mount Miwa (美和山), so they knew he was the child of a deity, and because three loops of hemp were left over, that place is called Miwa (美和). A note in the original text makes 意富多多泥古命 the ancestor of the 神君 and the 鴨君 (readings unconfirmed).

A plain retelling of what the original text of the Kojiki says (no published translation has been used).

In the original text

疫病多起。人民死爲盡。

Much pestilence arose, and the people were on the verge of dying out.

以意富多多泥古而。令祭我御前者。神氣不起。國安平。

The oracle of Ōmononushi-no-Ōkami: if you have me worshipped by 意富多多泥古*, the deity's influence will not arise and the land will be at peace.

以閇蘇紡麻貫針。刺其衣襴。

The parents' instruction: thread a needle with hemp thread of “閇蘇” (a word written phonetically) and stick it into the hem of the man's garment.

因其麻之三勾遺而。名其地謂美和也。

Because three loops of hemp were left over, that place was named Miwa (美和).

In the Nihon Shoki

Nihon Shoki, Book 5, Emperor Sujin, 7th year, spring, 2nd month (丁丑朔辛卯)

In the Nihon Shoki, when divination is performed at 神浅茅原* in the 2nd month of the 7th year of Emperor Sujin, Ōmononushi-no-Kami (大物主神) first possesses Yamato-totohi-momoso-hime-no-Mikoto (倭迹迹日百襲姫命) and demands to be worshipped, and afterwards appears in the emperor's dream and tells him to have 大田田根子* worship him. In the Kojiki, only the oracle of the dream is recorded.

神明憑倭迹迹日百襲姫命曰
Nihon Shoki, Book 5, Emperor Sujin, 7th year, autumn, 8th month (癸卯朔己酉)

In the Nihon Shoki, 大田田根子* is found at 陶邑* in 茅渟県* and answers that his father is Ōmononushi-no-Ōkami and his mother 活玉依媛*, so he is a child of Ōmononushi-no-Ōkami. In the Kojiki, he is found in the village of 美努* in Kawachi and is a descendant in the fourth generation from Ōmononushi-no-Ōkami.

父曰大物主大神。母曰活玉依媛。
Nihon Shoki, Book 5, Emperor Sujin, record of the 10th year, 9th month (是後, “after this”)

The Nihon Shoki has no story of tracing his identity with a hemp thread; instead it has the story of Yamato-totohi-momoso-hime-no-Mikoto becoming the wife of Ōmononushi-no-Kami and being startled to see a beautiful small snake in her comb box (the origin of 箸墓*).

遂有美麗小蛇。其長大如衣紐。
