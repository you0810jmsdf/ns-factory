The Beginning of Heaven and Earth

天地のはじまり

When heaven and earth first opened, Ame-no-Minakanushi-no-Kami appeared in the High Plain of Heaven (高天原), and then deities came into being one after another. The first five are grouped together as the "Separate Heavenly Deities" (別天神), and those from Kuni-no-Tokotachi-no-Kami through Izanagi-no-Kami and Izanami-no-Kami are grouped as the "Seven Generations of the Age of the Gods" (神世七代).

Read in the Nihon Shoki: The Opening of Heaven and Earth and the Seven Generations of the Age of the Gods (sections 1–3) ›

Key points to remember

  1. Main textThe first deity to appear in the Kojiki is Ame-no-Minakanushi-no-Kami, who came into being in the High Plain of Heaven (高天原). Next Takamimusubi-no-Kami and Kamumusuhi-no-Kami came into being, and all three, as solitary deities (独神), hid their forms.
  2. Main textThe five deities from Ame-no-Minakanushi-no-Kami through Umashiashikabihikoji-no-Kami and 天之常立神* are regarded as the "Separate Heavenly Deities" (別天神), and they are written about separately from the Seven Generations of the Age of the Gods that follow.
  3. Main textThe deities from Kuni-no-Tokotachi-no-Kami through Izanagi-no-Kami and Izanami-no-Kami form the "Seven Generations of the Age of the Gods" (神世七代). The two solitary deities are each counted as one generation, and the ten male and female deities are counted two to a generation. At the end of these appear Izanagi-no-Kami and Izanami-no-Kami, who become the central figures of the birth of the land.
  4. Compared with the Nihon ShokiThe main text of the Nihon Shoki (section 1) explains that heaven and earth separated out of a mixed state like a hen's egg, and gives Kuni-no-Tokotachi-no-Mikoto as the first deity. 天御中主尊*, who corresponds to Ame-no-Minakanushi-no-Kami, is only listed in variant account 4, introduced with "it is also said" (又曰), as a deity born in the High Plain of Heaven.
  5. CommentaryAccording to the Kokugakuin University database of deity names, the three deities Ame-no-Minakanushi-no-Kami, Takamimusubi-no-Kami and Kamumusuhi-no-Kami are called the "Three Deities of Creation" (造化三神), based on wording in the preface of the Kojiki. Because Ame-no-Minakanushi-no-Kami appears only in this opening passage and nothing it does is described, it is regarded as difficult to make clear what kind of deity it is. [source, Japanese]
  6. CommentaryAccording to the same database, many interpretations see the Seven Generations of the Age of the Gods in the Kojiki as having the birth of Izanagi-no-Kami and Izanami-no-Kami as their goal, with the process leading up to it expressed step by step through the appearance of deities. Opinions differ on what that process represents (the formation of the land, the completion of the bodies of the male and female deities, and so on). [source, Japanese]

The story

When heaven and earth first opened, the name of the deity who first came into being in the High Plain of Heaven (高天原) was Ame-no-Minakanushi-no-Kami. Next Takamimusubi-no-Kami came into being, and next Kamumusuhi-no-Kami. These three all came into being as "solitary deities" (独神) without partners, and hid their forms. A small note in the original instructs that the character 天 in 高天原 be read "ama".

Next, when the land was still young, like floating oil, and drifting like a jellyfish (the original writes this part phonetically as 久羅下那州多陀用弊流), the deity that came into being from something that sprouted and grew up like a reed shoot was Umashiashikabihikoji-no-Kami. Next 天之常立神* came into being. These two also came into being as solitary deities and hid their forms. The five deities up to this point are called the "Separate Heavenly Deities" (別天神).

Then Kuni-no-Tokotachi-no-Kami came into being, and next Toyokumono-no-Kami, and these two also hid their forms as solitary deities. After that, deities came into being in male and female pairs: Uhijini-no-Kami and his younger sister Suhichini-no-Kami; Tsunogui-no-Kami and his younger sister Ikugui-no-Kami; Ōtonoji-no-Kami and his younger sister Ōtonobe-no-Kami; Omodaru-no-Kami and his younger sister Ayakashikone-no-Kami; and Izanagi-no-Kami and his younger sister Izanami-no-Kami.

The deities from Kuni-no-Tokotachi-no-Kami through Izanami-no-Kami are together called the "Seven Generations of the Age of the Gods" (神世七代). According to a small note, the two solitary deities are each counted as one generation, and the ten male and female deities that follow are counted two to a generation. In this way Izanagi-no-Kami and Izanami-no-Kami appear in the seventh and last generation, leading on to the next story, the birth of the land.

A plain retelling of what the original text of the Kojiki says (no published translation has been used).

In the original text

天地初發之時，於高天原成神名，天之御中主神。

When heaven and earth first opened, the name of the deity who came into being in the High Plain of Heaven was Ame-no-Minakanushi-no-Kami.

如葦牙因萌騰之物而成神名

The name of the deity who came into being from something that sprouted and grew up like a reed shoot was (Umashiashikabihikoji-no-Kami).

上件五柱神者，別天神。

The five deities up to this point are the "Separate Heavenly Deities" (別天神).

上件自國之常立神以下、伊耶那美神以前，并稱神世七代

The deities from Kuni-no-Tokotachi-no-Kami through Izanami-no-Kami are together called the "Seven Generations of the Age of the Gods" (神世七代).

  • Tsunogui-no-Kami (角杙神)The male deity of one of the male-female pairs of the Seven Generations of the Age of the Gods.
  • Ikugui-no-Kami (活杙神)The female deity who appears paired with Tsunogui-no-Kami.
  • Ōtonoji-no-Kami (意富斗能地神)The male deity of one of the male-female pairs of the Seven Generations of the Age of the Gods.
  • Ōtonobe-no-Kami (大斗乃弁神)The female deity who appears paired with Ōtonoji-no-Kami.
  • Omodaru-no-Kami (於母陀流神)The male deity of one of the male-female pairs of the Seven Generations of the Age of the Gods.
  • Ayakashikone-no-Kami (阿夜訶志古泥神)The female deity who appears paired with Omodaru-no-Kami.

In the Nihon Shoki

Nihon Shoki, Book 1, section 1, main text

The main text of the Shoki describes the state before heaven and earth separated as a chaos like a hen's egg, and says that something like a reed shoot arose between heaven and earth and became a deity. The first deity is Kuni-no-Tokotachi-no-Mikoto, followed by 国狭槌尊* and 豊斟渟尊*; no deity corresponding to Ame-no-Minakanushi-no-Kami appears in the main text.

天地之中生一物，狀如葦牙，便化爲神，號國常立尊
Nihon Shoki, Book 1, section 1, variant account 4

天御中主尊, 高皇産霊尊 and 神皇産霊尊 (readings unconfirmed), who correspond to the first three deities of the Kojiki, are in the Shoki only listed in variant account 4, introduced with "it is also said" (又曰), as deities born in the High Plain of Heaven.

高天原所生神名，曰天御中主尊
Nihon Shoki, Book 1, section 3, main text (see also section 3, variant account 1)

The main text of the Shoki also calls the deities from Kuni-no-Tokotachi-no-Mikoto through Izanagi-no-Mikoto and Izanami-no-Mikoto the "Seven Generations of the Age of the Gods" (神世七代), but they consist of three male deities who came into being alone (section 1) and eight deities in four male-female pairs (section 2). 角樴尊 and 活樴尊 (readings unconfirmed), who correspond to Tsunogui-no-Kami and Ikugui-no-Kami in the Kojiki, appear not in the main text but in variant account 1 of section 3.

自國常立尊，迄伊弉諾尊、伊弉𠕋尊，是謂神世七代者矣
