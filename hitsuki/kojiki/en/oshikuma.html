The Rebellion of Oshikuma-no-Miko (忍熊王) and Kehi-no-Ōkami (気比大神)

忍熊王の反乱と気比大神

Returning from Silla, 息長帯日売命* went up to Yamato in a mourning ship, pretending that her son had died, and defeated the brothers 香坂王* and Oshikuma-no-Miko (忍熊王), who lay in wait for her. Afterwards, when Takeuchi-no-Sukune (建内宿禰) took the prince to 角鹿*, Izasawake-no-Ōkami-no-Mikoto (伊奢沙和気大神之命) asked to exchange names, and from this arose the name Miketsu-Ōkami (御食津大神; Kehi-no-Ōkami, 気比大神); when the prince returned to the capital, his mother welcomed him with sake and song.

Read in the Nihon Shoki: The revolt of Oshikuma-no-Miko and the years of the regency ›

Key points to remember

  1. Main text息長帯日売命* put her son on a mourning ship (喪船) and made it appear that he had died, deceiving 香坂王* and Oshikuma-no-Miko (忍熊王), who lay in wait. 香坂王 was devoured by an enraged boar in the middle of an oath-hunt (宇気比獦), and Oshikuma-no-Miko, defeated, entered the sea with 伊佐比宿禰* and died.
  2. Main textWhat decided the outcome was the stratagem of 建振熊命*, the general on the crown prince's side. He had it announced that "息長帯日売命 has died" and made a show of cutting his bowstrings, and after the enemy had put away their weapons, he strung the spare strings he had hidden in his hair and attacked.
  3. Main textAt 角鹿*, Izasawake-no-Ōkami-no-Mikoto (伊奢沙和気大神之命) asked to exchange names with the crown prince and drove 入鹿魚* fish onto the shore. Because the crown prince said, "You have given me fish for my meal (御食)," the name Miketsu-Ōkami (御食津大神) arose, and by the time the Kojiki was written the deity was called Kehi-no-Ōkami (気比大神). The place names 血浦 and 都奴賀* are also explained from this story.
  4. Compared with the Nihon ShokiIn the Nihon Shoki (Book 9), it is a red boar that appears in a divination hunt (祈狩) at 菟餓野* that devours 麛坂王 (香坂王)*, and the stratagem of hiding bowstrings in the hair and proposing a false peace is carried out by Takeuchi-no-Sukune (武内宿禰). Oshikuma-no-Miko sinks at the 瀬田* crossing together with 五十狭茅宿禰* and dies.
  5. Compared with the Nihon ShokiIn the Nihon Shoki, the crown prince worships the Great Deity 笥飯大神* of 角鹿 in the 2nd month of the 13th year of the regency (摂政), and the story of the exchange of names appears as an alternative account (一云) in a small-script note at the beginning of the book of Emperor Ōjin (Book 10); while it says the Great Deity is called 去来紗別神 and the crown prince 誉田別尊*, it states that there is no record of the original names and that they are "unknown" (未詳).
  6. CommentaryAccording to the Kokugakuin University deity-name database, the "須久那美迦微"* appearing in the mother's song is regarded as another name of Sukunahikona-no-Kami (少名毘古那神), and here it is praised as a deity of sake. Also, because the Kojiki does not say what happened as a result of the exchange of names, theories are divided on the meaning of the exchange. [source, Japanese] [source, Japanese]

The story

When 息長帯日売命* was returning to Yamato, she distrusted the people's loyalty, so she prepared a mourning ship (喪船), put her son on it, and had it spread about that the prince had already died. Hearing this, 香坂王* and Oshikuma-no-Miko (忍熊王) planned to lie in wait and strike, and went out to 斗賀野* and held an oath-hunt (宇気比獦). While 香坂王 was up in an 歴木* tree, a great enraged boar came out, dug the tree down, and devoured 香坂王.

His younger brother Oshikuma-no-Miko, unafraid of this, raised an army and lay in wait, intending to attack the mourning ship as if it were empty. Then soldiers came down from the mourning ship and battle began. On Oshikuma-no-Miko's side, 伊佐比宿禰*, ancestor of the 難波吉師部*, was general; on the crown prince's side, 難波根子建振熊命*, ancestor of the 丸邇臣*, was general. Oshikuma-no-Miko's army was driven back as far as 山代*, but there it turned to face them, and neither army retreated as they fought.

建振熊命* devised a stratagem: he had it announced, "息長帯日売命 has already died, so there is no further reason to fight," and cut his bowstrings to make it look as if he had surrendered. The opposing general believed this, unstrung his bows and put away his weapons. Then 建振熊命's side took out the spare strings (also called 宇佐由豆留*) they had hidden in their hair, restrung their bows, and pursued and attacked. Oshikuma-no-Miko's army fled to 逢坂* and fought again, but was cut down to the last man at 沙沙那美*.

Driven to the end, Oshikuma-no-Miko and 伊佐比宿禰* boarded a boat, floated out on the sea, and sang a song. Then they entered the sea together and died.

Takeuchi-no-Sukune-no-Mikoto (建内宿禰命) took the crown prince to perform purification (禊), went round the lands of Ōmi (淡海) and Wakasa (若狭), built a temporary palace at 角鹿 in 高志前*, and stayed there. That night, Izasawake-no-Ōkami-no-Mikoto (伊奢沙和気大神之命), who dwells in that place, appeared in a dream and said, "I wish to exchange my name for the prince's name." When they reverently agreed, the deity announced, "Tomorrow morning go out to the shore. I will make a gift as a sign of the exchange of names." The next morning, when they went out to the shore, 入鹿魚* fish with wounded noses filled an entire bay.

The prince had it said to the deity, "You have given me fish for my meal (御食)." Therefore the deity's name was praised and he was called Miketsu-Ōkami (御食津大神), and he is now called Kehi-no-Ōkami (気比大神). Also, because the blood from the noses of the 入鹿魚 smelled, that bay was named 血浦, and it is now called 都奴賀*, the text records.

When the prince returned to the capital, his mother 息長帯日売命* brewed waiting-sake (待酒) and presented it, singing a song as she offered the great sake. Takeuchi-no-Sukune-no-Mikoto sang an answering song on the prince's behalf, and the text records that these are songs of sake-revelry (酒楽歌). Finally it concludes that Emperor Chūai (帯中津日子天皇*) died at the age of 52, and that his tomb is at 長江 in 恵賀 in Kawachi*.

A plain retelling of what the original text of the Kojiki says (no published translation has been used).

In the original text

大怒猪出。堀其歴木。即咋食其香坂王

A great enraged boar came out, dug down that 歴木* tree, and devoured 香坂王.

息長帶日賣命者。既崩故無可更戰。即絶弓絃

He had it announced, "息長帯日売命 has already died, so there is no further need to fight," and made a show of cutting his bowstrings (the stratagem of 建振熊命*).

於我給御食之魚。故亦稱其御名。號御食津大神

"You have given me fish for my meal (御食)." Therefore the deity's name was praised and he was called Miketsu-Ōkami (御食津大神).

此者。酒樂之歌也

These are songs of sake-revelry (酒楽歌) (the name collectively given to the song of 息長帯日売命* and the answering song of Takeuchi-no-Sukune-no-Mikoto).

In the Nihon Shoki

Nihon Shoki, Book 9 — Empress Jingū (spring, 2nd month of the year after the attack on Silla)

In the Nihon Shoki, 麛坂王* and Oshikuma-no-Miko gathered soldiers under the pretense of building the emperor's tomb, and when they held a divination hunt (祈狩) at 菟餓野*, a red boar climbed onto the temporary viewing stand and devoured 麛坂王. The empress does not use a mourning ship; she has Takeuchi-no-Sukune (武内宿禰) hold the prince in his arms and go round by the southern sea to the 水門 of Kii (紀伊水門).

赤猪忽出之登假庪。咋麛坂王而殺焉
Nihon Shoki, Book 9 — Empress Jingū (entries from the 3rd month, 丙申朔庚子)

In the Nihon Shoki, it is Takeuchi-no-Sukune (武内宿禰) who has the whole army hide spare bowstrings in their hair, falsely proposes peace, and gets Oshikuma-no-Miko's army to cut their bowstrings and throw their weapons into the river (in the Kojiki it is 建振熊命*).

各以儲弦藏于髮中、且佩木刀
Nihon Shoki, Book 9 — the 13th year of the regency of Empress Jingū, spring, 2nd month (丁巳朔甲子)

In the Nihon Shoki, Takeuchi-no-Sukune (武内宿禰) takes the crown prince to worship the Great Deity 笥飯大神* of 角鹿, and when the crown prince returns, the empress dowager (皇太后) entertains him at a banquet and sings, and Takeuchi-no-Sukune sings a reply on the crown prince's behalf. The stories of the exchange of names and of the 入鹿魚 are not in this entry.
