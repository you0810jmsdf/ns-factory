The Chief Consort's Journey to 山代* and the Six Songs

大后の山代行きと六つの歌

While the chief consort 石之日売命* was away in 木国* to gather 御綱柏* leaves, the emperor married 八田若郎女*. When the chief consort learned of it, she threw the 御綱柏 into the sea and, without returning to the palace, headed for 山代*; the emperor sent messengers and songs, and in the end went himself to the house of 奴理能美* at 筒木* and sang.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textWhile the chief consort 石之日売命* was away in 木国* to gather 御綱柏* leaves, the emperor married 八田若郎女*. Learning of it from a laborer's (仕丁) talk on the boat home, the chief consort threw all the 御綱柏 into the sea (the origin of the name 御津前* for that place), and without entering the palace went up the 堀江 canal toward 山代*.
  2. Main textThe emperor's messenger, 丸邇臣口子*, in heavy rain, lay prostrate in the courtyard immersed in water up to his waist, and his blue-printed robe (青摺衣) was dyed red by the color of its crimson cords. His younger sister 口日売*, who served the chief consort, saw this and sang a song.
  3. Main textThe three, 口子臣, 口比売 and 奴理能美*, had it reported to the emperor that the chief consort had only come to see a wondrous insect that changes into three forms. Saying that he would see the insect, the emperor visited 奴理能美's house and sang at the door of the chief consort's hall. The six songs of the two are regarded as the "志都歌之歌返"*.
  4. Compared with the Nihon ShokiIn the Nihon Shoki, the empress goes to the cape of Kumano (熊野岬) in Kii (紀国), and the place where she threw the leaves into the sea is named "葉済"*. The empress built a palace south of the 筒城岡* hill in 山背* and lived there; even when the emperor went to the 筒城宮* palace she would not meet him, and she did not return, giving as her reason that he had made 八田皇女* his consort. The story of the wondrous insect does not appear, and the empress dies at the 筒城宮 palace in the 35th year.
  5. CommentaryThe Kokugakuin University deity-name database interprets "伊斯祁" (ishike), in the song the emperor entrusted to 鳥山*, as shiku, meaning "to reach, to catch up." [source, Japanese]
  6. CommentaryThe same database interprets "夜賀波延那須" (yagahaenasu), in the song the emperor sang at the door, as a simile for a figure leading a great number of retainers. [source, Japanese]

The story

After that, the chief consort went to 木国* to gather 御綱柏* leaves for a banquet (豊楽). Meanwhile the emperor married 八田若郎女*. While the chief consort was on her way home with her boat fully loaded with 御綱柏, a laborer (仕丁) from 児嶋 in the province of Kibi*, who had been employed in the Water Office (水取司) and was on his way home to his province, met at the great crossing (大渡) of Naniwa the boat of a storehouse woman (倉人女) who was following behind, and said, "The emperor has recently married 八田若郎女 and amuses himself with her day and night. The chief consort has not heard of this, which is surely why she is traveling at her leisure." The storehouse woman at once caught up with the chief consort's boat and reported it to her just as she had heard it.

The chief consort, deeply resentful and angry, threw all the 御綱柏* loaded on her boat into the sea. Therefore that place is called 御津前*. Without entering the palace, the chief consort had her boat hauled up the 堀江* canal, went up along the river to 山代*, and sang a song. Then, going round from 山代, she reached the entrance of 那良山* and sang another song.

The chief consort turned back and for a while stayed at the house of 奴理能美*, a 韓人 of 筒木*. Hearing that the chief consort had gone up by way of 山代, the emperor sent his attendant (舎人) 鳥山* with a song. He then sent 丸邇臣口子* with two songs.

When 口子臣* came to deliver these songs, heavy rain was falling. 口子臣, not avoiding the rain, lay prostrate at the front door of the hall, but the chief consort went out by the back door; when he lay prostrate at the back door, she went out by the front door. 口子臣 crawled forward and knelt in the middle of the courtyard, and the pooled water reached his waist. Because he was wearing a blue-printed robe (青摺衣) with crimson cords, the water touched the crimson cords and the blue robe turned completely red. 口子臣's younger sister 口日売*, who served the chief consort, saw this and sang a song.

When the chief consort asked the reason, 口日売* answered, "It is my elder brother, 口子臣." So the three, 口子臣, 口比売 and 奴理能美*, consulted together and had it reported to the emperor: "The reason the chief consort has come is only to see the insect that 奴理能美 keeps, which becomes a crawling insect, then a shell, then a flying bird, a wondrous insect that changes into three forms; she has no other intention." The emperor said, "Then I too wish to see it," and went up from the great palace and entered 奴理能美's house. 奴理能美 presented the insect that changes into three kinds to the chief consort, and the emperor stood at the door of the hall where the chief consort was and sang a song.

The text records that these six songs sung by the emperor and the chief consort are the "志都歌之歌返"*. Next, the emperor, longing for 八田若郎女*, sent her a song (continued in the next story).

A plain retelling of what the original text of the Kojiki says (no published translation has been used).

In the original text

載其御船之御綱柏者。悉投棄於海

She threw all the 御綱柏* loaded on her boat into the sea.

跪于庭中時。水潦至腰

When (口子臣*) knelt in the middle of the courtyard, the pooled water reached his waist.

有變三色之奇虫

There is a wondrous insect that changes into three forms (words reported as the reason the chief consort had come).

此天皇與大后所歌之六歌者。志都歌之歌返也

These six songs sung by the emperor and the chief consort are the 志都歌之歌返*.

Deities and people, and the shrines that enshrine them

  • 石之日賣命*The chief consort. Learning of her husband's marriage, she threw the 御綱柏 into the sea and, without returning to the palace, headed for 山代.
  • 大雀命 * (Emperor Nintoku)Emperor Nintoku. He married 八田若郎女 while the chief consort was away, and kept sending messengers and songs to her.
  • 八田若郎女*The emperor's half-sister. She married the emperor while the chief consort was away.
  • The laborer from 児嶋 in the province of Kibi (吉備國兒嶋之仕丁)*A man who had been employed in the Water Office (水取司). He told the storehouse woman about the emperor's marriage.
  • The storehouse woman (倉人女)A woman whose boat had fallen behind the chief consort's party. She passed the laborer's story on to the chief consort.
  • 御綱柏*The oak leaves the chief consort gathered in 木国 for the banquet (豊楽). In her anger she threw them all into the sea.
  • 奴理能美*A 韓人 of 筒木. He lodged the chief consort at his house for a while and presented the insect that changes into three forms.
  • 鳥山*The emperor's attendant (舎人). He delivered the first song to the chief consort.
  • 丸邇臣口子*The emperor's messenger. In heavy rain he lay prostrate in the courtyard, immersed in water up to his waist.
  • 口日賣*Younger sister of 口子臣 (also written 口比売). She served the chief consort and sang a song on seeing her brother's plight.
  • The wondrous insect (奇虫)The insect kept by 奴理能美, which changes into three forms: a crawling insect, a shell, and a flying bird.

In the Nihon Shoki

Nihon Shoki, Book 11 — the 30th year of Emperor Nintoku, autumn, 9th month (乙卯朔乙丑)

In the Nihon Shoki, the empress goes as far as the cape of Kumano (熊野岬) in Kii (紀国) to gather 御綱葉* leaves; at the crossing of Naniwa she hears that the emperor has taken 八田皇女* as wife, throws the leaves into the sea, and that sea was called "葉済"* (the Kojiki has "御津前"). After that the empress passes through 山背* and builds a palace south of the 筒城岡* hill, where she lives.

皇后遊行紀國到熊野岬
Nihon Shoki, Book 11 — the 30th year of Emperor Nintoku, winter, 10th month (甲申朔)

In the Nihon Shoki, the messenger who goes to call the empress back is 口持臣*, ancestor of the 的臣* (according to one account, 口子臣, ancestor of the 和珥臣*), and his younger sister's name is given as 国依媛*. The empress answers, "I will never return."

遣的臣祖口持臣喚皇后
Nihon Shoki, Book 11 — the 30th year of Emperor Nintoku, 11th month (甲寅朔庚申)

In the Nihon Shoki, even when the emperor goes himself to the 筒城宮* palace, the empress will not agree to meet him, and the emperor returns to his palace as things stand. The story of the insect that changes into three forms does not appear.
