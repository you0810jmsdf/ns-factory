The Dispatch along the Four Circuits and the Rebellion of 建波邇安王*

四道への派遣と建波邇安王の反乱

In the time of Emperor Sujin (御眞木入日子印惠命)*, Ōbiko-no-Mikoto, Takenunakawawake-no-Mikoto and 日子坐王* were sent to various regions, and a mysterious song sung by a maiden on the way revealed the treason of 建波邇安王*, the emperor's “elder half-brother” (庶兄). It is told that the rebellion was quelled by the arrow of 日子國夫玖命*, that Ōbiko-no-Mikoto and his son Takenunakawawake-no-Mikoto met at Aizu (相津), and that the realm was brought to order.

Read in the Nihon Shoki: The Generals of the Four Roads and the Rebellion of 武埴安彦*, up to Hashihaka and the Sacred Treasures of Izumo ›

The story

In the reign of Emperor Sujin, Ōbiko-no-Mikoto was sent along the Koshi road (高志道) and his son Takenunakawawake-no-Mikoto along the twelve circuits of the east (東方十二道), and they were commanded to soften and pacify the people who would not submit (in the original, “麻都漏波奴人等”). Also 日子坐王* was sent to the land of Tanba (旦波國) and commanded to kill a man called 玖賀耳之御笠*. As Ōbiko-no-Mikoto was heading for the land of Koshi (高志國), a maiden wearing a waist skirt (腰裳) stood at 幣羅坂* in Yamashiro (山代) and sang a song.

In that song the address “美麻紀伊理毘古波夜” was repeated again and again. Ōbiko-no-Mikoto, finding it strange, turned his horse back and asked what it meant, but the maiden answered, “I said nothing. I only sang a song,” and suddenly vanished, no one knew where. When Ōbiko-no-Mikoto returned to the capital and reported to the emperor, the emperor answered that this was a sign that his elder half-brother 建波邇安王*, who was in the land of Yamashiro (山代國), had formed an evil intent.

The emperor commanded Ōbiko-no-Mikoto to raise an army and go against him, and sent with him 日子國夫玖命*, ancestor of the 丸邇臣*. The party set sacred jars (忌瓮) at 丸邇坂* before advancing, and when they reached the 和訶羅河* in Yamashiro, 建波邇安王* had raised an army and was waiting for them. Because the two armies faced each other across the river and challenged each other, that place is called 伊杼美 * (now 伊豆美). When 日子國夫玖命 asked the other side to shoot their sacred arrow (忌矢) first, the arrow of 建波邇安王 did not hit, while the arrow shot by 國夫玖命 struck 建波邇安王 and killed him.

The army of 建波邇安王* was utterly defeated and fled in all directions. Because the soldiers, driven into a corner at the crossing of 久須婆*, soiled their loincloths with excrement, that place is called 屎褌 * (now 久須婆); because, when the fleeing army was cut off and cut down, they floated on the river like cormorants, that river is called the Cormorant River (鵜河); and because they cut the soldiers down and “波布理” them, that place is called 波布理曾能*.

When the pacification was over and they had reported it, Ōbiko-no-Mikoto set out for the land of Koshi as earlier commanded. Then Takenunakawawake (建沼河別), who had been sent to the east, and his father Ōbiko (大毘古) met at Aizu (相津), and so that place is called Aizu. Thus each pacified the land to which he had been sent and reported, and the realm was at great peace and the people grew rich and prospered. At this time the tribute of the bow-tip (弓端之調) from men and the tribute of the fingertips (手末之調) from women were first levied, and so, in praise of this reign, the emperor is called “所知初國之御眞木天皇”. In this reign the 依網池* and the 酒折池* at 輕* were also made; the emperor died at the age of one hundred and sixty-eight, and his tomb is on top of the hill 勾之岡* on the 山邊道*.

A plain retelling of what the original text of the Kojiki says (no published translation has been used).

In the original text

吾勿言。唯爲詠歌耳。

The maiden's answer: “I said nothing. I only sang a song.”

此者爲。在山代國。我之庶兄建波邇安王。起邪心之表耳。

The emperor's words: “This is a sign that my elder half-brother in the land of Yamashiro, 建波邇安王*, has formed an evil intent.”

故稱其御世。謂所知初國之御眞木天皇也。

It is said that, because of such things as first having tribute paid, this emperor was praised as “所知初國之御眞木天皇”.

In the Nihon Shoki

Nihon Shoki, Book 5, Emperor Sujin, 10th year, 9th month (丙戌朔甲午)

In the Nihon Shoki, Ōbiko-no-Mikoto (大彦命) is sent to the northern lands (北陸), Takenunakawawake (武渟川別) to the eastern sea region (東海), 吉備津彦* to the western circuit (西道) and 丹波道主命* to Tanba (丹波), and they are given seals and cords of office (印綬) and made generals. In the Kojiki it is 日子坐王* who goes to Tanba (旦波), and a dispatch along the western circuit is not written in this section.

以大彦命遣北陸。武渟川別遣東海。吉備津彦遣西道。
Nihon Shoki, Book 5, Emperor Sujin, 10th year, 9th month (壬子)

In the Shoki, the place where the maiden is met is on top of 和珥坂* (in a “one version says,” 一云, the 平坂 of 山背, readings unconfirmed), and the one who found the song suspicious and declared it a sign of treason is Yamato-totohi-momoso-hime-no-Mikoto (倭迹迹日百襲姫命), the emperor's aunt (姑). 武埴安彦* attacks together with his wife 吾田媛*; 吾田媛 is killed at Ōsaka (大坂) by 五十狹芹彦命*, and 武埴安彦, across the 輪韓河 * (later called the 挑河), is shot in the chest by 彦國葺* and killed.

是武埴安彦將謀反之表者也。
Nihon Shoki, Book 5, Emperor Sujin, 12th year, autumn, 9th month (甲辰朔己丑)

In the Shoki, the account of imposing the tribute of the bow-tip (弭調) on men and the tribute of the fingertips (手末調) on women, and of praising the emperor as “御肇國天皇”, is in the 9th month of the 12th year of Emperor Sujin.

此謂男之弭調。女之手末調也。
