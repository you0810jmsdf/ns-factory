The Rebellion of 沙本毘古王*

沙本毘古王の反乱

沙本毘賣命*, consort of Emperor Suinin (伊久米伊理毘古伊佐知命*), was handed a small dagger by her elder brother 沙本毘古王* so that she would stab the emperor to death, but she could not carry it out and confessed everything. When her brother shut himself up in a rice fortress (稻城), the consort went in as well; she handed over to the emperor only the child 本牟智和氣御子*, who was born amid the flames, and died together with her brother.

Read in the Nihon Shoki: The Accession of Emperor Suinin, the Rebellion of 狭穂彦王, and 誉津別命 (readings unconfirmed) ›

  2. Main textAsked by the emperor what his dream meant (a sudden shower coming from 沙本* and a small brocade-colored snake coiling around his neck), the consort revealed her brother's entire plot, and the emperor raised an army and surrounded the rice fortress (稻城).
  3. Main textThe consort entered her brother's rice fortress and sent out only the child born there. Because she had shaved her hair and let her jewel cords and clothes rot beforehand, the emperor's soldiers could not seize her.
  4. Main textBecause the child was born amid the fire of the burning rice fortress, he was named 本牟智和氣御子*. In the end 沙本比古王* was killed, and the consort died with him.

The empress carries the prince over the fortress wall and sends him out, recommends the five daughters of 丹波道主王* for the inner palace, and then dies inside the fortress together with her brother.

The story

Emperor Suinin ruled the realm from the 玉垣宮 of 師木*. He married 佐波遲比賣命*, younger sister of 沙本毘古命*, and 品牟都和氣命* was born; counting the children of his other consorts as well, he had sixteen princes and princesses. Of these, 大帶日子淤斯呂和氣命* ruled the realm, and 印色入日子命* made the 血沼 Pond, the 狹山 Pond and the 高津 Pond of 日下*, and had a thousand swords made at the 河上宮 of 鳥取* and dedicated them to Isonokami Shrine (石上神宮). Small-script notes also record that 倭比賣命* worshipped and served the Great Shrine of Ise (伊勢大神宮), and that 布多遲能伊理毘賣命* became the consort of Yamato Takeru (倭建命).

While the emperor had 沙本毘賣* as his consort, her elder brother 沙本毘古王* asked his sister, "Whom do you love more, your husband or your brother?", and the consort answered, "I love my brother." 沙本毘古王 then plotted, saying, "If you truly love me, let you and me rule the realm together." He had a cord dagger, the 八鹽折之紐小刀*, made and gave it to his sister, ordering her to stab the emperor to death with it while he slept. The emperor, knowing nothing of the plot, fell asleep with his head on the consort's lap. Three times the consort raised the cord dagger to stab the emperor in the neck, but she could not bear her grief and could not stab him, and her tears fell onto the emperor's face.

The emperor woke with a start and asked the consort, "I dreamed that a sudden shower came from the direction of 沙本* and wet my face, and that a small brocade-colored snake coiled around my neck. What does this signify?" The consort, thinking she could not evade the question, told him everything: that when her brother asked her she had answered "I love my brother," that she had been handed a cord dagger to kill the emperor, and that though she raised it three times she could not stab him, and her tears fell.

The emperor said, "I was nearly deceived," and raised an army, and 沙本毘古王* built a rice fortress (稻城) and met him in battle. The consort could not abandon her brother; she slipped out by the back gate and entered the rice fortress. At this time the consort was with child, and because she was pregnant and because he had loved and cherished her for three years, the emperor merely surrounded the fortress with his army and did not press the attack. Meanwhile the child was born, and the consort had the child placed outside the rice fortress and sent word that, if the emperor regarded it as his own child, he should take it and raise it.

Though the emperor resented her brother, he could not suppress his love for the consort. He chose strong and nimble soldiers and ordered them, when they received the child, to seize the mother too, by her hair or by her hands, and drag her out. Foreseeing this, the consort shaved off all her hair and covered her head with it, let her jewel cords rot and wound them three times around her hands, and rotted her clothes with sake so that they looked like ordinary clothes. The soldiers were able to receive the child, but when they grasped her hair it came away, when they grasped her hands the jewel cords broke, and when they grasped her clothes the clothes tore, so they could not bring the mother back. The emperor, full of regret, hated the people who had made the jewels and took away all their land, and it is said that from this arose the proverb "不得地玉作"*.

When the emperor asked the child's name, the consort answered that, since he was born in the fire at the very time the rice fortress was burning, it would be fitting to name him 本牟智和氣御子*. Asked how he should be raised, she answered that he should be brought up by appointing a 大湯坐 and a 若湯坐*; and asked who would untie the 美豆能小佩* that she had tied, she answered that 兄比賣 and 弟比賣*, daughters of 旦波比古多多須美智宇斯王*, would be suitable. Then the emperor at last killed 沙本比古王*, and the consort, his younger sister, followed her brother in death.

In the Nihon Shoki

Nihon Shoki, Book 6 — the 4th year of Emperor Suinin, autumn, 9th month (丙戌朔戊申)

In the Nihon Shoki, the question of 狹穗彦王* is "Whom do you love more, your brother or your husband?" He further argues that one who serves others with beauty loses favor as that beauty fades, and gives her a dagger (匕首) to stab the emperor. The empress hides it in her clothing.

汝孰愛兄與夫焉。
Nihon Shoki, Book 6 — the 5th year of Emperor Suinin, winter, 10th month (己卯朔)

In the Nihon Shoki, the emperor has 八綱田*, distant ancestor of the 上毛野君*, attack 狹穗彦*, and 狹穗彦 builds a rice fortress by piling up rice. In the end 八綱田 sets fire to the fortress; the empress, holding the prince, carries him over the fortress wall and sends him out, and then dies inside the fortress together with her brother. The Kojiki's episode of escaping the soldiers' hands by means of tricks with her hair, jewel cords and clothes, and the exchange about naming the child, do not appear in this entry.

狹穗彦與妹共死于城中。
