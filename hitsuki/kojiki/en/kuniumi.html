The Birth of the Land

国生み

Receiving a command from the heavenly deities, Izanagi-no-Mikoto and Izanami-no-Mikoto made Onogoro-shima with the heavenly spear 天沼矛*, married there, and gave birth to the islands that became the land. Their first union did not go well because the female deity spoke first; after following the divination of the heavenly deities and starting over, the islands, beginning with the land 大八嶋国*, were born.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textThe heavenly deities commanded Izanagi-no-Mikoto and Izanami-no-Mikoto to put the land in order and make it firm, and gave them the spear 天沼矛*. When the two stirred the sea with the spear from the bridge 天浮橋*, the brine that dripped from the tip of the spear piled up and Onogoro-shima was formed.
  2. Main textAt first, because Izanami-no-Mikoto spoke first, the child born, Hiruko, was set adrift in a reed boat, and Awashima was not counted among their children either. Through the heavenly deities' divination (太占) it became clear that this was "because the woman spoke first", and they started over with Izanagi-no-Mikoto speaking first.
  3. Main textAfter starting over, eight islands were born, from Awaji-no-Ho-no-Sawake-no-shima to Ōyamato-toyoakizu-shima, and because these were born first, the name 大八嶋国* arose. Then six more islands, including 吉備児嶋*, were also born.
  4. Compared with the Nihon ShokiIn the main text of the Nihon Shoki (section 4) there is no command from the heavenly deities; the two deities themselves say, "Surely there must be a land below," and probe with the spear. When the female deity calls out first, the male deity rebukes her on the spot and they start over, and there is no consultation with the heavenly deities or divination. The first island born is also different, 大日本豊秋津洲*, and 淡路洲* is called the "afterbirth" (胞).
  5. CommentaryAccording to the Kokugakuin University database of deity names, "Onogoro" is interpreted as meaning "to congeal by itself", and the name is said to indicate that it was an island that formed naturally, not one born from the marriage of the two deities. [source, Japanese]
  6. CommentaryAccording to the same database, views on the name "Hiruko" are divided between a view that it means, as the characters suggest, a child like a leech, and a view that the characters are phonetic substitutes for "日子" (child of the sun). In addition, since the statement that the child "could not stand on its legs even at three years old" is found only in section 5 of the Nihon Shoki, doubts have also been raised about applying it to the Hiruko of the Kojiki. [source, Japanese]

The story

The heavenly deities commanded the two deities Izanagi-no-Mikoto and Izanami-no-Mikoto to put this drifting land in order and make it firm, and gave them the spear 天沼矛*. The two stood on the bridge 天浮橋*, lowered the spear, stirred the sea water until it made a sound, and drew it up. The brine that dripped from the tip of the spear piled up and became an island. This is Onogoro-shima.

The two descended to that island and set up the pillar 天之御柱* and the hall 八尋殿*. When Izanagi-no-Mikoto asked how her body was formed, Izanami-no-Mikoto answered that "there is one place that did not grow together", and Izanagi-no-Mikoto said that he had "one place that grew in excess". Izanagi-no-Mikoto proposed that they join these and give birth to the land, and Izanami-no-Mikoto agreed. The two decided to go around 天之御柱 and meet, Izanami-no-Mikoto going around from the right and Izanagi-no-Mikoto from the left.

When they met, Izanami-no-Mikoto first said, "Ah, what a fine young man!" and afterwards Izanagi-no-Mikoto said, "Ah, what a fine young woman!" (the original writes these words phonetically). Izanagi-no-Mikoto told her that "it was not good that the woman spoke first", but they united anyway, and the child born was Hiruko. This child was put in a reed boat and set adrift, and Awashima, born next, was not counted among their children either.

The two consulted together, went up to the heavenly deities and asked for instructions. The heavenly deities divined by the method 太占* and told them that it had not been good because the woman spoke first, so they should go down again and say the words over. The two descended again and went around 天之御柱 as before, and this time Izanagi-no-Mikoto called out first and Izanami-no-Mikoto afterwards.

In this way the two united and gave birth to eight islands: Awaji-no-Ho-no-Sawake-no-shima, Iyo-no-Futana-no-shima, 隠伎之三子嶋*, Tsukushi-no-shima, 伊伎嶋*, 津嶋*, Sado-no-shima and Ōyamato-toyoakizu-shima. Iyo-no-Futana-no-shima and Tsukushi-no-shima each have one body and four faces, and each face has a name: 愛比売, 飯依比古, Ōgetsu-hime and 建依別; and 白日別, 豊日別, 建日向日豊久士比泥別 and 建日別 (readings unconfirmed). Because these eight islands were born first, the land is called 大八嶋国*.

After that, on their way back, they further gave birth to 吉備児嶋, 小豆嶋, 大嶋, 女嶋, 知訶嶋 and 両児嶋 (readings unconfirmed). These islands also each have another name: 建日方別, 大野手比売, 大多麻流別, 天一根, 天之忍男 and 天両屋 (readings unconfirmed). A small note counts the islands from 吉備児嶋 to 天両屋 together as six islands.

A plain retelling of what the original text of the Kojiki says (no published translation has been used).

In the original text

修理固成是多陀用幣流之國

Put this drifting land in order and make it firm (the command of the heavenly deities).

自其矛末垂落塩之累積成嶋，是淤能碁吕嶋

The brine that dripped from the tip of the spear piled up and became an island. This is Onogoro-shima.

女人先言不良

It is not good that the woman spoke first (the words of Izanagi-no-Mikoto).

故因此八嶋先所生，謂大八嶋國。

Because these eight islands were born first, the land is called 大八嶋国*.

In the Nihon Shoki

Nihon Shoki, Book 1, section 4, main text

In the main text of the Shoki there is no command from the heavenly deities; Izanagi-no-Mikoto (伊弉諾尊) and Izanami-no-Mikoto (伊弉冉尊) consult together on their own on the bridge 天浮橋*, probe with the spear 天之瓊矛*, and obtain Onogoro-shima (磤馭慮島). When they go around the pillar and the female deity calls out first, the male deity rebukes her on the spot and they start over; there is no consultation with the heavenly deities and no divination (太占).

底下豈無國歟
Nihon Shoki, Book 1, section 4, main text

The main text of the Shoki calls 淡路洲* the "afterbirth" (胞) and first gives birth to 大日本豊秋津洲*. 蛭児 and 淡洲 (readings unconfirmed) do not appear in the birth of the land in the main text, and islands such as 対馬 and 壱岐 (readings unconfirmed) are said to have formed from congealed sea foam.

先以淡路洲爲胞
Nihon Shoki, Book 1, section 4, variant account 1

Variant account 1 is close to the Kojiki: the command of the heavenly deities, setting 蛭児* adrift in a reed boat, not counting 淡洲* among the children, and starting over through divination (太占). However, when they first go around the pillar the female deity goes left and the male deity right, and when they start over they also switch directions. In the Kojiki the female deity goes right and the male deity left from the start, and they go around the same way when they start over.
