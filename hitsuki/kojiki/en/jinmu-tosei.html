Emperor Jinmu's Eastern Expedition (from Hyūga to Kumano and Yoshino)

神武天皇の東征（日向から熊野・吉野へ）

神倭伊波禮毘古命 * (Emperor Jinmu) sets out east from the Takachiho palace in Hyūga together with his elder brother Itsuse-no-Mikoto, but in the battle with 登美能那賀須泥毘古* Itsuse-no-Mikoto is wounded by an arrow and dies at the 男水門* in Kii. In Kumano, the party overcomes danger thanks to a sword sent down from heaven and to the guidance of Yatagarasu (八咫烏), sent by 高木大神*, and, meeting the earthly deities of Yoshino along the way, enters Uda.

Read in the Nihon Shoki: Emperor Jinmu's Eastern Expedition ›

Key points to remember

  1. Main textItsuse-no-Mikoto was wounded in the hand by an arrow of 登美毘古* and, saying that “it was wrong for the child of the sun deity to fight facing the sun,” chose a route circling round to the south so as to have the sun at their backs. On the way he died at the 男之水門* in the land of Kii (紀国), and it is recorded that his tomb is at 竈山*.
  2. Main textIn Kumano, when the party had fallen prostrate, the violent deities were cut down by a sword presented by 高倉下*, and the troops awoke. This sword had been sent down by Takemikazuchi-no-Kami at the command of Amaterasu-Ōmikami (天照大神) and 高木神*, and a note in the original text records its names, such as Futsunomitama (布都御魂), and that it is at Isonokami Shrine (石上神宮).
  3. Main textBeyond Kumano, Yatagarasu (八咫烏), sent from heaven by 高木大神*, served as guide. In Yoshino they met three earthly deities, Niemotsu-no-ko (贄持之子), Ihika (井氷鹿) and Iwaoshiwaku-no-ko (石押分之子), who are regarded as the ancestors, respectively, of the cormorant fishers of 阿陀*, the Yoshino no Obito (吉野首), and the 国巣* of Yoshino.
  4. Main textAfter leaving Hyūga, the party was entertained at 宇沙*, then stayed one year at the 岡田宮, seven years at the 多祁理宮 and eight years at the 高嶋宮 (readings unconfirmed). At 速吸門* they took on as guide an earthly deity who knew the sea routes, and named him Saonetsuhiko (槁根津日子).
  5. Compared with the Nihon ShokiIn the Nihon Shoki, the person met at 速吸之門* is 珍彦 * (later 椎根津彦), and the meeting happens just after departure, before Usa (宇佐). The stay at the 高嶋宮* in Kibi is also given as three years, which differs from the order and the numbers of years in the Kojiki.
  6. CommentaryThe Kokugakuin University database of deity names notes that the Kojiki writes Itsuse-no-Mikoto's death with “崩,” the character it uses for the death of an emperor, whereas the Nihon Shoki writes “薨,” used for the death of princes and the like, and it introduces the view that there was originally a tradition in which Itsuse-no-Mikoto was the hero of the eastern expedition. [source, Japanese]

The story

神倭伊波禮毘古命* and his elder brother Itsuse-no-Mikoto, the two of them, were at the Takachiho palace and discussed where they should live in order to govern the realm peacefully, and they decided to go east. Setting out from Hyūga, they went toward Tsukushi, and at 宇沙* in 豊国* the local people 宇沙都比古 and 宇沙都比賣 (readings unconfirmed) built the “足一騰宮” and presented a feast. After that they stayed one year at the 岡田宮* in Tsukushi, seven years at the 多祁理宮* in the land of 阿岐*, and eight years at the 高嶋宮* in Kibi.

As they went up from Kibi, at 速吸門* they met a man who came riding on the shell of a turtle, fishing, and flapping his arms like wings. He was an earthly deity named 宇豆毘古*; he knew the sea routes well and answered that he would serve them. So they held out a pole to him, drew him into the imperial boat, and gave him the name Saonetsuhiko (槁根津日子). A note in the original text makes him the ancestor of the provincial chiefs of Yamato (倭国造) and others.

When they passed the crossing of Naniwa (浪速) and moored their boats at 白肩津* of 青雲*, 登美能那賀須泥毘古* raised an army and lay in wait for them. Because they took the shields that had been placed in the boats and went ashore, that place is called 楯津 * (now 日下之蓼津). In this battle Itsuse-no-Mikoto was struck in the hand by an arrow of 登美毘古*, and he said, “Though I am the child of the sun deity, it was wrong of me to fight facing the sun. From now on let us circle round and strike with the sun on our backs,” and they went round to the south. He washed the blood from his hand in the sea of 血沼*, and at the 男之水門* in the land of Kii (紀国) he cried out valiantly, “Am I to die of a wound from a base fellow's hand?” and died (the original writes “崩”). His tomb is at 竈山* in Kii.

When they reached the village of Kumano, a great bear dimly appeared and at once vanished. Then 神倭伊波禮毘古命* suddenly became “遠延” (a word written phonetically), and all his troops fell prostrate. Just then 高倉下* of Kumano came bringing a sword and presented it, and the Child of the Heavenly Deities rose, saying, “I have slept long.” When he received the sword, the violent deities of the mountains of Kumano were cut down of themselves, and the troops who had fallen all awoke.

高倉下* explained how he had come by the sword. In a dream, Amaterasu-Ōmikami (天照大神) and 高木神* summoned Takemikazuchi-no-Kami and commanded him to go down, since the Central Land of the Reed Plains seemed to be in uproar and their children were not at peace. Takemikazuchi-no-Kami answered that even if he did not go down himself, there was the sword with which he had once pacified that land, and it would be enough to send that down. He told 高倉下 that he would make a hole in the roof of his storehouse and drop the sword in, and that he should find it in the morning and present it to the Child of the Heavenly Deities. When he awoke and looked in the storehouse, the sword was truly there, so he presented it. According to a note in the original text, this sword is also called 佐士布都神*, 甕布都神* and Futsunomitama (布都御魂), and it is at Isonokami Shrine (石上神宮).

Further, 高木大神* instructed that they must not go on into the interior from there, since there were many violent deities, and that he would send Yatagarasu (八咫烏) from heaven, so they should follow behind it. As they advanced following Yatagarasu, at the lower reaches of the Yoshino River they met Niemotsu-no-ko (贄持之子; ancestor of the cormorant fishers of 阿陀*), who was catching fish with a fish trap. Next they met Ihika (井氷鹿; ancestor of the Yoshino no Obito and others), a man with a tail who came out of a shining well, and when they entered the mountains, Iwaoshiwaku-no-ko (石押分之子; ancestor of the 国巣* of Yoshino), a man with a tail who came out pushing the rocks apart, came to meet them. All three gave their names as earthly deities. From there they crossed over the mountain paths and entered Uda (宇陀), and so that place is called “the 穿 of Uda” (宇陀之穿)*.

A plain retelling of what the original text of the Kojiki says (no published translation has been used).

In the original text

坐何地者。平聞看天下之政。猶思東行。

The brothers' discussion: where should we live in order to govern the realm peacefully? Let us go east after all.

吾者爲日神之御子。向日而戰不良。

The words of Itsuse-no-Mikoto: since I am the child of the sun deity, it was wrong to fight facing the sun.

負賤奴之手乎死。爲男建而崩。

He cried out valiantly, “Am I to die of a wound received from a base fellow?” and died. It is said that the name 男水門* comes from this.

今自天。遣八咫烏。故其八咫烏引道。

The oracle of 高木大神*: I will now send Yatagarasu from heaven, and that crow will guide you on the way.

  • 宇沙都比古 and 宇沙都比賣 (readings unconfirmed)Local people of 宇沙* in 豊国*. They built the 足一騰宮* and presented a feast to the party.
  • Saonetsuhiko (槁根津日子; originally named 宇豆毘古)An earthly deity who appeared at 速吸門* riding on a turtle's shell. Because he knew the sea routes, he was drawn into the boat and given this name. He is regarded as the ancestor of the provincial chiefs of Yamato (倭国造) and others.
  • 登美能那賀須泥毘古 * (also 登美毘古)Raised an army at 白肩津* to attack the party, and wounded Itsuse-no-Mikoto with his arrow.
  • Kumano-no-yama-no-araburu-kami (熊野山之荒神)The violent deities of the mountains of Kumano. When the sword was presented, they were cut down of themselves.
  • 熊野之高倉下*A person of Kumano (a note in the original says “此者人名,” this is a person's name). Following the instructions of a dream, he presented the sword that had been dropped into his storehouse to the Child of the Heavenly Deities, who had fallen prostrate.
  • Yatagarasu (八咫烏)The crow sent from heaven by 高木大神*. It guided the way beyond Kumano.
  • Niemotsu-no-ko (贄持之子)An earthly deity who was catching fish with a fish trap at the lower reaches of the Yoshino River. He is regarded as the ancestor of the cormorant fishers of 阿陀*.
  • Ihika (井氷鹿)An earthly deity with a tail who came out of a shining well. He is regarded as the ancestor of the Yoshino no Obito and others.
  • Iwaoshiwaku-no-ko (石押分之子)An earthly deity with a tail who came out pushing the rocks apart. Hearing that the Child of the Heavenly Deities was coming, he came out to meet him. He is regarded as the ancestor of the 国巣* of Yoshino.

In the Nihon Shoki

Nihon Shoki, Book 3, Emperor Jinmu, record before accession, the year 甲寅, winter, 10th month (丁巳朔辛酉)

In the Nihon Shoki, the meeting with a fisherman at 速吸之門* takes place right after setting out on the eastern expedition, before reaching Usa (宇佐). The fisherman's name is 珍彦*, and he is granted the name 椎根津彦* (in the Kojiki, the meeting comes after leaving Kibi, and 宇豆毘古 is named Saonetsuhiko).

乃特賜名爲椎根津彦
Nihon Shoki, Book 3, Emperor Jinmu, record before accession, the year 戊午, 5th month (丙寅朔癸酉)

In the Nihon Shoki, Itsuse-no-Mikoto strokes his sword and gives a war cry at the 山城水門* of 茅渟* (the place is called 雄水門*), then dies at 竈山* in the land of Kii and is buried at 竈山. His death is written “薨” (in the Kojiki, he cries out at the 男之水門 and “崩,” and his tomb is at 竈山).

而五瀬命薨于軍。因葬竃山。
Nihon Shoki, Book 3, Emperor Jinmu, record before accession, the year 戊午, 6th month (乙未朔丁巳)

In the Nihon Shoki, those who appear in 高倉下's dream are Amaterasu-Ōmikami (天照太神) and Takemikazuchi-no-Kami (武甕雷神), and 高木神* does not appear. Also, the sending of Yatagarasu (頭八咫烏) is announced by Amaterasu-Ōmikami in the emperor's own dream (in the Kojiki, 高木大神 announces it).

天照大神訓于天皇曰。朕今遣頭八咫烏。
