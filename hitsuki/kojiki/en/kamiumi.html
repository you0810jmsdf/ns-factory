The Birth of the Deities and the Fire Deity

神生みと火の神

Having finished giving birth to the land, Izanagi-no-Mikoto and Izanami-no-Mikoto gave birth one after another to deities of the sea, wind, trees, mountains, fields and so on, but Izanami-no-Mikoto was burned and died because she gave birth to the fire deity. When the grieving Izanagi-no-Mikoto cut down the fire deity Kagutsuchi-no-Kami (迦具土神), still more deities came into being from its blood and body.

Read in the Nihon Shoki: The Birth of the Three Noble Children, the Birth of the Deities, and Yomi (Section 5) ›

Key points to remember

  1. Main textBecause Izanami-no-Mikoto gave birth to the fire deity 火之夜芸速男神 * (also called 火之迦具土神), her private parts were burned, she fell ill, and at last she departed this world (神避). Even during her illness, deities such as 金山毗古神*, 波迩夜須毗売神*, Mitsuhanome-no-Kami and Wakumusubi-no-Kami came into being from her vomit, feces and urine.
  2. Main textThe grieving Izanagi-no-Mikoto cut off the head of his child Kagutsuchi-no-Kami with a ten-fist sword (十拳剣). From the blood on the sword came eight deities, including 建御雷之男神*, and from the body of Kagutsuchi-no-Kami came eight mountain deities (山津見神), such as Masakayamatsumi-no-Kami. The name of that sword is Ame-no-Ohabari (also 伊都之尾羽張)*.
  3. Main textIn the birth of the deities, the deity of the sea (大綿津見神)*, the deities of the river mouths (Hayaakitsuhiko-no-Kami and Hayaakitsu-hime-no-Kami), the deity of the wind (志那都比古神)*, the deity of trees (Kukunochi-no-Kami), the deity of mountains (Ōyamatsumi-no-Kami) and the deity of fields (Kayano-hime-no-Kami) are born with their roles named in the original. Further, the two river-mouth deities divide the rivers and the sea between them, and the two deities of mountains and fields divide the mountains and the fields between them, and give birth to further deities.
  4. Main textFrom the tears of Izanagi-no-Mikoto came Nakisawame-no-Kami, and Izanami-no-Kami was buried on 比婆之山*, on the border of Izumo Province and 伯伎国*. A small note counts what the two deities gave birth to together before Izanami-no-Kami departed as fourteen islands and thirty-five deities, and states that Onogoro-shima is not an island they gave birth to.
  5. Compared with the Nihon ShokiIn the main text of the Nihon Shoki (section 5) there is neither the birth of the fire deity nor the death of Izanami-no-Mikoto (伊弉冉尊); after the deities of mountains, rivers, grasses and trees, the two deities give birth to the sun deity, the moon deity, 蛭児* and Susanoo-no-Mikoto (素戔嗚尊). The fire deity (Kagutsuchi, 軻遇突智) and the death of Izanami-no-Mikoto are transmitted in the variant accounts, and variant account 6 tells a story close to that of the Kojiki.
  6. CommentaryAccording to the Kokugakuin University database of deity names, 建御雷之男神* is a deity who came into being from the blood at the base of the sword that cut down the fire deity, and is placed as the child of 伊都之尾羽張*, the deity of that sword. The element "futsu" in its other names 建布都神 and 豊布都神 (readings unconfirmed) is regarded as a word related to swords, and it has been pointed out that this deity has a strong character as a deity of swords. This deity later becomes an envoy in the scene of the transfer of the land (国譲り). [source, Japanese] [source, Japanese]

The story

Having finished giving birth to the land, the two deities went on to give birth to deities. First Ōkotooshio-no-Kami was born, and next Iwatsuchibiko-no-Kami, 石巣比売神, 大戸日別神, 天之吹男神 (readings unconfirmed), Ōyabiko-no-Kami and 風木津別之忍男神* were born. Further, 大綿津見神* was born as the deity of the sea, and Hayaakitsuhiko-no-Kami and his younger sister Hayaakitsu-hime-no-Kami as the deities of the river mouths.

Hayaakitsuhiko-no-Kami and Hayaakitsu-hime-no-Kami divided the rivers and the sea between them and gave birth to further deities. These are eight deities: 沫那芸神, 沫那美神, 頬那芸神, 頬那美神, 天之水分神, 国之水分神, 天之久比奢母智神 and 国之久比奢母智神 (readings unconfirmed). A small note sums these up as "eight deities in all" (并八神).

Next the two deities gave birth to the deity of the wind, 志那都比古神*, the deity of trees, Kukunochi-no-Kami, the deity of mountains, Ōyamatsumi-no-Kami, and the deity of fields, Kayano-hime-no-Kami (also called 野椎神)*. Ōyamatsumi-no-Kami and 野椎神 divided the mountains and the fields between them and gave birth to eight deities: 天之狭土神, 国之狭土神, 天之狭霧神, 国之狭霧神, 天之闇戸神, 国之闇戸神, 大戸惑子神 and 大戸惑女神 (readings unconfirmed). Then the two deities gave birth to 鳥之石楠船神 * (also called Ame-no-Torifune-no-Kami) and Ōgetsu-hime-no-Kami.

The next child they gave birth to was 火之夜芸速男神*, also called 火之炫毗古神 and 火之迦具土神 (readings unconfirmed). Because she gave birth to this child, Izanami-no-Mikoto had her private parts burned and fell ill. At that time 金山毗古神 and 金山毗売神 came into being from her vomit, 波迩夜須毗古神 and 波迩夜須毗売神 from her feces (readings unconfirmed), and Mitsuhanome-no-Kami and Wakumusubi-no-Kami from her urine; the child of Wakumusubi-no-Kami is called 豊宇気毗売神*. Thus, because she gave birth to the fire deity, Izanami-no-Kami at last departed this world (神避, that is, died).

Izanagi-no-Mikoto lamented, "I have exchanged my beloved wife for a single child," and wept, crawling about by Izanami-no-Mikoto's pillow and by her feet. The deity who came into being from his tears is Nakisawame-no-Kami, who dwells at the foot of the trees at 畝尾 on 香山 (readings unconfirmed). The dead Izanami-no-Kami was buried on 比婆之山*, on the border of Izumo Province and 伯伎国*.

Izanagi-no-Mikoto drew the ten-fist sword (十拳剣) he wore at his waist and cut off the head of his child Kagutsuchi-no-Kami. The blood on the tip of the sword spattered onto the rocks 湯津石村*, and Iwasaku-no-Kami, 根拆神 and 石筒之男神 (readings unconfirmed) came into being; from the blood at the base of the sword came Mikahayahi-no-Kami, 樋速日神* and 建御雷之男神 * (also called 建布都神 and 豊布都神). From the blood that gathered on the hilt of the sword and leaked out between his fingers came Kuraokami-no-Kami and 闇御津羽神*. A small note sums these up as eight deities born through the sword.

Deities also came into being from the body of the slain Kagutsuchi-no-Kami. These are eight deities: Masakayamatsumi-no-Kami at the head, 淤縢山津見神 at the chest, 奥山津見神 at the belly, 闇山津見神 at the private parts, 志芸山津見神 at the left hand, 羽山津見神 at the right hand, 原山津見神 at the left foot and 戸山津見神 at the right foot (readings unconfirmed). The name of the sword that cut down Kagutsuchi-no-Kami is Ame-no-Ohabari, also called 伊都之尾羽張*.

A plain retelling of what the original text of the Kojiki says (no published translation has been used).

In the original text

伊耶那美神者，因生火神，遂神避坐也

Because she gave birth to the fire deity, Izanami-no-Kami at last died.

愛我那迩妹命乎！謂易子之一木乎！

My beloved wife! Have I exchanged you for a single child? (the lament of Izanagi-no-Mikoto)

拔所御佩之十拳釼，斬其子迦具土神之頸

He drew the ten-fist sword he wore at his waist and cut off the head of his child Kagutsuchi-no-Kami.

故所斬之刀名謂天之尾羽張

The name of the sword that cut down Kagutsuchi-no-Kami is Ame-no-Ohabari.

In the Nihon Shoki

Nihon Shoki, Book 1, section 5, main text

In the main text of the Shoki, after giving birth to the sea, the rivers, the mountains, the ancestor of trees and the ancestor of grasses, the two deities consult about giving birth to a "ruler of all under heaven" and give birth to the sun deity (Ōhirume-no-Muchi, 大日孁貴), the moon deity, 蛭児* and Susanoo-no-Mikoto (素戔嗚尊). The birth of the fire deity and the death of Izanami-no-Mikoto (伊弉冉尊) are not written in the main text.

何不生天下之主者歟
Nihon Shoki, Book 1, section 5, variant account 6

Variant account 6 is close to the Kojiki: Izanami-no-Mikoto (伊弉冉尊) dies with the birth of Kagutsuchi (軻遇突智), Izanagi-no-Mikoto (伊弉諾尊) laments that he has exchanged his wife for a single child, and 啼沢女命* comes into being from his tears. However, the details differ: for example, Kagutsuchi is cut into three pieces, and deities such as Mikahayahi-no-Kami (甕速日神), 熯速日神* and Takemikazuchi-no-Kami (武甕槌神) come into being from the blood dripping from the sword.

唯以一兒替我愛之妹者乎
Nihon Shoki, Book 1, section 5, variant account 5

On the place where Izanami-no-Mikoto (伊弉冉尊) was buried, variant account 5 gives the village 有馬村* in Kumano, Kii Province, which differs from the Kojiki's "比婆之山 on the border of Izumo Province and 伯伎国".
