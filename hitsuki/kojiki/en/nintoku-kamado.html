The Accession of Emperor Nintoku and the People's Hearths

仁徳天皇の即位と民のかまど

大雀命 * (Emperor Nintoku) ruled the realm from the 高津宮* in Naniwa, and built one after another embankments and ponds, canals and ports. Seeing from a high mountain that no cooking smoke rose anywhere in the land, he exempted the people from labor levies and taxes (課役) for three years, and as the people grew prosperous, his reign was praised as "the reign of the sage emperor" (聖帝世).

Key points to remember

  1. Main text大雀命 * (Emperor Nintoku) looked over the land from a high mountain and, because no cooking smoke rose, knew that the whole land was poor, and exempted the people from all labor levies and taxes (課役) for three years. During that time, even when the palace fell into disrepair and rain leaked in, he had it left unrepaired and made do by catching the rain in vessels.
  2. Main textSeeing that smoke filled the land, the emperor imposed the labor levies and taxes again, but the common people (百姓) prospered and did not suffer from the labor. Therefore this reign was praised as "the reign of the sage emperor" (聖帝世).
  3. Main textIn this reign, using the 秦人* people, the 茨田 embankment and the 三宅 of 茨田* were made, the 丸邇 Pond and the 依網 Pond* were made, the 堀江* canal of Naniwa was dug to connect with the sea, the 小椅江* was dug, and the port of Suminoe (墨江津) was established.
  4. Main textOf the four sons born of the chief consort 石之日売命*, three, 伊邪本和気命, 水歯別命 and 男浅津間若子宿禰命*, later ruled the realm.
  5. Compared with the Nihon ShokiIn the Nihon Shoki (Book 11), he sees that no smoke rises from a "high tower" (高台); in the 2nd month of the 4th year he speaks of it to his ministers, and in the 3rd month he orders a three-year exemption from labor levies and taxes. In the 4th month of the 7th year, seeing that smoke filled the land, he has an exchange with the empress, and in the 10th month of the 10th year he imposes the labor levies and taxes and has the palace built, and the common people worked willingly day and night. The Kojiki gives no year or month.
  6. CommentaryThe Kokugakuin University deity-name database introduces the view that the land of 墨江 (住吉, Sumiyoshi) was anciently called Suminoe, and that the role of the port (大津) serving as the royal power's base for external relations was carried by the port of Sumiyoshi (住吉津) until the first half of the sixth century, when it is said to have shifted to the port of Naniwa (難波津). [source, Japanese]

The story

大雀命 * (Emperor Nintoku) ruled the realm from the 高津宮* in Naniwa. He made 石之日売命*, daughter of 葛城之曾都毘古*, his chief consort, and four sons were born: 大江之伊邪本和気命, 墨江之中津王, 蝮之水歯別命 and 男浅津間若子宿禰命*. He also married 髪長比売*, daughter of 牛諸, the 諸県君 of Hyūga*, and 波多毘能大郎子 (also named 大日下王) and 波多毘能若郎女 (also named 長日比売命 and 若日下部命)* were born.

The emperor also married his half-sisters 八田若郎女 and 宇遅能若郎女*, but these two had no children. The text records that his children numbered six in all (five princes and one princess), and that three of them, 伊邪本和気命, 蝮之水歯別命 and 男浅津間若子宿禰命*, later ruled the realm. As name-bearing groups (御名代), the 葛城部 was established for the chief consort, the 壬生部 for the crown prince 伊邪本和気命, the 蝮部 for 水歯別命, the 大日下部 for 大日下王, and the 若日下部 for 若日下部王*.

In this reign, using the 秦人* people, the 茨田 embankment and the 三宅 of 茨田* were made, and the 丸邇 Pond and the 依網 Pond* were made. Also, the 堀江* canal of Naniwa was dug to connect with the sea, the 小椅江* was dug, and the port of Suminoe (墨江津) was established.

Once the emperor climbed a high mountain, looked out over the lands in all four directions, and ordered, "No smoke is rising in the land. The whole land is poor. Therefore, from now on, for three years, exempt the people from all labor levies and taxes (課役)." Because of this the palace fell into disrepair and rain leaked in everywhere, but he did not have it repaired at all; he caught the leaking rain in vessels and moved to places where it did not leak.

Later, when he looked over the land, smoke filled the land. Judging that the people had become prosperous, the emperor imposed the labor levies and taxes again. Thus the common people (百姓) prospered and did not suffer from the labor. The text records that therefore that reign was praised and called "the reign of the sage emperor" (聖帝世).

A plain retelling of what the original text of the Kojiki says (no published translation has been used).

In the original text

於國中烟不發。國皆貧窮

No smoke is rising in the land. The whole land is poor (the emperor's words).

悉除人民之課役

Exempt the people from all labor levies and taxes (課役).

以椷受其漏雨

He caught the leaking rain in vessels.

稱其御世。謂聖帝世也

That reign is praised and called "the reign of the sage emperor" (聖帝世).

In the Nihon Shoki

Nihon Shoki, Book 11 — the 4th year of Emperor Nintoku, spring, 2nd month (己未朔甲子)

In the Nihon Shoki, the emperor gazes far out from a high tower (高台), sees that no smoke rises, and issues an edict to his ministers lamenting the poverty of the common people. The way the place is described differs from the Kojiki's "climbed a high mountain," and the year and month are recorded.

朕登高臺以遠望之。烟氣不起於域中
Nihon Shoki, Book 11 — the 7th year of Emperor Nintoku, summer, 4th month (辛未朔)

In the Nihon Shoki, there is an exchange in which the emperor, seeing that smoke filled the land, says to the empress, "We are already rich," and, when the empress asks how this can be when even the palace fence is in ruins, answers that when the common people are rich, the emperor too is rich. The Kojiki does not have this exchange.

百姓富之。則朕富也
Nihon Shoki, Book 11 — the 10th year of Emperor Nintoku, winter, 10th month

In the Nihon Shoki, even after receiving the requests of the provinces he still waits, and only in the 10th month of the 10th year does he impose labor levies and taxes and have the palace built; the common people, bringing the old and the young, carried the timber themselves, and it was soon completed, and that is why he is still called "the sage emperor" (聖帝) today.
