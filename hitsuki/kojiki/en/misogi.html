Kojiki, Part 1 · story 5 of 17

Purification and the Three Noble Children

禊祓と三貴子

When Izanagi-no-Mikoto rinsed himself in the middle shallows of a river, deities arising from defilement, deities that set right that misfortune, and deities of the sea were born one after another, and finally, from his left eye, right eye and nose, the Three Noble Children (三貴子) were born: Amaterasu-Ōmikami, Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto and Susanoo-no-Mikoto. Izanagi-no-Mikoto entrusted to the three deities, respectively, the High Plain of Heaven (高天原), the realm of the night (夜の食国) and the sea plain, but Susanoo-no-Mikoto did not obey, kept weeping and wailing, and was driven out.

Read in the Nihon Shoki: The Birth of the Three Noble Children, the Birth of the Deities, and Yomi (Section 5) ›

Key points to remember

  1. Main textWhen Izanagi-no-Mikoto washed his left eye in the purification (禊), Amaterasu-Ōmikami was born; with his right eye, Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto; and with his nose, Susanoo-no-Mikoto (written 建速須佐之男命). Izanagi-no-Mikoto rejoiced greatly, saying, "At the last I have obtained three noble children." These are the "Three Noble Children" (三貴子).
  2. Main textIzanagi-no-Mikoto gave Amaterasu-Ōmikami the jewels of his necklace (Mikuratana-no-Kami) and entrusted her with ruling the High Plain of Heaven (高天原); he entrusted Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto with ruling the realm of the night (夜の食国), and Susanoo-no-Mikoto (written 建速須佐之男命) with ruling the sea plain.
  3. Main textSusanoo-no-Mikoto did not rule the sea plain but wept and wailed, "I want to go to 根の堅洲国*, the land of my mother," withering the mountains and drying up the rivers and seas, and so the angry Izanagi-no-Mikoto drove him out of this land.
  4. Main textIn the purification, first Yasomagatsuhi-no-Kami and Ōmagatsuhi-no-Kami were born from the defilement of the Land of Yomi (黄泉国), and then Kamunaobi-no-Kami, Ōnaobi-no-Kami and Izunome, who set that misfortune right, were born. From the bottom, middle and top of the water, deities of the 綿津見神 and 筒之男命 (readings unconfirmed) were born, three of each.
  5. Compared with the Nihon ShokiIn the main text of section 5 of the Nihon Shoki, the sun deity (Ōhirume-no-Muchi, 大日孁貴), the moon deity and Susanoo-no-Mikoto (素戔嗚尊) are regarded as children born together by the two deities Izanagi-no-Mikoto (伊弉諾尊) and Izanami-no-Mikoto (伊弉冉尊); the story in which the three deities are born when he washes his eyes and nose in the purification is found in variant account 6.
  6. CommentaryAccording to the Kokugakuin University database of deity names, the Magatsuhi deities (禍津日神) and the Naobi deities (直毘神) have paired names. Views on the Magatsuhi deities are divided, among others, between a view that sees them as deities that cause misfortune and a view that sees them as deities that censure wrongdoing and make it be corrected, and the debate continues. [source, Japanese]

The story

Izanagi-no-Mikoto said, "The upper shallows are fast-flowing and the lower shallows are weak-flowing," and he dived into the middle shallows and rinsed himself. At that time, first Yasomagatsuhi-no-Kami and Ōmagatsuhi-no-Kami were born. These two deities are recorded as having been born from the filth from when he went to a land full of defilement. Next, in order to set that misfortune right, three deities were born: Kamunaobi-no-Kami, Ōnaobi-no-Kami and Izunome.

When he rinsed himself at the bottom of the water, 底津綿津見神 and 底筒之男命 were born; when he rinsed himself in the middle, 中津綿津見神 and 中筒之男命; and when he rinsed himself at the top of the water, 上津綿津見神 and 上筒之男命 (readings unconfirmed). The three 綿津見神* are deities worshipped as ancestral deities by the 阿曇連* and others, and the 阿曇連 and others are said to be descendants of Utsushihikanasaku-no-Mikoto, a child of the 綿津見神. The three 筒之男命* are recorded as being the three great deities of 墨江 (墨江の三前の大神)*.

Next, when he washed his left eye, Amaterasu-Ōmikami was born; when he washed his right eye, Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto; and when he washed his nose, Susanoo-no-Mikoto (written 建速須佐之男命). The fourteen deities from Yasomagatsuhi-no-Kami to Susanoo-no-Mikoto (written 速須佐之男命) are regarded as deities born from his rinsing himself. Izanagi-no-Mikoto rejoiced greatly and said, "I have been giving birth to child after child, and at the last I have obtained three noble children." Then he shook the jewel cord he wore around his neck, gave it to Amaterasu-Ōmikami, and entrusted her, saying, "You shall rule the High Plain of Heaven (高天原)." The jewels of this necklace are called Mikuratana-no-Kami. Next he entrusted Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto with ruling the realm of the night (夜の食国), and Susanoo-no-Mikoto with ruling the sea plain.

But Susanoo-no-Mikoto (written 速須佐之男命) alone did not rule the land entrusted to him, and kept weeping and wailing until his long beard reached down to his chest. His weeping was such that it turned green mountains into withered mountains and wept the waters of the rivers and seas completely dry. Because of this, the voices of evil deities filled everything like flies, and every kind of calamity arose.

When Izanagi-no-Mikoto asked the reason, Susanoo-no-Mikoto answered, "I am weeping because I want to go to 根の堅洲国*, the land of my late mother." Izanagi-no-Mikoto was furious, said, "Then you shall not live in this land," and drove him out. The Kojiki records that Izanagi-no-Ōkami is enshrined at 多賀 in 淡海 (readings unconfirmed).

A plain retelling of what the original text of the Kojiki says (no published translation has been used).

In the original text

上瀨者瀨速，下瀨者瀨弱。

The upper shallows flow too fast and the lower shallows flow too weakly (so he rinses himself in the middle shallows).

吾者生々子而於生終得三貴子。

I have been giving birth to child after child, and at the end of my giving birth I have obtained three noble children.

汝命者，所知高天原矣。

You shall rule the High Plain of Heaven (words to Amaterasu-Ōmikami).

僕者欲罷妣國根之堅洲國，故哭。

I am weeping because I want to go to the land of my late mother, 根の堅洲国*.

In the Nihon Shoki

Nihon Shoki, Book 1, section 5, main text

In the main text of section 5, Izanagi-no-Mikoto (伊弉諾尊) and Izanami-no-Mikoto (伊弉冉尊) consult together, saying, "Let us give birth to one who will be ruler of all under heaven," and together give birth to the sun deity, whom they name Ōhirume-no-Muchi (大日孁貴); it does not say that she was born from the purification.

於是共生日神，號大日孁貴
Nihon Shoki, Book 1, section 5, variant account 6

Even in variant account 6, where the three deities are born from the purification, the places entrusted to them differ from the Kojiki: Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto (月読尊) is commanded to rule the eightfold layers of the tides of the blue sea plain, and Susanoo-no-Mikoto (素戔嗚尊) to rule all under heaven.

月讀尊者，可以治滄海原潮之八百重也
Nihon Shoki, Book 1, section 5, variant account 6

In variant account 6, the misfortune deity born when he rinses himself in the middle shallows is only one, 八十枉津日神*, the deities that set it right are two, 神直日神 and 大直日神 (readings unconfirmed), and no deity corresponding to Izunome appears.

因以生神號曰八十枉津日神
