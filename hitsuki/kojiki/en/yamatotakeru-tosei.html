The Eastern Campaign of Yamato Takeru (倭建命)

倭建命の東征

Ordered to pacify the twelve circuits of the east (東方十二道) soon after returning from the western campaign, Yamato Takeru (倭建命) lamented to his aunt 倭比賣命* at Ise and received from her the Kusanagi sword (草那藝劍) and a bag (御嚢). He came through a grass fire in 相武國*; at the 走水海* sea his consort 弟橘比賣命* entered the sea in his place; and on the 足柄* slope he lamented "阿豆麻波夜"* before reaching the 酒折宮* in Kai.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textOrdered to pacify the twelve circuits of the east, Yamato Takeru (倭建命) lamented and wept to his aunt 倭比賣命* at Ise, saying, "The emperor has my death in mind," and was given the Kusanagi sword (草那藝劍) and a bag (御嚢).
  2. Main textIn 相武國*, deceived by the governor (國造) and surrounded by a grass fire, Yamato Takeru cut the grass with his sword, lit a counter-fire with the fire-striker in the bag, escaped, and destroyed the governor and his men. This is regarded as the origin of the place name 燒津*.
  3. Main textWhen the crossing deity (渡神) of the 走水海* sea raised the waves, his consort 弟橘比賣命* spread mats on the waves and entered the sea; the waves calmed and the ship could go on. Seven days later the consort's comb washed ashore, and a tomb was made in which it was laid.
  4. Main textOn the 足柄* slope Yamato Takeru struck dead the slope deity, who had become a white deer, with a piece of 蒜 (garlic); then at the top of the slope he lamented three times, "阿豆麻波夜"*. This is regarded as the origin of the name "阿豆麻" for that land.
  5. Compared with the Nihon ShokiIn the Nihon Shoki (the 40th year of Emperor Keikō), Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto (日本武尊) volunteered on his own to take on the eastern campaign; he meets the grass fire in Suruga, laments "吾嬬者耶"* at the 碓日嶺* pass, and kills the mountain deity in the form of a white deer with garlic (蒜) in the mountains of Shinano.
  6. CommentaryThe Kokugakuin University deity-name database (entry for Shinano-no-Saka-no-Kami, 科野之坂神) notes that it has been pointed out that the story in the Nihon Shoki, in which the mountain deity of the slope in Shinano becomes a white deer and is killed with garlic, closely resembles the Kojiki story of the deity of the 足柄* slope. [source, Japanese]

The story

The emperor again ordered Yamato Takeru (倭建命) to subdue and pacify (言向和) the unruly deities and unsubmissive people of the twelve circuits of the east (東方十二道). He assigned 御鉏友耳建日子*, ancestor of the 吉備臣* and others, to accompany him, and gave him the eight-fathom spear of 比比羅木*. On setting out, Yamato Takeru went to the Great Shrine of Ise (伊勢大御神宮) and said to his aunt 倭比賣命*, "Does the emperor think I should die? I have only just returned from striking down the evil men of the west, and yet without even giving me an army he now sends me again to pacify the evil men of the twelve circuits of the east. Surely he wishes for my death," and he lamented and wept. 倭比賣命 gave him the Kusanagi sword (草那藝劍), and also gave him a bag (御嚢), telling him to untie its mouth if anything urgent happened.

On reaching Owari, Yamato Takeru entered the house of Miyazu-hime (美夜受比賣), ancestor of the governors of Owari (尾張國造). He intended to marry her, but thinking again that he would marry her on his way back, he made a promise with her and headed for the eastern provinces, subduing and pacifying the unruly deities of the mountains and rivers and the unsubmissive people. When he reached 相武國*, its governor (國造) falsely told him, "In the middle of this plain there is a large marsh, and a very violent deity lives there." When Yamato Takeru entered the plain to see that deity, the governor set fire to the plain.

Realizing he had been deceived, Yamato Takeru opened the mouth of the bag given him by 倭比賣命* and looked, and there was a fire-striker inside. First he cut away the grass with his sword, then struck fire with the fire-striker and lit a counter-fire, burned back the approaching fire, and came out of the plain. He then cut down and destroyed all the governor and his men and set fire to them and burned them, so that place is still called 燒津*.

Going on from there, he was about to cross the 走水海* sea when the crossing deity (渡神) raised the waves and spun the ship round and round so that it could not go forward. His consort 弟橘比賣命* offered, "I will enter the sea in place of the prince. Let the prince accomplish the task he was sent on and report to the emperor." She spread mats of sedge (菅疊), of hide (皮疊) and of silk (絁疊), each eightfold, on the waves and stepped down onto them. Then the rough waves calmed of themselves, and the ship was able to go on. At this time the consort composed a song.

Seven days later the consort's comb washed up on the shore, so the comb was taken, a tomb was made, and the comb was laid in it. Yamato Takeru went further, subdued all the unruly Emishi (蝦夷), pacified the unruly deities of the mountains and rivers, and started home. When he was eating at the foot of the 足柄* slope, the slope deity appeared, having become a white deer, and stood there; he struck it with a leftover scrap of 蒜 (garlic), hit it in the eye, and killed it.

Yamato Takeru climbed to the top of the slope, stood there and lamented three times, saying, "阿豆麻波夜"*. Therefore that land is called 阿豆麻. Crossing from there into Kai, while he was at the 酒折宮*, Yamato Takeru composed a song, and an old man who was tending the fires (御火) continued the song with one of his own.

In the Nihon Shoki

Nihon Shoki, Book 7 — the 40th year of Emperor Keikō, autumn, 7th month (癸未朔戊戌)

In the Nihon Shoki, when the ministers could not decide whom to send against the rebellion in the east, Prince 大碓* ran away and hid, whereas Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto (日本武尊) took on the pacification himself; the emperor gave him an axe of authority (斧鉞) and said, "In form you are my son, but in truth you are a divine man." There is no scene, as in the Kojiki, in which Yamato Takeru laments that "my father wishes for my death."

形則我子。實則神人。
Nihon Shoki, Book 7 — the 40th year of Emperor Keikō, winter, 10th month (戊午), and "this year" (是歳)

In the Nihon Shoki, what 倭姫命* gives him at Ise is only the Kusanagi sword (草薙釼), and he meets the grass fire in Suruga. In the main text he lights a counter-fire with a fire-striker (燧) and escapes; an alternative account (一云) relates that the 叢雲* sword he wore drew itself and mowed down the grass, and that it was therefore named "Kusanagi" (草薙).

王所佩釼叢雲自抽之薙攘王之傍草。
Nihon Shoki, Book 7 — the 40th year of Emperor Keikō, "this year" (是歳)

In the Nihon Shoki, 弟橘媛* is described as a consort (妾), daughter of 忍山宿禰 of the 穗積* clan, and the storm arises after Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto says, "This is a small sea; I could leap across it." Also, after pacifying the Emishi of Mutsu, he laments three times "吾嬬者耶"* at the 碓日嶺* pass, longing for 弟橘媛, and in the mountains of Shinano he kills with garlic (蒜) the mountain deity transformed into a white deer.

是小海耳。可立跳渡。
