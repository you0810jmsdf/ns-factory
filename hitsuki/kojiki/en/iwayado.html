Kojiki, Part 1 · story 7 of 17

The Heavenly Rock-Cave Door

天の岩屋戸

When Amaterasu-Ōmikami shut herself inside the Heavenly Rock-Cave Door (天石屋戸), both the High Plain of Heaven (高天原) and the Central Land of Reed Plains (葦原中国) grew dark, and all kinds of calamities arose. The myriad deities (八百万の神々) consulted together, gathered the implements for a ritual, lured Amaterasu-Ōmikami out with the dance of Ame-no-Uzume-no-Mikoto and the laughter of the deities, and restored light to the world.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textWhen Amaterasu-Ōmikami closed the Heavenly Rock-Cave Door (天石屋戸) and shut herself in, both the High Plain of Heaven (高天原) and the Central Land of Reed Plains (葦原中国) became pitch dark, night went on and on, and every kind of calamity arose.
  2. Main textThe myriad deities gathered at the riverbed of the heavenly river 天の安の河*, had Omoikane-no-Kami (思金神) draw up a plan, had 伊斯許理度売命* make a mirror and Tamanooya-no-Mikoto make strings of curved jewels (勾玉), hung them on the branches of a sakaki tree (榊), and prayed.
  3. Main textWhen Ame-no-Uzume-no-Mikoto became possessed by the deity and danced before the door, the myriad deities laughed; they showed the mirror to Amaterasu-Ōmikami, who had opened the door wondering at this, and Ame-no-Tajikarao-no-Kami took her by the hand and drew her out, and so light returned to the world.
  4. Compared with the Nihon ShokiIn the main text of section 7 of the Nihon Shoki as well, the sequence is shared: the plan of Omoikane-no-Kami (思兼神), the prayers of Ame-no-Koyane-no-Mikoto (天児屋命) and Futodama-no-Mikoto (太玉命), the dance of Ame-no-Uzume-no-Mikoto (天鈿女命), and the drawing out by 手力雄神*. However, there is no scene in which a mirror is held out and shown to Amaterasu-Ōmikami (天照大神); instead, Amaterasu-Ōmikami herself opens the door a crack and peeps out, and 手力雄神 draws her out.
  5. CommentaryAccording to the Kokugakuin University database of deity names, regarding Ame-no-Uzume-no-Mikoto's act of baring her breasts and private parts, there are views such as that it aimed at the revival of nature through laughter, and that it relates to rites for pacifying the spirit (鎮魂), and in each of them its meaning as a rite for the revival of the sun deity is given great weight. [source, Japanese]
  6. CommentaryThe same database explains that the name of Omoikane-no-Kami (思金神) is often interpreted, from the Nihon Shoki spelling 思兼神, as a deity combining many kinds of thought, and that it is regarded as a deity that appears throughout the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki as an adviser to Amaterasu-Ōmikami. [source, Japanese]

The story

Seeing the violence of Susanoo-no-Mikoto told in the previous story, Amaterasu-Ōmikami was afraid, closed the Heavenly Rock-Cave Door (天石屋戸), and shut herself inside. Then the High Plain of Heaven (高天原) became completely dark, the Central Land of Reed Plains (葦原中国) too was wrapped in darkness, and there was nothing but night. The voices of many deities filled the surroundings like swarms of flies, and every kind of calamity arose.

So the myriad deities (八百万の神々) gathered at the riverbed of the heavenly river 天の安の河* and had Omoikane-no-Kami (思金神), a child of Takamimusubi-no-Kami, devise a plan. The deities gathered the long-crying birds of 常世* and made them cry, took hard stones from the upper reaches of the heavenly river 天の安の河 and iron from the heavenly metal mountain (天の金山), and sought out the smith Amatsumara. They had 伊斯許理度売命* make a mirror, and Tamanooya-no-Mikoto make strings of long curved jewels (八尺の勾玉).

Further, they summoned Ame-no-Koyane-no-Mikoto and Futodama-no-Mikoto (布刀玉命) and had them divine, pulling out the shoulder bone of a stag from 天香山* and using wood of the 波波迦* tree from 天香山. They dug up a sakaki tree (榊) of 天香山 by the roots, and hung the strings of jewels on its upper branches, the mirror 八尺鏡* on its middle branches, and white and blue cloth on its lower branches. Futodama-no-Mikoto held this up as an offering (御幣), Ame-no-Koyane-no-Mikoto recited a ritual prayer (祝詞), and Ame-no-Tajikarao-no-Kami stood hidden beside the door.

Ame-no-Uzume-no-Mikoto hung the 日影 vine* of 天香山 across her shoulders as a sash, made a hair ornament of the 真拆 vine*, and held bundled bamboo-grass leaves in her hand. Then she turned a tub (汙氣) upside down in front of the Heavenly Rock-Cave Door and stamped on it until it resounded, became possessed by the deity, bared her breasts, and pushed the cord of her skirt down to her private parts. At this the myriad deities all laughed at once, so that the High Plain of Heaven rang with it.

Amaterasu-Ōmikami wondered at this, opened the door a crack, and asked, "Since I have shut myself away, the world should be dark, so why is Ame-no-Uzume dancing so merrily and why are the deities laughing?" Ame-no-Uzume-no-Mikoto answered, "We are rejoicing and laughing because there is a deity here more noble than you." Meanwhile Ame-no-Koyane-no-Mikoto and Futodama-no-Mikoto held out the mirror and showed it to her, and Amaterasu-Ōmikami, wondering more and more, came out from the door little by little.

At that moment Ame-no-Tajikarao-no-Kami, who had been standing hidden, took Amaterasu-Ōmikami by the hand and drew her out, and Futodama-no-Mikoto at once stretched a rope (尻久米縄)* across behind her and said, "You cannot go back inside beyond this." When Amaterasu-Ōmikami thus came out, both the High Plain of Heaven and the Central Land of Reed Plains became bright and illuminated of themselves.

A plain retelling of what the original text of the Kojiki says (no published translation has been used).

In the original text

閇天石屋戶而，刺許母理〔此三字以音〕坐也

She closed the Heavenly Rock-Cave Door and shut herself away inside.

高天原動而八百萬神共咲

The myriad deities all laughed at once, so that the High Plain of Heaven rang with it.

益汝命而貴神坐，故歡喜咲樂

(Ame-no-Uzume-no-Mikoto's answer) We are rejoicing, laughing and making merry because there is a deity here more noble than you.

高天原及葦原中國，自得照明

Both the High Plain of Heaven and the Central Land of Reed Plains became bright and illuminated of themselves.

In the Nihon Shoki

Nihon Shoki, Book 1, section 7, main text

The main text of the Nihon Shoki says that Ame-no-Uzume-no-Mikoto (天鈿女命) held in her hand a spear wound with reeds (茅) and performed skillfully as an entertainer (俳優, wazaogi) in front of the door of the heavenly rock cave (天石窟); this is depicted differently from the Kojiki's figure who becomes possessed with bamboo-grass leaves in her hand.

手持茅纒之矟
Nihon Shoki, Book 1, section 7, main text

In the main text of the Nihon Shoki, Amaterasu-Ōmikami (天照大神), hearing of Ame-no-Uzume-no-Mikoto's (天鈿女命) behavior, opens the door a crack with her own hand and peeps out, and at that point 手力雄神* draws her out; there is no scene, as in the Kojiki, in which a mirror is held out and shown to her.

乃以御手細開磐戶窺之
Nihon Shoki, Book 1, section 7, variant account 2

Variant account 2 records that when the mirror was put into the rock cave it touched the door and received a small scratch, which still remains today, and states that this mirror is the great deity enshrined at Ise. This statement is not in the Kojiki.
