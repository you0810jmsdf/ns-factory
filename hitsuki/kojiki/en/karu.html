Kojiki, Part 3 · story 7 of 13

Emperor Ingyō, Crown Prince 軽 and 軽大郎女 (readings unconfirmed)

允恭天皇と軽太子・軽大郎女

男浅津間若子宿禰命 * (Emperor Ingyō) at first declined to take the throne on account of illness, but his illness was cured by an envoy from Silla (新良), and he corrected the confusion in clan names and titles. After the emperor’s death, the heir 木梨之軽太子* had relations with his full sister 軽大郎女*, was abandoned by the people and exiled to the hot springs of Iyo (伊余湯), and he and 軽大郎女, who followed him, took their own lives together.

Read in the Nihon Shoki: Emperor Ingyō's oath ordeal and 衣通郎姫*, and the death of Emperor Ankō ›

Key points to remember

  1. Main text男浅津間若子宿禰命 * (Emperor Ingyō) once declined to take the throne because of a long illness, but at the earnest request of the chief consort and the ministers, he ruled the realm. The chief envoy 金波鎮漢紀武*, sent by the ruler of Silla (新良), cured the emperor’s illness with his knowledge of medicine.
  2. Main textTroubled by the confusion in clan names and titles (氏姓), the emperor set up 玖訶瓮 vessels* at 言八十禍津日前 in 味白檮 (readings unconfirmed) and fixed the clan names and titles of the many hereditary groups (八十友緒) of the realm.
  3. Main textAfter the emperor’s death, 木梨之軽太子*, who had been designated heir (日継), had relations with his full sister 軽大郎女* before his accession, so the officials and the people of the realm turned against him and went over to his younger brother, 穴穂御子*.
  4. Main textCrown Prince 軽 fled to the house of 大前小前宿禰*, but the 宿禰 seized him and handed him over to 穴穂御子*, and he was exiled to the hot springs of Iyo (伊余湯). 軽大郎女 (衣通王) (readings unconfirmed), unable to bear her longing, followed him, and the two took their own lives together. Their songs are recorded under names such as 志良宜歌, 天田振 and 読歌 (readings unconfirmed).
  5. Compared with the Nihon ShokiIn the Nihon Shoki, the affair of the crown prince and 軽大娘皇女* is placed in the lifetime of his father, Emperor Ingyō (the 23rd and 24th years); it is the princess who is exiled to Iyo, and the crown prince is not punished because he is the heir apparent. The crown prince later takes his own life at the house of 大前宿禰*, and it is also recorded, as one account, that he was exiled to Iyo Province. In addition, “衣通郎姫”* in the Nihon Shoki is the name by which 弟姫*, the empress’s younger sister, was known.
  6. CommentaryKokugakuin University’s database of deity names notes that, because 言八十禍津日前 (written 辞禍戸岬 in the Nihon Shoki; readings unconfirmed) is a place name resembling the deity name 八十禍津日神*, there is also a conjecture, though indirect, that the Magatsuhi deities are connected with kukatachi (盟神探湯), the ordeal for judging whether words are true or false. [source, Japanese]

The story

男浅津間若子宿禰命 * (Emperor Ingyō) ruled the realm from the Far Asuka palace (遠飛鳥宮). He married 忍坂之大中津比売命*, younger sister of 意富本杼王*, and nine children were born: 木梨之軽王, 長田大郎女, 境之黒日子王, 穴穂命, 軽大郎女, 八瓜之白日子王, 大長谷命, 橘大郎女 and 酒見郎女 (readings unconfirmed). A small note says that 軽大郎女’s other name was 衣通郎女*, and that because the radiance of her body shone through her clothes, she bore the name 衣通王. Of the nine, 穴穂命 and 大長谷命 ruled the realm.

When the emperor was about to receive the succession (日継), he declined, saying, “I have a long illness, so I cannot receive the succession.” But because the chief consort and the ministers urged him strongly, he ruled the realm. At this time the ruler of Silla (新良) sent eighty-one ships of tribute, and their chief envoy, 金波鎮漢紀武*, knew the methods of medicine well and cured the emperor’s illness.

Troubled that the clan names and titles (氏姓) of the people of the various clans and names of the realm were in confusion, the emperor set up 玖訶瓮 vessels* at 言八十禍津日前 in 味白檮 (readings unconfirmed) and fixed the clan names and titles of the many hereditary groups (八十友緒) of the realm. He also established the 軽部 as the namesake group (御名代) of 木梨之軽太子, the 刑部 as the namesake group of the chief consort, and the 河部 as the namesake group of the chief consort’s younger sister 田井中比売 (readings unconfirmed). The emperor lived to the age of seventy-eight, and his tomb is at 長枝 in 恵賀, Kawachi (readings unconfirmed).

After the emperor died, it was settled that 木梨之軽太子* would receive the succession. But before his accession he had relations with his full sister 軽大郎女* and sang songs (志良宜歌 and 夷振之上歌; readings unconfirmed). For this reason the officials and the people of the realm turned against Crown Prince 軽 and went over to 穴穂御子*. Crown Prince 軽 took fright, fled into the house of the minister 大前小前宿禰* and prepared weapons. A small note says that the arrows made at this time had copper on the inner part of the arrow and were called “軽箭”, and that 穴穂御子 also made weapons, and those arrows are the arrows of today, called “穴穂箭” (readings unconfirmed).

穴穂御子* raised an army and surrounded the house of 大前小前宿禰*. When he reached the gate, a heavy icy rain fell, and 穴穂御子 sang a song. 大前小前宿禰 came out raising his hands and striking his knees, dancing and singing (宮人振)*, and said, “Do not turn your troops against your royal brother born of the same mother. If you do, people will surely laugh at you. I will seize him and hand him over.” When 穴穂御子 withdrew his troops, 大前小前宿禰 seized Crown Prince 軽 and handed him over. The captured crown prince sang two songs.

Crown Prince 軽 was exiled to the hot springs of Iyo (伊余湯). As he was about to be sent away, the crown prince again sang songs (天田振 and 夷振之片下; readings unconfirmed). 衣通王 (軽大郎女) (readings unconfirmed) also offered a song.

Later, 衣通王*, unable to bear her longing, followed after the crown prince and sang a song. When she caught up with him, the crown prince, who had been waiting for her, sang two songs (読歌)*. Having finished singing in this way, the two took their own lives together.

A plain retelling of what the original text of the Kojiki says (no published translation has been used).

In the original text

我者。有一長病。不得所知日繼

I have a long illness, so I cannot receive the succession (the emperor’s words).

背輕太子而。歸穴穂御子

(The officials and the people of the realm) turned against Crown Prince 軽 and went over to 穴穂御子 (readings unconfirmed).

故其輕太子者。流於伊余湯也

Thus Crown Prince 軽* was exiled to the hot springs of Iyo (伊余湯).

如此歌。即共自死

Having sung in this way, the two took their own lives together.

In the Nihon Shoki

Nihon Shoki, Book 13, the 7th year of Emperor Ingyō, winter, 12th month (壬戌朔)

In the Nihon Shoki, “衣通郎姫”* is the name by which 弟姫*, the empress’s younger sister, was known, given because her beauty shone through her clothes. In the Kojiki, 衣通郎女 (衣通王) is another name of 軽大郎女 (readings unconfirmed), so the names refer to different people.

是以。時人號曰衣通郎姫也
Nihon Shoki, Book 13, the 24th year of Emperor Ingyō, summer, 6th month

In the Nihon Shoki, in the 6th month of the 24th year, the soup for the emperor’s meal froze, divination pointed to internal disorder, and the affair of the crown prince and his full sister came to light. The crown prince was not punished because he was the heir apparent, and 軽大娘皇女* was exiled to Iyo. In the Kojiki, this happens after the father’s death, and it is the crown prince who is exiled.

則流輕大娘皇女於伊豫
Nihon Shoki, Book 13, Emperor Ankō, record before accession (the 42nd year of Emperor Ingyō)

In the Nihon Shoki, after Emperor Ingyō’s death, the crown prince takes refuge in the house of 物部大前宿禰*, and the 宿禰 begs Prince 穴穂 (穴穂皇子)* not to harm the crown prince, but the crown prince takes his own life in that house; as one account, it is said that he was exiled to Iyo Province.
