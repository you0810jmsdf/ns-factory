The Rebellion of Tagishimimi-no-Mikoto

当芸志美美命の反逆

After Emperor Jinmu died, Tagishimimi-no-Mikoto (當藝志美美命), an elder half-brother, took the empress 伊須氣余理比賣* as his wife and plotted to kill his three younger half-brothers. Of the younger brothers, who learned of this through their mother's songs, the youngest, 神沼河耳命*, struck down Tagishimimi-no-Mikoto, and, with the throne yielded to him by his elder brother Kamuyaimimi-no-Mikoto, ruled the realm.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textWhen Emperor Jinmu died, Tagishimimi-no-Mikoto, the elder half-brother born in Hyūga, took the empress 伊須氣余理比賣* as his wife and plotted to kill her children, his three younger half-brothers. 伊須氣余理比賣 warned the princes of the danger in songs.
  2. Main textThe elder brother Kamuyaimimi-no-Mikoto trembled hand and foot and could not kill Tagishimimi-no-Mikoto, so the younger brother 神沼河耳命* took the weapon from his elder brother and struck him down. Because of this deed, 神沼河耳命 was also called 建沼河耳命*.
  3. Main textKamuyaimimi-no-Mikoto, saying that since he had failed to kill the enemy he should not stand above, even though he was the elder, yielded the position to his younger brother and became a ritual abstainer (忌人) to serve him. Thus 神沼河耳命 * (Emperor Suizei) ruled the realm.
  4. Main textEmperor Jinmu's age is recorded as one hundred and thirty-seven years, and his tomb as being on top of 白檮尾*, north of Mount Unebi (畝火山).
  5. Compared with the Nihon ShokiIn the Nihon Shoki, a bow and arrow are used to strike down 手研耳命 * (Tagishimimi-no-Mikoto), and those targeted are “two younger brothers” (二弟). Emperor Jinmu's age is also given as one hundred and twenty-seven years, which differs from the Kojiki.

The story

The children born to 伊須氣余理比賣* were three: 日子八井命*, Kamuyaimimi-no-Mikoto (神八井耳命) and 神沼河耳命*. After the emperor died, their elder half-brother Tagishimimi-no-Mikoto took 伊須氣余理比賣, who had been the emperor's principal wife, as his wife and plotted to kill his three younger brothers. Their mother 伊須氣余理比賣, distressed by this, warned the princes in songs (C27, C29). The words “佐韋賀波” (the Sai River, 狭井河) and “宇泥備夜麻” (Mount Unebi, 畝傍山) appear in the songs.

The princes were startled to hear this and resolved to kill Tagishimimi-no-Mikoto. 神沼河耳命* asked his elder brother Kamuyaimimi-no-Mikoto to take a weapon, go in and kill Tagishimimi-no-Mikoto. Kamuyaimimi-no-Mikoto went in with the weapon, but his hands and feet trembled (written phonetically in the original as “和那那岐弖”) and he could not kill him. So the younger brother 神沼河耳命 asked for the weapon his elder brother was holding, went in and killed Tagishimimi-no-Mikoto. For this reason 神沼河耳命 is also called 建沼河耳命*.

Kamuyaimimi-no-Mikoto yielded the position to his younger brother, saying: “I could not kill the enemy, but you were able to kill him. Therefore, though I am the elder, I should not stand above. You shall stand above and rule the realm. I will help you and serve you as a ritual abstainer (忌人).” A note in the original text makes 日子八井命* the ancestor of the 茨田連 and 手嶋連 (readings unconfirmed), and Kamuyaimimi-no-Mikoto the ancestor of many lineages, including the 意富臣, the 小子部連, the 阿蘇君 and the provincial chiefs of Shinano (科野国造) (readings unconfirmed). 神沼河耳命 ruled the realm.

It is recorded that the age of this emperor 神倭伊波禮毘古* was one hundred and thirty-seven years, and that his tomb is on top of 白檮尾*, north of Mount Unebi (畝火山).

A plain retelling of what the original text of the Kojiki says (no published translation has been used).

In the original text

將殺其三弟而。謀之間。

When Tagishimimi-no-Mikoto was plotting to kill his three younger brothers.

手足和那那岐弖不得殺。

Kamuyaimimi-no-Mikoto trembled hand and foot and could not kill him.

吾者不能殺仇。汝命既得殺仇。

The words of Kamuyaimimi-no-Mikoto: I could not kill the enemy, but you have already killed the enemy.

御年。壹佰參拾漆歳。

Emperor Jinmu's age was one hundred and thirty-seven years.

In the Nihon Shoki

Nihon Shoki, Book 4, Emperor Suizei, record before accession, the year 己卯, winter, 11th month

In the Nihon Shoki, 神渟名川耳尊 * (神沼河耳命) has a bow and arrows made and targets 手研耳命*; at 片丘*, where 手研耳命 was sleeping alone on a large bed, he takes the bow and arrows from his elder brother, who is trembling and cannot shoot, and shoots him dead. The Kojiki writes only “兵” (weapon) and does not record the place either.

掣取其兄所持弓矢。而射手研耳命。
Nihon Shoki, Book 4, Emperor Suizei, record before accession

In the Nihon Shoki, those targeted by 手研耳命* are “two younger brothers” (二弟), and in the record of Emperor Jinmu as well, the princes born to the empress are two, 神八井命* and 神渟名川耳尊*. In the Kojiki they are three younger brothers (日子八井命, Kamuyaimimi-no-Mikoto and 神沼河耳命).

圖害二弟
Nihon Shoki, Book 3, Emperor Jinmu, 76th year, spring, 3rd month (甲午朔甲辰)

In the Nihon Shoki, Emperor Jinmu dies at the Kashihara palace (橿原宮) in spring, 3rd month, of his 76th year (甲午朔甲辰), at the age of one hundred and twenty-seven, and is buried the following year in a tomb north-east of Mount Unebi (畝傍山). In the Kojiki he is one hundred and thirty-seven, and his tomb is on top of 白檮尾*, north of Mount Unebi (畝火山).
