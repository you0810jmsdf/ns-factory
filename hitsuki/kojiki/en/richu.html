Emperor Richū, Emperor Hanzei and the Revolt of 墨江中王*

履中天皇・反正天皇と墨江中王の反乱

While 伊邪本和気命 * (Emperor Richū) lay asleep drunk, his younger brother 墨江中王* set fire to his great hall; rescued by 阿知直*, he escaped to Isonokami Shrine (石上神宮). His younger brother 水歯別命*, ordered to clear himself of suspicion, had the Hayato 曾婆訶理* kill 墨江中王 and then cut down that Hayato as well; later 水歯別命 ruled the realm as Emperor Hanzei.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textAt the banquet (豊明) of the Great Thanksgiving (大嘗) at the Naniwa palace (難波宮), while 伊邪本和気命 * (Emperor Richū) lay asleep drunk on sake, his younger brother 墨江中王* set fire to the great hall, intending to kill the emperor. 阿知直*, ancestor of the 倭漢直*, put the emperor on a horse and sent him away to Yamato (倭).
  2. Main textAt the entrance to Mount 大坂山*, told by a woman, the emperor went round by the 当岐麻道 road* and entered Isonokami Shrine (石上神宮). He suspected his younger brother 水歯別命*, who came there, and ordered him to kill 墨江中王* before coming to him.
  3. Main text水歯別命* promised the Hayato 曾婆訶理* the rank of minister and had him kill 墨江中王* in the privy; but holding that killing one’s own lord was unrighteous, at the banquet where he conferred the rank of minister, he cut off 曾婆訶理’s head. The place he went up to the next day was named Near Asuka (近飛鳥), and the place where he stayed to perform purification (祓禊) was named Far Asuka (遠飛鳥).
  4. Main textEmperor Richū lived to the age of sixty-four, and his tomb is at Mozu (毛受). The following ruler, 水歯別命 * (Emperor Hanzei), governed from the 柴垣宮 palace at 多治比 (readings unconfirmed). It is recorded that he was nine shaku two and a half sun tall and that his teeth, upper and lower, were even, like strung beads. He lived to the age of sixty, and his tomb is in the plain of Mozu (毛受野).
  5. Compared with the Nihon ShokiIn the Nihon Shoki, the incident begins when 住吉仲皇子* goes to 黒媛* using the crown prince’s name, and the crown prince is rescued by three men: 平群木菟宿禰, 物部大前宿禰 and 阿知使主 (readings unconfirmed). The one who stabs 仲皇子 is his attendant, the Hayato 刺領巾*, and 木菟宿禰 advises that a man who has killed his own lord cannot be allowed to live, whereupon 刺領巾 is killed.
  6. CommentaryKokugakuin University’s database of deity names explains that Isonokami Shrine (石上神宮) served as a divine storehouse and armoury holding the sacred treasures of the royal power, and that, together with Ise and Izumo, it was one of the few shrines called a “jingū” (神宮) in the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki. [source, Japanese]

The story

伊邪本和気命 * (Emperor Richū) ruled the realm from the 若桜宮 palace at 伊波礼 (readings unconfirmed). He married 黒比売命*, daughter of 葦田宿禰*, son of 葛城之曾都毘古*, and 市辺之忍歯王, 御馬王 and 青海郎女 (also called 飯豊郎女) (readings unconfirmed) were born.

When the emperor was still at the Naniwa palace (難波宮), at the banquet (豊明) after the Great Thanksgiving (大嘗), he became drunk on sake and fell asleep. At that time his younger brother 墨江中王* set fire to the great hall, intending to kill the emperor. 阿知直*, ancestor of the 倭漢直*, carried the emperor out, put him on a horse and sent him toward Yamato (倭). When the emperor awoke at 多遅比野* and asked, “Where is this?”, 阿知直 answered that 墨江中王 had set fire to the great hall, so he was taking him and fleeing to Yamato. The emperor sang a song, and when he reached the 波邇賦坂 slope* and looked back toward the Naniwa palace, the fire was still burning, so he sang again.

At the entrance to Mount 大坂山* he met a woman. She told him, “Many armed men are blocking this mountain. Go round by the 当岐麻道 road* and cross that way,” and the emperor sang a song. In this way the emperor went up and entered Isonokami Shrine (石上神宮).

His younger brother by the same mother, 水歯別命*, came to him, but the emperor sent word: “I suspect that you are of the same mind as 墨江中王*, so I will not meet and speak with you.” When 水歯別命 replied, “I have no impure heart, and I am not of one mind with 墨江中王,” the emperor ordered, “Then go back to Naniwa and kill 墨江中王 and come. Then I will certainly meet and speak with you.” 水歯別命 returned to Naniwa and approached 曾婆訶理*, a Hayato who served close to 墨江中王, saying, “If you do as I say, I will become emperor, make you a minister, and rule the realm,” and he gave him many goods. 曾婆訶理 watched for his master to enter the privy and stabbed him to death with a spear.

On the way up to Yamato with 曾婆訶理*, 水歯別命* reflected at the entrance to Mount 大坂山*: “曾婆訶理 has done me a great service, but killing his own lord is unrighteous. Yet if I do not reward his service, I will be faithless. Even as I keep my promise, his heart is fearsome. So I will reward his service, but destroy him.” He therefore built a temporary palace, held a banquet (豊楽), conferred the rank of minister on the Hayato, and had the officials bow to him. It came about that he would drink sake from the same cup as the delighted Hayato; sake was poured into a bowl so large it hid the face, and the prince drank first and the Hayato after. As the Hayato drank, the great bowl covered his face, and the prince took out the sword he had placed beneath his seat and cut off his head.

Because he went up the next day, that place is called Near Asuka (近飛鳥). On reaching Yamato he said, “Today I will stay here and perform purification (祓禊), and tomorrow I will go up and worship at the shrine,” so that place is called Far Asuka (遠飛鳥). He then went to Isonokami Shrine (石上神宮) and sent word that “the matter has already been settled,” and the emperor called him in and spoke with him. The emperor appointed 阿知直* for the first time as an official of the storehouses (蔵官) and granted him land for provisions (粮地). In this reign he also granted the names or titles 若桜部 and 比売陀君 (readings unconfirmed) and established the 伊波礼部*. The emperor lived to the age of sixty-four, and his tomb is at Mozu (毛受).

水歯別命 * (Emperor Hanzei) ruled the realm from the 柴垣宮 palace at 多治比 (readings unconfirmed). The emperor was nine shaku two and a half sun tall, and his teeth were one sun long and two bu wide, even above and below, like strung beads. He married 都怒郎女*, daughter of 許碁登臣 of 丸邇 (readings unconfirmed), and 甲斐郎女 and 都夫良郎女 (readings unconfirmed) were born; he married 弟比売*, another daughter of the same 臣, and 財王 and 多訶弁郎女 (readings unconfirmed) were born. His children numbered four in all. He lived to the age of sixty, and his tomb is in the plain of Mozu (毛受野).

Deities and people, and the shrines that enshrine them

  • 伊邪本和氣命*Emperor Richū. His great hall was burned by his younger brother 墨江中王*, and he escaped to Isonokami Shrine. He lived to the age of sixty-four.
  • 墨江中王*The emperor’s younger brother. He set fire to the great hall intending to kill the emperor, and was later killed by a Hayato.
  • 阿知直*Ancestor of the 倭漢直*. He carried the emperor out of the burning hall and helped him flee to Yamato, and was later appointed an official of the storehouses.
  • 水齒別命*The emperor’s younger brother by the same mother. He cleared himself of suspicion by bringing down 墨江中王*, and later ruled as Emperor Hanzei.
  • 曾婆訶理*A Hayato who served 墨江中王* (also written 曾婆加理). He killed his master but was cut down by 水歯別命.
  • 黑比賣命*Daughter of 葦田宿禰*. Wife of Emperor Richū and mother of 市辺之忍歯王 and others.
  • 市邊之忍齒王*Child of Emperor Richū and 黒比売命*.
  • Isonokami Shrine (石上神宮)Where the emperor stayed after escaping danger. 水歯別命* came here to report.
  • 都怒郎女*Daughter of 許碁登臣 of 丸邇 (readings unconfirmed). Wife of Emperor Hanzei.

In the Nihon Shoki

Nihon Shoki, Book 12, Emperor Richū, record before accession (the 87th year of Emperor Nintoku)

In the Nihon Shoki, the messenger 住吉仲皇子*, using the crown prince’s (Richū’s) name, visits 黒媛*, whom the crown prince intended to make his consort, and fearing that this would become known, 仲皇子 raises troops and burns the crown prince’s palace. The Kojiki records only that 墨江中王* set the fire and gives no reason.

時仲皇子冒太子名
Nihon Shoki, Book 12, Emperor Richū, record before accession (the 87th year of Emperor Nintoku)

In the Nihon Shoki, the Hayato who stabbed 仲皇子* to death in the privy is named 刺領巾*, and he is killed on the advice of 木菟宿禰*. There is no story, as with the Kojiki’s 曾婆訶理, of a banquet held to confer the rank of minister at which he is cut down.

乃殺刺領巾
Nihon Shoki, Book 12, the 6th year of Emperor Richū, 3rd month (壬午朔丙申)

In the Nihon Shoki, Emperor Richū dies at the 稚桜宮 palace* in the 3rd month of the 6th year, at the age of seventy. The Kojiki gives his age as sixty-four.
