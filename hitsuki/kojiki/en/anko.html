Emperor Ankō and the Incident of 目弱王*

安康天皇と目弱王の変

Emperor Ankō (穴穂御子)*, believing the slander of his messenger 根臣*, killed 大日下王* and made his wife empress, but he was cut down during an afternoon nap by 目弱王*, the empress’s son by her former husband. The emperor’s younger brother 大長谷王子* attacked his elder brothers and 都夫良意美*, and then shot dead 市辺之忍歯王*, so 忍歯王’s sons 意富祁王 and 袁祁王 (readings unconfirmed) fled to Harima Province (針間国).

Key points to remember

  1. Main textThe seven-year-old 目弱王* overheard words saying that it was Emperor Ankō who had killed his father, 大日下王*; he cut off the head of the napping emperor with a sword lying nearby and fled to the house of 都夫良意富美*. It is recorded that the emperor died at the age of fifty-six.
  2. Main textThe affair began with the theft and slander of the messenger 根臣*. 根臣 stole the jewelled headdress (玉縵) that was a gift of courtesy and falsely reported that 大日下王* would not obey the command, so the emperor killed 大日下王 and made his wife, 長田大郎女*, empress.
  3. Main text大長谷王子 * (later Emperor Yūryaku) cut down his elder brother 黒日子王*, who was not shocked by the incident, buried his other elder brother 白日子王* alive, and attacked the house of 都夫良意美*, who was sheltering 目弱王*. 意美 stabbed 目弱王 and then died himself.
  4. Main textDuring a hunt at 蚊屋野*, 大長谷王* shot dead 市辺之忍歯王*, and the latter’s sons 意富祁王 and 袁祁王 (readings unconfirmed) hid in the house of 志自牟* in Harima Province (針間国) and became keepers of horses and oxen.
  5. Compared with the Nihon ShokiIn the Nihon Shoki, after a drinking party in a tall pavilion, Emperor Ankō is lying asleep drunk with his head on the empress’s lap when he is stabbed by 眉輪王 * (corresponding to 目弱王), and 眉輪王 is burned to death in the minister’s house together with Prince 坂合黒彦 (坂合黒彦皇子) and the minister 円 (円大臣) (readings unconfirmed).
  6. Compared with the Nihon ShokiIn the Nihon Shoki, 大泊瀬 * (Yūryaku) killed Prince 市辺押磐 (市辺押磐皇子)* because he resented the fact that Emperor Ankō had intended to pass the country to 押磐皇子, and it records that he shot him dead after falsely saying, “There is a boar.”

The story

穴穂御子* ruled the realm from the 穴穂宮 palace at Isonokami (石上). Seeking to obtain 若日下王*, younger sister of 大日下王*, as a wife for his younger brother 大長谷王子*, the emperor sent 根臣*, ancestor of the 坂本臣* and others, as his messenger. 大日下王 bowed four times and consented, and entrusted him with the jewelled headdress 押木之玉縵* as a gift of courtesy. But 根臣 stole the headdress and falsely told the emperor that 大日下王 had refused the command and was angry.

The emperor was greatly enraged and killed 大日下王*, and made his principal wife, 長田大郎女*, empress. Once, while taking an afternoon nap on a sacred couch (神牀), the emperor told the empress that he was always anxious that when her son 目弱王* grew up and learned that it was he who had killed his father, he might turn against him. The seven-year-old 目弱王, playing beneath the palace, heard this; he took the sword lying beside the sleeping emperor, cut off his head, and fled into the house of 都夫良意富美*. It is recorded that the emperor lived to the age of fifty-six and that his tomb is at 伏見岡 in 菅原 (readings unconfirmed).

大長谷王子*, who was still a boy, was furious when he heard this and went to his elder brother 黒日子王*, but 黒日子王 was not shocked and seemed indifferent. 大長谷王子 reviled his brother, seized him by the collar, dragged him out and cut him down. His next elder brother, 白日子王*, behaved the same way, so he took him to 小治田*, dug a pit and buried him standing; it is recorded that when he had been buried to the waist, both his eyes burst out and he died.

大長谷王子* raised an army, surrounded the house of 都夫良意美*, and the two sides shot arrows fiercely at each other. 意美 laid down his weapons, bowed eight times, and said he would offer his daughter 訶良比売*, whom the prince had earlier sought, together with five granaries (屯宅). But, saying that he had heard of ministers hiding in royal palaces but never of a prince hiding in a minister’s house, and that he would not abandon a prince who had come to rely on him even if it meant death, he fought again. When his strength and arrows were exhausted, he followed the prince’s words, stabbed the prince to death, and then cut his own throat and died.

After that, 韓帒*, ancestor of the 佐佐紀山君* of Ōmi, reported that there were many boar and deer at 蚊屋野 in 久多綿 (readings unconfirmed), and 大長谷王* went to Ōmi taking 市辺之忍歯王* with him; each stayed in his own temporary lodging. The next morning, 忍歯王, quite unguarded, rode up on horseback beside 大長谷王’s lodging and invited him to the hunting ground. Because his attendants urged him to be on his guard, 大長谷王 went out wearing armour under his clothes, and as soon as their horses were side by side he shot 忍歯王 down with an arrow, cut up his body, put it in a horse’s feeding trough, and buried it level with the ground.

The sons of 市辺王*, 意富祁王 and 袁祁王 (readings unconfirmed), heard of this disturbance and fled. While they were eating at 苅羽井 in Yamashiro (山代)*, an old man with a tattooed face came and took their food, naming himself the boar-keeper (猪甘) of Yamashiro. The two crossed the 玖須婆 river*, reached Harima Province (針間国), hid themselves in the house of a man named 志自牟*, and worked as keepers of horses and oxen.

根臣。即盜取其禮物之玉縵。

根臣* stole the jewelled headdress, the gift of courtesy entrusted to him by 大日下王*.

取其傍大刀。乃打斬。其天皇之頸。

目弱王* took the sword lying beside the sleeping emperor and cut off the emperor’s head.

聞臣連。隱於王宮。未聞王子。隱於臣家。

The words of 都夫良意美*: I have heard of ministers fleeing into royal palaces, but I have never heard of a prince hiding in a minister’s house.

意富祁王。袁祁王〔二柱〕聞此亂而逃去。

The two sons of 市辺王, 意富祁王 and 袁祁王 (readings unconfirmed), heard of this disturbance and fled.

In the Nihon Shoki

Nihon Shoki, Book 13, the 1st year of Emperor Ankō, spring, 2nd month (戊辰朔)

In the Nihon Shoki too, 根使主 * (corresponding to 根臣) is captivated by the beauty of the jewelled headdress 押木珠縵*, keeps it for himself, and through slander has 大草香皇子 * (corresponding to 大日下王) killed. The Nihon Shoki further records that 難波吉師日香蛟* and his sons, who had served 大草香皇子, killed themselves beside their master’s body.

根使主見押木珠縵。感其麗美。
Nihon Shoki, Book 14, Emperor Yūryaku, record before accession (the 3rd year of Emperor Ankō, 8th month)

In the Nihon Shoki, after holding a drinking party in the tall pavilion of the mountain palace (山宮), Emperor Ankō is taking an afternoon nap, drunk, with his head on the empress’s lap when he is stabbed by 眉輪王*. (In the Kojiki, he is napping on a sacred couch (神牀), and his head is cut off with a sword.)

穴穗天皇枕皇后膝。晝醉眠臥。
Nihon Shoki, Book 14, Emperor Yūryaku, record before accession (the 3rd year of Emperor Ankō, 8th month)

In the Nihon Shoki, Prince 八釣白彦 (八釣白彦皇子)* is cut down, and Prince 坂合黒彦 (坂合黒彦皇子)* flees with 眉輪王* to the house of the minister 円 (円大臣)*; the minister offers his daughter 韓媛* and seven residences in Katsuragi (葛城) and begs for pardon, but he is not forgiven, and all three are burned to death together with the house. This differs from the Kojiki’s accounts of 白日子王* being buried alive and of 意美* stabbing the prince and killing himself.

於是。大臣與黒彦皇子。眉輪王。倶被燔死。
