Nigihayahi-no-Mikoto Comes to Serve, and the Choice of an Empress

邇藝速日命の参上と皇后選び

Nigihayahi-no-Mikoto (邇藝速日命), who had come down from heaven, presents himself before the Child of the Heavenly Deities and serves him, and the Child of the Heavenly Deities rules the realm from the Kashihara palace (白檮原宮) at Unebi (畝火). As empress, 伊須氣余理比賣*, daughter of Ōmononushi-no-Kami of Miwa (美和), is welcomed, through the mediation of 大久米命* and an exchange of songs.

Read in the Nihon Shoki: The Pacification of Yamato and the Enthronement ›

Key points to remember

  1. Main textNigihayahi-no-Mikoto came down from heaven following the Child of the Heavenly Deities, presented heavenly tokens (天津瑞) and served him. Umashimaji-no-Mikoto (宇摩志麻遲命), born to him and the younger sister of 登美毘古*, is regarded as the ancestor of the Mononobe no Muraji (物部連), the 穂積臣* and the 婇臣*.
  2. Main textAfter subduing the violent deities and the people who would not submit, the Child of the Heavenly Deities ruled the realm from the Kashihara palace (白檮原宮) at Unebi (畝火).
  3. Main text伊須氣余理比賣*, who became empress, was a daughter born after Ōmononushi-no-Kami of Miwa (美和) turned himself into a vermilion-painted arrow and visited 勢夜陀多良比賣*, and for that reason she was called “the child of a deity.” Her original name was 富登多多良伊須須岐比賣命*.
  4. Main textWith 大久米命* acting as go-between, 伊須氣余理比賣* was chosen from among seven maidens at 高佐士野*. Songs are exchanged between the emperor and 大久米命, and between the maiden and 大久米命.
  5. Compared with the Nihon ShokiIn the Nihon Shoki, the empress Himetatara-isuzu-hime-no-Mikoto (媛蹈韛五十鈴媛命) is said to be the child of Kotoshironushi-no-Kami and 玉櫛媛 * (daughter of 三嶋溝橛耳神*), so her parents differ from those in the Kojiki, Ōmononushi-no-Kami and 勢夜陀多良比賣 * (daughter of 三嶋湟咋*). Also, in the Nihon Shoki, Nigihayahi-no-Mikoto (饒速日命) kills Nagasunebiko (長髄彦) and submits, and the accession is placed in spring, 1st month, of the year 辛酉.
  6. CommentaryThe Kokugakuin University database of deity names states that, whereas the Kojiki's account of Nigihayahi-no-Mikoto is brief, the Nihon Shoki's account has a stronger character as a tradition telling of the deeds of the founding ancestor of the Mononobe clan, and it notes that views are divided on which is the older form. [source, Japanese]

The story

Nigihayahi-no-Mikoto came to present himself and said that, having heard that the Child of the Heavenly Deities had come down from heaven, he had come down following after him; he presented heavenly tokens (天津瑞) and served him. Nigihayahi-no-Mikoto took as his wife 登美夜毘賣*, younger sister of 登美毘古*, and Umashimaji-no-Mikoto (宇摩志麻遲命) was born. A note in the original text makes him the ancestor of the Mononobe no Muraji, the 穂積臣* and the 婇臣*. Thus, having made the violent deities submit and driven off the people who would not submit, he ruled the realm from the Kashihara palace (白檮原宮) at Unebi (畝火).

When the emperor was in Hyūga, he had taken as his wife 阿比良比賣*, younger sister of the 小椅君 of 阿多 (readings unconfirmed), and had two children, 多藝志美美命* and 岐須美美命*. But when he looked anew for a woman to make his chief consort, 大久米命* said, “Here there is a maiden who is called the child of a deity.”

The reason that maiden was called the child of a deity is as follows. 勢夜陀多良比賣*, daughter of 三嶋湟咋*, was beautiful, and Ōmononushi-no-Kami of Miwa (美和) saw her and was drawn to her. Ōmononushi-no-Kami changed himself into a vermilion-painted arrow, floated down the ditch where the maiden was relieving herself, and struck her 富登 (private parts). The startled maiden took the arrow home and laid it beside her bed, and at once the arrow became a fine man, who took her as his wife. The child born was 富登多多良伊須須岐比賣命*, who later, disliking the word “富登,” changed her name to 比賣多多良伊須氣余理比賣*.

Seven maidens were amusing themselves at 高佐士野*, and 伊須氣余理比賣* was among them. 大久米命* saw them and addressed the emperor in a song (C17). The emperor knew in his heart that 伊須氣余理比賣 was standing at the very front, and answered in a song (C19).

When 大久米命* conveyed the emperor's words to 伊須氣余理比賣*, she saw 大久米命's sharp, tattooed eyes, found them strange, and asked about them in a song (C21). 大久米命 also answered in a song (C23), and the maiden said that she would serve. The maiden's house was beside the Sai River (狭井河), and the emperor went there and spent a night with her. A note in the original text records that because there were many 山由理草 (lily plants) by the river, and their original name was “佐韋,” it was named the Sai River (佐韋河). Later, when the maiden came to the palace, the emperor composed a song (C25).

A plain retelling of what the original text of the Kojiki says (no published translation has been used).

In the original text

即獻天津瑞以。仕奉也。

Nigihayahi-no-Mikoto presented heavenly tokens and served him.

坐畝火之白檮原宮。治天下也。

Dwelling at the Kashihara palace (白檮原宮) at Unebi, he ruled the realm.

化丹塗矢。自其爲大便之溝流下。

Ōmononushi-no-Kami changed himself into a vermilion-painted arrow and floated down the ditch.

故其孃子。白之仕奉也。

In the Nihon Shoki

Nihon Shoki, Book 3, Emperor Jinmu, record before accession, the year 戊午, 12th month (癸巳朔丙申)

In the Nihon Shoki, Nigihayahi-no-Mikoto (饒速日命) is the lord whom Nagasunebiko (長髄彦) serves; after the emperor and Nagasunebiko show each other tokens of being children of the heavenly deities (a heavenly feathered arrow, 天羽羽矢, and a quiver, 歩靫), Nigihayahi-no-Mikoto kills Nagasunebiko and submits, leading his troops. In the Kojiki, Nigihayahi-no-Mikoto merely comes to present himself and offers heavenly tokens (天津瑞), and there is no story of killing 登美毘古*.

乃殺之。帥其衆而歸順焉。
Nihon Shoki, Book 3, Emperor Jinmu, record before accession, the year 庚申, autumn, 8th month (癸丑朔戊辰)

In the Nihon Shoki, Himetatara-isuzu-hime-no-Mikoto (媛蹈韛五十鈴媛命), welcomed as principal consort, is said to be the child of Kotoshironushi-no-Kami and 玉櫛媛*, daughter of 三嶋溝橛耳神*. In the Kojiki, her father is Ōmononushi-no-Kami of Miwa (美和), and her mother is 勢夜陀多良比賣*, daughter of 三嶋湟咋*.

事代主神共三嶋溝橛耳神之女玉櫛媛
Nihon Shoki, Book 3, Emperor Jinmu, 1st year, the year 辛酉, spring, 1st month (庚辰朔)

In the Nihon Shoki, he accedes at the Kashihara palace (橿原宮) in spring, 1st month, of the year 辛酉 (庚辰朔), and this year is made the emperor's first year. The Kojiki records that he ruled the realm from the Kashihara palace (白檮原宮), but gives no year or month.
