Emperor Kenzō and Emperor Ninken: 置目老媼* and Their Father’s Tomb

顕宗天皇・仁賢天皇（置目老媼と父の御陵）

Emperor Kenzō (袁祁之石巣別命)*, guided by an old woman of Ōmi, found and buried the remains of his father, 市辺王*; he gave the old woman the name 置目老媼* and treated her generously. He tried to destroy the tomb of Emperor 大長谷 (大長谷天皇)*, his father’s enemy, but his elder brother 意祁命* only dug a little at its side and remonstrated with him; after the emperor’s death, that elder brother (Emperor Ninken) ruled the realm.

Read in the Nihon Shoki: Emperors Seinei, Kenzō and Ninken, and the brothers 億計 and 弘計 (readings unconfirmed) ›

Key points to remember

  1. Main textEmperor Kenzō (袁祁之石巣別命)*, on the offer of an old woman of Ōmi, found the remains of his father, 市辺王*, built a tomb on the mountain east of 蚊屋野* and buried him there. The old woman was given the name 置目老媼*, lived beside the palace, and was summoned by a bell (鐸) hung at the door of the great hall.
  2. Main textThe emperor tried to destroy the tomb of Emperor 大長谷 (大長谷天皇)*, his father’s enemy, but his elder brother 意祁命* dug only a little at its side and remonstrated that he was their uncle and also an emperor who had ruled the realm. The emperor too acknowledged this as reasonable.
  3. Main textIt is recorded that the old boar-keeper (猪甘) who had taken their food while they were fleeing was sought out and beheaded on the bank of the Asuka River (飛鳥河), and that the tendons of his kinsmen’s knees were cut.
  4. Main textEmperor Kenzō died at the age of thirty-eight (he reigned eight years, and his tomb is on 石坏岡 hill at 片岡; readings unconfirmed). He was followed by his elder brother 意祁命 * (Emperor Ninken), who ruled the realm from the 広高宮 palace at Isonokami (石上)*, and Ninken’s child 小長谷若雀命* ruled the realm next.
  5. Compared with the Nihon ShokiIn the Nihon Shoki, the crown prince 億計* remonstrates with the emperor, who is trying to destroy the tomb, and the emperor has the work stopped; there is no story of digging a little at the side of the tomb.

The story

袁祁之石巣別命* ruled the realm for eight years from the Near Asuka palace (近飛鳥宮). He married 難波王*, daughter of 石木王*, but they had no children.

When the emperor was searching for the remains of his father, 市辺王*, an old woman of low status from Ōmi Province came to him and said that she alone knew well where the prince’s remains had been buried, and that they could be identified even by the teeth (a small note says that the teeth were 押歯, divided into three). So people were gathered to dig up the earth, and the remains were found; a tomb was built on the mountain east of 蚊屋野* and he was buried there, and the sons of 韓帒* were made to guard the tomb.

On returning to the capital, the emperor summoned the old woman, praised her for not forgetting the place and keeping it in sight, and gave her the name 置目老媼*; he called her into the palace and cared for her generously. He built a dwelling for the old woman beside the palace and summoned her without fail every day, and for summoning her he hung a bell (鐸) at the door of the great hall and rang it. The emperor composed a song about this. Later, when the old woman had grown very old and asked to retire to her home district, the emperor saw her off and composed a song.

He sought out the old boar-keeper (猪甘) who had once taken the two princes’ food while they were fleeing, summoned him to the capital, beheaded him on the bank of the Asuka River (飛鳥河), and cut the tendons of his kinsmen’s knees. It is recorded that for this reason, to this day, his descendants always limp on the days when they go up to Yamato (倭). Because the old man’s whereabouts had been well found out (“志米岐”), that place is called 志米須*.

The emperor deeply hated Emperor 大長谷 (大長谷天皇)*, who had killed his father, and, wishing to avenge himself on his spirit, was about to send men to destroy his tomb. His elder brother 意祁命* offered to go himself, and he dug only a little at the side of the tomb and came back. When the emperor asked why he had not destroyed all of it, 意祁命 answered that, although Emperor 大長谷 was their father’s enemy, he was their uncle and also an emperor who had ruled the realm; if they destroyed the whole tomb, later generations would surely condemn them; digging a little was enough to show his shame to later ages. The emperor too acknowledged this as reasonable.

The emperor lived to the age of thirty-eight, ruled the realm for eight years, and his tomb is on 石坏岡 hill at 片岡 (readings unconfirmed). After the emperor’s death, 意祁命* ruled the realm, residing at the 広高宮 palace at Isonokami (石上)*. He married 春日大郎女*, child of Emperor 大長谷若建 (大長谷若建天皇)*, and 高木郎女, 財郎女, 久須毘郎女, 手白髪郎女, 小長谷若雀命 and 真若王 (readings unconfirmed) were born; and from 糠若子郎女*, daughter of 丸邇日爪臣*, 春日山田郎女* was born. His children numbered seven in all, and of them 小長谷若雀命 ruled the realm.

A plain retelling of what the original text of the Kojiki says (no published translation has been used).

In the original text

譽其不失見置知其地以。賜名號置目老媼。

Praising her for not forgetting the place of the remains and keeping it in sight, he gave her the name 置目老媼*.

故鐸懸大殿戶。

A bell (鐸) was hung at the door of the great hall to summon the old woman.

還爲我之從父。亦治天下之天皇。

The words of 意祁命*: Emperor 大長谷* is our uncle and also an emperor who ruled the realm.

天皇。御年。參拾捌歲。治天下八歲。

Emperor Kenzō lived to the age of thirty-eight and ruled the realm for eight years.

Deities and people, and the shrines that enshrine them

  • 袁祁之石巣別命 * (Emperor Kenzō)The emperor who ruled the realm from the Near Asuka palace (近飛鳥宮). He searches for and buries his father’s remains, and tries to destroy the tomb of his father’s enemy.
  • 難波王*Daughter of 石木王*. Wife of Emperor Kenzō; they had no children.
  • 市邊王*Father of Emperors Kenzō and Ninken. His remains are found at 蚊屋野*.
  • 置目老媼*An old woman of Ōmi Province. She reveals where the remains of 市辺王* lie, is given this name, and lives beside the palace.
  • Bell (鐸)The bell hung at the door of the great hall to summon the old woman.
  • The sons of 韓帒 (韓帒之子等)*They are made to guard the tomb of 市辺王* built on the mountain east of 蚊屋野*.
  • The old boar-keeper (猪甘老人)The old man who took the two princes’ food while they were fleeing. He is sought out and beheaded.
  • 大長谷天皇*The emperor who killed the two princes’ father. His tomb is nearly destroyed.
  • 意祁命, also 意意祁命 * (Emperor Ninken)Elder brother of Emperor Kenzō. He remonstrates by only digging a little at the tomb, and after his younger brother’s death rules the realm from the 広高宮 palace at Isonokami (石上)*.
  • 春日大郎女*Child of Emperor 大長谷若建*. She becomes the wife of Emperor Ninken and bears 小長谷若雀命* and others.
  • 小長谷若雀命*Child of Emperor Ninken and 春日大郎女*. He ruled the realm next.

In the Nihon Shoki

Nihon Shoki, Book 15, the 1st year of Emperor Kenzō, 2nd month, this month

In the Nihon Shoki, the emperor and the crown prince 億計*, guided by 置目*, dug up the remains at 蚊屋野 in 来田綿, Ōmi (readings unconfirmed), but because they were mixed with the bones of 佐伯部仲子*, an attendant (帳内), the bones of the hands and feet could not be told apart, so two tombs very much alike were built at 蚊屋野. The Kojiki records that the remains could be identified by the teeth, and there is a single tomb.

仍於蚊屋野中造起雙陵。相似如一。
Nihon Shoki, Book 15, the 2nd year of Emperor Kenzō, autumn, 8th month (己未朔)

In the Nihon Shoki, when the emperor says he wishes to destroy the tomb of Emperor 大泊瀬 (大泊瀬天皇)* and scatter his bones, the crown prince 億計* remonstrates with him, giving two reasons, and the emperor says “善哉” (“Well said”) and has the work stopped. There is no story, as in the Kojiki, of the elder brother digging a little at the side of the tomb.

天皇曰。善哉。令罷役。
Nihon Shoki, Book 15, the 3rd year of Emperor Kenzō, summer, 4th month (庚辰)

In the Nihon Shoki, Emperor Kenzō dies at the 八釣宮 palace* on the day 庚辰 of the 4th month, summer, in the third year after his accession, which differs in the number of years from the Kojiki’s “ruled the realm for eight years” (治天下八歳).

庚辰。天皇崩干八釣宮。
