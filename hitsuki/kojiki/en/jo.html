Kojiki, Part 1 · preface

The Preface: how the Kojiki came to be written

序（安万侶が記す、古事記の成り立ち）

The preface placed at the head of the Kojiki. It opens with the words 臣安萬侣言 ("Your subject 安萬侣* says"). After looking back over the ages from the time of the gods, it records that the emperor who ruled from the palace 飛鳥清原大宮* wished to correct the imperial chronicles and the old tales and hand them down, that the attendant 稗田阿礼* learned them by recitation, and that 安萬侣, receiving an edict on the eighteenth day of the ninth month of the fourth year of 和銅*, recorded them in three parts. The date at the end is the twenty-eighth day of the first month of the fifth year of 和銅.

Reading time for the story: about 5 min　/　Key points only

The story

The words 臣安萬侣言 and the age of the gods

The preface begins with the words 臣安萬侣言, "Your subject 安萬侣* says." It first looks back briefly on the age of the gods. When the mingled origin had begun to congeal, no sign had yet appeared; there was no name and no action, and no one knew its form. Then 乾 and 坤 first divided, and the "three deities" (叁神) became the beginning of creation; yin and yang opened, and the "two spirits" (二霊) became the ancestors of all things. The sun and moon appeared at the washing of the eyes, and the deities appeared at the cleansing of the body, floating and sinking in the sea water.

From the peak 高千嶺* to 遠飛鳥*

It goes on to mention that a mirror was hung and jewels were spat out and a hundred kings followed one another; that a sword was bitten and a serpent cut and the myriad deities flourished; that there was deliberation at 安河 and the realm was pacified, and discussion at 小濱 and the land was made clean. 番仁岐命* first descended to the peak 高千嶺*, and the emperor 神倭天皇* passed through 秋津嶋*. When a transformed bear put out its claws, a heavenly sword was obtained at 高倉; when one with a tail blocked the path, a great crow gave guidance at 吉野. Dances were ranged to drive off the bandits, and at the hearing of a song the enemies were subdued. It further mentions a "wise sovereign" (賢后) who was awakened by a dream and revered the deities, a "sage emperor" (聖帝) who looked for the smoke and cared for the people, the fixing of borders and opening of lands at 近淡海*, and the correcting of clan titles and choosing of clans at 遠飛鳥*. Though their paces differed, it concludes, every reign looked to antiquity to set right ways that had decayed, and lit up the present to mend teachings that were about to perish.

The emperor of the palace 飛鳥清原大宮*

Next it tells of the reign of the emperor who ruled the Great Eight Islands (大八洲) from the palace 飛鳥清原大宮*. Hearing a song in a dream, he thought of succeeding to the task; coming to the water at night, he knew he would inherit the foundation. But the time had not yet come, so he withdrew to the southern mountain like a cicada shedding its shell; when the support of the people was complete, he strode through the eastern land like a tiger. The armies shook like thunder; spears were raised in might; crimson banners made the weapons shine, and the enemy collapsed like tiles. Before many days had passed the foul air cleared of itself; the oxen were turned loose and the horses rested, and he returned. Then, when the year stood in 大梁 and the month in 挾鍾, he ascended the heavenly throne at the palace 清原大宮. His way is praised as surpassing that of 軒后 and his virtue as surpassing that of 周王, and he is said to have searched deeply into high antiquity and seen clearly the former ages.

The emperor's edict and the attendant 阿礼

Then the emperor issued an edict: "I hear that the imperial chronicles (帝紀) and the original tales (本辞) held by the various houses already differ from the truth and have had many falsehoods added. If these errors are not corrected now, before many years have passed their meaning will be lost. These are the warp and weft of the state and the great foundation of the sovereign's influence. Therefore I wish to select and record the imperial chronicles, examine the old tales (舊辞), remove what is false and settle what is true, and hand them down to later ages." At that time there was an attendant (舍人) whose family name was 稗田 and personal name 阿礼 (readings unconfirmed), twenty-eight years of age. He was intelligent: what passed before his eyes he could recite, and what touched his ears he fixed in his mind. The emperor commanded 阿礼 to learn by recitation the imperial succession (帝皇日継) and the old tales of former ages (先代舊辞). But the times moved on and the age changed, and the matter was not carried out.

The edict of the fourth year of 和銅*

安萬侣* then praises the virtue of Her or His Majesty the Emperor (the original says only 皇帝陛下 and gives no name). That virtue reaches as far as horses' hooves can go, and that influence shines as far as the prows of ships can reach; records of auspicious signs never cease, and no month passes without tribute arriving. Regretting the errors in the old tales and wishing to correct the mistakes in the former chronicles, the Emperor on the eighteenth day of the ninth month of the fourth year of 和銅* gave 安萬侣 this edict: "Select and record the old tales of the imperial command that 稗田阿礼* recites, and present them." In obedience to the edict, 安萬侣 gathered them in detail.

Sound and meaning together, and the three parts presented

But in high antiquity both words and meanings were plain, and it was difficult to set out sentences and compose phrases in characters. If everything is told by meaning (訓), the words do not reach the sense; if everything is strung together by sound (音), the account becomes too long. So within a single phrase 安萬侣* used sound and meaning together, or within a single matter recorded everything by meaning. Where the sense was hard to see he made it clear with a note, and where it was easy to understand he added none. Usages such as writing the family name 日下 for 玖沙訶 and the character 帶 in a name for 多羅斯 were left as in the source and not changed. What is recorded runs from the opening of heaven and earth to the reign of 小治田*: from 天御中主神 down to 日子波限建鵜草葺不合命 is Part 1; from 神倭伊波礼毗古天皇 down to the reign of 品陀 is Part 2; from 大雀 down to the palace 小治田大宮 is Part 3 (readings of these names as written in the preface unconfirmed). The three parts are recorded together and respectfully presented. At the end is written: the twenty-eighth day of the first month of the fifth year of 和銅*, 太朝臣安萬侣*, Senior Fifth Rank Upper, Fifth Order of Merit.

Key points of this story

  1. Main textThe preface begins with the words 臣安萬侣言 and ends with the date and signature: the twenty-eighth day of the first month of the fifth year of 和銅*, 太朝臣安萬侣*, Senior Fifth Rank Upper, Fifth Order of Merit.

  3. Main textThe attendant 稗田阿礼*, aged twenty-eight, was commanded to learn by recitation the imperial succession (帝皇日継) and the old tales of former ages (先代舊辞). But the times changed and the matter was not carried out.
  4. Main textOn the eighteenth day of the ninth month of the fourth year of 和銅*, an edict was given to 安萬侣*: "Select and record the old tales of the imperial command that 稗田阿礼 recites, and present them."
  5. Main text安萬侣* explains that writing everything by meaning (訓) leaves the words short of the sense, and writing everything by sound (音) makes the text too long; so within one phrase he used sound and meaning together, and added notes where the sense was hard to see. Spellings such as 日下 and 帶 were left as in the source.
  6. Main textThe record runs from the opening of heaven and earth to the reign of 小治田*, in three parts: Part 1 from 天御中主神 to 日子波限建鵜草葺不合命, Part 2 from 神倭伊波礼毗古天皇 to the reign of 品陀, and Part 3 from 大雀 to the palace 小治田大宮 (readings of these names as written in the preface unconfirmed).

Deities and people, and the shrines that enshrine them

  • 安萬侣*The writer of the preface. The original calls him 臣安萬侣 at the beginning and in the text, and 正五位上勳五等太朝臣安萬侣 at the end. He recorded the three parts on receiving the edict of the eighteenth day of the ninth month of the fourth year of 和銅.
  • 稗田阿礼*An attendant (舍人). The original says: family name 稗田, personal name 阿礼, twenty-eight years of age. By imperial command he learned by recitation the imperial succession and the old tales of former ages.
  • The emperor of the palace 飛鳥清原大宮*Written in the original as 飛鳥清原大宫御大八洲天皇. He issued the edict to correct the imperial chronicles and the old tales and hand them down.
