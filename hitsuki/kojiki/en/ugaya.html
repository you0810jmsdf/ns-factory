The Birth of Ugayafukiaezu-no-Mikoto

鵜葺草葺不合命の誕生

Toyotama-hime, daughter of the sea deity, came to the land and gave birth to a child in a birth hall whose roof was only partly thatched with cormorant feathers. But her plea “Do not look” was broken, she was seen in the form of an eight-fathom wani (八尋和迩), and she closed off the sea boundary (海坂) and returned to the land of the sea. The child born, Ugayafukiaezu-no-Mikoto, later took his aunt Tamayori-hime-no-Mikoto (玉依毘売命) as his wife and had four children, including 若御毛沼命 * (神倭伊波礼毗古命).

Read in the Nihon Shoki: The Birth of 神日本磐余尊* (Section 11) ›

Key points to remember

  1. Main textToyotama-hime asked him “Do not look,” but when 火遠理命* peeked, she was crawling and writhing in the form of an eight-fathom wani (八尋和迩), so he fled. Ashamed, Toyotama-hime left the child behind, closed off the sea boundary (海坂) and returned to the land of the sea.
  2. Main textBecause he was born before the roof of the birth hall on the seashore, being thatched with cormorant feathers, was finished, the child was named 天津日高日子波限建鵜葺草葺不合命 (Ugayafukiaezu-no-Mikoto).
  3. Main textUgayafukiaezu-no-Mikoto took his aunt Tamayori-hime-no-Mikoto (玉依毘売命) as his wife and had four children: Itsuse-no-Mikoto, Inahi-no-Mikoto, 御毛沼命* and 若御毛沼命*. The youngest, 若御毛沼命, is also called 神倭伊波礼毗古命*.
  4. Main textEven after their parting, Toyotama-hime, out of longing, sent a song entrusted to her younger sister Tamayori-hime (玉依毘売), and 火遠理命* answered in a song that he would never forget his wife as long as he lived. It is recorded that 火遠理命 remained at the Takachiho palace for five hundred and eighty years.
  5. Compared with the Nihon ShokiIn the main text of the Nihon Shoki, section 10, Toyotama-hime (豊玉姫) takes the form of a dragon at the time of the birth, wraps the child in grass, leaves it on the seashore and departs. In variant account 1 she has the form of an eight-fathom great 熊鰐 * (八尋大熊鰐), and in variant account 3 that of an eight-fathom great 鰐 * (八尋大鰐).
  6. CommentaryAccording to the Kokugakuin University database of deity names, the influential view is that this story, in which a wife whose “do not look” promise is broken leaves for her original world, is one of the stories of non-human wives called the “Melusine type,” found widely around the world. There is also a view that the scene of closing off the sea boundary tells the origin of why the sea and the land can no longer be travelled between, and serves to close the world of myth at the end of Part 1. [source, Japanese]

The story

Toyotama-hime-no-Mikoto, daughter of the sea deity, came to the land of her own accord and said, “I am with child, and the birth is near. I thought that the child of the heavenly deity should not be born in the sea plain, and so I have come.” So at the water's edge on the seashore a birth hall was built, using cormorant feathers as the thatch for its roof. But before the roof had been fully thatched, unable to bear the sudden pains, Toyotama-hime entered the birth hall.

When the time came to give birth, Toyotama-hime said to her husband, “People of other lands, when they give birth, take the form of their home land to give birth. So I too will now give birth in my original form. Please do not look at me.” 火遠理命* found these words strange, and secretly peeked as she was about to give birth. Then he saw that Toyotama-hime had become an eight-fathom wani (八尋和迩) and was crawling and writhing, and in astonishment and fear he fled.

Toyotama-hime, knowing she had been spied upon, felt ashamed. Leaving the child she had borne, she said, “I had always wished to come and go by the sea road. But to have my form spied upon is a great shame,” and she closed off the sea boundary (海坂) and went back to the sea. The child thus born was named 天津日高日子波限建鵜葺草葺不合命 (Ugayafukiaezu-no-Mikoto). A reading note says that “波限” is read 那藝佐 (nagisa) and “葺草” is read 加夜 (kaya).

Afterwards, though Toyotama-hime resented his spying heart, she could not suppress her longing, and, on the occasion of having the child raised, she entrusted a song to her younger sister Tamayori-hime (玉依毘売) and sent it. The song said that while she was touched by the gleam of red jewels, the form of her husband, like white jewels, seemed precious to her. Her husband sang in reply that he would never forget, as long as he lived, the wife with whom he had slept on the island where the wild ducks, birds of the open sea, come to shore.

日子穂穂手見命 (Hikohohodemi; 火遠理命) remained at the Takachiho palace for five hundred and eighty years, and his tomb is to the west of the mountain of Takachiho. 天津日高日子波限建鵜葺草葺不合命 (Ugayafukiaezu-no-Mikoto) took his aunt Tamayori-hime-no-Mikoto as his wife and had four children: Itsuse-no-Mikoto, Inahi-no-Mikoto, 御毛沼命* and 若御毛沼命*. 若御毛沼命 is also called 豊御毛沼命* and 神倭伊波礼毗古命*. 御毛沼命 crossed over to Tokoyo-no-Kuni (常世国), treading on the crests of the waves, and Inahi-no-Mikoto entered the sea plain, as his mother's land (the original reads “妣國”).

A plain retelling of what the original text of the Kojiki says (no published translation has been used).

In the original text

天神之御子不可生海原

The child of the heavenly deity should not be born in the sea plain (the reason Toyotama-hime came to the land).

化八尋和迩而匍匐委虵

(When he peeked, Toyotama-hime) had taken the form of a wani eight fathoms long, and was crawling and writhing.

即塞海坂而返入。

So she closed off the boundary of the sea (海坂) and went back to the sea.

亦名神倭伊波礼毗古命

(The) other name (of 若御毛沼命) is 神倭伊波礼毗古命 (readings unconfirmed).

In the Nihon Shoki

Nihon Shoki, Book 2, section 10, main text

In the main text of the Shoki, Toyotama-hime (豊玉姫) is said to take the form of a dragon at the time of the birth. In the Kojiki it is the form of an eight-fathom wani (八尋和迩).

豐玉姫方產化爲龍
Nihon Shoki, Book 2, section 10, main text

In the main text of the Shoki, Toyotama-hime wraps the child in grass, abandons it on the seashore, closes the sea road and departs. In the Kojiki, she leaves the child she has borne, closes off the sea boundary (海坂) and returns, and later sends her younger sister Tamayori-hime to raise him.

乃以草裹兒棄之海邊閉海途而徑去矣
Nihon Shoki, Book 2, section 11, main text

In the main text of section 11 of the Shoki as well, Ugayafukiaezu-no-Mikoto (波瀲武鸕鷀草葺不合尊) takes his aunt Tamayori-hime (玉依姫) as his consort and has four sons, Itsuse-no-Mikoto (五瀨命), 稻飯命*, Mikeirino-no-Mikoto (三毛入野命) and 神日本磐余尊*; but it further records that he died at the 西洲之宮* and was buried at the 吾平山上陵* in Hyūga. The corresponding part of the Kojiki has no account of the death or tomb of Ugayafukiaezu-no-Mikoto.
