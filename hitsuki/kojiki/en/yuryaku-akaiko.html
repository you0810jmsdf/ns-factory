Kojiki, Part 3 · story 9 of 13

Emperor Yūryaku with 若日下部王, 赤猪子 and the Maiden of Yoshino (readings unconfirmed)

雄略天皇と若日下部王・赤猪子・吉野の童女

The first half of the section on Emperor Yūryaku (大長谷若建命)* tells of his courtship of 若日下部王* and the white dog; of 引田部赤猪子*, who waited eighty years for the emperor’s word; of the dance of the maiden of Yoshino; and of the origin of the name 阿岐豆野*. Each story is accompanied by songs of the emperor or of the woman concerned.

Read in the Nihon Shoki: The accession of Emperor Yūryaku and Hitokotonushi-no-Kami (一事主神) of 葛城山* ›

Key points to remember

  1. Main text引田部赤猪子* waited eighty years for the emperor’s words, “I will send for you in time,” but the emperor had forgotten them completely. The emperor was astonished and pitied her, but, held back by her great age, he did not marry her; he gave her songs and many gifts and sent her home.
  2. Main textThe great district chief (大県主) of 志幾* raised katsuogi ridge-logs (堅魚木) on his roof in imitation of the emperor’s palace, so his house was nearly burned; he was pardoned after presenting a white dog. The emperor made that dog his courtship gift to 若日下部王*.
  3. Main textThe origin of the name 阿岐豆野*: during a hunt, a horsefly that had bitten the emperor’s arm was eaten by a dragonfly (蜻蛉, akizu), so that field was called 阿岐豆野.
  4. Main textThe emperor, who married a maiden he met on the bank of the Yoshino River, later played the koto at the same place and had that maiden dance, and he composed a song about her dance.
  5. Compared with the Nihon ShokiThe Nihon Shoki also has a story of a dragonfly eating a horsefly (the 4th year, autumn, 8th month), but it places the event in the 河上小野 field* after a visit to the Yoshino palace, and says that the emperor tried to have his ministers compose a song praising the dragonfly, but no one could, so the emperor sang one himself.
  6. Main textGenealogy: the emperor married 若日下部王 * (there were no children) and 韓比売*, daughter of 都夫良意富美*, and from 韓比売 were born 白髪命 and 若帯比売命 (readings unconfirmed).

The story

大長谷若建命* ruled the realm from the 朝倉宮 palace at 長谷 (readings unconfirmed). He married 若日下部王*, younger sister of 大日下王*, but they had no children; he also married 韓比売*, daughter of 都夫良意富美*, and 白髪命 and 若帯比売命 (readings unconfirmed) were born. As the namesake group (御名代) of Crown Prince 白髪 he established the 白髪部, and he also established the 長谷部舎人 and the 河瀬舎人 (readings unconfirmed). It is recorded that at this time people from 呉 (呉人)* came across the sea, and they were settled at 呉原*, and that the place came to be called 呉原.

When 若日下部王* was still living at 日下*, the emperor went to Kawachi by the 直越 road of 日下 (readings unconfirmed). Looking out over the land from the top of the mountain, he saw a house with katsuogi ridge-logs (堅魚木) raised on its roof. Told that it was the house of the great district chief (大県主) of 志幾*, the emperor grew angry that it had been built in imitation of his own palace and was about to have it burned. The chief, in fear, apologised and presented a white dog led on a cloth and hung with a bell, so the emperor had them stop setting the fire.

The emperor had the dog delivered to 若日下部王* as a courtship gift, calling it a rare thing he had obtained on the road. 若日下部王 replied that, since his coming with the sun at his back was a matter for great awe, she would come to him herself and serve him. As the emperor returned to his palace, he stood at the top of the mountain slope and composed a song, which he sent back with the messenger.

Once, when the emperor went out to the banks of the 美和河 river*, there was a beautiful maiden washing clothes. When he asked her name, she answered 引田部赤猪子*, and the emperor told her, “Do not marry a husband. I will send for you in time,” and returned to his palace. 赤猪子 kept waiting for those words until eighty years had passed and her looks had faded; but unable to keep from showing that she had waited, she came to the palace bringing many gifts. The emperor had completely forgotten his earlier words and asked what old woman she was and why she had come.

When 赤猪子* explained what had happened, the emperor was greatly astonished and deeply pitied her for having kept her resolve and let the years of her prime pass in vain. In his heart he wished to marry her, but held back by her very great age, he did not do so, and sent her two songs. 赤猪子 wept until the sleeves of her red-dyed (丹摺) garment were soaked through, and answered with two songs. The emperor gave her many gifts and sent her home; it is recorded that these four songs are 志都歌*.

When the emperor went to the Yoshino palace, there was a maiden of beautiful appearance on the bank of the Yoshino River, so he married her and returned to his palace. Later, when he went to Yoshino again, he stopped at the place where he had met the maiden, sat on his great seat (大御呉床), played the koto and had the maiden dance. Because her dance was so fine, the emperor composed a song.

Next, while he was hunting at 阿岐豆野*, a horsefly bit the arm of the emperor as he sat on his seat (呉床), and a dragonfly (蜻蛉, akizu) flew up, ate the horsefly and flew away. The emperor composed a song about this, and it is said that from that time the field has been called 阿岐豆野.

A plain retelling of what the original text of the Kojiki says (no published translation has been used).

In the original text

汝不嫁夫。今將喚而。

The emperor’s words to 赤猪子*: Do not marry a husband; I will send for you in time.

仰待天皇之命。既經八十歲。

赤猪子* kept waiting for the emperor’s words, and eighty years had already passed.

爾赤猪子之泣涙。悉濕。其所服之丹摺袖。

On hearing the emperor’s songs, 赤猪子* wept until the sleeves of the red-dyed (丹摺) garment she wore were soaked through.

故自其時。號其野。謂阿岐豆野也。

From this time that field came to be called 阿岐豆野*.

Deities and people, and the shrines that enshrine them

  • 大長谷若建命 * (Emperor Yūryaku)The emperor who ruled the realm from the 朝倉宮 palace at 長谷 (readings unconfirmed). He courts 若日下部王* and sends songs to 赤猪子 and the maiden of Yoshino.
  • 若日下部王*Younger sister of 大日下王* and wife of the emperor. She is sent a white dog and replies that she will come to him herself.
  • 韓比賣*Daughter of 都夫良意富美*. She bears 白髪命 and 若帯比売命 (readings unconfirmed).
  • 白髮命, also 白髮太子*Child of the emperor and 韓比売*. The 白髪部 is established as his namesake group (御名代).
  • 呉人*People who came across the sea and were settled at 呉原*. Regarded as the origin of the place name “呉原”.
  • 志幾之大縣主*Rebuked for building his house in imitation of the emperor’s palace, he is pardoned after presenting a white dog.
  • White dog (白犬)A dog presented led on a cloth and hung with a bell. It becomes the courtship gift to 若日下部王*.
  • 引田部赤猪子*A maiden who was washing clothes at the 美和河 river*. She waits eighty years for the emperor’s word and comes to the palace as an old woman.
  • The maiden of Yoshino (original text: “童女”)She meets the emperor on the bank of the Yoshino River and marries him, and later dances to the emperor’s koto.
  • Great seat (大御呉床, also 呉床)The seat on which the emperor sat at Yoshino and at 阿岐豆野*.
  • Akizu, dragonfly (蜻蛉)The insect that ate the horsefly that had bitten the emperor’s arm. It is the origin of the name 阿岐豆野* (reading note: “訓蜻蛉云阿岐豆”).

In the Nihon Shoki

Nihon Shoki, Book 14, the 1st year of Emperor Yūryaku, 3rd month, this month

The Nihon Shoki also says that 韓媛*, daughter of the minister 葛城円 (葛城円大臣)*, bore Prince 白髪 (白髪皇子; reading unconfirmed; Emperor Seinei) and Princess 稚足姫 (稚足姫皇女)*, which agrees with the Kojiki; but the Nihon Shoki writes her father as “葛城円大臣”, and also lists 吉備稚媛 and 童女君 (readings unconfirmed) as consorts.

元妃葛城圓大臣女曰韓媛。
Nihon Shoki, Book 14, the 4th year of Emperor Yūryaku, autumn, 8th month (庚戌)

In the Nihon Shoki, when the emperor was hunting in the 河上小野 field* after a visit to the Yoshino palace, a horsefly bit the emperor’s arm, and a dragonfly carried the horsefly off in its mouth. The emperor tried to have his ministers compose a song praising the dragonfly, but no one could, so he sang one himself and named the place 蜻蛉野*.

群臣莫能敢賦者。天皇乃口號曰。
Nihon Shoki, Book 14, the 14th year of Emperor Yūryaku, 3rd month

In the Nihon Shoki, the reception of envoys from 呉*, the settling of the 呉 people at 桧隈野*, and the naming of that place 呉原* are placed in the 3rd month of the 14th year of Emperor Yūryaku. Entries corresponding to the stories of 赤猪子* and the maiden of Yoshino could not be confirmed in the Yūryaku chapter of the Nihon Shoki.

即安置呉人於桧隈野。因名呉原。
