Kojiki, Part 1 · story 6 of 17

The Pledge at the Heavenly River 天の安の河*

天の安の河の誓約

When the banished Susanoo-no-Mikoto came up to the High Plain of Heaven (高天原) to bid farewell, Amaterasu-Ōmikami suspected that he had come to seize her land and met him armed. The two deities made a pledge (誓約, ukei) across the heavenly river 天の安の河* and each gave birth to children; Susanoo-no-Mikoto claimed victory, saying that the purity of his heart had been shown, and committed acts of violence in the High Plain of Heaven.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textAmaterasu-Ōmikami suspected that Susanoo-no-Mikoto had come to seize her land and met him armed; so that Susanoo-no-Mikoto could show the purity of his heart, the two deities made a pledge (誓約) across the river 天安河* and each gave birth to children from the other's belongings.
  2. Main textAmaterasu-Ōmikami gave birth to three female deities from Susanoo-no-Mikoto's ten-fist sword (十拳剣), and Susanoo-no-Mikoto gave birth to five male deities, including Ame-no-Oshihomimi-no-Mikoto, from Amaterasu-Ōmikami's jewels; Amaterasu-Ōmikami decided, according to whose belongings they came from, that the male deities were her children and the female deities were Susanoo-no-Mikoto's children.
  3. Main textIn the Kojiki, no agreement is written before the pledge about which children being born would mean a pure heart. Susanoo-no-Mikoto declared on his own, "I have won," on the grounds that the children born were girls.
  4. Main textEmboldened by his victory, Susanoo-no-Mikoto ravaged the rice fields and defiled the palace, and further dropped a piebald horse into the sacred weaving hall (忌服屋), causing the death of the heavenly weaving maiden (天衣織女). Immediately after this, the story of the "Heavenly Rock-Cave Door" (天の岩屋戸) begins.
  5. Compared with the Nihon ShokiIn the main text of section 6 of the Nihon Shoki, before the pledge Susanoo-no-Mikoto sets out the condition, "If girls are born, my heart is impure; if boys are born, my heart is pure," so the way victory and defeat are decided differs from the Kojiki.
  6. CommentaryAccording to the Kokugakuin University database of deity names, the names of the three female deities, and which goddess dwells in which shrine, disagree among the various traditions of the Kojiki and the Nihon Shoki, and the shrine's own tradition at Munakata Taisha (宗像大社) is said to worship them in an order that follows the main text of the Nihon Shoki. The story

Susanoo-no-Mikoto said, "Then I will take my leave after informing Amaterasu-Ōmikami," and went up to heaven. At that time the mountains and rivers all shook, and the whole land trembled. Amaterasu-Ōmikami heard this and was alarmed, and said, "Susanoo-no-Mikoto's coming up is not from a good heart. He means to seize my land."

Amaterasu-Ōmikami undid her hair and retied it in loops (mizura), and on the left and right loops, on her hair ornament, and on her left and right hands she wound strings of many long curved jewels (勾玉). On her back she bore a quiver holding a thousand arrows, at her side she fastened a quiver holding five hundred, on her arm she put a bamboo arm guard, and she brandished her bow. Then she stamped into the hard ground up to her thighs, kicking it about like powdery snow, gave a valiant war cry, and demanded, "Why have you come up?"

Susanoo-no-Mikoto answered, "I have no evil heart. When I was asked why I was weeping and said that I wanted to go to my mother's land, I was driven out, so I have only come to bid you farewell." Amaterasu-Ōmikami asked, "Then how can I know that your heart is pure and bright?" Susanoo-no-Mikoto answered, "Let us each make a pledge and give birth to children."

The two deities made the pledge across the river 天安河*. First Amaterasu-Ōmikami took Susanoo-no-Mikoto's ten-fist sword, broke it into three, rinsed it in the well 天の真名井* and chewed it up, and from the mist of her breath as she blew it out three deities were born: 多紀理毘売命 (also called 奥津島比売命), 市寸島比売命 (also called 狭依毘売命) and 多岐都比売命 (readings unconfirmed). Susanoo-no-Mikoto took the jewels wound on Amaterasu-Ōmikami's left hair loop and did the same, and 正勝吾勝勝速日* Ame-no-Oshihomimi-no-Mikoto was born. Further, from the jewels of the right hair loop, the hair ornament, the left hand and the right hand were born Ame-no-Hohi-no-Mikoto, Amatsuhikone-no-Mikoto, Ikutsuhikone-no-Mikoto and Kumanokusubi-no-Mikoto.

Amaterasu-Ōmikami divided them and decided, "The five boys born afterwards came into being from my belongings, so they are my children. The three girls born first came into being from your belongings, so they are your children." The three female deities are said to dwell in the 奥津宮, 中津宮 and 辺津宮 shrines of 胸形, and to be the three great deities worshipped by the 胸形君 (readings unconfirmed) and others. It is also recorded that Takehiratori-no-Mikoto, the child of Ame-no-Hohi-no-Mikoto (written 天菩比命), is the ancestor of the Izumo provincial chieftains (出雲国造) and others, and that Amatsuhikone-no-Mikoto is the ancestor of the 凡川内国造* and others.

Susanoo-no-Mikoto said, "Because my heart is pure and bright, the children I gave birth to were graceful girls. So I have won." Then, emboldened by his victory, he broke down the ridges between Amaterasu-Ōmikami's rice fields and filled in the ditches, and scattered excrement in the palace where she partook of the great harvest offering (大嘗). Even so, Amaterasu-Ōmikami did not rebuke him but put a better face on it, saying, "What looks like excrement must be what he vomited when drunk. Breaking the ridges and filling the ditches must be because he begrudged the land being wasted."

But Susanoo-no-Mikoto's violence did not stop and grew even worse. When Amaterasu-Ōmikami was having garments for the deities woven in the sacred weaving hall (忌服屋), Susanoo-no-Mikoto made a hole in its roof and dropped in a heavenly piebald horse that he had flayed backwards. The heavenly weaving maiden (天衣織女) who was weaving at the loom was startled, struck her private parts with the shuttle, and died.

A plain retelling of what the original text of the Kojiki says (no published translation has been used).

In the original text

我那勢命之上來由者，必不善心，欲奪我國耳。

Susanoo-no-Mikoto's coming up is surely not from a good heart. He means only to seize my land.

然者汝心之清明，何以知？

Then how am I to know that your heart is pure and bright?

是後所生五柱男子者，物實因我物所成，故自吾子也。

The five boys born afterwards came into being from my belongings, so they are my children.

我心清明，故我所生子，得手弱女。因此言者，自我勝。

Because my heart is pure and bright, the children I gave birth to were graceful girls. So naturally I have won.

  • Ikutsuhikone-no-Mikoto (活津日子根命)The fourth male deity, born from the jewels of the left hand.
  • Kumanokusubi-no-Mikoto (熊野久須毗命)The fifth male deity, born from the jewels of the right hand.
  • Takehiratori-no-Mikoto (建比良鳥命)The child of Ame-no-Hohi-no-Mikoto (written 天菩比命). He is recorded as the ancestor of seven clans, including the Izumo provincial chieftains (出雲国造) and the 无邪志国造*.
  • 十拳釼*The ten-fist sword Susanoo-no-Mikoto wore. Amaterasu-Ōmikami broke it into three and chewed it up, and three female deities were born.
  • 八尺勾璁之五百津之美須麻流之珠*The jewels Amaterasu-Ōmikami wound on her hair loops, hair ornament and both hands. Susanoo-no-Mikoto gave birth to five male deities from them.
  • 天衣織女*The woman who was weaving garments for the deities in the sacred weaving hall (忌服屋). Startled by the piebald horse dropped in, she struck her private parts with the shuttle and died.

In the Nihon Shoki

Nihon Shoki, Book 1, section 6, main text

In the main text of section 6, before the pledge Susanoo-no-Mikoto (素戔鳴尊) sets out the condition, "If those born are girls, you may regard my heart as impure; if boys, as pure." In the Kojiki there is no prior agreement, and Susanoo-no-Mikoto claims victory on the grounds that girls were born.

如吾所生是女者，則可以爲有濁心
Nihon Shoki, Book 1, section 6, variant account 1

In variant account 1 there is no exchange of belongings: the sun deity chews the ten-span, nine-span and eight-span swords (十握剣, 九握剣, 八握剣) that she herself wears and gives birth to the three goddesses, and Susanoo-no-Mikoto (素戔鳴尊) gives birth to the five male deities from the jewels hung around his own neck.

先食所帶十握劒生兒，號瀛津嶋姬
Nihon Shoki, Book 1, section 7, main text

In the Kojiki the one who dies while weaving at the loom is the heavenly weaving maiden (天衣織女), but in the main text of section 7 it is Amaterasu-Ōmikami (天照大神) herself, weaving garments for the deities, who is startled and injures herself with the shuttle, and because of this she shuts herself in the heavenly rock cave (天石窟).

是時天照大神驚動。以梭傷身。
