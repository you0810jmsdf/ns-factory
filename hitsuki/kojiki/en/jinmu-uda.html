Uda, 忍坂, 登美毘古 and the Kume Songs (readings unconfirmed)

宇陀・忍坂・登美毘古と久米歌

In Uda, 兄宇迦斯* is caught in the trap he himself set in a hall and dies, and at 忍坂* the Eighty Braves (八十建) of the earth-spiders (土雲) are struck down all at once at the signal of a song. In the following scenes of attacking 登美毘古* and 兄師木 and 弟師木 (readings unconfirmed), songs containing words such as “久米能古良” are set down one after another.

Read in the Nihon Shoki: Emperor Jinmu's Eastern Expedition ›

Key points to remember

  1. Main text兄宇迦斯* of Uda pretended to submit and set a crushing trap (押機) in a hall, but it was exposed through the secret report of 弟宇迦斯*; driven in by 道臣命 and 大久米命 (readings unconfirmed), he died in his own trap. That place is called the Blood Field of Uda (宇陀之血原).
  2. Main textIn the great pit-dwelling (大室) at 忍坂*, each of the Eighty Braves (八十建) was entertained with a cook-servant (膳夫) bearing a sword assigned to him, and, as signalled by the order “When you hear the song, cut them down all at once,” they were struck down at one stroke in time with the song.
  3. Main text道臣命* is recorded as the ancestor of the Ōtomo no Muraji and others, and 大久米命* as the ancestor of the Kume no Atai and others. The songs in this section contain the words “久米能古” and “久米能古良”.
  4. Main textThe part about the fighting with 登美毘古* and with 兄師木 and 弟師木 (readings unconfirmed) consists almost entirely of songs, and the course of the fighting and its outcome are not written. In the next section (C16), it is summed up that Nigihayahi-no-Mikoto (邇藝速日命) came to present himself, and that the violent deities and the people who would not submit were subdued.
  5. Compared with the Nihon ShokiThe Kojiki gives no name to these songs, but the Nihon Shoki calls the songs of the same scenes “來目歌” (Kume songs). In the Nihon Shoki, it is 道臣命* alone who questions 兄猾*, and in the fighting with Nagasunebiko (長髄彦) a golden kite alights on the emperor's bow.
  6. CommentaryThe Kokugakuin University database of deity names introduces a view that takes “宇加比賀登母” (the band of cormorant fishers) in the song of the scene with 兄師木 and 弟師木 (readings unconfirmed) to refer to the cormorant fishers of 阿陀*, whose ancestor was Niemotsu-no-ko (贄持之子), met in Yoshino, and which points to a connection between the cormorant fishers of 阿陀 and the Kume-be (来目部). [source, Japanese]

The story

In Uda there were two brothers, 兄宇迦斯 and 弟宇迦斯 (readings unconfirmed). First, Yatagarasu (八咫烏) was sent to ask whether they would serve, since the Child of the Heavenly Deities had come, but 兄宇迦斯 shot the messenger back with a whistling arrow (鳴鏑). The place where that whistling arrow fell is called 訶夫羅前*. 兄宇迦斯 meant to meet them in battle but could not gather an army, so he pretended to serve, built a large hall, made a crushing trap (押機; a device that crushes) inside it, and lay in wait.

弟宇迦斯* came first to present himself and revealed his elder brother's scheme. Then the two, 道臣命*, ancestor of the Ōtomo no Muraji and others, and 大久米命*, ancestor of the Kume no Atai and others, summoned 兄宇迦斯* and reviled him, telling him to go first himself into the hall he had built and show plainly how he meant to serve. When the two drove him in, gripping the hilts of their swords, pointing their spears and fitting arrows to their bows, 兄宇迦斯 was struck by the crushing trap he himself had made, and died. Because they dragged him out and cut him to pieces, that place is called the Blood Field of Uda (宇陀之血原).

The feast presented by 弟宇迦斯* was all distributed among the troops, and a song was sung at this time (C3). After the song there are notes on words in the song, and one of them is recorded as being “a thing of mocking laughter.” 弟宇迦斯 is regarded as the ancestor of the water-bearers (水取) of Uda and others.

When they advanced from there and reached the great pit-dwelling (大室) at 忍坂*, the Eighty Braves (八十建) of the tailed earth-spiders (土雲) were waiting in that dwelling. By command of the Child of the Heavenly Deities, the Eighty Braves were given a feast, one cook-servant (膳夫) was assigned to each of them, and each servant was made to carry a sword. They were instructed in advance, “When you hear the song, cut them down all at once,” and when the song was sung (C6), they drew their swords and struck them down at one stroke.

After that, a song was sung when they were about to attack 登美毘古*, and two further songs are recorded in succession (C9, C11, C13). Each of these songs has a line resembling “宇知弖斯夜麻牟”. Also, when they attacked 兄師木 and 弟師木 (readings unconfirmed), the troops were for a while exhausted, and a song was sung (C15). In this part the Kojiki only sets down the songs in a row, and does not write how the fight with 登美毘古 was settled.

A plain retelling of what the original text of the Kojiki says (no published translation has been used).

In the original text

乃己所作押見打而死。

兄宇迦斯* was struck by the crushing trap he himself had made, and died.

聞歌之者。一時共斬。

The order to the cook-servants: when you hear the song, cut them down all at once.

美都美都斯。久米能古良賀。

The opening of the song sung when they were about to attack 登美毘古* (C8). It is written in man'yōgana, one character per syllable.

又撃兄師木。弟師木之時。御軍暫疲。

When they attacked 兄師木 and 弟師木 (readings unconfirmed), the troops were for a while exhausted. A song follows after this.

In the Nihon Shoki

Nihon Shoki, Book 3, Emperor Jinmu, record before accession, the year 戊午, autumn, 8th month (甲午朔乙未)

In the Nihon Shoki, the one sent to verify the scheme of 兄猾 * (兄宇迦斯) is 道臣命* alone, and the name of 大久米命 does not appear. The song at the banquet after 兄猾 dies by stepping on his own device is called a “來目歌” (Kume song).

天皇即遣道臣命察其逆状
Nihon Shoki, Book 3, Emperor Jinmu, record before accession, the year 戊午, winter, 10th month (癸巳朔)

In the Nihon Shoki, it is stated explicitly that the one who sings the signal song at the banquet in the great pit-dwelling at 忍坂* is 道臣命* (the Kojiki does not say who sings).

時道臣命乃起而歌之曰
Nihon Shoki, Book 3, Emperor Jinmu, record before accession, the year 戊午, 12th month (癸巳朔丙申)

In the Nihon Shoki, in the fighting with Nagasunebiko (長髄彦; 登美毘古), a golden kite flies in and alights on the tip of the emperor's bow, and its light leaves the enemy soldiers unable to fight. This story of the kite is not in the Kojiki.
