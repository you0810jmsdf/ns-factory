The Songs of 八千矛神* and Ōkuninushi-no-Kami's Making of the Land

八千矛神の歌と大国主神の国作り

八千矛神 * (Ōkuninushi-no-Kami) seeks the hand of Nunakawa-hime of 高志國* with a song, and is reconciled with his jealous principal wife Suseri-bime by exchanging songs with her as well. After that, Ōkuninushi-no-Kami makes and firms up the land together with Sukunahikona-no-Kami (少名毗古那神), a child of Kamumusuhi-no-Kami, and after Sukunahikona-no-Kami leaves, he carries the making of the land forward by worshipping on 御諸山* a deity who came lighting up the sea.

Read in the Nihon Shoki: Yamata-no-Orochi and Ōanamuchi-no-Kami (Section 8) ›

Key points to remember

  1. Main textSukunahikona-no-Kami (少名毗古那神), who came from the sea riding in the boat 天之羅摩船*, was revealed by Kuebiko to be a child of Kamumusuhi-no-Kami, and, at Kamumusuhi-no-Kami's command, made and firmed up the land side by side with 大穴牟遲 * (Ōkuninushi-no-Kami). Later Sukunahikona-no-Kami crossed over to 常世國*.
  2. Main textAfter Sukunahikona-no-Kami left, a deity who approached lighting up the sea said, "If you worship me, I will make the land with you," and asked to be worshipped on the mountain to the east of the green fence of mountains (青垣) of Yamato (倭). The Kojiki records this as "the deity who dwells on 御諸山*" and does not write its name here.
  3. Main text八千矛神* sought the hand of Nunakawa-hime of 高志國* with a song; Nunakawa-hime too returned a song from inside the door, and they were united the following night. When his principal wife Suseri-bime (須勢理毗賣命) was jealous, 八千矛神 sang as he was about to set off for Yamato (倭); the consort offered a wine cup and sang in reply, and the two have remained settled together to this day. The Kojiki calls these songs "divine tales" (神語).
  4. Main textAs children of Ōkuninushi-no-Kami are recorded 阿遲鉏高日子根神 * (now 迦毛大御神)* and 高比賣命*, born of 多紀理毗賣命*, and Kotoshironushi-no-Kami, born of Kamuyatate-hime-no-Mikoto. In the genealogy of Ōtoshi-no-Kami that follows appear the deity of the kitchen hearth worshipped by the people, and Ōyamakui-no-Kami, who dwells at 日枝山 and 松尾 (readings unconfirmed).
  5. Compared with the Nihon ShokiIn variant account 6 of section 8 of the Nihon Shoki, Sukunahikona-no-Mikoto (少彥名命) is said to have come riding in a boat of 白蘞 bark and wearing the feathers of a wren (鷦鷯), and his parent is 高皇產靈尊*. The deity who came lighting up the sea names itself the "幸魂奇魂" of Ōanamuchi (大己貴神) and is regarded as the deity of 大三輪* who dwells on 三諸山*. In the Kojiki the parent is Kamumusuhi-no-Kami, and the deity of 御諸山 is written of as a deity separate from Ōkuninushi-no-Kami.
  6. CommentaryAccording to the Kokugakuin University database of deity names, although the Kojiki calls him Ōkuninushi-no-Kami in this section, only in the one sentence about making and firming up the land with Sukunahikona-no-Kami does it use the name "大穴牟遲"*. This is thought to be because there was originally a myth pairing 大穴牟遲 and Sukunahikona, which was gathered together under the name of Ōkuninushi-no-Kami. [source, Japanese] [source, Japanese]

The story

When 八千矛神* arrived at the house of Nunakawa-hime of 高志國*, he sang a song. The song tells how, having heard that there was a wise and beautiful maiden in the distant land of 高志, he came courting her, and how, as he kept standing in front of the board door of the house where the maiden slept, the nue birds and the pheasants, and the fowl in the yard, began to cry. Nunakawa-hime, without opening the door, returned a song from inside, answering that when night came she would come out. They did not meet that night, and on the night of the following day the two were united.

His principal consort (適后) Suseri-bime (須勢理毗賣命) was intensely jealous. The husband deity prepared for a journey to go up from Izumo to the land of Yamato (倭國), and with one hand on the saddle of his horse and one foot in the stirrup, he sang. In the song he calls to his wife: trying on black robes and blue robes, he throws them off as unbecoming, puts on a dyed robe and says this one is good; and if he goes away like a flock of birds, though she says she will not weep, she will weep.

The consort took a large wine cup in her hand, drew close to her husband, and sang as she offered it to him. In the song she sings that since he is a man he can have a wife on every island and every rocky shore wherever he goes, but that she, a woman, has no husband other than him, and invites him to rest together beneath the soft bedclothes and to drink the wine. Having sung thus, the two "宇伎由比" and "宇那賀氣理" (both words written by the sounds of the characters), and have remained settled together to this day. These songs are called "divine tales" (神語).

Ōkuninushi-no-Kami took as wife 多紀理毗賣命*, who dwells at the 奧津宮 shrine of 胸形 (readings unconfirmed), and 阿遲鉏高日子根神 and his younger sister 高比賣命 (also called 下光比賣命) (readings unconfirmed) were born. 阿遲鉏高日子根神 is the deity now called 迦毛大御神*. He also took Kamuyatate-hime-no-Mikoto as wife, and Kotoshironushi-no-Kami was born. The genealogy of descendants continues further, and the deities from Yashimajinumi-no-Kami to 遠津山岬帶神* are summed up as "the seventeen generations of deities" (十七世の神).

When Ōkuninushi-no-Kami was at the cape 御大の御前* in Izumo, a deity came along the crests of the waves riding in the boat 天之羅摩船*, wearing as clothing the whole skin stripped from a goose (鵝). When asked its name it did not answer, and the deities attending him did not know either, but Taniguku said, "Kuebiko should know." Kuebiko answered, "This is Sukunahikona-no-Kami (少名毗古那神), the child of Kamumusuhi-no-Kami," and Kamumusuhi-no-Kami also declared, "He is truly my child, and among my children he is the one who slipped out between the fingers of my hand. Become brothers with you, 葦原色許男命*, and make and firm up the land." So the two deities 大穴牟遲* and Sukunahikona made and firmed up the land side by side, but later Sukunahikona-no-Kami crossed over to 常世國*. Kuebiko, who revealed Sukunahikona-no-Kami's name, is written to be what is now Yamada-no-Sohodo, a deity who cannot walk on its feet but knows everything under heaven.

Ōkuninushi-no-Kami lamented, "How could I make this land alone? With which deity could I make it together?" At that moment there was a deity who approached lighting up the sea and said, "If you worship me well, I will complete the making of the land with you. Otherwise the land will be hard to complete." When Ōkuninushi-no-Kami asked how he should worship it, the deity answered, "Worship me with reverence on the mountain to the east of the green fence of mountains (青垣) of Yamato (倭)." This is the deity who dwells on 御諸山*.

Next the genealogy of the descendants of Ōtoshi-no-Kami is recorded. Ōtoshi-no-Kami took 伊怒比賣 as wife and fathered Ōkunimitama-no-Kami, 韓神, 曾富理神, 白日神 and 聖神; took 香用比賣 as wife and fathered 大香山戶臣神 and 年御神; and took 天知迦流美豆比賣 as wife and fathered 奧津日子神 (readings unconfirmed), Okutsu-hime-no-Mikoto (also called 大戶比賣神)* and others. Okutsu-hime-no-Mikoto (大戶比賣神) is recorded as "the deity of the kitchen hearth worshipped by the people", and Ōyamakui-no-Kami (also called 山末之大主神)* is written to dwell at 日枝山 in 近淡海國 and at 松尾 in 葛野 (readings unconfirmed), and to be a deity who uses a whistling arrow (鳴鏑). The children of Ōtoshi-no-Kami number sixteen deities in all, and further, Hayamato-no-Kami took 大氣都比賣神* as wife, and eight deities, including 若山咋神 and 若年神 (readings unconfirmed), were born.

A plain retelling of what the original text of the Kojiki says (no published translation has been used).

In the original text

自波穗乘天之羅摩船而内剥鵝皮剥為衣服

Sukunahikona-no-Kami came from the crests of the waves riding in the boat 天之羅摩船*, wearing as clothing the whole skin stripped from a goose.

大穴牟遲与少名毗古那二柱神相並，作堅此國

The two deities 大穴牟遲* and Sukunahikona, side by side, made and firmed up this land.

吾者，伊都岐奉于倭之青垣東山上。

The words of the deity who came lighting up the sea: "Worship me with reverence on the mountain to the east of the green fence of mountains of Yamato."

此謂之神語也。

The text concludes that the exchange of songs among 八千矛神*, Nunakawa-hime and Suseri-bime is called "divine tales" (神語).

In the Nihon Shoki

Nihon Shoki, Book 1, section 8, variant account 6

Sukunahikona-no-Mikoto (少彥名命) is said to have come riding on the tide with 白蘞 bark as his boat and the feathers of a wren (鷦鷯) as his clothing, and his parent is 高皇產靈尊*. In the Kojiki it is the boat 天之羅摩船* and the skin of a goose (鵝), and the parent is Kamumusuhi-no-Kami.

以白蘞皮爲舟。以鷦鷯羽爲衣
Nihon Shoki, Book 1, section 8, variant account 6

The deity who came lighting up the sea names itself the "幸魂奇魂" of Ōanamuchi (大己貴神), and is regarded as the deity of 大三輪* who dwells on 三諸山*. In the Kojiki this deity does not give its name, and is written of as "the deity who dwells on 御諸山"*, separate from Ōkuninushi-no-Kami.

吾是汝之幸魂奇魂也
Nihon Shoki, Book 1, section 8, variant account 6

It is said that Sukunahikona-no-Mikoto (少彥名命) went from the cape 御碕* of Kumano to 常世郷*, and also that on Awashima (淡嶋) he climbed a stalk of millet, was flicked off, and crossed over to 常世郷. The Kojiki records only that he crossed over to 常世國*. Passages corresponding to the songs of 八千矛神* with Nunakawa-hime and Suseri-bime could not be confirmed.
