The Death of Emperor Chūai and the Silla Expedition of 息長帯日売命*

仲哀天皇の死と息長帯日売命の新羅遠征

After Emperor Seimu (若帯日子天皇*) established the provincial governors (国造) and district chiefs (県主), Emperor Chūai (帯中日子天皇*) refuses to believe a divine oracle and dies suddenly. His chief consort 息長帯日売命*, who followed the oracle, crossed the sea and brought Silla into submission, and after returning to Tsukushi she gave birth to a son (later Emperor Ōjin).

Key points to remember

  1. Main textEmperor Chūai rejected as a "lying deity" the oracle promising to give him a land of treasures in the west, and died on the spot while playing the koto (琴). The deity then announced anew that that land was to be ruled by the child (a boy) in the chief consort's womb.
  2. Main textThe deity of the oracle revealed its name: it was the will of Amaterasu-Ōmikami (天照大神), and it was the three great deities 底筒男, 中筒男 and 上筒男*. Having brought Silla into submission, the chief consort enshrined the rough spirit (荒御魂) of the Great Deity of 墨江* in Silla as a deity to guard the land.
  3. Main text大鞆和気命* (品陀和気命, later Emperor Ōjin) received this name because at birth he had flesh on his arm like a bracer (鞆). The birth took place after the return from Silla to Tsukushi, and the place where he was born is called 宇美*.
  4. Compared with the Nihon ShokiIn the Nihon Shoki (Book 8), Emperor Chūai attacks the Kumaso without believing the deity's words, cannot win and returns, and in the 2nd month of his 9th year he suddenly falls ill and dies the next day. It does not say, as the Kojiki does, that he died in the middle of playing the koto.
  5. Compared with the Nihon ShokiIn the Nihon Shoki (Book 9), after the emperor's death the empress herself becomes the ritual medium (神主) and has Takeuchi-no-Sukune (武内宿禰) play the koto, and the deities name, besides 表筒男, 中筒男 and 底筒男*, the deity dwelling in the 五十鈴宮* of Ise, Kotoshironushi-no-Kami (事代主神) and others. It does not say, as the Kojiki does, "the will of Amaterasu-Ōmikami."
  6. CommentaryAccording to the Kokugakuin University deity-name database, the three deities who revealed their names are 底箇之男命, 中箇之男命 and 上箇之男命 (墨江之三前大神)*, who were born in the purification (禊) section of Part 1; they are enshrined at Sumiyoshi Taisha (住吉大社) and have long been worshipped as deities who protect seafaring. The story

Emperor Seimu (若帯日子天皇*) ruled the realm from the 高穴穂宮 of 志賀* in Ōmi (近淡海). He took as wife 弟財郎女*, daughter of 建忍山垂根*, ancestor of the 穂積臣* and others, and 和訶奴気王* was born. The emperor made Takeuchi-no-Sukune (建内宿禰) his great minister (大臣), and established the governors (国造) of the large and small provinces, the boundaries between provinces, and the district chiefs (県主) of the large and small districts. The emperor died at the age of 95, and his tomb is at 多他那美 in 沙紀*.

Emperor Chūai (帯中日子天皇*) ruled the realm from the 豊浦宮 of 穴門* and the 訶志比宮 of Tsukushi*. With 大中津比売命*, daughter of 大江王*, he had 香坂王* and Oshikuma-no-Miko (忍熊王); with his chief consort 息長帯日売命* he had 品夜和気命 and 大鞆和気命 (also named 品陀和気命)*. The name 大鞆和気命 comes from the fact that at birth he had flesh on his arm like a bracer (鞆). The Kojiki records that from this it is known that he was destined to rule the land from the time he was in his mother's womb. In this reign the 屯家 of 淡道* was established.

The chief consort 息長帯日売命* was a person through whom deities spoke. When the emperor, at the 訶志比宮*, was about to attack the land of 熊曾*, the emperor played the koto, and the great minister Takeuchi-no-Sukune (建内宿禰), in the ritual ground (沙庭), sought the words of the deity. The deity who possessed the chief consort announced, "In the west there is a land with many dazzling treasures, gold and silver first among them. I will give you that land." The emperor answered, "When I climb a high place and look west, I see no land, only the great sea," and, declaring it a deity that spoke lies, pushed the koto away, stopped playing and fell silent. The deity was greatly angered and said, "This realm is not a land for you to rule. Head for the one road (一道)."

Urged by Takeuchi-no-Sukune, the emperor drew the koto a little closer and began to play, but soon the sound of the koto could no longer be heard. When they lit a light and looked, the emperor was already dead. In fear, the people placed the emperor in a temporary mortuary palace (殯宮), gathered great offerings (大奴佐) from throughout the land, sought out every kind of offense, and performed a great purification (大祓) of the land. When Takeuchi-no-Sukune again sought the words of the deity, the deity announced as before that this land was to be ruled by the child in the chief consort's womb, and answered that the child was a boy. When asked its name, the deity revealed that this was the will of Amaterasu-Ōmikami (天照大神), and also that it was the three great deities 底筒男, 中筒男 and 上筒男*.

The deity instructed them to make offerings (幣帛) to all the heavenly and earthly deities and to the deities of the mountains, rivers and seas, to enshrine its own spirit on the ships, to put the ash of 真木* wood into gourds, and to make many chopsticks and 比羅伝*, scatter them floating on the sea, and so cross. When they arrayed the army and lined up the ships as instructed and crossed, the fish of the sea, large and small alike, bore the ships on their backs, a strong following wind blew, and the waves carried the ships halfway into the land of Silla. The king of Silla, in awe, offered that from then on he would serve as the emperor's horse-keepers (御馬甘), sending ships in a row every year without rest. Thus the land of Silla was designated as the horse-keepers and the land of Paekche as the 屯家 across the sea (渡屯家)*; the chief consort thrust her staff into the gate of the ruler of Silla, enshrined the rough spirit (荒御魂) of the Great Deity of 墨江* as a deity to guard the land, and returned.

Before that work was finished, the chief consort reached the month of her delivery, so to calm her womb she took stones and wound them around the waist of her skirt, and she gave birth to the child after crossing to Tsukushi. Therefore the place where the child was born is called 宇美*, and the stones she wound in her skirt are in the village of 伊斗* in Tsukushi. Also, the chief consort took a meal on the riverbank at the village of 玉嶋 in the district of 末羅* in the first ten days of the 4th month; she sat on a rock in the river, pulled out threads from her skirt, used grains of rice as bait, and fished for the sweetfish (年魚) of that river. The text records that for this reason, even now, in the first ten days of the 4th month women never fail to pull threads from their skirts and fish for sweetfish with grains of rice as bait.

A plain retelling of what the original text of the Kojiki says (no published translation has been used).

In the original text

西方有國。金銀爲本。目之炎耀。種種珍寶。多在其國

In the west there is a land. Many treasures of all kinds, gold and silver first among them, so dazzling they blind the eyes, are in that land (words the deity announced through the mouth of the chief consort).

凡此國者。坐汝命御腹之御子。所知國者也

This land is a land to be ruled by the child in your (the chief consort's) womb (words the deity announced again after the emperor's death).

如鞆宍生御腕。故著其御名

Because he had flesh on his arm like a bracer (鞆), that name (大鞆和気命*) was given.

海原之魚。不問大小。悉負御船而渡

The fish of the sea, large and small alike, all bore the ships on their backs across.

In the Nihon Shoki

Nihon Shoki, Book 8 — the 9th year of Emperor Chūai, spring, 2nd month (癸卯朔丁未)

In the Nihon Shoki, Emperor Chūai, not believing the words of the deity, attacks the Kumaso, cannot win and returns, then suddenly falls ill and dies the next day; it records that he died early because he did not heed the deity's words. An alternative account (一云) in a small-script note gives another tradition, that he attacked the Kumaso and died after being hit by an enemy arrow.

天皇忽有痛身。而明日崩。時年五十二
Nihon Shoki, Book 9 — Empress Jingū (the 9th year of Emperor Chūai, 3rd month, 壬申朔)

In the Nihon Shoki, after the emperor's death the empress chooses an auspicious day, enters a ritual palace (斎宮), becomes the ritual medium (神主) herself, has Takeuchi-no-Sukune (武内宿禰) play the koto, and has 中臣烏賊津使主* act as the interpreter of the divine words (審神者) to ask the names of the deities. In the Kojiki it is the emperor who plays the koto, and Takeuchi-no-Sukune is in the ritual ground (沙庭).

皇后選吉日、入齋宮、親爲神主
Nihon Shoki, Book 9 — Empress Jingū (the 9th year of Emperor Chūai, summer, 4th month, 壬寅朔甲辰)

In the Nihon Shoki, fishing in the stream at the village of 玉嶋* using thread from her skirt as a fishing line takes place before the crossing to Silla, as a divination of whether her wish to obtain the land of treasures in the west would be fulfilled, and it is given as the origin of calling that place the land of 梅豆羅 (now 松浦)*. In the Kojiki, it is placed as a story after the birth of the child.
