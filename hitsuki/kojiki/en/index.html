Kojiki Archive

The Kojiki, completed in 712, tells of Japan from the age of the gods to Empress Suiko in three parts. Here it is divided into 47 stories. Each page begins with the key points to remember, followed by the story, short passages of the original text, the deities who appear, and how the Nihon Shoki tells the same events.

How this archive is written

A small * after a name in kanji means that its reading could not be confirmed from the sources used here, so the name is left in its original characters.

Everything here has been checked against the original text of the Kojiki. Where a later interpretation is given, it is marked "Commentary" and its source is a public institution's explanation. Each page also links to the same events in the Nihon Shoki Archive.

Part 1: the Age of the Gods (17 stories)

  1. 1The Beginning of Heaven and EarthThe first deity to appear in the Kojiki is Ame-no-Minakanushi-no-Kami, who came into being in the High Plain of Heaven (高天原). Next Takamimusubi-no-Kami and Kamumusuhi-no-Kami came into being, and all three, as solitary deities (独神), hid their forms.
  2. 2The Birth of the LandThe heavenly deities commanded Izanagi-no-Mikoto and Izanami-no-Mikoto to put the land in order and make it firm, and gave them the spear 天沼矛*. When the two stirred the sea with the spear from the bridge 天浮橋*, the brine that dripped from the tip of the spear piled up and Onogoro-shima was formed.
  3. 3The Birth of the Deities and the Fire DeityBecause Izanami-no-Mikoto gave birth to the fire deity 火之夜芸速男神 * (also called 火之迦具土神), her private parts were burned, she fell ill, and at last she departed this world (神避). Even during her illness, deities such as 金山毗古神*, 波迩夜須毗売神*, Mitsuhanome-no-Kami and Wakumusubi-no-Kami came into being from her vomit, feces and urine.
  4. 4The Land of YomiIzanagi-no-Mikoto broke Izanami-no-Mikoto's request, "Do not look at me while I talk with the deities of Yomi (黄泉神)," lit a light and looked, and so came to be pursued by Izanami-no-Mikoto, who had been put to shame.
  5. 5Purification and the Three Noble ChildrenWhen Izanagi-no-Mikoto washed his left eye in the purification (禊), Amaterasu-Ōmikami was born; with his right eye, Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto; and with his nose, Susanoo-no-Mikoto (written 建速須佐之男命). Izanagi-no-Mikoto rejoiced greatly, saying, "At the last I have obtained three noble children." These are the "Three Noble Children" (三貴子).
  6. 6The Pledge at the Heavenly River 天の安の河*Amaterasu-Ōmikami suspected that Susanoo-no-Mikoto had come to seize her land and met him armed; so that Susanoo-no-Mikoto could show the purity of his heart, the two deities made a pledge (誓約) across the river 天安河* and each gave birth to children from the other's belongings.
  7. 7The Heavenly Rock-Cave DoorWhen Amaterasu-Ōmikami closed the Heavenly Rock-Cave Door (天石屋戸) and shut herself in, both the High Plain of Heaven (高天原) and the Central Land of Reed Plains (葦原中国) became pitch dark, night went on and on, and every kind of calamity arose.
  8. 8The Banishment of Susanoo-no-Mikoto and Yamata-no-OrochiSusanoo-no-Mikoto made Yamata-no-Orochi, which had eight heads and eight tails, drink strong sake, and when it was drunk and asleep he cut it to death with his ten-fist sword (十拳剣) and saved 櫛名田比売*.
  9. 9The Hare (素兎) of 稲羽*大穴牟遅神 * (Ōkuninushi-no-Kami) taught the hare, which was suffering after being skinned, to wash its body in the water of the river mouth and roll about on kama-no-hana (蒲黄) spread out beneath it, and the hare's body was healed as it had been before.
  10. 10The Persecution by the Eighty Deities and the Land 根の堅州国*As 大穴牟遲神* fled from 根堅州國*, Susanoo-no-Mikoto called out to him from 黄泉比良坂*: "Drive away your brothers with the living sword (生大刀) and the living bow and arrows (生弓矢), become Ōkuninushi-no-Kami and 宇都志國主神*, make Suseri-bime (須世理毗賣) your principal wife, build a palace at the foot of 宇迦能山*, and live there." With that sword and bow 大穴牟遲神 drove away the Eighty Deities and for the first time made the land.
  11. 11The Songs of 八千矛神* and Ōkuninushi-no-Kami's Making of the LandSukunahikona-no-Kami (少名毗古那神), who came from the sea riding in the boat 天之羅摩船*, was revealed by Kuebiko to be a child of Kamumusuhi-no-Kami, and, at Kamumusuhi-no-Kami's command, made and firmed up the land side by side with 大穴牟遲 * (Ōkuninushi-no-Kami). Later Sukunahikona-no-Kami crossed over to 常世國*.
  12. 12Ame-no-Wakahiko (天若日子)As envoys to bring the Central Land of the Reed Plains into submission, Ame-no-Hohi-no-Kami (天菩比神) was sent first, but he fawned on Ōkuninushi-no-Kami and made no report for three years. Ame-no-Wakahiko, who was sent next, also married Shitateru-hime (下照比売), tried to make the land his own, and made no report for eight years.
  13. 13The Ceding of the LandAs envoy for the ceding of the land, Takemikazuchi-no-Kami, son of 伊都之尾羽張神*, was sent with Ame-no-Torifune-no-Kami as his companion. The two deities stood a ten-fist sword (十掬剣) upside down on the crest of a wave on the small beach of 伊耶佐* in Izumo, sat on its point, and demanded that Ōkuninushi-no-Kami cede the land.
  14. 14The Descent of the Heavenly GrandsonAmaterasu-Ōmikami and 高木神* at first commanded Ame-no-Oshihomimi-no-Mikoto to go down, but while he was making ready his son Ninigi-no-Mikoto was born, so at Ame-no-Oshihomimi-no-Mikoto's request it was decided that Ninigi-no-Mikoto would go down in his place.
  15. 15Konohanasakuya-hime and IwanagahimeBecause Ninigi-no-Mikoto sent back the elder sister Iwanagahime as ugly and took only the younger sister Konohanasakuya-hime as his wife, the lives of the children of the heavenly deity became as fleeting as the blossoms of trees, just as Ōyamatsumi-no-Kami had said. The Kojiki says that this is why, even now, the lives of the emperors are not long.
  16. 16Umisachi and Yamasachi (海幸山幸)火遠理命 * (Yamasachibiko) exchanged tools with his elder brother Hoderi-no-Mikoto (Umisachibiko), but lost the fishhook in the sea. The elder brother would not accept even a thousand fishhooks made from a sword and demanded the original fishhook, so 火遠理命 set out for the palace of Watatsumi-no-Kami in the small boat of Shiotsuchi-no-Kami.
  17. 17The Birth of Ugayafukiaezu-no-MikotoToyotama-hime asked him “Do not look,” but when 火遠理命* peeked, she was crawling and writhing in the form of an eight-fathom wani (八尋和迩), so he fled. Ashamed, Toyotama-hime left the child behind, closed off the sea boundary (海坂) and returned to the land of the sea.

Part 2: from Emperor Jinmu to Emperor Ōjin (17 stories)

  1. 1Emperor Jinmu's Eastern Expedition (from Hyūga to Kumano and Yoshino)Itsuse-no-Mikoto was wounded in the hand by an arrow of 登美毘古* and, saying that “it was wrong for the child of the sun deity to fight facing the sun,” chose a route circling round to the south so as to have the sun at their backs. On the way he died at the 男之水門* in the land of Kii (紀国), and it is recorded that his tomb is at 竈山*.
  2. 2Uda, 忍坂, 登美毘古 and the Kume Songs (readings unconfirmed)兄宇迦斯* of Uda pretended to submit and set a crushing trap (押機) in a hall, but it was exposed through the secret report of 弟宇迦斯*; driven in by 道臣命 and 大久米命 (readings unconfirmed), he died in his own trap. That place is called the Blood Field of Uda (宇陀之血原).
  3. 3Nigihayahi-no-Mikoto Comes to Serve, and the Choice of an EmpressNigihayahi-no-Mikoto came down from heaven following the Child of the Heavenly Deities, presented heavenly tokens (天津瑞) and served him. Umashimaji-no-Mikoto (宇摩志麻遲命), born to him and the younger sister of 登美毘古*, is regarded as the ancestor of the Mononobe no Muraji (物部連), the 穂積臣* and the 婇臣*.
  4. 4The Rebellion of Tagishimimi-no-MikotoWhen Emperor Jinmu died, Tagishimimi-no-Mikoto, the elder half-brother born in Hyūga, took the empress 伊須氣余理比賣* as his wife and plotted to kill her children, his three younger half-brothers. 伊須氣余理比賣 warned the princes of the danger in songs.
  5. 5From Emperor Suizei to Emperor KaikaThe accounts of these eight reigns center on genealogy, recording palaces, wives and children, the ancestors of lineages, ages and tombs, and there is hardly any narrative. Among them, the section on Emperor Kōrei records that 大吉備津日子命 (Ōkibitsuhiko-no-Mikoto) and 若建吉備津日子命* pacified the land of Kibi, using Harima (針間) as the entrance.
  6. 6Emperor Sujin and Ōmononushi-no-Ōkami of MiwaIn the time of Emperor Sujin, when people were on the verge of dying out from pestilence, Ōmononushi-no-Ōkami declared in a dream, “This comes from my will,” and asked to be worshipped by 意富多多泥古*. When the emperor sought out 意富多多泥古 from the village of 美努* in Kawachi and had him worship as the officiating priest at Mount 御諸*, the pestilence ceased.
  7. 7The Dispatch along the Four Circuits and the Rebellion of 建波邇安王*The emperor interpreted the maiden's song that Ōbiko-no-Mikoto had heard at 幣羅坂* as “a sign that my elder half-brother 建波邇安王* has formed an evil intent,” and sent Ōbiko-no-Mikoto and 日子國夫玖命* to Yamashiro (山代) to subdue him.
  8. 8The Rebellion of 沙本毘古王*Asked by her elder brother 沙本毘古王* "Whom do you love more, your husband or your brother?", the consort 沙本毘賣命* answered "My brother." She was given a cord dagger to kill the emperor, but though she raised it three times she could not stab him, and her falling tears woke the emperor.
  9. 9Prince 本牟智和気御子* and 多遅摩毛理*Prince 本牟智和氣御子* did not speak even when his eight-fist-long beard reached his chest. Through the emperor's dream and the great divination (太占) it was found that this was a curse arising from the will of the Great Deity of Izumo, so the prince was sent to worship the Great Deity at Izumo.
  10. 10The Western Campaign of 小碓命* (熊曾建* and Izumo Takeru)Ordered to "泥疑"* his elder brother 大碓命*, 小碓命* seized his brother in the privy, tore off his limbs, wrapped him in a straw mat (薦) and threw him away. The emperor feared his violent heart and sent him to subdue the 熊曾建* in the west.
  11. 11The Eastern Campaign of Yamato Takeru (倭建命)Ordered to pacify the twelve circuits of the east, Yamato Takeru (倭建命) lamented and wept to his aunt 倭比賣命* at Ise, saying, "The emperor has my death in mind," and was given the Kusanagi sword (草那藝劍) and a bag (御嚢).
  12. 12The Death of Yamato Takeru (倭建命) and the White BirdLeaving the Kusanagi sword (草那藝劔) with Miyazu-hime (美夜受比賣), Yamato Takeru set out saying he would seize the mountain deity of Ibuki (伊服岐) bare-handed; because he made a declaration (言挙) that a white boar was the deity's messenger, he was struck and confounded by a great icy rain. A small-script note in the original text states that the white boar was in fact the deity itself.
  13. 13The Death of Emperor Chūai and the Silla Expedition of 息長帯日売命*Emperor Chūai rejected as a "lying deity" the oracle promising to give him a land of treasures in the west, and died on the spot while playing the koto (琴). The deity then announced anew that that land was to be ruled by the child (a boy) in the chief consort's womb.
  14. 14The Rebellion of Oshikuma-no-Miko (忍熊王) and Kehi-no-Ōkami (気比大神)息長帯日売命* put her son on a mourning ship (喪船) and made it appear that he had died, deceiving 香坂王* and Oshikuma-no-Miko (忍熊王), who lay in wait. 香坂王 was devoured by an enraged boar in the middle of an oath-hunt (宇気比獦), and Oshikuma-no-Miko, defeated, entered the sea with 伊佐比宿禰* and died.
  15. 15Emperor Ōjin's Courtships and 髪長比売*Emperor Ōjin asked 大山守命 and 大雀命* which was dearer, an elder child or a younger child, and approved the words of 大雀命, who, grasping the emperor's mind, answered "the younger child." As a result it was decided that 宇遅能和紀郎子* would be the one to inherit the heavenly succession (天津日継), 大山守命 the one to take charge of the government of the mountains and seas, and 大雀命 the one to take charge of the government of the realm (食国).
  16. 16The Age of Emperor Ōjin and the Rebellion of 大山守命*When Emperor Ōjin died, 大山守命* defied his father's command, aimed at the realm, and tried to kill his younger brother 宇遅能和紀郎子*. 宇遅能和紀郎子, who had prepared after being warned by 大雀命*, disguised himself as the boatman at the steering oar, tipped the boat, and made 大山守命 fall into the river and sink.
  17. 17Ame-no-Hiboko (天之日矛) and the Brothers of Autumn Mountain and Spring MountainAme-no-Hiboko (天之日矛) was the son of the ruler of Silla. He crossed over in pursuit of his wife, who had been born from a red jewel borne by a woman touched by the light of the sun, and who had fled to Naniwa. But he was blocked by the deity of the crossing and could not enter Naniwa, so he stayed in the province of 多遅摩*.

Part 3: from Emperor Nintoku to Empress Suiko (13 stories)

  1. 1The Accession of Emperor Nintoku and the People's Hearths大雀命 * (Emperor Nintoku) looked over the land from a high mountain and, because no cooking smoke rose, knew that the whole land was poor, and exempted the people from all labor levies and taxes (課役) for three years. During that time, even when the palace fell into disrepair and rain leaked in, he had it left unrepaired and made do by catching the rain in vessels.
  2. 2The Jealousy of 石之日売命* and 黒日売*The chief consort 石之日売命* was extremely jealous, and 黒日売*, daughter of the 海部直* of Kibi, who had been summoned by the emperor, fled back to her home in fear of the chief consort. Still angry, the chief consort had 黒日売 driven off the boat at 大浦* and made her walk home.
  3. 3The Chief Consort's Journey to 山代* and the Six SongsWhile the chief consort 石之日売命* was away in 木国* to gather 御綱柏* leaves, the emperor married 八田若郎女*. Learning of it from a laborer's (仕丁) talk on the boat home, the chief consort threw all the 御綱柏 into the sea (the origin of the name 御津前* for that place), and without entering the palace went up the 堀江 canal toward 山代*.
  4. 4八田若郎女, 女鳥王 and 速総別王 (readings unconfirmed)Using his younger brother 速総別王* as a go-between, the emperor sought 女鳥王*, but she refused, saying, “Because the chief consort is so strong, the emperor has not even taken 八田若郎女* as his wife,” and she became the wife of 速総別王. 速総別王 gave the emperor no reply.
  5. 5The Song of the Wild Goose’s Eggs and the Ship 枯野*In a song, the emperor asked Takeuchi-no-Sukune-no-Mikoto (建内宿禰) about a wild goose having laid eggs on 日女嶋*, and Takeuchi-no-Sukune answered in a song. The song he then sang after being granted the emperor’s koto is described as “the 片歌 of the 本岐歌” (readings unconfirmed).
  6. 6Emperor Richū, Emperor Hanzei and the Revolt of 墨江中王*At the banquet (豊明) of the Great Thanksgiving (大嘗) at the Naniwa palace (難波宮), while 伊邪本和気命 * (Emperor Richū) lay asleep drunk on sake, his younger brother 墨江中王* set fire to the great hall, intending to kill the emperor. 阿知直*, ancestor of the 倭漢直*, put the emperor on a horse and sent him away to Yamato (倭).
  7. 7Emperor Ingyō, Crown Prince 軽 and 軽大郎女 (readings unconfirmed)男浅津間若子宿禰命 * (Emperor Ingyō) once declined to take the throne because of a long illness, but at the earnest request of the chief consort and the ministers, he ruled the realm. The chief envoy 金波鎮漢紀武*, sent by the ruler of Silla (新良), cured the emperor’s illness with his knowledge of medicine.
  8. 8Emperor Ankō and the Incident of 目弱王*The seven-year-old 目弱王* overheard words saying that it was Emperor Ankō who had killed his father, 大日下王*; he cut off the head of the napping emperor with a sword lying nearby and fled to the house of 都夫良意富美*. It is recorded that the emperor died at the age of fifty-six.
  9. 9Emperor Yūryaku with 若日下部王, 赤猪子 and the Maiden of Yoshino (readings unconfirmed)引田部赤猪子* waited eighty years for the emperor’s words, “I will send for you in time,” but the emperor had forgotten them completely. The emperor was astonished and pitied her, but, held back by her great age, he did not marry her; he gave her songs and many gifts and sent her home.
  10. 10Hitokotonushi-no-Kami of Katsuragi and the Songs of Emperor YūryakuOn Mount Katsuragi he met a company with the same attire and the same number of people as the emperor’s, and when he asked its name, the other named himself “Hitokotonushi-no-Kami of Katsuragi (葛城之一言主大神)”. The emperor, filled with awe, presented swords, bows and arrows and the clothing of his officials, and the Great Deity escorted the emperor as far as the entrance to the mountains of 長谷*.
  11. 11Emperor Seinei and 意祁命 and 袁祁命 (readings unconfirmed): the Song Gathering with 志毘臣*At the house-warming banquet of 志自牟* in Harima Province (針間国), the younger brother 袁祁命*, who had been working as a boy tending the fire, declared his identity while dancing, and it became known that they were sons of 押歯王 of 市辺 (readings unconfirmed). 山部連小楯*, astonished, sheltered the two and sent word to the capital.
  12. 12Emperor Kenzō and Emperor Ninken: 置目老媼* and Their Father’s TombEmperor Kenzō (袁祁之石巣別命)*, on the offer of an old woman of Ōmi, found the remains of his father, 市辺王*, built a tomb on the mountain east of 蚊屋野* and buried him there. The old woman was given the name 置目老媼*, lived beside the palace, and was summoned by a bell (鐸) hung at the door of the great hall.
  13. 13From Emperor Buretsu to Empress SuikoEmperor Buretsu (小長谷若雀命)* had no crown prince, and after his death 袁本杼命 * (Emperor Keitai), a fifth-generation descendant of Emperor 品太*, was brought from 近淡海国*, married to 手白髪命*, and given the realm.

Key points of every story

For review: the first two key points of each story.

Part 1, story 1: The Beginning of Heaven and Earth

  • The first deity to appear in the Kojiki is Ame-no-Minakanushi-no-Kami, who came into being in the High Plain of Heaven (高天原). Next Takamimusubi-no-Kami and Kamumusuhi-no-Kami came into being, and all three, as solitary deities (独神), hid their forms.
  • The five deities from Ame-no-Minakanushi-no-Kami through Umashiashikabihikoji-no-Kami and 天之常立神* are regarded as the "Separate Heavenly Deities" (別天神), and they are written about separately from the Seven Generations of the Age of the Gods that follow.

Part 1, story 2: The Birth of the Land

  • The heavenly deities commanded Izanagi-no-Mikoto and Izanami-no-Mikoto to put the land in order and make it firm, and gave them the spear 天沼矛*. When the two stirred the sea with the spear from the bridge 天浮橋*, the brine that dripped from the tip of the spear piled up and Onogoro-shima was formed.
  • At first, because Izanami-no-Mikoto spoke first, the child born, Hiruko, was set adrift in a reed boat, and Awashima was not counted among their children either. Through the heavenly deities' divination (太占) it became clear that this was "because the woman spoke first", and they started over with Izanagi-no-Mikoto speaking first.

Part 1, story 3: The Birth of the Deities and the Fire Deity

  • Because Izanami-no-Mikoto gave birth to the fire deity 火之夜芸速男神 * (also called 火之迦具土神), her private parts were burned, she fell ill, and at last she departed this world (神避). Even during her illness, deities such as 金山毗古神*, 波迩夜須毗売神*, Mitsuhanome-no-Kami and Wakumusubi-no-Kami came into being from her vomit, feces and urine.
  • The grieving Izanagi-no-Mikoto cut off the head of his child Kagutsuchi-no-Kami with a ten-fist sword (十拳剣). From the blood on the sword came eight deities, including 建御雷之男神*, and from the body of Kagutsuchi-no-Kami came eight mountain deities (山津見神), such as Masakayamatsumi-no-Kami. The name of that sword is Ame-no-Ohabari (also 伊都之尾羽張)*.

Part 1, story 4: The Land of Yomi

  • Izanagi-no-Mikoto broke Izanami-no-Mikoto's request, "Do not look at me while I talk with the deities of Yomi (黄泉神)," lit a light and looked, and so came to be pursued by Izanami-no-Mikoto, who had been put to shame.
  • After 黄泉比良坂* was blocked with a rock that would take a thousand men to pull (千引石), Izanami-no-Mikoto said, "I will kill a thousand people a day," and Izanagi-no-Mikoto replied, "I will build fifteen hundred birthing huts a day." The Kojiki tells this as the origin of why, each day, a thousand people surely die and fifteen hundred are born.

Part 1, story 5: Purification and the Three Noble Children

  • When Izanagi-no-Mikoto washed his left eye in the purification (禊), Amaterasu-Ōmikami was born; with his right eye, Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto; and with his nose, Susanoo-no-Mikoto (written 建速須佐之男命). Izanagi-no-Mikoto rejoiced greatly, saying, "At the last I have obtained three noble children." These are the "Three Noble Children" (三貴子).
  • Izanagi-no-Mikoto gave Amaterasu-Ōmikami the jewels of his necklace (Mikuratana-no-Kami) and entrusted her with ruling the High Plain of Heaven (高天原); he entrusted Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto with ruling the realm of the night (夜の食国), and Susanoo-no-Mikoto (written 建速須佐之男命) with ruling the sea plain.

Part 1, story 6: The Pledge at the Heavenly River 天の安の河*

  • Amaterasu-Ōmikami suspected that Susanoo-no-Mikoto had come to seize her land and met him armed; so that Susanoo-no-Mikoto could show the purity of his heart, the two deities made a pledge (誓約) across the river 天安河* and each gave birth to children from the other's belongings.
  • Amaterasu-Ōmikami gave birth to three female deities from Susanoo-no-Mikoto's ten-fist sword (十拳剣), and Susanoo-no-Mikoto gave birth to five male deities, including Ame-no-Oshihomimi-no-Mikoto, from Amaterasu-Ōmikami's jewels; Amaterasu-Ōmikami decided, according to whose belongings they came from, that the male deities were her children and the female deities were Susanoo-no-Mikoto's children.

Part 1, story 7: The Heavenly Rock-Cave Door

  • When Amaterasu-Ōmikami closed the Heavenly Rock-Cave Door (天石屋戸) and shut herself in, both the High Plain of Heaven (高天原) and the Central Land of Reed Plains (葦原中国) became pitch dark, night went on and on, and every kind of calamity arose.
  • The myriad deities gathered at the riverbed of the heavenly river 天の安の河*, had Omoikane-no-Kami (思金神) draw up a plan, had 伊斯許理度売命* make a mirror and Tamanooya-no-Mikoto make strings of curved jewels (勾玉), hung them on the branches of a sakaki tree (榊), and prayed.

Part 1, story 8: The Banishment of Susanoo-no-Mikoto and Yamata-no-Orochi

  • Susanoo-no-Mikoto made Yamata-no-Orochi, which had eight heads and eight tails, drink strong sake, and when it was drunk and asleep he cut it to death with his ten-fist sword (十拳剣) and saved 櫛名田比売*.
  • When he cut the middle tail of the serpent, the blade of his sword was chipped, and a great sword was found inside the tail. Susanoo-no-Mikoto presented it to Amaterasu-Ōmikami, and this is Kusanagi-no-Tachi.

Part 1, story 9: The Hare (素兎) of 稲羽*

  • 大穴牟遅神 * (Ōkuninushi-no-Kami) taught the hare, which was suffering after being skinned, to wash its body in the water of the river mouth and roll about on kama-no-hana (蒲黄) spread out beneath it, and the hare's body was healed as it had been before.
  • The hare tricked the wani (和迩) by proposing a contest to compare the numbers of their kin, and crossed from 淤岐嶋* to the cape of 気多* by stepping on their backs; but just before it finished crossing it spoke of having tricked them, so it was caught by the wani and had its fur stripped off.

Part 1, story 10: The Persecution by the Eighty Deities and the Land 根の堅州国*

  • As 大穴牟遲神* fled from 根堅州國*, Susanoo-no-Mikoto called out to him from 黄泉比良坂*: "Drive away your brothers with the living sword (生大刀) and the living bow and arrows (生弓矢), become Ōkuninushi-no-Kami and 宇都志國主神*, make Suseri-bime (須世理毗賣) your principal wife, build a palace at the foot of 宇迦能山*, and live there." With that sword and bow 大穴牟遲神 drove away the Eighty Deities and for the first time made the land.
  • Susanoo-no-Mikoto set 大穴牟遲神* one ordeal after another: the chamber of snakes, the chamber of centipedes and bees, the field set on fire, and the picking of centipedes from his head. With the help of Suseri-bime's scarves (比礼), the tree nuts and red earth, and a mouse, 大穴牟遲神 got through them all.

Part 1, story 11: The Songs of 八千矛神* and Ōkuninushi-no-Kami's Making of the Land

  • Sukunahikona-no-Kami (少名毗古那神), who came from the sea riding in the boat 天之羅摩船*, was revealed by Kuebiko to be a child of Kamumusuhi-no-Kami, and, at Kamumusuhi-no-Kami's command, made and firmed up the land side by side with 大穴牟遲 * (Ōkuninushi-no-Kami). Later Sukunahikona-no-Kami crossed over to 常世國*.
  • After Sukunahikona-no-Kami left, a deity who approached lighting up the sea said, "If you worship me, I will make the land with you," and asked to be worshipped on the mountain to the east of the green fence of mountains (青垣) of Yamato (倭). The Kojiki records this as "the deity who dwells on 御諸山*" and does not write its name here.

Part 1, story 12: Ame-no-Wakahiko (天若日子)

  • As envoys to bring the Central Land of the Reed Plains into submission, Ame-no-Hohi-no-Kami (天菩比神) was sent first, but he fawned on Ōkuninushi-no-Kami and made no report for three years. Ame-no-Wakahiko, who was sent next, also married Shitateru-hime (下照比売), tried to make the land his own, and made no report for eight years.
  • On the advice of Ame-no-Sagume (天佐具売), Ame-no-Wakahiko shot dead the pheasant Nakime (鳴女), who had come to ask him why. 高木神* threw that arrow back, saying “If he has an evil heart, let it strike him,” and the arrow struck Ame-no-Wakahiko in the chest as he lay asleep, and he died. The Kojiki presents this as the origin of the “returning arrow” (還矢) and of the “pheasant's one-way errand” (雉之頓使).

Part 1, story 13: The Ceding of the Land

  • As envoy for the ceding of the land, Takemikazuchi-no-Kami, son of 伊都之尾羽張神*, was sent with Ame-no-Torifune-no-Kami as his companion. The two deities stood a ten-fist sword (十掬剣) upside down on the crest of a wave on the small beach of 伊耶佐* in Izumo, sat on its point, and demanded that Ōkuninushi-no-Kami cede the land.
  • Ōkuninushi-no-Kami left the answer to his son. Kotoshironushi-no-Kami consented, saying “I will give this land to the child of the heavenly deities,” then trod on his boat to tip it over, clapped his hands in the reverse “heavenly” manner (天逆手), and hid inside a fence of green brushwood (青柴垣).

Part 1, story 14: The Descent of the Heavenly Grandson

  • Amaterasu-Ōmikami and 高木神* at first commanded Ame-no-Oshihomimi-no-Mikoto to go down, but while he was making ready his son Ninigi-no-Mikoto was born, so at Ame-no-Oshihomimi-no-Mikoto's request it was decided that Ninigi-no-Mikoto would go down in his place.
  • At the Eightfold Crossroads of Heaven (天八衢), there was a deity shining on both Takamanohara and the Central Land of the Reed Plains. When Ame-no-Uzume-no-Kami questioned him, he gave his name as Sarutahiko-no-Kami, an earthly deity, and answered that he had come to meet the Heavenly Grandson in order to guide him.

Part 1, story 15: Konohanasakuya-hime and Iwanagahime

  • Because Ninigi-no-Mikoto sent back the elder sister Iwanagahime as ugly and took only the younger sister Konohanasakuya-hime as his wife, the lives of the children of the heavenly deity became as fleeting as the blossoms of trees, just as Ōyamatsumi-no-Kami had said. The Kojiki says that this is why, even now, the lives of the emperors are not long.
  • Konohanasakuya-hime, suspected by Ninigi-no-Mikoto because she had conceived in a single night, said, “If it is the child of the heavenly deity, it will be born safely,” entered a hall with no door, set it on fire and gave birth, bearing three children: Hoderi-no-Mikoto, Hosuseri-no-Mikoto and 火遠理命*.

Part 1, story 16: Umisachi and Yamasachi (海幸山幸)

  • 火遠理命 * (Yamasachibiko) exchanged tools with his elder brother Hoderi-no-Mikoto (Umisachibiko), but lost the fishhook in the sea. The elder brother would not accept even a thousand fishhooks made from a sword and demanded the original fishhook, so 火遠理命 set out for the palace of Watatsumi-no-Kami in the small boat of Shiotsuchi-no-Kami.
  • At the sea deity's palace, 火遠理命* married Toyotama-hime and lived there for three years. The sea deity, hearing the reason for his coming, gathered the fish of the sea and found the fishhook in the 赤海鯽魚*, a fish whose throat was sore.

Part 1, story 17: The Birth of Ugayafukiaezu-no-Mikoto

  • Toyotama-hime asked him “Do not look,” but when 火遠理命* peeked, she was crawling and writhing in the form of an eight-fathom wani (八尋和迩), so he fled. Ashamed, Toyotama-hime left the child behind, closed off the sea boundary (海坂) and returned to the land of the sea.
  • Because he was born before the roof of the birth hall on the seashore, being thatched with cormorant feathers, was finished, the child was named 天津日高日子波限建鵜葺草葺不合命 (Ugayafukiaezu-no-Mikoto).

Part 2, story 1: Emperor Jinmu's Eastern Expedition (from Hyūga to Kumano and Yoshino)

  • Itsuse-no-Mikoto was wounded in the hand by an arrow of 登美毘古* and, saying that “it was wrong for the child of the sun deity to fight facing the sun,” chose a route circling round to the south so as to have the sun at their backs. On the way he died at the 男之水門* in the land of Kii (紀国), and it is recorded that his tomb is at 竈山*.
  • In Kumano, when the party had fallen prostrate, the violent deities were cut down by a sword presented by 高倉下*, and the troops awoke. This sword had been sent down by Takemikazuchi-no-Kami at the command of Amaterasu-Ōmikami (天照大神) and 高木神*, and a note in the original text records its names, such as Futsunomitama (布都御魂), and that it is at Isonokami Shrine (石上神宮).

Part 2, story 2: Uda, 忍坂, 登美毘古 and the Kume Songs (readings unconfirmed)

  • 兄宇迦斯* of Uda pretended to submit and set a crushing trap (押機) in a hall, but it was exposed through the secret report of 弟宇迦斯*; driven in by 道臣命 and 大久米命 (readings unconfirmed), he died in his own trap. That place is called the Blood Field of Uda (宇陀之血原).
  • In the great pit-dwelling (大室) at 忍坂*, each of the Eighty Braves (八十建) was entertained with a cook-servant (膳夫) bearing a sword assigned to him, and, as signalled by the order “When you hear the song, cut them down all at once,” they were struck down at one stroke in time with the song.

Part 2, story 3: Nigihayahi-no-Mikoto Comes to Serve, and the Choice of an Empress

  • Nigihayahi-no-Mikoto came down from heaven following the Child of the Heavenly Deities, presented heavenly tokens (天津瑞) and served him. Umashimaji-no-Mikoto (宇摩志麻遲命), born to him and the younger sister of 登美毘古*, is regarded as the ancestor of the Mononobe no Muraji (物部連), the 穂積臣* and the 婇臣*.
  • After subduing the violent deities and the people who would not submit, the Child of the Heavenly Deities ruled the realm from the Kashihara palace (白檮原宮) at Unebi (畝火).

Part 2, story 4: The Rebellion of Tagishimimi-no-Mikoto

  • When Emperor Jinmu died, Tagishimimi-no-Mikoto, the elder half-brother born in Hyūga, took the empress 伊須氣余理比賣* as his wife and plotted to kill her children, his three younger half-brothers. 伊須氣余理比賣 warned the princes of the danger in songs.
  • The elder brother Kamuyaimimi-no-Mikoto trembled hand and foot and could not kill Tagishimimi-no-Mikoto, so the younger brother 神沼河耳命* took the weapon from his elder brother and struck him down. Because of this deed, 神沼河耳命 was also called 建沼河耳命*.

Part 2, story 5: From Emperor Suizei to Emperor Kaika

  • The accounts of these eight reigns center on genealogy, recording palaces, wives and children, the ancestors of lineages, ages and tombs, and there is hardly any narrative. Among them, the section on Emperor Kōrei records that 大吉備津日子命 (Ōkibitsuhiko-no-Mikoto) and 若建吉備津日子命* pacified the land of Kibi, using Harima (針間) as the entrance.
  • The section on Emperor Kōgen records that Takeuchi-no-Sukune (建内宿禰) was born as the son of 比古布都押之信命*, and that his nine children became the ancestors of many lineages, such as the 波多臣, the 許勢臣, the Soga no Omi (蘇我臣), the 平群臣 and the 木臣 (readings unconfirmed).

Part 2, story 6: Emperor Sujin and Ōmononushi-no-Ōkami of Miwa

  • In the time of Emperor Sujin, when people were on the verge of dying out from pestilence, Ōmononushi-no-Ōkami declared in a dream, “This comes from my will,” and asked to be worshipped by 意富多多泥古*. When the emperor sought out 意富多多泥古 from the village of 美努* in Kawachi and had him worship as the officiating priest at Mount 御諸*, the pestilence ceased.
  • A hemp thread was stuck into the garment of the man who visited 活玉依毘賣* night after night, and when the thread was followed, it led to the shrine of the deity of Mount Miwa (美和山). It is recorded that because three loops (三勾) of hemp were left over, that place is called “Miwa” (美和).

Part 2, story 7: The Dispatch along the Four Circuits and the Rebellion of 建波邇安王*

  • The emperor interpreted the maiden's song that Ōbiko-no-Mikoto had heard at 幣羅坂* as “a sign that my elder half-brother 建波邇安王* has formed an evil intent,” and sent Ōbiko-no-Mikoto and 日子國夫玖命* to Yamashiro (山代) to subdue him.
  • In the battle across the 和訶羅河*, the arrow that 建波邇安王* shot first missed, while the arrow of 日子國夫玖命* shot 建波邇安王 dead, and the rebel army collapsed completely.

Part 2, story 8: The Rebellion of 沙本毘古王*

  • Asked by her elder brother 沙本毘古王* "Whom do you love more, your husband or your brother?", the consort 沙本毘賣命* answered "My brother." She was given a cord dagger to kill the emperor, but though she raised it three times she could not stab him, and her falling tears woke the emperor.
  • Asked by the emperor what his dream meant (a sudden shower coming from 沙本* and a small brocade-colored snake coiling around his neck), the consort revealed her brother's entire plot, and the emperor raised an army and surrounded the rice fortress (稻城).

Part 2, story 9: Prince 本牟智和気御子* and 多遅摩毛理*

  • Prince 本牟智和氣御子* did not speak even when his eight-fist-long beard reached his chest. Through the emperor's dream and the great divination (太占) it was found that this was a curse arising from the will of the Great Deity of Izumo, so the prince was sent to worship the Great Deity at Izumo.
  • On the way back from worshipping at Izumo, on the bank of the 肥河*, the prince saw the leafy green "mountain" decoration set up by 岐比佐都美* and spoke for the first time. The emperor rejoiced and had 菟上王* build the shrine of the deity at Izumo.

Part 2, story 10: The Western Campaign of 小碓命* (熊曾建* and Izumo Takeru)

  • Ordered to "泥疑"* his elder brother 大碓命*, 小碓命* seized his brother in the privy, tore off his limbs, wrapped him in a straw mat (薦) and threw him away. The emperor feared his violent heart and sent him to subdue the 熊曾建* in the west.
  • Disguising himself as a young girl by putting on the robe and skirt (御衣御裳) of his aunt 倭比賣命*, 小碓命 slipped into the 熊曾建* brothers' "御室樂"* feast and stabbed the brothers with his sword.

Part 2, story 11: The Eastern Campaign of Yamato Takeru (倭建命)

  • Ordered to pacify the twelve circuits of the east, Yamato Takeru (倭建命) lamented and wept to his aunt 倭比賣命* at Ise, saying, "The emperor has my death in mind," and was given the Kusanagi sword (草那藝劍) and a bag (御嚢).
  • In 相武國*, deceived by the governor (國造) and surrounded by a grass fire, Yamato Takeru cut the grass with his sword, lit a counter-fire with the fire-striker in the bag, escaped, and destroyed the governor and his men. This is regarded as the origin of the place name 燒津*.

Part 2, story 12: The Death of Yamato Takeru (倭建命) and the White Bird

  • Leaving the Kusanagi sword (草那藝劔) with Miyazu-hime (美夜受比賣), Yamato Takeru set out saying he would seize the mountain deity of Ibuki (伊服岐) bare-handed; because he made a declaration (言挙) that a white boar was the deity's messenger, he was struck and confounded by a great icy rain. A small-script note in the original text states that the white boar was in fact the deity itself.
  • Along the route the weakened Yamato Takeru traveled, the origins of place names such as 居寤清泉, 當藝, 杖衝坂 and 三重* are told; at 能煩野* Yamato Takeru composed songs longing for his homeland (思國歌), and when he finished singing he died.

Part 2, story 13: The Death of Emperor Chūai and the Silla Expedition of 息長帯日売命*

  • Emperor Chūai rejected as a "lying deity" the oracle promising to give him a land of treasures in the west, and died on the spot while playing the koto (琴). The deity then announced anew that that land was to be ruled by the child (a boy) in the chief consort's womb.
  • The deity of the oracle revealed its name: it was the will of Amaterasu-Ōmikami (天照大神), and it was the three great deities 底筒男, 中筒男 and 上筒男*. Having brought Silla into submission, the chief consort enshrined the rough spirit (荒御魂) of the Great Deity of 墨江* in Silla as a deity to guard the land.

Part 2, story 14: The Rebellion of Oshikuma-no-Miko (忍熊王) and Kehi-no-Ōkami (気比大神)

  • 息長帯日売命* put her son on a mourning ship (喪船) and made it appear that he had died, deceiving 香坂王* and Oshikuma-no-Miko (忍熊王), who lay in wait. 香坂王 was devoured by an enraged boar in the middle of an oath-hunt (宇気比獦), and Oshikuma-no-Miko, defeated, entered the sea with 伊佐比宿禰* and died.
  • What decided the outcome was the stratagem of 建振熊命*, the general on the crown prince's side. He had it announced that "息長帯日売命 has died" and made a show of cutting his bowstrings, and after the enemy had put away their weapons, he strung the spare strings he had hidden in his hair and attacked.

Part 2, story 15: Emperor Ōjin's Courtships and 髪長比売*

  • Emperor Ōjin asked 大山守命 and 大雀命* which was dearer, an elder child or a younger child, and approved the words of 大雀命, who, grasping the emperor's mind, answered "the younger child." As a result it was decided that 宇遅能和紀郎子* would be the one to inherit the heavenly succession (天津日継), 大山守命 the one to take charge of the government of the mountains and seas, and 大雀命 the one to take charge of the government of the realm (食国).
  • The mother of 宇遅能和紀郎子* is 宮主矢河枝比売* (daughter of 比布礼能意富美 of 丸邇*), whom the emperor met at a fork in the road in the village of 木幡*. The next day the emperor was entertained at her house, accepted a cup, and sang a long song.

Part 2, story 16: The Age of Emperor Ōjin and the Rebellion of 大山守命*

  • When Emperor Ōjin died, 大山守命* defied his father's command, aimed at the realm, and tried to kill his younger brother 宇遅能和紀郎子*. 宇遅能和紀郎子, who had prepared after being warned by 大雀命*, disguised himself as the boatman at the steering oar, tipped the boat, and made 大山守命 fall into the river and sink.
  • 大雀命 and 宇遅能和紀郎子* each yielded the realm to the other, and meanwhile the fishermen (海人) carrying the great tribute offerings (大贄) wept, worn out from going back and forth. Because 宇遅能和紀郎子 died young, 大雀命* came to rule the realm.

Part 2, story 17: Ame-no-Hiboko (天之日矛) and the Brothers of Autumn Mountain and Spring Mountain

  • Ame-no-Hiboko (天之日矛) was the son of the ruler of Silla. He crossed over in pursuit of his wife, who had been born from a red jewel borne by a woman touched by the light of the sun, and who had fled to Naniwa. But he was blocked by the deity of the crossing and could not enter Naniwa, so he stayed in the province of 多遅摩*.
  • Between 多遅摩比多訶 and 由良度美*, descendants of Ame-no-Hiboko (天之日矛), was born 葛城之高額比売命*, and her child is 息長帯比売命* (Empress Jingū). In the Kojiki, 息長帯比売命, who crossed over to Silla, is regarded as a descendant of Ame-no-Hiboko.

Part 3, story 1: The Accession of Emperor Nintoku and the People's Hearths

  • 大雀命 * (Emperor Nintoku) looked over the land from a high mountain and, because no cooking smoke rose, knew that the whole land was poor, and exempted the people from all labor levies and taxes (課役) for three years. During that time, even when the palace fell into disrepair and rain leaked in, he had it left unrepaired and made do by catching the rain in vessels.
  • Seeing that smoke filled the land, the emperor imposed the labor levies and taxes again, but the common people (百姓) prospered and did not suffer from the labor. Therefore this reign was praised as "the reign of the sage emperor" (聖帝世).

Part 3, story 2: The Jealousy of 石之日売命* and 黒日売*

  • The chief consort 石之日売命* was extremely jealous, and 黒日売*, daughter of the 海部直* of Kibi, who had been summoned by the emperor, fled back to her home in fear of the chief consort. Still angry, the chief consort had 黒日売 driven off the boat at 大浦* and made her walk home.
  • The emperor deceived the chief consort, saying, "I wish to view 淡道嶋"*, and set out; after gazing far out from 淡道嶋 and singing, he went from island to island to the province of Kibi and met 黒日売*.

Part 3, story 3: The Chief Consort's Journey to 山代* and the Six Songs

  • While the chief consort 石之日売命* was away in 木国* to gather 御綱柏* leaves, the emperor married 八田若郎女*. Learning of it from a laborer's (仕丁) talk on the boat home, the chief consort threw all the 御綱柏 into the sea (the origin of the name 御津前* for that place), and without entering the palace went up the 堀江 canal toward 山代*.
  • The emperor's messenger, 丸邇臣口子*, in heavy rain, lay prostrate in the courtyard immersed in water up to his waist, and his blue-printed robe (青摺衣) was dyed red by the color of its crimson cords. His younger sister 口日売*, who served the chief consort, saw this and sang a song.

Part 3, story 4: 八田若郎女, 女鳥王 and 速総別王 (readings unconfirmed)

  • Using his younger brother 速総別王* as a go-between, the emperor sought 女鳥王*, but she refused, saying, “Because the chief consort is so strong, the emperor has not even taken 八田若郎女* as his wife,” and she became the wife of 速総別王. 速総別王 gave the emperor no reply.
  • When the emperor visited 女鳥王, she was weaving at her loom. Learning her feelings through an exchange of songs, the emperor went back for the time being, but when he heard the song 女鳥王 sang when 速総別王 came to her, he immediately raised an army.

Part 3, story 5: The Song of the Wild Goose’s Eggs and the Ship 枯野*

  • In a song, the emperor asked Takeuchi-no-Sukune-no-Mikoto (建内宿禰) about a wild goose having laid eggs on 日女嶋*, and Takeuchi-no-Sukune answered in a song. The song he then sang after being granted the emperor’s koto is described as “the 片歌 of the 本岐歌” (readings unconfirmed).
  • The tall tree west of the 免寸河 river* was so large that its shadow in the morning sun reached Awaji Island (淡道嶋) and its shadow in the evening sun crossed Mount 高安山*. The fast ship made from this tree was “枯野”*, and morning and evening it carried cold spring water from Awaji Island as drinking water for the emperor (大御水).

Part 3, story 6: Emperor Richū, Emperor Hanzei and the Revolt of 墨江中王*

  • At the banquet (豊明) of the Great Thanksgiving (大嘗) at the Naniwa palace (難波宮), while 伊邪本和気命 * (Emperor Richū) lay asleep drunk on sake, his younger brother 墨江中王* set fire to the great hall, intending to kill the emperor. 阿知直*, ancestor of the 倭漢直*, put the emperor on a horse and sent him away to Yamato (倭).
  • At the entrance to Mount 大坂山*, told by a woman, the emperor went round by the 当岐麻道 road* and entered Isonokami Shrine (石上神宮). He suspected his younger brother 水歯別命*, who came there, and ordered him to kill 墨江中王* before coming to him.

Part 3, story 7: Emperor Ingyō, Crown Prince 軽 and 軽大郎女 (readings unconfirmed)

  • 男浅津間若子宿禰命 * (Emperor Ingyō) once declined to take the throne because of a long illness, but at the earnest request of the chief consort and the ministers, he ruled the realm. The chief envoy 金波鎮漢紀武*, sent by the ruler of Silla (新良), cured the emperor’s illness with his knowledge of medicine.
  • Troubled by the confusion in clan names and titles (氏姓), the emperor set up 玖訶瓮 vessels* at 言八十禍津日前 in 味白檮 (readings unconfirmed) and fixed the clan names and titles of the many hereditary groups (八十友緒) of the realm.

Part 3, story 8: Emperor Ankō and the Incident of 目弱王*

  • The seven-year-old 目弱王* overheard words saying that it was Emperor Ankō who had killed his father, 大日下王*; he cut off the head of the napping emperor with a sword lying nearby and fled to the house of 都夫良意富美*. It is recorded that the emperor died at the age of fifty-six.
  • The affair began with the theft and slander of the messenger 根臣*. 根臣 stole the jewelled headdress (玉縵) that was a gift of courtesy and falsely reported that 大日下王* would not obey the command, so the emperor killed 大日下王 and made his wife, 長田大郎女*, empress.

Part 3, story 9: Emperor Yūryaku with 若日下部王, 赤猪子 and the Maiden of Yoshino (readings unconfirmed)

  • 引田部赤猪子* waited eighty years for the emperor’s words, “I will send for you in time,” but the emperor had forgotten them completely. The emperor was astonished and pitied her, but, held back by her great age, he did not marry her; he gave her songs and many gifts and sent her home.
  • The great district chief (大県主) of 志幾* raised katsuogi ridge-logs (堅魚木) on his roof in imitation of the emperor’s palace, so his house was nearly burned; he was pardoned after presenting a white dog. The emperor made that dog his courtship gift to 若日下部王*.

Part 3, story 10: Hitokotonushi-no-Kami of Katsuragi and the Songs of Emperor Yūryaku

  • On Mount Katsuragi he met a company with the same attire and the same number of people as the emperor’s, and when he asked its name, the other named himself “Hitokotonushi-no-Kami of Katsuragi (葛城之一言主大神)”. The emperor, filled with awe, presented swords, bows and arrows and the clothing of his officials, and the Great Deity escorted the emperor as far as the entrance to the mountains of 長谷*.
  • In the Nihon Shoki (the 4th year of Emperor Yūryaku, spring, 2nd month), the deity 一事主神* appears as a “tall man” (長人) resembling the emperor, hunts side by side with the emperor and escorts him as far as 来目水*, and the people are said to have called him “an emperor of virtue” (有徳天皇).

Part 3, story 11: Emperor Seinei and 意祁命 and 袁祁命 (readings unconfirmed): the Song Gathering with 志毘臣*

  • At the house-warming banquet of 志自牟* in Harima Province (針間国), the younger brother 袁祁命*, who had been working as a boy tending the fire, declared his identity while dancing, and it became known that they were sons of 押歯王 of 市辺 (readings unconfirmed). 山部連小楯*, astonished, sheltered the two and sent word to the capital.
  • Emperor Seinei (白髪大倭根子命)* had neither empress nor children, and the 白髪部* was established as his namesake group (御名代). It is recorded that after his death there was no prince to succeed him, and that 飯豊王*, younger sister of 市辺忍歯別王*, was at the 高木角刺宮 palace at 忍海 in Katsuragi (readings unconfirmed).

Part 3, story 12: Emperor Kenzō and Emperor Ninken: 置目老媼* and Their Father’s Tomb

  • Emperor Kenzō (袁祁之石巣別命)*, on the offer of an old woman of Ōmi, found the remains of his father, 市辺王*, built a tomb on the mountain east of 蚊屋野* and buried him there. The old woman was given the name 置目老媼*, lived beside the palace, and was summoned by a bell (鐸) hung at the door of the great hall.
  • The emperor tried to destroy the tomb of Emperor 大長谷 (大長谷天皇)*, his father’s enemy, but his elder brother 意祁命* dug only a little at its side and remonstrated that he was their uncle and also an emperor who had ruled the realm. The emperor too acknowledged this as reasonable.

Part 3, story 13: From Emperor Buretsu to Empress Suiko

  • Emperor Buretsu (小長谷若雀命)* had no crown prince, and after his death 袁本杼命 * (Emperor Keitai), a fifth-generation descendant of Emperor 品太*, was brought from 近淡海国*, married to 手白髪命*, and given the realm.
  • In the reign of Emperor Keitai, because 竺紫君石井* did not obey the emperor’s commands and was often disrespectful, 物部荒甲之大連 and 大伴之金村連 (readings unconfirmed) were sent and killed 石井.
