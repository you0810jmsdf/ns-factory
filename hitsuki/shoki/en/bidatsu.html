Nihon Shoki, Book 20 — Emperor Bidatsu · page 36 of 50

Emperor Bidatsu, the Koguryŏ Letter, and the Beginnings of the Buddhist Dharma

敏達天皇と高麗の国書・仏法の始まり

Emperor Bidatsu was the second son of Emperor Kinmei, and it is recorded that he did not believe in the Buddhist Dharma and was fond of literature and history. Book 20 records, year by year, 王辰爾*, who deciphered a letter from Koguryŏ; 日羅*, who was summoned from Paekche to help restore Mimana and was killed; the promotion of the Buddhist Dharma by Soga no Umako and its destruction by Mononobe no Moriya; and the emperor's death and the conflict at the site of his temporary enshrinement.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textAn entry before his accession says that the emperor did not believe in the Buddhist Dharma and loved literature and history. The conflict over the Buddhist Dharma in this book takes place in this emperor's reign.
  2. Main textIn the 5th month of the 1st year, none of the scribes could read a memorial from Koguryŏ even after three days, and only 王辰爾*, ancestor of the 船史*, deciphered it. It says that the characters written on crow feathers were copied onto silk by steaming the feathers over the steam of cooking rice.
  3. Main textIn the 13th year, Umako received a stone image of Maitreya and other images that had come from Paekche; taking as teacher the former monk 恵便*, who had returned to lay life and was found in Harima, he had 嶋*, daughter of Shiba Tatto, and others take holy orders (as the nun Zenshin), and built a Buddha hall. The main text records "佛法之初自而作".
  4. Main textIn the 14th year, citing the spread of an epidemic, Mononobe no Moriya and Nakatomi no Katsumi petitioned that the Buddhist Dharma be stopped; Moriya cut down the pagoda and burned it, and threw the Buddha images that survived the fire into the canal (堀江) at Naniwa. In the 6th month, Umako alone was permitted to practice the Buddhist Dharma.
  5. Main textIn the 12th year, the emperor summoned 日羅*, a Paekche official of the rank 達率*, from Paekche to consult about restoring Mimana. 日羅 advised not to hurry into war but to make the people prosperous, but he was killed by 徳爾* and others who had accompanied him.
  6. Compared with the KojikiThe section on Emperor Bidatsu in the Kojiki gives his palace as the "他田宮" and records only that he ruled the realm for fourteen years, together with his empresses and consorts, children and tomb; entries on 王辰爾*, 日羅* or the Buddhist Dharma could not be confirmed there.

What the main text says

Emperor Bidatsu (渟中倉太珠敷天皇*) was the second son of 天國排開廣庭天皇*, and his mother was Empress 石姫*. He became Crown Prince in the 29th year, and in the 4th month of the 32nd year 天國排開廣庭天皇 died. He ascended the throne in the 4th month of the 1st year, set up his palace at 百済大井*, kept Mononobe no Yuge no Moriya as Great Chieftain, and made Soga no Umako no Sukune Great Minister. In the 5th month, when none of the scribes could read the memorial from Koguryŏ, 王辰爾* deciphered it; he was praised by the emperor and the Great Minister and came to serve in close attendance within the palace. In the 6th month, the deputy envoys of Koguryŏ killed their ambassador and falsely claimed it was because the ambassador had refused a wife given to him by the emperor.

In the 5th month of the 2nd year, Koguryŏ envoys reached the coast of Koshi, but their ship broke up and many drowned; the court, suspicious that they kept losing their way, sent them home without entertaining them and had 吉備海部直難波* escort them. In the 7th month, 難波* set out after exchanging people between the two ships, but, fearing the waves, he threw two of the Koguryŏ men into the sea. On his return in the 8th month, 難波 reported that his ship had been blocked by a whale, but the emperor saw through the lie and did not let him return to his province. In the 7th month of the 3rd year, Koguryŏ envoys asked why the escort had not come, so the emperor held 難波 guilty of deceiving the court and of drowning the envoys of a neighboring country. In the 10th month, Umako was sent to the province of Kibi to enlarge the 白猪屯倉* estate and its field laborers, and the register of the field laborers was given to 白猪史膽津*.

In the 1st month of the 4th year, 広姫*, daughter of Prince 息長眞手王*, was made empress, and 押坂彦人大兄皇子* and others were born. In this year, after divination, a palace was built at 譯語田* and called the 幸玉宮*. In the 11th month 広姫 died, and in the 3rd month of the 5th year 豐御食炊屋姫尊* was made empress. In the 11th month of the 6th year, the king of Paekche entrusted to Prince 大別王* and others sutras and treatises and six people — a vinaya master, a meditation master, a nun, a spell-master, a maker of Buddha images and a temple builder — as a gift; they were placed in the temple of Prince 大別王 in Naniwa. In the intercalary 2nd month of the 10th year, 綾糟* and the other chiefs of the Emishi who had raided the frontier went down into the river at 泊瀬*, sipped the water facing 三諸岳*, and swore that they and their descendants would serve with pure hearts.

In the 7th month of the 12th year, to plan the restoration of Mimana that his father had been unable to accomplish, the emperor sought to summon 日羅*, holder of the rank 達率*, who was in Paekche and was the son of 阿利斯登*, Kuni-no-Miyatsuko of 火葦北*. The king of Paekche, unwilling to lose 日羅, would not let him go, but when 吉備海部直羽嶋* pressed hard as 日羅 himself had advised, 日羅 came to the court. 日羅 said that the people should first be made prosperous so that food and troops were sufficient, and that many ships should then be built to confront Paekche; he also advised preparing against Paekche's scheme to obtain Tsukushi. However, 徳爾* and others, incited by the 恩率* and 参官* of Paekche, killed 日羅 on the last day of the 12th month. 日羅 came back to life once, said that this was the work of the slaves he employed and not of Silla, and died. 徳爾 and the others were handed over to 葦北君* and others and killed, and 日羅 was reburied at 葦北*.

In the 9th month of the 13th year, 鹿深臣*, who had come from Paekche, had one stone image of Maitreya, and 佐伯連* had one Buddha image. In this year Umako received these two images, had Shiba Tatto and 池辺氷田* search for practitioners, and took as his teacher 恵便* of Koguryŏ, who had returned to lay life in Harima. He had 嶋*, daughter of Shiba Tatto, take holy orders as the nun Zenshin, then had the nuns 禅蔵尼* and 恵善尼* take holy orders, built a Buddha hall to the east of his residence, and enshrined the stone image of Maitreya there. A relic obtained from the food at a Buddhist feast could not be broken even when placed on an iron stand and struck with a hammer, and when thrown into water it floated or sank as one wished, so Umako and the others came to believe deeply in the Buddhist Dharma. In the 2nd month of the 14th year, Umako built a pagoda north of 大野丘* and placed the relic in the top of the pagoda's pillar.

In the 2nd month of the 14th year, Umako fell ill, and the diviner answered that it was a curse of the Buddha-deity worshiped in his father's time; with the emperor's permission he worshiped the stone image and prayed for his life to be prolonged. Around this time an epidemic spread in the country and many people died; in the 3rd month Moriya and Nakatomi no Katsumi petitioned that this was because the Soga had promoted the Buddhist Dharma, and the emperor ordered that the Buddhist Dharma be stopped. Moriya went to the temple himself, cut down the pagoda and burned it, burned the Buddha images and the Buddha hall too, threw the images that survived the fire into the canal (堀江) at Naniwa, and had the nun Zenshin and the others stripped of their robes and flogged at 海石榴市*. The emperor and Moriya also suddenly fell ill with sores; people dying of the sores filled the country, and people whispered among themselves that it was punishment for burning the Buddha images. In the 6th month, when Umako petitioned that without the power of the Three Treasures his illness would not be cured, the emperor permitted Umako alone to practice the Buddhist Dharma and returned the three nuns to him.

In the 8th month of the 14th year, the emperor died in the great hall, and a temporary enshrinement palace was set up at 広瀬*. When Umako, wearing a sword, delivered his eulogy, Moriya laughed that he looked "like a sparrow shot with an arrow." When Moriya delivered his eulogy with his hands and feet trembling, Umako laughed that "it would be good to hang bells on him," and resentment arose between the two. 三輪君逆* had the Hayato guard the enshrinement court, and Prince Anahobe, who sought to take the realm, angrily asked why they served in the court of a dead king and not at the side of a living king.

Variant accounts

After its main text, the Nihon Shoki often sets out other versions, introduced with words such as "a certain book says" (一書曰).

A certain text says (或本云)

In a certain text, those who plotted to destroy the Buddhist Dharma by burning the temple and pagoda and throwing away the Buddha images were three men — the Great Chieftain Mononobe no Yuge no Moriya, 大三輪逆君* and 中臣磐余連* — and Umako opposed them and did not comply. In the main text, those who petitioned that the Buddhist Dharma be stopped were Moriya and Nakatomi no Katsumi.

An old text (舊本)

Regarding the 恩率* and 参官* of Paekche who directed the killing of 日羅*, a note says that the old text treats them as one 恩率 and one 参官.

In the original text

天皇不信佛法而愛文史

The emperor did not believe in the Buddhist Dharma and was fond of literature and history.

辰爾乃蒸羽於飯氣。以帛印羽。悉冩其字

王辰爾* steamed the feathers over the steam of cooking rice, pressed silk against them, and copied all the characters.

佛法之初自而作

The beginning of the Buddhist Dharma arose from this.

如中獵箭之雀烏焉

Moriya's words mocking Umako, who was delivering his eulogy while wearing a sword, as being "like a sparrow shot with an arrow."

Deities and people, and the shrines that enshrine them

  • 渟中倉太珠敷天皇*Emperor Bidatsu. Second son of 天國排開廣庭天皇*; it is recorded that he did not believe in the Buddhist Dharma.
  • Soga no Umako no Sukune (蘇我馬子宿禰)Great Minister. He received Buddha images, had nuns take holy orders, and built a Buddha hall and a pagoda.
  • The Great Chieftain Mononobe no Yuge no Moriya (物部弓削守屋大連)Great Chieftain. Together with Nakatomi no Katsumi he petitioned that the Buddhist Dharma be stopped, and burned the pagoda and the Buddha images.
  • 王辰爾*Ancestor of the 船史*. He deciphered the Koguryŏ memorial that no one else could read.
  • 日羅*Son of 阿利斯登*, Kuni-no-Miyatsuko of 火葦北*, and a Paekche official of the rank 達率*. He was summoned to consult on restoring Mimana and was killed by 徳爾* and others.
  • 吉備海部直難波*Escort for the Koguryŏ envoys. He threw two of the Koguryŏ men into the sea, made a false report, and was held guilty.
  • 吉備海部直羽嶋*The envoy who was sent to Paekche and brought back 日羅*.
  • Shiba Tatto (司馬達等)A 鞍部村主*. He searched for practitioners and had his daughter 嶋* take holy orders. He obtained a relic at a Buddhist feast and presented it to Umako.
  • The nun Zenshin (善信尼)嶋*, daughter of Shiba Tatto. She took holy orders at the age of eleven, and on Moriya's orders was stripped of her robes and flogged.
  • 恵便* of Koguryŏ (高麗惠便)A former monk who had returned to lay life in Harima. He became Umako's teacher.
  • Nakatomi no Katsumi (中臣勝海)A high official (大夫) who, together with Moriya, petitioned that the epidemic was caused by the promotion of the Buddhist Dharma.
  • 廣姫*Daughter of Prince 息長眞手王*. She became empress in the 4th year, gave birth to 押坂彦人大兄皇子*, and died in the same year.
  • 豐御食炊屋姫尊*She became empress in the 5th year. Later Empress Suiko.
  • Prince Anahobe (穴穂部皇子)The prince who, at the court of the emperor's temporary enshrinement, spoke angry words in his desire to take the realm.

In the Kojiki

Kojiki, Part 3 — opening of the section on Emperor Bidatsu (沼名倉太玉敷命*)

The Kojiki gives his palace as the 他田宮* and records that he ruled the realm for fourteen years. The Nihon Shoki says that he set up his palace at 百済大井* in the 1st year and built the 幸玉宮* palace at 譯語田* in the 4th year, and places his death in the 8th month of the 14th year.

坐他田宮。治天下壹拾肆歲也
Kojiki, Part 3 — entry on the empresses, consorts and children in the section on Emperor Bidatsu

The Kojiki lists first his half-sister 豐御食炊屋比賣命* and gives her eight children, whereas the Nihon Shoki says that 広姫* was made empress in the 4th year and, after her death, 豐御食炊屋姫尊* was made empress in the 5th year, and gives her children as two sons and five daughters.

娶庶妹。豐御食炊屋比賣命
Kojiki, Part 3 — end of the section on Emperor Bidatsu (tomb)

The section on Emperor Bidatsu in the Kojiki ends with his empresses and consorts, children and tomb; for the entries on 王辰爾*, 日羅*, and the promotion and destruction of the Buddhist Dharma, no corresponding entries could be confirmed in the Kojiki.
