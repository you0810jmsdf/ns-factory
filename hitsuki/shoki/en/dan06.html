Nihon Shoki, Book 1 — Age of the Gods (part 1) · page 4 of 50

Susanoo-no-Mikoto's Ascent to Heaven and the Pledge (Section 6)

素戔嗚尊の昇天と誓約（第六段）

Susanoo-no-Mikoto ascends to heaven to see his elder sister before going to the Root Land, and Amaterasu-Ōmikami, suspecting that he has come to seize her land, meets him armed. The two deities make a pledge (ukei) and produce children; the five male deities born from the jewels are determined to be Amaterasu-Ōmikami's children, and the three goddesses born from the sword to be Susanoo-no-Mikoto's children.

Read in the Kojiki: The Pledge at the Heavenly River 天の安の河* ›

Key points to remember

  1. Main textIn the main text, Susanoo-no-Mikoto himself proposes the terms of the pledge, “if a female is born, an impure heart; if a male is born, a pure heart,” and Amaterasu-Ōmikami produces three goddesses from Susanoo-no-Mikoto's ten-span sword, while Susanoo-no-Mikoto produces five male deities from Amaterasu-Ōmikami's string of Yasakani jewels (八坂瓊之御統).
  2. Main textThe children's affiliation is determined by the owner of the “original object” (物根). Since the jewels were Amaterasu-Ōmikami's, the five male deities are regarded as Amaterasu-Ōmikami's children, and since the sword was Susanoo-no-Mikoto's, the three goddesses are regarded as Susanoo-no-Mikoto's children; the three goddesses are recorded as the deities worshipped by the 胸肩君* and others of Tsukushi.
  3. Variant accountThe three variant accounts each differ in the terms of the pledge, the objects used, and the names and order of the deities born. In variant accounts 1 and 3, Amaterasu-Ōmikami eats her own swords (of ten, nine, and eight spans) to produce the three goddesses, and in variant account 3 Susanoo-no-Mikoto produces six male deities.
  4. Variant accountIn variant account 1 the three goddesses are sent down to the island of Tsukushi (筑紫洲) and commanded to stay “on the way” (道中) and assist the Heavenly Grandson; variant account 3 records that they descended to 宇佐嶋* in the Central Land of Reed Plains (葦原中国), and that they are now on the 海北道中 (the way north across the sea) and are called 道主貴*.
  5. CommentaryThe deity-name database of Kokugakuin University tabulates how the names and birth order of the three goddesses differ between the main text and each variant account, and introduces a theory that sees the command in variant account 1 to “assist the Heavenly Grandson” as reflecting a history in which rituals for the sea route to the Korean Peninsula were held in high regard. [source, Japanese]
  6. Compared with the KojikiIn the Kojiki, Susanoo-no-Mikoto (須佐之男命) does not state terms in advance, and declares his own victory on the grounds that the children born were “graceful maidens” (手弱女). Terms like those of the main text of the Nihon Shoki, “if male, a pure heart,” are not found in the Kojiki.

What the main text says

Susanoo-no-Mikoto asked to be allowed, before going to the Root Land, to go for a while to the High Plain of Heaven to see his elder sister and then depart forever; this was permitted, and he ascended to heaven. After this, Izanagi-no-Mikoto, his work completed, built a hidden palace (幽宮) on the island of Awaji (淡路洲) and quietly withdrew there. As another version, it is also recorded that he ascended to heaven to report and remained in the Little Palace of the Sun (日之少宮, wakamiya).

When Susanoo-no-Mikoto ascended, the sea heaved and the mountains roared. Amaterasu-Ōmikami had long known that her younger brother was violent, and, alarmed that he might intend to seize her land, she bound up her hair into topknots and tied up her skirt to make trousers. She wound the string of five hundred Yasakani jewels (八坂瓊之五百箇御統, misumaru) around her topknots and arms, slung on her back quivers holding a thousand arrows and five hundred arrows, put on a high arm-guard (高鞆), brandished her bow, grasped the hilt of her sword, stamped into the hard ground up to her thighs, and, raising a war cry, questioned him.

Susanoo-no-Mikoto answered that he had never had a black heart, and had come only because he wished to see his elder sister before going to the Root Land at his parents' command. When Amaterasu-Ōmikami asked how he would prove the purity of his heart, Susanoo-no-Mikoto said that he and his sister should make a pledge and produce children, and offered that if what was born was female it would mean an impure heart, and if male a pure heart.

Amaterasu-Ōmikami took Susanoo-no-Mikoto's ten-span sword, broke it into three pieces, rinsed them in the 天真名井*, and crunched them, and from the mist of the breath she blew out were born three goddesses: Takori-hime (田心姫), Takitsu-hime (湍津姫), and Ichikishima-hime (市杵嶋姫). Next Susanoo-no-Mikoto took the string of five hundred Yasakani jewels wound around Amaterasu-Ōmikami's topknots and arms and did the same, and five male deities were born: 正哉吾勝勝速日天忍穂耳尊*, Ame-no-Hohi-no-Mikoto (天穂日命), Amatsuhikone-no-Mikoto (天津彦根命), Ikutsuhikone-no-Mikoto (活津彦根命), and Kumanokusubi-no-Mikoto (熊野橡樟日命).

Amaterasu-Ōmikami said that, tracing the original objects, the jewel string was hers, so the five male deities were all her children, and she took them and raised them. She also said that since the ten-span sword was Susanoo-no-Mikoto's, the three goddesses were Susanoo-no-Mikoto's children, and gave them to Susanoo-no-Mikoto. These three goddesses are the deities worshipped by the 胸肩君* and others of Tsukushi. A small note states that Ame-no-Hohi-no-Mikoto is the ancestor of the 出雲臣, the 土師連, and others, and Amatsuhikone-no-Mikoto the ancestor of the 凡川内直, the 山代直, and others (readings unconfirmed).

A plain retelling of what the main text of the Nihon Shoki says (no published translation has been used).

Variant accounts

After its main text, the Nihon Shoki often sets out other versions, introduced with words such as "a certain book says" (一書曰).

Variant account 1

The Sun Deity (Amaterasu-Ōmikami) met him wearing three swords of her own, of ten, nine, and eight spans, and the pledge is that “if the heart is pure, males will be born.” The Sun Deity ate her own swords and produced Okitsushima-hime (瀛津嶋姫), Takitsu-hime, and Takori-hime, and Susanoo-no-Mikoto produced five male deities from the jewels of the string hung around his neck and obtained the sign of victory. The Sun Deity sent the three goddesses down to the island of Tsukushi and instructed them to stay on the way, assist the Heavenly Grandson, and be worshipped by the Heavenly Grandson.

Variant account 2

As Susanoo-no-Mikoto was ascending, a deity named 羽明玉* presented him with the 瑞八坂瓊 curved jewel (曲玉). Amaterasu-Ōmikami and Susanoo-no-Mikoto exchanged sword and curved jewel; Amaterasu-Ōmikami bit the curved jewel and produced Ichikishima-hime-no-Mikoto (the distant sea, 遠瀛), Takori-hime-no-Mikoto (the middle sea, 中瀛), and Takitsu-hime-no-Mikoto (the seashore, 海浜), and Susanoo-no-Mikoto bit the sword and produced five male deities, Ame-no-Hohi-no-Mikoto and the others. The terms are “if female, a black heart; if male, a red heart,” and no conclusion about victory or defeat is recorded.

Variant account 3

The two deities faced each other across the 天安河*, and the Sun Deity pledged, “If males are born, I will make them my children and have them rule the Heavenly Plain (天原).” The Sun Deity ate her own sword and produced three goddesses, and Susanoo-no-Mikoto held in his mouth the jewels hung on his topknot and around his neck and produced six male deities from his palms, arms, and feet. The Sun Deity made those six males her own children and had them rule the Heavenly Plain; the account records that the three goddesses descended to 宇佐嶋* in the Central Land of Reed Plains, are now on the 海北道中 (the way north across the sea) and are called 道主貴*, and are worshipped by the 水沼君* and others of Tsukushi.

In the original text

吾元無黑心，但父母已有嚴勅，將永就乎根國。

I have never had a black (evil) heart. By my parents' strict command, I am now about to go forever to the Root Land: Susanoo-no-Mikoto's defense of himself.

如吾所生是女者，則可以爲有濁心；若是男者，則可以爲有清心。

If the child I produce is female, regard me as having an impure heart; if male, regard me as having a pure heart: the terms of the pledge.

八坂瓊之五百箇御統者，是吾物也，故彼五男神悉是吾兒。

The Yasakani jewel string is mine, so the five male deities are all my children: the words of Amaterasu-Ōmikami.

汝三神宜降居道中，奉助天孫，而爲天孫所祭也。

You three deities, descend and stay on the way, assist the Heavenly Grandson, and be worshipped by the Heavenly Grandson: the Sun Deity's instruction in variant account 1.

In the Kojiki

Kojiki, Part 1 — the pledge (after the pledge between Amaterasu-Ōmikami and Susanoo-no-Mikoto)

In the Kojiki, Susanoo-no-Mikoto says only “各宇氣比而，生子” without stating terms beforehand, and declares his own victory on the grounds that those born were female. The main text of the Nihon Shoki sets the terms first, “if female, an impure heart; if male, a pure heart,” so the way the result is read is reversed.

我心清明，故我所生子，得手弱女。
Kojiki, Part 1 — the pledge

The point that the children's affiliation is divided according to the “original object” is the same, but the Kojiki specifically records that the three goddesses dwell respectively in the offshore shrine (奥津宮), middle shrine (中津宮), and shore shrine (辺津宮) of Munakata (胸形, 宗像), and names the first goddess 多紀理毗賣命*.

物實因我物所成，故自吾子也。
Kojiki, Part 1 — end of the section on the division of rule among the three noble children

Regarding Izanagi-no-Mikoto's end, which is recorded before the pledge, the main text of the Nihon Shoki says that he withdrew into the hidden palace on the island of Awaji, whereas the Kojiki records that he dwells at 多賀* in Ōmi (淡海).

其伊耶那岐大神者，坐淡海之多賀也
