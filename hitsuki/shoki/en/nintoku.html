Nihon Shoki, Book 11 — Emperor Nintoku · page 25 of 50

Emperor Nintoku, the people's cooking fires, and Empress 磐之媛*

仁徳天皇と民のかまど・磐之媛皇后

After the death of Emperor Homuta (譽田天皇), 大鷦鷯尊* and the Crown Prince 菟道稚郎子* each yielded the throne to the other, and when the Crown Prince took his own life, 大鷦鷯尊 ascended the throne at the 高津宮 palace* in Naniwa. The story of the "people's cooking fires", in which he exempted the people from taxes and labor for three years, the works on the 堀江 canal* and the 茨田 embankment*, the conflict over Empress 磐之媛* and 八田皇女*, the wars with Silla and the Emishi, and other matters are recorded year by year up to his death in the 87th year.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textIn the 2nd month of the 4th year, the Emperor looked out over the land from a high tower and, seeing that no cooking smoke was rising, realized that the people were poor; in the 3rd month he exempted them from all taxes and labor for three years. Even when the palace fences fell down and the rain leaked in, he did not repair them, and in the 4th month of the 7th year, seeing smoke filling the land, he told the Empress, "When the people are rich, I am rich."
  2. Main textIn the 9th year, although the provinces asked to pay taxes and repair the palace, the Emperor did not allow it. When he reimposed taxes and labor in the 10th month of the 10th year, the people, helping the old and bringing their small children, carried timber of their own accord, and the palace buildings were soon completed. The original text records that for this reason "he is called the sage emperor (聖帝) to this day".
  3. Main textBefore the accession, 菟道稚郎子* and 大鷦鷯尊* yielded the throne to each other for three years, and fresh fish presented by fishermen went back and forth between 菟道* and Naniwa. The Crown Prince, saying that he could not take away his elder brother's resolve, took his own life and entrusted his full sister 八田皇女* to his brother.
  4. Main textIn the 9th month of the 30th year, Empress 磐之媛命*, hearing that the Emperor had taken 八田皇女* into the palace, threw into the sea the 御綱葉 leaves* she had gathered at Cape Kumano, and instead of returning to Naniwa built a palace south of the 筒城 hill* in Yamashiro and lived there. Even when the Emperor visited her in person she would not meet him; she died there in the 35th year, and in the 38th year 八田皇女 became Empress.
  5. Main textIn the 11th year the 堀江 canal* was dug and the 茨田 embankment* built. At two difficult places, following the instruction of a deity who appeared in a dream, 強頚*, a man of Musashi, sank into the water and died, but 茨田連衫子*, a man of Kawachi, tested the deity by throwing two gourds into the water; because the gourds did not sink, he did not have to die, and he completed the embankment.
  6. Compared with the KojikiThe Kojiki also records the three-year exemption from taxes and labor and the "reign of the sage emperor" (聖帝世), but it says that the Emperor climbed a high mountain to look over the land, and it sums the story up briefly in a single section. The year-by-year progress of the 4th, 7th and 10th years and the exchange with the Empress found in the Nihon Shoki do not appear in the Kojiki.

What the main text says

Emperor 大鷦鷯 (大鷦鷯天皇)* was the fourth son of Emperor Homuta (譽田天皇), and his mother was Nakatsuhime-no-Mikoto (仲姫命). When the previous emperor died in the 2nd month of the 41st year of Emperor Homuta, the Crown Prince 菟道稚郎子* and 大鷦鷯尊* each yielded the throne to the other. Meanwhile 額田大中彦皇子*, who tried to take control of the imperial rice fields (屯田) of Yamato, was rebuffed on the basis of a rule from the reign of the emperor of the 纒向玉城宮 palace*, reported by 吾子篭*, who had been recalled from the Korean lands (韓国). 大山守皇子* plotted to kill the Crown Prince and take the throne, but the Crown Prince, forewarned by 大鷦鷯尊, mingled with the ferrymen and tipped the boat in the middle of the 菟道 river*, throwing the prince into the water and causing his death. Still the Crown Prince did not ascend the throne, and it stood empty for three years. Fresh fish presented by fishermen were sent back and forth between 菟道 and Naniwa many times, and the fishermen threw the fish away and wept; this is regarded as the origin of the proverb about the fisherman who weeps over his own goods. The Crown Prince, saying that he could not take away his elder brother's resolve, took his own life. 大鷦鷯尊 hurried to him, loosened his hair, straddled the body and called out three times, and the Crown Prince briefly revived, entrusted his full sister 八田皇女* to his brother, died again, and was buried on the mountain of 菟道.

In the 1st month of the first year, 大鷦鷯尊* ascended the throne, made his mother Empress Dowager, and placed the capital at Naniwa, calling it the 高津宮 palace*. The palace was a plain one: its fences and pillars were not painted, and the thatch of its roofs was not even trimmed. On the day the Emperor was born a horned owl (木菟) flew into the birthing hall, and on the same day a wren (鷦鷯) flew into the birthing hut of Takeuchi-no-Sukune's wife, so Emperor Homuta (譽田天皇) exchanged the names of the two birds in naming the children, and the minister's son became 木菟宿禰* (founding ancestor of the 平群臣)*. In the 3rd month of the 2nd year, 磐之媛命* was made Empress, and 去來穗別, 住吉仲皇子, 瑞齒別 and 雄朝津間稚子宿禰 (readings unconfirmed) were born; the consort 日向髮長媛* bore 大草香皇子* and 幡梭皇女*.

In the 2nd month of the 4th year, the Emperor gazed into the distance from a high tower and, seeing no cooking smoke rising, realized that the people were poor. In the 3rd month he exempted them from all taxes and labor for three years; he did not replace his own clothes or footwear until they wore out, and even when the palace fences fell down and the rain leaked in, he did not repair them. In the 4th month of the 7th year, seeing smoke filling the land, the Emperor told the Empress, "I am already rich." When the Empress asked how this could be when the palace was in ruins, he answered that a ruler takes the people as his foundation. In the 9th year, although the provinces asked to pay taxes and repair the palace, he did not allow it. When he imposed taxes and labor again in the 10th month of the 10th year, the people worked with all their strength day and night to build the palace buildings, and it is recorded that for this reason he is called the sage emperor (聖帝) to this day.

In the 11th year, the Emperor, troubled that the water overflowed and there were few rice fields, had the plain north of the palace dug in the 10th month to lead the water to the western sea, naming the channel the 堀江*, and built the 茨田 embankment*. In this work 強頚* sank into the water and died, while 衫子* escaped death through his clever use of gourds; the two places were called 強頚斷間 and 衫子斷間 (readings unconfirmed). In the 12th year, of an iron shield and target presented by Koguryŏ (高麗), only 盾人宿禰*, ancestor of the 的臣*, shot through them, and he was given the name 的戸田宿禰*. After that the great canal of 栗隈県* in Yamashiro, the 茨田屯倉 granary*, the 和珥池 pond*, the 横野堤 embankment*, the bridge at 猪甘津*, the great road within the capital, the great canal of 感玖* and other works were built one after another, and more than forty thousand 頃 of rice fields are said to have been opened.

In the 16th year, the Emperor gave the court lady 桑田玖賀媛*, whom he could not approach because of the Empress's jealousy, to 速待*, ancestor of the 播磨国造*, but 玖賀媛 would not accept him and died of illness while being sent back to 桑田*. In the 22nd year the Emperor asked the Empress in a song to let him take 八田皇女* as a consort, but the Empress answered with a song and did not allow it. In the 9th month of the 30th year, the Empress, on her way back from the land of 紀国*, heard that the Emperor had taken 八田皇女 into the palace; she did not enter Naniwa but went up the Yamashiro river and built a palace south of the 筒城 hill*. 口持臣*, sent in the 10th month, kept lying prostrate in the courtyard, soaked by rain and snow, and his younger sister 国依媛* expressed her concern for her brother in a song, but the Empress answered that she would not return; in the 11th month, even when the Emperor visited the 筒城宮 palace in person, she would not meet him. In the 6th month of the 35th year the Empress died at the 筒城宮 palace; in the 37th year she was buried at 那羅山*, and in the 1st month of the 38th year 八田皇女 was made Empress.

In the 7th month of the 38th year, the call of the deer on 菟餓野*, which the Emperor and Empress had heard every night, ceased. The Emperor concluded that a deer presented by the 佐伯部* was the deer that had been calling, and moved the 佐伯部 to 渟田* in Aki. In the 40th year, the Emperor, wishing to take 雌鳥皇女* as a consort, used 隼別皇子* as go-between, but 隼別皇子 secretly took the princess as his own wife. Hearing a song of his attendants, the Emperor grew angry and had the two pursued, and they were killed at 蒋代野* in Ise. 佐伯直阿俄能胡*, who had taken the princess's jewels, was found out later at the banquet of the Niiname (新嘗) festival; he escaped the death penalty by presenting his private land, and that land is said to have been called 玉代*. In the 41st year 紀角宿禰* was sent to Paekche. In the 43rd year, a bird called 倶知* in Paekche (the present-day hawk) was tamed by 酒君*, the Emperor hunted with it on 百舌鳥野*, and the hawk-keepers' group (鷹甘部) was established. In the 50th year a wild goose laid eggs on the 茨田 embankment, and the Emperor and Takeuchi-no-Sukune exchanged questions and answers in song.

In the 53rd year, 竹葉瀬*, ancestor of the 上毛野君*, and his younger brother 田道* were sent to Silla, which had stopped sending tribute; 田道 defeated the Silla army by striking at a weakness of the enemy learned from a captive. In the 55th year 田道 fought the rebellious Emishi and died at the harbor of 伊寺水門*, but it is said that later, when the Emishi dug up 田道's grave, a great serpent came out and killed many of the Emishi with its poison. In the 62nd year a ship was built from a great tree in Tōtōmi, and in the same year 額田大中彦皇子* discovered an ice house at 闘鷄* and presented ice; from then on ice was stored in winter and distributed at the spring equinox. In the 65th year 宿儺* of Hida, who had one body and two faces, was put to death by 難波根子武振熊*, ancestor of the 和珥臣*. In the 67th year a tomb site was fixed on the 石津原 plain* in Kawachi; because on the day construction began a shrike (百舌鳥) flew out of the ear of a deer that had collapsed, the place was named 百舌鳥耳原*. In the same year 縣守*, ancestor of the 笠臣*, slew a creature in a deep pool of the 川嶋河 river* in Kibi that had been spitting poison and tormenting people. It is said that afterward, because the Emperor lightened taxes and cared for the people, there were no troubles for more than twenty years. In the 1st month of the 87th year the Emperor died, and in the 10th month he was buried in the 百舌鳥野陵 tomb*.

A plain retelling of what the main text of the Nihon Shoki says (no published translation has been used).

Variant accounts

After its main text, the Nihon Shoki often sets out other versions, introduced with words such as "a certain book says" (一書曰).

One version (一云)

In the main text, the envoy sent to bring back the Empress from the 筒城宮 palace* is 口持臣*, ancestor of the 的臣*, but this "one version says" (一云) makes the envoy 口子臣*, ancestor of the 和珥臣*.

Popular account (俗曰)

The main text tells how the 佐伯部* presented the deer of 菟餓野*, but a different story is also given as a popular account. Beside a man staying overnight on the plain of 菟餓*, a stag dreamed that it was covered with white frost; another deer interpreted this as a sign that it would be shot by a man and smeared with white salt, and before dawn a hunter shot the stag dead.

In the original text

百姓貧之。則朕貧也。百姓富之。則朕富也。

The Emperor's words: if the people are poor, then I am poor; if the people are rich, then I am rich.

故於今稱聖帝也。

For that reason he is still praised (as) the sage emperor today.

有海人耶。因己物以泣。

A proverb: though no fisherman, one weeps over one's own goods. The story of the fish offering being sent back and forth is regarded as its origin.

烟氣不起於域中。以爲百姓既貧。

Because no cooking smoke was rising within the land, he concluded that the people had already become poor.

In the Kojiki

Kojiki, Part 3, beginning of the section on Emperor Nintoku (the story of the people's cooking fires, following the entries on consorts, children and public works)

In the Kojiki, the Emperor climbs a "high mountain" (高山), looks over the land and learns that no smoke is rising, and a single short section covers everything from the exemption from taxes and labor to the reign being called the "reign of the sage emperor" (聖帝世). The year-by-year progress and the exchange with the Empress found in the Nihon Shoki do not appear.

於國中烟不發。國皆貧窮。
Kojiki, Part 3, section on Emperor Nintoku, the Great Empress's journey to Yamashiro (G12–G25)

In the Kojiki, the Great Empress, learning of the Emperor's marriage on her way back from the land of 木国*, enters the house of 奴理能美* in Yamashiro (山代); 口子臣* and others smooth things over by saying she went to see "a wondrous insect that changes into three colors", and the Emperor visits the house and sings. In the Nihon Shoki the Empress does not return from the 筒城宮 palace* and is said to have died there in the 35th year; the insect story could not be confirmed there.

有變三色之奇虫。
Kojiki, Part 3, section on Emperor Nintoku, the story of 女鳥王 and 速總別王 (readings unconfirmed) (G30–G40)

In the Kojiki, 速總別王* and 女鳥王* cross the 倉椅山 mountain* and are killed at 蘇邇* in 宇陀*; the one who took 女鳥王's jeweled bracelet (玉釧) is the general 山部大楯連*, who is sentenced to death by the Great Empress. In the Nihon Shoki the two are killed at 蒋代野* in Ise, and 佐伯直阿俄能胡*, who took the jewels, escapes death by presenting land.

取其女鳥王。所纒御手之玉釧而。與己妻。
