Nihon Shoki, Book 7 — Emperor Keikō · page 17 of 50

The Accession of Emperor Keikō and His Tour of Kyūshū

景行天皇の即位と九州巡幸

After recording the accession of Emperor Keikō and his consorts and children, including the twins 大碓 and 小碓 (readings unconfirmed; 小碓 is Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto), this page records the tour in which, after the Kumaso rebelled in the 12th year, the emperor himself set out for Tsukushi, travelled through Buzen, Bungo, Hyūga, Hi and Chikugo, and subdued the tsuchigumo (土蜘蛛) and the Kumaso. Many origins of local place names are told, and the page extends to the return to the capital in the 19th year and Takeuchi-no-Sukune's inspection of the eastern lands.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textIn the 2nd year, the empress 播磨稲日大郎姫* bore Prince 大碓 and 小碓尊 (readings unconfirmed) as twins on a single day, and these names are said to have arisen because the emperor cried out toward a mortar (碓). 小碓尊 was also called 日本童男* and Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto (日本武尊); he was one jō (一丈) tall and strong enough to lift a cauldron (鼎).
  2. Main textIn the 12th year, because the Kumaso neglected their tribute, the emperor set out for Tsukushi. The text records that at 娑麼 in 周芳 (readings unconfirmed), a female chieftain, 神夏磯媛*, hung a sword, a mirror and a jewel (瓊) on a sakaki tree (賢木), raised a white flag and submitted, and that at 碩田* in Bungo the tsuchigumo were struck down with mallets made of camellia wood (海石榴), giving rise to the place names 海石榴市 and 血田 (readings unconfirmed).
  3. Main textAt the 高屋宮* palace in Hyūga, the emperor deliberated on attacking the Kumaso, and won over 市乾鹿文*, daughter of the Kumaso chieftain, by pretending to favor her. 市乾鹿文 made her father drunk and cut his bowstring, and an attendant killed her father, but the emperor, hating her lack of filial piety, put 市乾鹿文 to death, and in the 5th month of the 13th year he pacified the land of 襲国*.
  4. Main textIn the 17th year, at 子湯県*, the emperor looked to the east and named the land "Hyūga" (日向), saying that it faced straight toward the direction where the sun rises; climbing a large rock in the fields, he sang the "Song of Longing for Home" (思邦歌), yearning for the capital.
  5. Compared with the KojikiThe "Song of Longing for Home" (思邦歌) that Emperor Keikō sings in Hyūga in the Nihon Shoki has almost the same words as the "Song of Longing for the Land" (思国歌) that Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto (倭建命) sings at 能煩野* just before his death in the Kojiki. The singer and the scene differ between the Kojiki and the Nihon Shoki.
  6. Compared with the KojikiOn the return journey in the 18th year, the origins of the names of various places, such as the land of Hi (火国), Aso, 御木 and 八女 (readings unconfirmed), are told as the words of the emperor or the answers of local people. In the section on Emperor Keikō in the Kojiki, no entry corresponding to a tour of Tsukushi by the emperor himself could be confirmed.

What the main text says

大足彦忍代別天皇 * (Emperor Keikō) was the third son of Emperor Suinin, and his mother was the empress 日葉洲媛命*, daughter of 丹波道主王*. He became crown prince in the 37th year of Emperor Suinin, and when his father died in the 99th year, he acceded to the throne in the 7th month of that year and changed the era (改元). This year was the year (辛未). In the 3rd month of the 2nd year, he made 播磨稲日大郎姫* his empress, and the empress bore Prince 大碓 and 小碓尊 (readings unconfirmed) as twins on the same day. 小碓尊 was also called 日本童男*, or Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto (日本武尊); he was brave from his childhood, and when he grew up he was one jō tall and strong enough to lift a cauldron. In the 3rd year, the emperor intended to worship the deities in the province of Kii, but the divination was inauspicious, so he sent 屋主忍男武雄心命*. He lived for nine years at the 柏原 of 阿備 (readings unconfirmed), married 影媛*, daughter of 菟道彦*, a distant ancestor of the 紀直*, and Takeuchi-no-Sukune (武内宿禰) was born.

In the 4th year, the emperor sought 弟媛*, daughter of Prince 八坂入彦*, in Mino, but 弟媛 hid in a bamboo grove. The emperor stayed for a time at the 泳宮* palace, set carp floating in the pond and amused himself with them, and detained 弟媛 when she came secretly to look. 弟媛, saying that she did not care for relations between men and women, asked that her elder sister 八坂入媛* be taken into the women's quarters of the palace in her place. 八坂入媛 bore seven sons and six daughters, including 稚足彦天皇 * (Emperor Seimu), and the emperor's children numbered eighty in all. Apart from Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto, 稚足彦天皇 and Prince 五百城入彦*, the seventy-odd others were enfeoffed in provinces and districts, and it is said that the "別" of the various provinces are their descendants. In this month, 大碓命*, who had been sent to see the two beautiful daughters of 神骨*, the provincial chief (国造) of Mino, secretly had relations with them and did not report back, so the emperor resented 大碓命. In the 11th month the emperor returned to 纒向* and made the 日代宮* palace his capital.

In the 7th month of the 12th year, the Kumaso rebelled, and in the 8th month the emperor set out for Tsukushi. In the 9th month, at 娑麼 in 周芳 (readings unconfirmed), he saw much smoke rising to the south and sent messengers, whereupon the female chieftain 神夏磯媛* submitted and told him that there were four bandits: 鼻垂, 耳垂, 麻剥 and 土折猪折 (readings unconfirmed). 武諸木* and others lured out 麻剥's followers with red garments and other gifts, and captured and killed all four. The emperor built a temporary palace at 長峡県* in the province of Buzen, and named that place 京 ("the capital").

In the 10th month, the emperor reached the land of 碩田国*, and heard from 速津媛*, the female chieftain of the village of 速見邑*, that tsuchigumo (土蜘蛛) — 青 and 白 of the 鼠 rock cave, and 打猿, 八田 and 国摩侶 of 禰疑野 (readings unconfirmed) — would not submit. The emperor had mallets made of camellia wood (海石榴) and gave them to his picked soldiers, and they struck down the tsuchigumo of the rock cave at the upper reaches of the 稲葉* river. It is said that the place was called 海石榴市 and the place where the blood flowed 血田 (readings unconfirmed). Next he defeated 八田, and 打猿, seeing that he could not win, asked to surrender, but this was not permitted, and they all threw themselves into a valley and died. When the emperor kicked a stone on the great plain of 柏峡*, it flew up like an oak leaf, so the stone was called 蹈石*.

In the 11th month, he reached the province of Hyūga and built the 高屋宮* palace. In the 12th month, as a plan to strike down 厚鹿文*, chieftain of the Kumaso, and his men, he tricked the chieftain's two daughters with gifts and invited them. The elder, 市乾鹿文*, made her father drunk, cut his bowstring, and had an attendant kill her father, but the emperor, hating her lack of filial piety, put 市乾鹿文 to death, and gave the younger sister, 市鹿文*, to the 火国造*. In the 5th month of the 13th year he pacified the land of 襲国*, and during the six years he stayed at the 高屋宮 palace he took Mihakashi-hime (御刀媛) as consort, and Prince 豊国別* was born. In the 17th year, at 子湯県*, he named the land "Hyūga" (日向), and sang the Song of Longing for Home (思邦歌), yearning for the capital.

In the 18th year, the emperor toured Tsukushi while heading for the capital. At 熊県* he had soldiers strike down 弟熊*, who had not come to him; at a small island in 葦北*, when he prayed, clear water sprang from a cliff, so he named it 水島*. The text records that the name of the land of Hi (火国) came from his reaching the shore guided by a fire of unknown origin on the sea at night; the name Aso (阿蘇) from the deities 阿蘇都彦 and 阿蘇都媛 (readings unconfirmed) appearing in human form on a plain where no houses could be seen; the name of the land of 御木国* from a great fallen tree, a 歴木*; and the name 八女* from the goddess 八女津媛* who dwells in the mountains. In the 9th month of the 19th year, the emperor returned from Hyūga.

In the 2nd month of the 20th year, Princess 五百野* was sent to worship Amaterasu-Ōmikami. In the 7th month of the 25th year, Takeuchi-no-Sukune was sent to the provinces of Hokuriku and the east to investigate the lie of the land and the condition of the people. Takeuchi-no-Sukune, who returned in the 2nd month of the 27th year, reported that among the eastern barbarians there was the land of 日高見国*, whose people tied their hair up like a mallet, tattooed their bodies and were fierce, and were called Emishi (蝦夷); that the land was fertile and broad; and that it should be attacked and taken.

Variant accounts

After its main text, the Nihon Shoki often sets out other versions, introduced with words such as "a certain book says" (一書曰).

One account (一云): small note in the entry for the 3rd month of the 2nd year

It gives the empress's name as 稲日稚郎姫*.

Variant account (一書云): small note in the entry for the 3rd month of the 2nd year

It says that the empress bore three sons, the third being Prince 稚倭根子*.

One account (一云): small note in the entry for the 2nd month of the 3rd year

It gives the name of 屋主忍男武雄心命* as 武猪心*.

One account (一云): small note in the entry for the 2nd month of the 4th year

It gives Prince 国背別*, borne by 襲武媛*, as Prince 宮道別*.

In the original text

一日同皰而雙生。天皇異之。則誥於碓。

They were born as twins on the same day, and the emperor, finding this strange, cried out toward a mortar (碓). This is the origin of the names 大碓 and 小碓 (readings unconfirmed).

故時人其作海石榴椎之處曰海石榴市。

The place where the camellia-wood (海石榴) mallets were made was called 海石榴市* by the people of the time.

天皇則惡其不孝之甚而誅市乾鹿文。

The emperor, hating her extreme lack of filial piety, killed 市乾鹿文*, who had betrayed her father.

是國也直向於日出方。故號其國曰日向也。

Because this land faces straight toward the direction where the sun rises, it was named Hyūga (日向).

In the Kojiki

Kojiki, Part 2, section on Emperor Keikō (the death of Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto)

The Song of Longing for Home (思邦歌) that Emperor Keikō sings at 子湯県* in Hyūga in the Nihon Shoki appears in the Kojiki as the Song of Longing for the Land (思国歌) that Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto (倭建命) sings at 能煩野* on his way back from the eastern campaign, and the words are almost the same.

夜麻登波。久爾能麻本呂婆。
Kojiki, Part 2, section on Emperor Keikō (consorts and children)

Concerning 大碓命*, who was sent to summon the two daughters in Mino, the Kojiki says that he married the daughters himself and presented other women in their place, pretending they were the daughters. The Nihon Shoki records only that he secretly had relations with them and did not report back, so the emperor resented him.

即己自婚其孃子
Kojiki, Part 2, section on Emperor Kōgen (genealogy)

Regarding the parents of Takeuchi-no-Sukune (武内宿禰), the Kojiki, in the section on Emperor Kōgen, makes him the child of 比古布都押之信命* and 山下影日売*, younger sister of 宇豆比古*, ancestor of the 木国造*. The Nihon Shoki, in the entry for the 3rd year of Emperor Keikō, makes him the child of 屋主忍男武雄心命* and 影媛*, daughter of 菟道彦*, a distant ancestor of the 紀直*.
