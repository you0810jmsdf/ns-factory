Nihon Shoki, Book 1 — Age of the Gods (part 1) · page 6 of 50

Yamata-no-Orochi and Ōanamuchi-no-Kami (Section 8)

八岐大蛇と大己貴神（第八段）

Susanoo-no-Mikoto, driven out of heaven, slays Yamata-no-Orochi upstream on the 簸 River* in Izumo and saves Inada-hime (奇稲田姫); he presents the Kusanagi Sword, which came out of the serpent's tail, to the heavenly deities, builds a palace at Suga (清地), and fathers Ōanamuchi-no-Kami (大己貴神). The six variant accounts transmit separately such matters as the setting, the names of the swords, the counting of generations of descendants, the tree-planting of Itakeru-no-Kami (五十猛神), and the making of the land by Ōanamuchi-no-Mikoto and Sukunahikona-no-Mikoto.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textIn the main text, Susanoo-no-Mikoto meets the earthly deities Ashinazuchi (脚摩乳) and Tenazuchi (手摩乳) and their daughter Inada-hime upstream on the 簸 River* in the province of Izumo. Since their daughters had been swallowed by Yamata-no-Orochi year after year, Susanoo-no-Mikoto changed the maiden into a many-toothed comb (湯津爪櫛) and stuck it in his topknot, filled eight vats with eight-times-brewed sake (八醞酒) to make the serpent drunk, and cut it down with his ten-span sword.
  2. Main textWhen he cut the serpent's tail, the edge of his sword was slightly chipped, and when he split the tail open there was a sword inside. This is the Kusanagi Sword (草薙剣), and a writing quoted in a small note records that its original name was 天叢雲剣* and that it was renamed the Kusanagi Sword in the time of Prince Yamato Takeru (日本武皇子). Susanoo-no-Mikoto said, “I cannot make this my own,” and presented it to the heavenly deities.
  3. Main textIn the main text, Susanoo-no-Mikoto says, “My heart is refreshed (sugasugashi),” at Suga (清地) in Izumo and builds a palace there, and with Inada-hime he begets Ōanamuchi-no-Kami. Ashinazuchi and Tenazuchi are made chiefs of the palace and given the name 稲田宮主神*, and Susanoo-no-Mikoto afterward goes to the Root Land.
  4. Variant accountIn the variant accounts, the setting, the names of the swords, and where the swords are kept differ. In variant account 2 the setting is upstream on the 可愛 River* in the province of Aki, and the account holds that the Kusanagi Sword is at 吾湯市村* in the province of Owari (in the charge of the priests of Atsuta), and that the sword that cut the serpent, 蛇之麁正*, is at 石上*. In variant account 3 the sword that cut the serpent is the 蛇韓鋤之剣* and is with the 神部 of Kibi, and in variant account 4 the serpent is cut with the 天蝿斫之剣*, and Susanoo-no-Mikoto crosses from 曾尸茂梨* in Silla to Izumo in a boat of clay.
  5. Variant accountVariant accounts 4 and 5 transmit that Itakeru-no-Kami (or -no-Mikoto) brought back the seeds of trees and planted them from Tsukushi throughout the Land of the Great Eight Islands, making the mountains green, and that Susanoo-no-Mikoto pulled out his hairs and turned them into cryptomeria, cypress, and other trees. Variant account 6 records that Ōanamuchi-no-Mikoto and Sukunahikona-no-Mikoto joined forces to make the land, established methods of curing illness and rites of spell-casting, and that the 幸魂 and 奇魂 (sakimitama and kushimitama) of Ōanamuchi-no-Kami, which appeared lighting up the sea, came to dwell on Mount 三諸山* and became the deity of 大三輪*.
  6. CommentaryThe deity-name database of Kokugakuin University introduces both a theory that regards variant account 1, which tells only of the marriage without the slaying of the serpent, as the oldest form and holds that the serpent-slaying was added later, and the opinion that the serpent-slaying was simply omitted because variant account 1 extracted only the parts that differ from the main text. [source, Japanese]

What the main text says

Susanoo-no-Mikoto descended from heaven and arrived upstream on the 簸 River* in the province of Izumo. Hearing the sound of weeping from upstream, he went to see, and found an old man and an old woman weeping as they stroked a young girl placed between them. When Susanoo-no-Mikoto asked who they were, the old man answered that he was the earthly deity Ashinazuchi, that his wife was Tenazuchi, and that this daughter was Inada-hime (奇稲田姫).

They said the reason they were weeping was that they had once had eight daughters, but each year one had been swallowed by Yamata-no-Orochi, and now this daughter too was about to be swallowed, and there was no way to escape. When Susanoo-no-Mikoto asked whether they would give the daughter to him, the two answered that they would offer her as he wished. Susanoo-no-Mikoto changed Inada-hime into a many-toothed comb and stuck it in his own topknot, had Ashinazuchi and Tenazuchi brew eight-times-brewed sake, made eight temporary platforms (sazuki, 棚), placed on each one a vat filled with sake, and waited.

When the time came, the serpent did indeed appear. Its head and tail were each divided into eight, its eyes were like red ground-cherries (akakagachi, 赤酸醤), pines and oaks (柏) grew on its back, and it stretched across eight hills and eight valleys. The serpent put each of its heads into a vat, drank the sake, became drunk, and fell asleep. Susanoo-no-Mikoto drew the ten-span sword he wore and hacked the serpent to pieces.

When he cut the tail, the edge of his sword was slightly chipped, so he split the tail open to look, and inside there was a sword. This is the so-called Kusanagi Sword (kusanagi no tsurugi). A writing quoted in a small note records that its original name was 天叢雲剣*, probably because there were always clouds above where the serpent was, and that it was renamed the Kusanagi Sword in the time of Prince Yamato Takeru. Susanoo-no-Mikoto said that this was a divine sword and that he could not make it his own, and presented it to the heavenly deities.

After that, Susanoo-no-Mikoto looked for a place to marry and arrived at Suga (清地) in Izumo, where he said, “My heart is refreshed,” and built a palace there. A small note records a song beginning 「夜句茂多菟」 as one that Susanoo-no-Mikoto composed at that time. Susanoo-no-Mikoto was united with Inada-hime and begot Ōanamuchi-no-Kami, and he made Ashinazuchi and Tenazuchi chiefs of the palace and gave them the name 稲田宮主神*. After this, Susanoo-no-Mikoto departed for the Root Land.

A plain retelling of what the main text of the Nihon Shoki says (no published translation has been used).

Variant accounts

After its main text, the Nihon Shoki often sets out other versions, introduced with words such as "a certain book says" (一書曰).

Variant account 1

The slaying of the serpent is not told. Upstream on the 簸 River*, Susanoo-no-Mikoto meets and is united with 稲田媛 (Inada-hime), daughter of 稲田宮主簀狭之八箇耳*, and begets 清之湯山主三名狭漏彦八嶋篠 * (two other names are also given). The account holds that the descendant of this deity in the fifth generation is Ōkuninushi-no-Kami (大国主神).

Variant account 2

The setting is upstream on the 可愛 River* in the province of Aki; 稲田宮主簀狭之八箇耳*, the wife of 脚摩手摩*, is pregnant and fears that the child to be born will again be swallowed by the serpent. Susanoo-no-Mikoto has sake brewed from fruit in eight jars, calls out to the serpent, “You are a deity to be held in awe,” makes it drink, and cuts it down. The account holds that the Kusanagi Sword is now at 吾湯市村* in the province of Owari, in the charge of the priests of Atsuta, and that the sword that cut the serpent, 蛇之麁正*, is at 石上*. The child born, 真髪触奇稲田媛*, is raised upstream on the 簸 River in Izumo and becomes his consort, and her descendant in the sixth generation is said to be Ōanamuchi-no-Mikoto (大己貴命, ōanamuchi).

Variant account 3

Ashinazuchi and Tenazuchi ask that, if he is to marry their daughter, he first kill the serpent. The serpent was a fearsome thing, with a rock-pine (石松) on each head and mountains on both flanks. Susanoo-no-Mikoto makes it drink poisoned sake and cuts its heads and belly with the 蛇韓鋤之剣*. The account holds that the Kusanagi Sword that came out of the tail is now in the province of Owari and that the sword that did the cutting is now with the 神部 of Kibi, and it places the setting on a mountain upstream on the 簸 River in Izumo.

Variant account 4

The banished Susanoo-no-Mikoto takes his child Itakeru-no-Kami and descends to 曾尸茂梨* in the land of Silla, but, saying he does not wish to live there, crosses eastward in a boat of clay and arrives at the peak of 鳥上* upstream on the 簸 River in Izumo. He cuts down a man-swallowing serpent with the 天蝿斫之剣*, and has his descendant in the fifth generation, 天之葺根神*, present the sword that came out of its tail to heaven. Itakeru-no-Kami descended carrying the seeds of many trees, and, without planting them in the land of 韓*, planted them from Tsukushi throughout the Land of the Great Eight Islands, making the mountains green; for this reason he is regarded as a deity of merit, and the account holds that he is the great deity who dwells in the province of Kii.

Variant account 5

Susanoo-no-Mikoto said that the islands of 韓郷* have gold and silver, and that it would not be good if the land ruled by his child had no floating treasures (浮宝); he pulled out his beard and turned it into cryptomeria (杉), his chest hair into cypress (桧), the hair of his buttocks into podocarpus (柀), and his eyebrows into camphor trees (櫲樟), and fixed their respective uses (floating treasures, timber for palaces, and things on which people lie). Itakeru-no-Mikoto and his younger sisters Ōyatsuhime-no-Mikoto (大屋津姫命) and 採津姫命* spread the seeds of the trees and crossed over to the province of Kii. Susanoo-no-Mikoto lived on the peak of 熊成峯* and later entered the Root Land.

Variant account 6

It records the many other names of Ōkuninushi-no-Kami (Ōmononushi-no-Kami (大物主神), 国作大己貴命, 葦原醜男, 八千戈神 (readings unconfirmed), Ōkunitama-no-Kami (大国玉神), and Utsushikunitama-no-Kami (顕国玉神)), and that he had one hundred and eighty-one children. Ōanamuchi-no-Mikoto and Sukunahikona-no-Mikoto joined forces to make the land, and established methods of treating illness for people and livestock and rites of spell-casting to ward off calamities caused by birds, beasts, and insects. Sukunahikona-no-Mikoto departed for the Eternal Land (常世郷) from the cape of Kumano (or, flung off by a millet stalk at 淡嶋*), and Ōanamuchi-no-Kami finished making the rest alone. The 幸魂 and 奇魂 (sakimitama and kushimitama) of Ōanamuchi-no-Kami, which appeared lighting up the sea, wished to dwell on Mount 三諸山* in the land of 日本 (Yamato); this is the deity of 大三輪*, whose child was Himetatara-isuzu-hime-no-Mikoto (姫蹈鞴五十鈴姫命), who became the consort of Emperor 神日本磐余彦火火出見*, according to the account (which also gives alongside this a tradition that Kotoshironushi-no-Kami (事代主神) became an eight-fathom bear-wani (八尋熊鰐), visited a woman, and begot her). Finally it tells how a small man who appeared in a boat made of the bark of 白蘞 was found to be Sukunahikona-no-Mikoto, child of Takamimusubi-no-Mikoto (高皇産霊尊).

In the original text

頭尾各有八岐。眼如赤酸醤。

The serpent's appearance: its head and tail were each divided into eight, and its eyes were like red ground-cherries (赤酸醤).

是神劒也。吾何敢私以安乎。乃上獻於天神也。

“This is a divine sword. How could I dare make it my own?” he said, and presented it to the heavenly deities.

乃言曰。吾心清清之。

Arriving at Suga (清地), he said, “My heart is refreshed.” This is regarded as the origin of the place name Suga (清).

吾是汝之幸魂奇魂也。

“I am your sakimitama and kushimitama (幸魂, 奇魂)”: the words with which the deity who appeared from the sea answered Ōanamuchi-no-Kami (variant account 6).

In the Kojiki

Kojiki, Part 1 — the slaying of Yamata-no-Orochi

In the Kojiki, Susanoo-no-Mikoto presents the sword obtained from the serpent's tail to “Amaterasu-Ōmikami,” but the main text of the Nihon Shoki records the recipient only as “the heavenly deities.” In addition, the Kojiki makes Ashinazuchi (足名椎) a child of Ōyamatsumi-no-Kami (大山津見神), and places the setting at 鳥髪* upstream on the 肥 River*.

思異物而白上於天照大御神也
Kojiki, Part 1 — the palace at Suga and the genealogy of Susanoo-no-Mikoto

In the Kojiki, the child of Susanoo-no-Mikoto and 櫛名田比売* is 八嶋士奴美神*, and Ōkuninushi-no-Kami is born in a later generation of that lineage. In the main text of the Nihon Shoki, the child of the two deities is directly Ōanamuchi-no-Kami; variant account 1 makes him a descendant in the fifth generation, and variant account 2 in the sixth generation.

久美度迩起而所生（神）名，謂八嶋士奴美神
Kojiki, Part 1 — Sukunahikona-no-Kami and the deity of 御諸山*

In the Kojiki, 神産巣日神* says that Sukunahikona-no-Kami (少名毗古那神) is a child who slipped through the fingers of that deity's hand, and the deity who came lighting up the sea says to worship it on the mountain east of the green fence of Yamato (倭). In variant account 6 of the Nihon Shoki, the parent of Sukunahikona-no-Mikoto is Takamimusubi-no-Mikoto, and the deity who came from the sea dwells on Mount 三諸山* as Ōanamuchi-no-Kami's own sakimitama and kushimitama.
