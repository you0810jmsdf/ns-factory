Nihon Shoki, Book 24 — Empress Kōgyoku · page 41 of 50

Empress Kōgyoku — The Fall of the Royal House of the Upper Palace and the Killing of Soga no Iruka

皇極天皇 ― 上宮王家の滅亡と蘇我入鹿の誅殺

The empress of Emperor Jomei took the throne. Iruka, son of the Great Minister Soga no Emishi, seized control of the government, and in the 2nd year he destroyed Prince Yamashiro no Ōe and others at Ikaruga. In the sixth month of the 4th year, Prince Naka-no-Ōe, Nakatomi no Kamako and others cut Iruka down in the Great Audience Hall (大極殿), and the empress yielded the throne to Prince Karu.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textIn the eleventh month of the 2nd year, Soga no Iruka sent troops to attack Prince Yamashiro no Ōe and others at Ikaruga. The prince rejected a plan to raise troops in the eastern provinces, saying he did not want the people to suffer for his sake alone, and took his own life at Ikaruga-dera (斑鳩寺) together with his consorts, children and younger kinsmen.
  2. Main textIn the sixth month of the 4th year, at the ceremony where the memorial of the Three Han (三韓) was read aloud, Prince Naka-no-Ōe, together with 佐伯連子麻呂 and 葛城稚犬養連網田 (readings unconfirmed), cut down Iruka before the empress in the Great Audience Hall. Prince Naka-no-Ōe told the empress that Iruka had been destroying the imperial family and was trying to overturn the imperial throne.
  3. Main textThe next day, Soga no Emishi and others, who were about to be put to death, burned the Tennōki (天皇記), the Kokki (国記) and their treasures, but 船史恵尺* pulled out the Kokki as it was about to burn and presented it to Prince Naka-no-Ōe. The day after that, the empress yielded the throne to Prince Karu and made Prince Naka-no-Ōe crown prince.
  4. Main textNakatomi no Kamako became close to Prince Naka-no-Ōe after picking up and handing back the shoe that had come off the prince’s foot during a ball game (打毱) under the zelkova tree at Hōkō-ji (法興寺), and the two worked out their plans on the way to and from Master 南淵 (南淵先生)*, under whom they studied the teachings of the Duke of Zhou and Confucius (周孔). Kamako also advised that the daughter of Soga no Kurayamada no Maro (蘇我倉山田麻呂) be made Prince Naka-no-Ōe’s consort.
  5. Main textIn the seventh month of the 1st year, neither the prayers taught by the village priests (祝部) nor the Soga Great Minister’s rotating reading of Mahayana sutras at the temples brought rain; but in the eighth month, when the empress bowed to the four directions and prayed to Heaven on the upper reaches of the river at 南淵*, thunder and rain continued for five days, and the people praised her as “the Emperor of Perfect Virtue” (至徳天皇).
  6. Main textIn this year (the 1st year), Emishi set up an ancestral shrine at 高宮* in Katsuragi and performed the dance of eight rows (八佾). He mobilized people from all over the country to build twin tombs (the Great Tomb and the Lesser Tomb) for himself and Iruka at 今来*, even putting to work the people of the 乳部* of the Upper Palace (上宮). 上宮大娘姫王* was deeply angered at this.

What the main text says

Empress Kōgyoku (天豊財重日足姫天皇) was a granddaughter of Prince 押坂彦人大兄 (押坂彦人大兄皇子)* and a daughter of Prince 茅渟 (茅渟王)*, and she became empress in the 2nd year of Emperor Jomei. She took the throne in the first month of the 1st year and kept Soga no Emishi on as Great Minister, but his son Iruka took charge of the government himself, and his power surpassed his father’s. From the same month, Paekche envoys reported that their country was in great disorder and that members of the royal family such as 翹岐* had been banished to an island, and Koguryŏ envoys also reported that the minister 伊梨柯須彌* had killed the king. 翹岐 was lodged at the house of 阿曇山背連* and was entertained by the Soga Great Minister.

In the seventh month of the 1st year, in response to a drought, the ministers, following the teaching of the village priests (祝部), killed cattle and horses to worship the gods of the shrines, moved the markets, and prayed to the river god (河伯), but without effect. The Soga Great Minister proposed reading Mahayana sutras in rotation at the temples to pray for rain; in the south court of the Great Temple (大寺), images of buddhas and bodhisattvas and of the Four Heavenly Kings were set up and the Great Cloud Sutra (大雲経) and other sutras were read, but only a little rain fell, and the reading was stopped. On the first day of the eighth month, when the empress bowed to the four directions and looked up to Heaven in prayer on the upper reaches of the river at 南淵*, thunder rolled and heavy rain fell for five days; the people cried out “ten thousand years” (万歳) and called her “the Emperor of Perfect Virtue” (至徳天皇).

In the ninth month the empress called up laborers from Ōmi and Koshi to build a great temple, and ordered timber and labor to be gathered from the provinces from Tōtōmi in the east to Aki in the west in order to build palace buildings by the twelfth month. Several thousand Emishi from the Koshi frontier came over and submitted. In the twelfth month the funeral of Emperor Jomei was held and he was buried at 滑谷崗*, and the empress moved to the Oharida Palace (小墾田宮). In this year Emishi set up an ancestral shrine at 高宮* in Katsuragi and performed the dance of eight rows (八佾), and built twin tombs for himself and Iruka at 今来*, even putting to work the people of the 乳部* of the Upper Palace (上宮), so that 上宮大娘姫王* lamented in anger.

In the fourth month of the 2nd year the empress moved to the new Itabuki Palace (板蓋宮) in Asuka. In the ninth month Emperor Jomei was buried at the 押坂陵*, and 吉備嶋皇祖母命* died. In the tenth month Emishi, who was ill and did not attend court, secretly gave Iruka the purple cap, treating him as though he held the rank of Great Minister. Iruka plotted on his own to set aside the princes of the Upper Palace and to make Furuhito no Ōe (古人大兄) emperor, and around this time a children’s song (童謡) was sung.

In the eleventh month Iruka sent 巨勢徳太臣 and 土師娑婆連 (readings unconfirmed) to attack Prince Yamashiro no Ōe and others at Ikaruga. The servant 三成* and others fought them off, and the prince threw a horse’s bones into his sleeping chamber and fled to Mount Ikoma (膽駒山) with his consorts, children and younger kinsmen. 三輪文屋君* urged that if they raised troops on the basis of the 乳部* in the eastern provinces they would certainly win, but the prince refused, saying he did not want the people to suffer for his sake alone. The princes returned to Ikaruga-dera and, surrounded by troops, took their own lives together with their consorts, children and younger kinsmen. When Emishi heard of this, he cursed Iruka’s foolishness.

In the first month of the 3rd year, Nakatomi no Kamako was appointed Head of the Office of Divinities (神祇伯) but declined and withdrew to 三嶋*. Later he became close to Prince Naka-no-Ōe at a ball game, and they consulted again and again on the way to and from Master 南淵*, under whom they studied the teachings of the Duke of Zhou and Confucius. On Kamako’s advice, the daughter of Soga no Kurayamada no Maro became Prince Naka-no-Ōe’s consort. In the seventh month 大生部多*, from beside the 不尽河* in the eastern provinces, urged people to worship an insect as the god of Tokoyo (常世神), and many threw away their wealth, so Hata no Kawakatsu (秦造河勝) of 葛野* struck 多. In the eleventh month Emishi and Iruka built their houses side by side on Amakashi Hill (甘檮崗) and equipped them with stockades and armories.

In the sixth month of the 4th year, Prince Naka-no-Ōe revealed to Kurayamada no Maro (倉山田麻呂臣) the plan to have him read the memorial of the Three Han. On the day the empress came out to the Great Audience Hall, while the memorial was being read, Prince Naka-no-Ōe, 子麻呂* and others cut Iruka down, and Prince Naka-no-Ōe set out Iruka’s crimes before the empress. Prince Naka-no-Ōe entered Hōkō-ji and made ready, and at the words of 高向臣国押*, the 漢直* and others on Emishi’s side dispersed. The next day Emishi and others burned the Tennōki, the Kokki and their treasures, and 船史恵尺* pulled out the Kokki and presented it to Prince Naka-no-Ōe. The day after that, the empress yielded the throne to Prince Karu and made Prince Naka-no-Ōe crown prince.

A plain retelling of what the main text of the Nihon Shoki says (no published translation has been used).

Variant accounts

After its main text, the Nihon Shoki often sets out other versions, introduced with words such as "a certain book says" (一書曰).

A certain text says (或本云)

The main text names the commanders who attacked Prince Yamashiro no Ōe as 巨勢徳太臣 and 土師娑婆連 (readings unconfirmed), but a certain text names them as 巨勢徳太臣 and 倭馬飼首*.

A certain text says (或本云)

Concerning the rain after the empress’s prayer, a certain text records that it rained for five days and the nine grains (九穀) ripened well.

A certain text says (或本云)

The main text says that the place to which the empress moved in the twelfth month of the 1st year was the Oharida Palace, but a certain text says it was a temporary palace in the south court of the Eastern Palace (東宮).

A certain text says (或本云)

Attached to the record of the burial at the 押坂陵* in the ninth month of the 2nd year, a certain text records that Emperor Jomei was called “the 高市 Emperor” (高市天皇)*.

An old text says (舊本云)

Concerning the record that in the first month of the 4th year the cries of monkeys were heard in various places but no monkeys were seen, the old text says that this was an omen that in this year the capital would be moved to Naniwa and the Itabuki Palace would become a ruin. The main text says that people of the time called them messengers of the Great Deity of Ise (伊勢大神).

In the original text

夫損身固國，不亦丈夫者歟。

The words of Prince Yamashiro no Ōe: is it not the mark of a true man to give up his own life, rather than fight, in order to make the country secure?

鞍作盡滅天宗，將傾日位

Prince Naka-no-Ōe’s appeal to the empress that 鞍作 * (that is, Iruka) had wiped out the imperial family and was about to overturn the imperial throne.

自學周孔之教於南淵先生所

The record that Prince Naka-no-Ōe and Nakatomi no Kamako studied the teachings of the Duke of Zhou and Confucius under Master 南淵*.

天下百姓倶稱萬歳，曰：「至德天皇。」

Rain fell after the empress’s prayer, and the people cried out “ten thousand years” and praised her as “the emperor whose virtue is supreme.”
