Empress Jitō — Rule before Enthronement, and Accession

持統天皇の称制と即位

Gathers the records of Empress Jitō’s background and of how, after the death of Emperor Tenmu, she took charge of government and then took the throne in the first month of the 4th year. Among the events recorded are Prince Ōtsu’s rebellion, the death of Prince Kusakabe, the funeral of Emperor Tenmu, the distribution of the code (令), and the appointment of Prince Takechi as Grand Chancellor.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textWhen Emperor Tenmu (天渟中原瀛真人天皇) died in the ninth month of the 1st year of Shuchō, the empress came to court and took charge of government (臨朝称制). In the first month of the 4th year, 物部麻呂朝臣* set up the great shield, 中臣大嶋朝臣* read the words of blessing of the heavenly deities (天神寿詞), and 忌部宿禰色夫知* presented the sacred sword and mirror (神璽), and the empress took the imperial throne.
  2. Main textIn the tenth month of the 1st year of Shuchō, Prince Ōtsu’s rebellion was discovered, and the prince was ordered to die at his house at 訳語田* (he was twenty-four). His consort, Princess 山辺 (山辺皇女)*, ran there with her hair disheveled and her feet bare and died with him, and it is said that all who saw it wept.
  3. Main textOf Prince Ōtsu the main text records that he was of fine bearing and excellent speech, that he was loved by Emperor Tenji (天命開別天皇), that he was fond of learning and writing, and that the flourishing of poetry and rhapsodies (詩賦) began with Ōtsu.
  4. Main textAt the temporary enshrinement palace of Emperor Tenmu, the crown prince led the high officials and the hundred officials in wailing again and again, and the Hayato and the Emishi also delivered eulogies. In the eleventh month of the 2nd year, after a eulogy recounting the order in which the imperial ancestors had succeeded to the throne, the emperor was buried at the 大内陵*. In the fourth month of the following year, the 3rd, the crown prince, Prince Kusakabe (草壁皇子尊), died.
  5. Main textIn the sixth month of the 3rd year a code (令), one work in twenty-two scrolls, was distributed to the government offices, and in the ninth month of the 4th year an edict ordered that household registers be made according to the household statutes (戸令). In the seventh month of the 4th year Prince Takechi was made Grand Chancellor (太政大臣) and 丹比嶋真人* Minister of the Right; in the tenth month Prince Takechi, and in the twelfth month the empress herself, went to view the palace site at Fujiwara (藤原).
  6. Main textIn the tenth month of the 4th year, the empress issued an edict praising 大伴部博麻*. 博麻 had been taken prisoner by the Tang army in the war to aid Paekche, had sold himself so that his comrades could return home, and had remained in a foreign land for thirty years, so he was given rank, rice land and other rewards.

What the main text says

Empress Jitō (高天原広野姫天皇) had the childhood name Princess Uno no Sarara (鸕野讃良皇女); she was the second daughter of Emperor Tenji (天命開別天皇), and her mother was 遠智娘*. In the 3rd year of 天豊財重日足姫天皇 (Empress Kōgyoku, later Empress Saimei) she became a consort of Emperor Tenmu (天渟中原瀛真人天皇), and in the 1st year of Emperor Tenji she bore Prince Kusakabe at the Ōtsu Palace. In the tenth month of the 10th year of Emperor Tenji she followed Emperor Tenmu, who had taken holy orders, into Yoshino, avoiding the suspicion of the court. In the sixth month of the 1st year of Emperor Tenmu she fled with him to the eastern provinces and settled their plans, and in the seventh month Prince Ōtomo was struck down. In the 2nd year she became empress, and it is said that she helped the emperor and often gave her views on government.

In the ninth month of the 1st year of Shuchō Emperor Tenmu died, and the empress took charge of government. In the tenth month Prince Ōtsu’s rebellion was discovered, and more than thirty people whom the prince had deceived were also arrested. The prince was ordered to die at his house at 訳語田*, and his consort Princess 山辺* died with him. The empress pardoned his followers, saying they had had no choice, banishing only 礪杵道作* to Izu and moving the Silla monk 行心* to a temple in Hida. In the eleventh month Princess 大来 (大来皇女)*, who had been serving at the shrine of the god of Ise (伊勢神祠), returned to the capital.

Through the 1st and 2nd years, records of wailing, eulogies and offerings (奠) at the temporary enshrinement palace follow one after another. People from Koguryŏ and Silla who had come over and submitted were settled in Hitachi, 下毛野* and Musashi and given rice land. In the ninth month the Silla prince 金霜林* came; informed in Tsukushi of the emperor’s death, he put on mourning clothes, bowed toward the east, and wailed. In the tenth month the crown prince, leading the high officials, the hundred officials and people of the provinces, began building the 大内陵*.

In the seventh month of the 2nd year, because of drought, a great rain ritual (大雩) was held, and when the Paekche monk 道蔵* was made to pray for rain, rain fell across the realm before the morning was out. In the eleventh month the crown prince led the high officials, the hundred officials and the guests from the various foreign lands (諸蕃) in wailing at the temporary enshrinement palace, and the ministers each delivered eulogies on how their ancestors had served. The Emishi too delivered eulogies, bearing tribute, and after 当麻真人智徳* delivered a eulogy on the order in which the imperial ancestors had succeeded to the throne, the emperor was buried at the 大内陵*.

In the first month of the 3rd year the empress went to the Yoshino Palace. In the fourth month the crown prince, Prince Kusakabe, died. In the fifth month the empress had the Silla condolence envoys told that, because among other things the envoy’s rank was lower than before and there was only one ship, their tribute and gifts would be sealed and returned. In the sixth month the Office for Selecting Good Words (撰善言司) was set up, and a code, one work in twenty-two scrolls, was distributed to the government offices. In the intercalary eighth month she ordered that household registers be made this winter, that vagrants be brought under control, and that one quarter of each province’s men be selected as soldiers and trained in military arts.

In the first month of the 4th year the empress took the throne. In the fourth month the way of granting rank according to officials’ years of service and the colors of court dress were fixed, and in the seventh month the high officials and the hundred officials wore the new court dress for the first time. In the same month Prince Takechi was made Grand Chancellor and 丹比嶋真人* Minister of the Right, and appointments in the eight ministries and the hundred offices were changed. In the tenth month 大伴部博麻* was praised, and Prince Takechi viewed the palace site at Fujiwara. In the eleventh month, by imperial order, the 元嘉 and 儀鳳 calendars (元嘉暦, 儀鳳暦) were used for the first time, and in the twelfth month the empress herself went to Fujiwara to view the palace site.

A plain retelling of what the main text of the Nihon Shoki says (no published translation has been used).

In the original text

皇后臨朝稱制

After the emperor’s death, the empress, without taking the throne, came to court and took charge of government.

詩賦之興，自大津始也

An assessment of Prince Ōtsu: the flourishing of poetry and rhapsodies (詩賦) began with him.

奉上神璽劍、鏡於皇后。皇后即天皇位

The sacred sword and mirror, the divine tokens, were presented to the empress, and the empress took the imperial throne.

朕嘉厥尊朝愛國，賣己顯忠

The empress’s words praising him for honoring the court, loving the country, and showing his loyalty by selling himself.

Deities and people, and the shrines that enshrine them

  • Emperor Tenji天智天皇Enshrined at: Ōmi Jingū
  • Princess Uno no Sarara (鸕野讚良皇女)The childhood name of Empress Jitō. Second daughter of Emperor Tenji (天命開別天皇), she became the empress of Emperor Tenmu (天渟中原瀛真人天皇).
  • Prince Ōtsu (皇子大津)Third son of Emperor Tenmu. His rebellion was discovered and he was ordered to die at the age of twenty-four.
  • 皇女山邊*Consort of Prince Ōtsu. She ran to him at his death and died with him.
  • Prince Kusakabe (草壁皇子尊)As crown prince he led the rites at the temporary enshrinement palace, but he died in the fourth month of the 3rd year.
  • Prince Takechi (皇子高市)Became Grand Chancellor in the seventh month of the 4th year, and in the tenth month went to view the palace site at Fujiwara.
  • 大伴部博麻*A soldier (軍丁) who was taken prisoner by Tang in the war to aid Paekche, sold himself so that his comrades could return home, and came back thirty years later.
