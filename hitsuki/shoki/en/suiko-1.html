Nihon Shoki, Book 22 — Empress Suiko (part 1) · page 38 of 50

The Accession of Empress Suiko, the Twelve Cap Ranks and the Seventeen-Article Constitution

推古天皇の即位と冠位十二階・憲法十七条

After Emperor Sushun was killed, Empress Suiko, urged three times by the officials, ascended the throne at the Toyura Palace, made Prince Umayado no Toyotomimi Crown Prince, and entrusted all government to him. For the 1st to the 12th years, the record includes the edict to promote the Three Treasures and the completion of Hōkō-ji, the war with Silla and the death of Prince Kume, the establishment of the twelve cap ranks, and the Crown Prince's composition of the Seventeen-Article Constitution.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textIn the 4th month of the 1st year, Prince Umayado no Toyotomimi was made Crown Prince and, as regent, was entrusted with all affairs of state. It is written that the prince was born upon coming up against the door of a stable of the horse office, and that he could hear the petitions of ten people at once without error.
  2. Main textIn the 4th month of the 12th year, the Crown Prince himself composed the Seventeen-Article Constitution. Article 1 teaches that harmony is to be valued; Article 2, that the Three Treasures (the Buddha, the Dharma and the Sangha) are to be sincerely revered; and Article 3, that when an imperial command is received it must be obeyed with care.
  3. Main textIn the 12th month of the 11th year, cap ranks were put into effect for the first time; twelve grades, from Greater Virtue down to Lesser Knowledge, were established, and in the 1st month of the 12th year they were conferred on the ministers.
  4. Main textIn the 2nd month of the 2nd year, the empress ordered the Crown Prince and the Great Minister to promote the Three Treasures, and the Omi, Muraji and others competed in building Buddhist halls. In the 3rd year the Koguryŏ monk Eji came and became the Crown Prince's teacher, and in the 11th month of the 4th year Hōkō-ji was completed.
  5. Main textIn the 10th year, Prince Kume was made General for Attacking Silla and twenty-five thousand men were assembled in Tsukushi, but the prince fell ill and died in Tsukushi in the 2nd month of the 11th year. Prince 当麻*, who replaced him, also turned back because of his wife's death, and the expedition was not carried out.
  6. Compared with the KojikiThe Kojiki gives Empress Suiko's palace only as the "小治田宮", but the Nihon Shoki distinguishes between her accession at the Toyura Palace and her move to the Oharida Palace in the 10th month of the 11th year.

What the main text says

Empress Suiko (豊御食炊屋姫天皇*) was a middle daughter of 天國排開廣庭天皇* and the full younger sister of 橘豊日天皇*; when young she was called Princess Nukatabe. At eighteen she became empress of 渟中倉太玉敷天皇*, and when she was thirty-four that emperor died. When she was thirty-nine, 泊瀬部天皇* was killed by the Great Minister Umako no Sukune in the 11th month of his 5th year and the throne became vacant, and the ministers asked the empress to ascend the throne. The empress declined, but when the officials submitted memorials three times urging her, she agreed, and the imperial seal was presented to her. In the 12th month, the empress ascended the throne at the Toyura Palace.

In the 1st month of the 1st year, a Buddha relic was placed inside the foundation stone of the central pillar of Hōkō-ji, and the next day the central pillar was erected. In the 4th month, Prince Umayado no Toyotomimi was made Crown Prince and regent and was entrusted with all affairs of state. It is recorded that the prince was the second son of 橘豊日天皇* and his mother was Princess Anahobe no Hashihito; that he spoke as soon as he was born; that when grown he could hear the petitions of ten people at once without error; and that he studied Buddhist teachings under the Koguryŏ monk Eji and non-Buddhist classics under the scholar 覚哿*. In the 9th month, 橘豊日天皇 was reburied in the 磯長陵* tomb in Kawachi, and in this year construction of Shitennō-ji began at 荒陵* in Naniwa.

In the 2nd month of the 2nd year, the empress ordered the Crown Prince and the Great Minister to promote the Three Treasures; the Omi, Muraji and others competed in building Buddhist halls for the sake of the kindness of their lords and parents, and these were called temples. In the 4th month of the 3rd year, aloeswood (沈水) drifted ashore on Awaji Island; the islanders, not knowing what it was, burned it with firewood, and because the fragrance spread far, they presented it to the court. In the same year the Koguryŏ monk Eji came to settle and became the Crown Prince's teacher, the Paekche monk Esō also came, and the two spread Buddhism and became the pillars of the Three Treasures. In the 11th month of the 4th year Hōkō-ji was completed; 善徳臣*, son of the Great Minister, became its temple administrator, and Eji and Esō began to live there. In the 5th year the king of Paekche sent Prince 阿佐*; in the 6th year Silla presented a peacock, and in the 7th year Paekche presented a camel and other animals; and in the 4th month of the 7th year, an earthquake destroyed houses and the empress had the god of earthquakes worshiped.

In the 2nd month of the 8th year, because Silla and Mimana were attacking each other, the empress sought to rescue Mimana. In this year, with 境部臣* as commander-in-chief and 穂積臣* as deputy general, more than ten thousand troops were sent out; they captured five Silla fortresses, and the king of Silla raised a white flag, ceded six fortresses and asked to surrender. Silla and Mimana sent envoys bearing tribute and submitted a memorial saying that from then on they would not attack each other and would pay tribute every year; but when the generals were called back, Silla again invaded Mimana. In the 2nd month of the 9th year, the Crown Prince built a palace at Ikaruga for the first time. In the 3rd month, 大伴連囓* was sent to Koguryŏ and 坂本臣糠手* to Paekche, with orders to urge them to rescue Mimana quickly, and in the 9th month 迦摩多*, a Silla spy captured on Tsushima, was exiled to Kōzuke.

In the 2nd month of the 10th year, Prince Kume was made General for Attacking Silla and was given an army of twenty-five thousand men including the 神部*, the Kuni-no-Miyatsuko and the Tomo-no-Miyatsuko; in the 4th month the prince set up camp in 嶋郡* in Tsukushi and gathered ships and provisions. But in the 6th month the prince fell ill, so the expedition could not be carried out. In the 10th month, the Paekche monk Kanroku presented calendars, books of astronomy and geography, and books of 遁甲* and occult arts, and selected students studied them and mastered those fields. In the 2nd month of the 11th year, Prince Kume died in Tsukushi, and the empress grieved that he had not been able to accomplish the great undertaking before him. In the 4th month, his elder brother Prince 当麻* was made general in his place, but in the 7th month, because his wife 舎人姫王*, who was accompanying him, died at 赤石*, Prince 当麻 turned back, and the expedition was not carried out.

In the 10th month of the 11th year, the empress moved to the Oharida Palace. In the 11th month, Hata no Kawakatsu received a revered Buddha image that the Crown Prince possessed, worshiped it, and built 蜂岡寺*. In the 12th month, cap ranks were put into effect for the first time, in twelve grades: Greater Virtue, Lesser Virtue, Greater Benevolence, Lesser Benevolence, Greater Propriety, Lesser Propriety, Greater Faith, Lesser Faith, Greater Righteousness, Lesser Righteousness, Greater Knowledge and Lesser Knowledge; caps sewn from silk (絁) of the color of each rank were to be used. In the 1st month of the 12th year, cap ranks were conferred on the ministers for the first time.

In the 4th month of the 12th year, the Crown Prince himself composed the Seventeen-Article Constitution. Article 1 teaches that harmony is to be valued, Article 2 requires that the Three Treasures be sincerely revered, and Article 3 that when an imperial command is received it must be obeyed with care. There follow articles such as: that propriety is to be the foundation; that lawsuits are to be judged fairly; that officials are to come to court early and leave late; that trustworthiness is the foundation of righteousness; that one is to turn away from private interest and toward the public good; and that the people are to be employed only at suitable times; and Article 17 teaches that important matters are not to be decided by one person alone but discussed with many. In the 9th month, court etiquette was revised: it was decided that when entering or leaving the palace gate one should place both hands on the ground and kneel on both knees, and after crossing the threshold rise and walk. In the same month, the 黄書画師* and 山背画師* painters were established.

A plain retelling of what the main text of the Nihon Shoki says (no published translation has been used).

In the original text

以和爲貴，無忤爲宗

Value harmony above all, and make not opposing others the first principle (Article 1 of the Seventeen-Article Constitution).

篤敬三寶。三寶者，佛法僧也

Sincerely revere the Three Treasures. The Three Treasures are the Buddha, the Dharma and the Sangha (Article 2).

一聞十人訴，以勿失能辨

He could hear the petitions of ten people at once and distinguish them without error.

天上有神，地有天皇

In heaven above there are gods, and on earth below there is the emperor (words from the memorial of Silla and Mimana).

Deities and people, and the shrines that enshrine them

  • 豊御食炊屋姫天皇*Empress Suiko. Empress of 渟中倉太玉敷天皇*, she ascended the throne after the death of Emperor Sushun.
  • Prince Umayado no Toyotomimi (廐户豐聰耳皇子)Prince Umayado. He became Crown Prince and regent and, after the establishment of the cap ranks, composed the Seventeen-Article Constitution.
  • The Great Minister Umako no Sukune (大臣馬子宿禰)Great Minister. Together with the Crown Prince he was ordered to promote the Three Treasures. His son 善徳臣* became temple administrator of Hōkō-ji.
  • Eji (慧慈)A monk of Koguryŏ. He came in the 3rd year, became the Crown Prince's teacher, and lived at Hōkō-ji.
  • Esō (慧聰)A monk of Paekche. Together with Eji he was regarded as a pillar of the Three Treasures.
  • 境部臣*The commander-in-chief who attacked Silla in the 8th year to rescue Mimana.
  • Prince Kume (來目皇子)He became General for Attacking Silla but fell ill, and died in Tsukushi in the 11th year.
  • 當麻皇子*Elder brother of Prince Kume. He became general in his place but turned back because of his wife's death.
  • Kanroku (觀勒)A monk of Paekche. He presented calendars, books of astronomy and geography, and books of 遁甲* and occult arts.
  • Hata no Kawakatsu (秦造河勝)He received the Crown Prince's Buddha image and built 蜂岡寺*.

In the Kojiki

Kojiki, Part 3, final section — section on Empress Suiko (豐御食炊屋比賣命*)

The Kojiki gives Empress Suiko's palace only as the 小治田宮*. The Nihon Shoki says that she ascended the throne at the Toyura Palace and moved to the Oharida Palace in the 10th month of the 11th year.

豐御食炊屋比賣命。坐小治田宮
Kojiki, Part 3 — entry on the empresses, consorts and children in the section on Emperor Kinmei

In the Kojiki, "豐御氣炊屋比賣命" appears among the children born to Emperor Kinmei by 岐多斯比賣*, daughter of the Great Minister Iname no Sukune. The Nihon Shoki makes her a middle daughter of 天國排開廣庭天皇* and the full younger sister of 橘豊日天皇*, and records her childhood name as Princess Nukatabe, but this childhood name could not be confirmed in the Kojiki.

次豐御氣炊屋比賣命
Kojiki, Part 3 — entry on the empresses, consorts and children in the section on Emperor Yōmei

In the section on Emperor Yōmei, the Kojiki only gives the name "上宮之厩戶豐聰耳命" among his children; for his becoming Crown Prince and regent, or for the twelve cap ranks and the Seventeen-Article Constitution, no corresponding entries could be confirmed in the Kojiki.
