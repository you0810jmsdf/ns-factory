Nihon Shoki, Book 27 — Emperor Tenji · page 45 of 50

Emperor Tenji — Hakusukinoe and the Move of the Capital to Ōmi

天智天皇と白村江・近江遷都

After the death of Empress Saimei, the crown prince ruled without formally ascending the throne (称制) and continued the effort to aid Paekche; this book records how, after his defeat by the Tang fleet at Hakusukinoe (白村江) in the eighth month of the 2nd year, he strengthened the defenses with border guards (防人), beacons (烽), the Water Fortress (水城) and castles. It covers the move of the capital to Ōmi in the 6th year, his accession in the 7th year, the appointment of Prince Ōtomo as Grand Chancellor (太政大臣) in the 10th year, and the Crown Prince (東宮; the Imperial Younger Brother, 大皇弟) taking holy orders and entering Yoshino, up to the emperor’s death at the Ōmi Palace in the twelfth month.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textIn the eighth month of the 2nd year, the Japanese (日本) fleet fought the Tang fleet at Hakusukinoe; attacking without regard to the conditions, it was caught from left and right, many drowned, and it was defeated. King 豐璋* of Paekche fled to Koguryŏ, and in the ninth month the fortress of 州柔 (州柔城)* surrendered to Tang.
  2. Main textAfter the defeat, in this year (the 3rd year), guards (防) and beacons (烽) were placed on Tsushima and Iki and in Tsukushi, and in Tsukushi a great embankment was built to hold water and was named the “Water Fortress” (水城). In the 4th year castles were built in Nagato province, and two castles, 大野 and 椽 (readings unconfirmed), in Tsukushi.
  3. Main textIn the second month of the 3rd year the emperor ordered the Imperial Younger Brother (大皇弟) to reform the cap ranks into twenty-six grades, granted great swords, small swords, and shields, bows and arrows to the clan heads (氏上), and fixed the 民部 and 家部.
  4. Main textIn the third month of the 6th year the capital was moved to Ōmi, but the people did not want the move; many remonstrated indirectly, there were many children’s songs (童謡), and fires were frequent. The crown prince took the throne in the first month of the 7th year (a certain text says he took the throne in the third month of the 6th year).
  5. Main textIn the tenth month of the 8th year, the emperor visited the ailing Inner Minister Fujiwara (藤原内大臣) and sent the Imperial Younger Brother to confer on him the Great Woven Cap (大織冠) and the rank of Great Minister, and to grant him the surname Fujiwara. The Inner Minister died the next day.
  6. Main textIn the first month of the 10th year Prince Ōtomo became Grand Chancellor. In the tenth month, when the ailing emperor wished to entrust affairs after his death to the Crown Prince (東宮), the Crown Prince asked that they be entrusted instead to the Empress (大后) and Prince Ōtomo (大友王), took holy orders, and entered Yoshino. The emperor died at the Ōmi Palace in the twelfth month.

What the main text says

Emperor Tenji (天命開別天皇) was the heir (太子) of Emperor Jomei (息長足日廣額天皇), and his mother was Empress Saimei (天豐財重日足姬天皇). He became crown prince in the 4th year of his mother’s reign, and when his mother died in the seventh month of the 7th year, he wore mourning clothes and governed without ascending the throne (称制). The crown prince moved to the 長津宮 palace* to receive news of military affairs overseas, and in the eighth month sent 阿曇比邏夫連*, Abe no Hikita no Hirafu (阿倍引田比邏夫臣) and others to aid Paekche. In the ninth month he conferred the Woven Cap (織冠) on Prince 豐璋* of Paekche, married him to the younger sister of 多臣蔣敷*, and had 狹井連檳榔 and 秦造田來津 (readings unconfirmed) escort him to his home country with an army of more than five thousand. When 豐璋 entered the country, 鬼室福信* welcomed him and entrusted all the affairs of the state to him.

In the first month of the 1st year, 福信 was given a hundred thousand arrows, seed rice and other goods. In the third month Tang and Silla attacked Koguryŏ, and at Koguryŏ’s request Japan stationed commanders at the fortress of 疏留 (疏留城)*. In the fifth month 阿曇比邏夫連* escorted 豐璋 and others to Paekche with a fleet of 170 ships, conveyed the imperial command and had 豐璋 succeed to the kingship, and gave 福信 a golden tablet (金策), stroking his back in praise. In the twelfth month 豐璋 and 福信 discussed moving to 避城*, saying that 州柔* was far from the fields and its land was poor; 朴市田來津* alone remonstrated that 避城 was too close to the enemy, but he was not heeded.

In the second month of the 2nd year, Silla burned the four southern provinces of Paekche and took strategic places, so 避城* was too close to the enemy to live in, and, just as 田來津 had said, they returned to 州柔*. In the third month 上毛野君稚子* and others led 27,000 men against Silla, and in the sixth month took the two fortresses of 沙鼻 and 岐奴江 (readings unconfirmed). In the same month 豐璋 suspected 福信 of harboring rebellious intentions, seized him and had him beheaded. In the eighth month the Tang commanders drew up 170 warships at Hakusukinoe, and the Japanese fleet had the worst of the first engagement. The next day the Japanese commanders and the king of Paekche advanced, saying, “If we compete to be first, the enemy will surely retreat,” but they were caught from left and right by the Tang army and defeated; many fell into the water and died, 田來津 too was killed, and 豐璋 fled by boat to Koguryŏ. When the fortress of 州柔 surrendered in the ninth month, the people of Paekche said, “The name of Paekche ends today,” and set out for Japan with the Japanese fleet from the fortress of 弖禮 (弖禮城)*.

In the second month of the 3rd year the emperor ordered the Imperial Younger Brother to set the cap ranks at twenty-six grades, and to fix the clan heads (氏上) and the 民部 and 家部. In the fifth month 郭務悰* and others, envoys of 劉仁願*, the garrison commander in Paekche (百済鎮将), arrived. In this year guards and beacons were placed on Tsushima and Iki and in Tsukushi, and the Water Fortress was built in Tsukushi. In the second month of the 4th year Empress Hashihito (間人大后) died; the official ranks of Paekche were examined and the rank 小錦下 was conferred on 鬼室集斯*, and more than four hundred Paekche men and women were settled in 神前 district (神前郡)* in Ōmi province. In the eighth month Paekche officials of 達率 rank and others were made to build castles in Nagato province and at 大野 and 椽 (readings unconfirmed) in Tsukushi, and in the ninth month 劉德高* and others came from Tang. In winter of the 5th year more than two thousand Paekche men and women were settled in the eastern provinces.

In the second month of the 6th year, Empress Saimei (天豐財重日足姬天皇) and Princess Hashihito (間人皇女) were buried together in the 小市岡上陵*, and the crown prince told the ministers that, following his mother’s words, he would not impose on the people the labor of building a stone burial chamber (石槨). In the third month the capital was moved to Ōmi, but the people did not want it; many remonstrated, there were many children’s songs, and fires broke out frequently by day and by night. In the eleventh month the castles of 高安 in Yamato (倭国), 屋嶋 in Sanuki province and 金田 in Tsushima province (readings unconfirmed) were built. In the first month of the 7th year the crown prince took the throne, and in the second month he made 倭姬王*, daughter of Prince Furuhito no Ōe, his empress and took four consorts (嬪). The text records that among their children Princess Uno (鸕野皇女) and Princess 阿陪 (阿陪皇女)* later ruled the realm, and that Prince Iga (伊賀皇子), son of 伊賀采女宅子娘*, was later called Prince Ōtomo.

In the fifth month of the 7th year, the emperor went hunting on 蒲生野*, accompanied by the Imperial Younger Brother, the princes, the Inner Minister and the ministers. In the tenth month the Tang commander-in-chief 英公* destroyed Koguryŏ. In this year the monk 道行* stole the Kusanagi sword (草薙剣) and tried to flee to Silla, but lost his way in wind and rain on the road and came back. In the eighth month of the 8th year the emperor climbed the peak of 高安 (高安嶺)* and discussed repairing the castle, but gave it up out of consideration for the people’s weariness. In the tenth month, when he visited the ailing Inner Minister Fujiwara, the Inner Minister asked that his funeral be kept simple; the emperor sent the Imperial Younger Brother to confer on him the Great Woven Cap, the rank of Great Minister and the surname Fujiwara, but the Inner Minister died the next day.

In the second month of the 9th year household registers were compiled, and robbers and vagrants were cracked down on. In the middle of the night in the fourth month Hōryū-ji burned down, and not a single building was left. In the first month of the 10th year Prince Ōtomo was made Grand Chancellor, Soga no Akae (蘇我赤兄臣) Minister of the Left and 中臣金連* Minister of the Right, and the cap ranks and laws were put into effect. In the fourth month the water clock (漏剋) that the emperor had made himself when he was crown prince was set on a new platform and struck the hours for the first time. In the ninth month the emperor fell ill, and in the tenth month he summoned the Crown Prince (東宮) and entrusted him with affairs after his death, but the Crown Prince declined and asked that they be entrusted to the Empress (大后) and Prince Ōtomo (大友王); he shaved his head on the spot, became a monk (沙門), and entered Yoshino. In the eleventh month Prince Ōtomo and five senior ministers, holding censers before the woven Buddha image in the inner palace, swore to obey the emperor’s commands, and in the twelfth month the emperor died at the Ōmi Palace and was laid in temporary enshrinement in the new palace.

A plain retelling of what the main text of the Nihon Shoki says (no published translation has been used).

Variant accounts

After its main text, the Nihon Shoki often sets out other versions, introduced with words such as "a certain book says" (一書曰).

A certain text says (或本云)

The main text says that the crown prince took the throne in the first month of the 7th year, but a certain text says he took the throne in the third month of the 6th year (丁卯).

A certain text says (或本云)

It names the daughter of the Great Minister Soga no Yamada no Ishikawa no Maro (蘇我山田石川麻呂大臣) not as 遠智娘 but as 茅渟娘 (readings unconfirmed), and gives the children she bore as Princess 大田 (大田皇女) and Princess 娑羅羅 (娑羅羅皇女) (readings unconfirmed); the main text gives Princess 大田, Princess Uno (鸕野皇女) and Prince 建 (建皇子)*.

A certain text says (或本云)

The main text says that the Crown Prince, the Imperial Younger Brother (東宮太皇弟), proclaimed the enforcement of the cap ranks and laws, but a certain text says that Prince Ōtomo issued the proclamation.

A certain text says (或本云)

The main text says that the emperor fell ill in the ninth month, but a certain text says the eighth month.

The 日本世記* and an inscription (碑)

The main text gives no age for Inner Minister Fujiwara, but the 日本世記*, quoted in a note, says that he died at his private residence at the age of fifty, while the inscription quoted after it says fifty-six.

In the original text

官軍敗績。赴水溺死者眾。艫舳不得迴旋。

The scene at the battle of Hakusukinoe in which the Japanese army was defeated: many fell into the water and drowned, and the ships could not even turn around.

於對馬嶋、壹岐嶋、筑紫國等，置防與烽。

The record that in this year (the 3rd year), guards (防) and beacons (烽) were placed on Tsushima and Iki, in Tsukushi and elsewhere.

天下百姓不願遷都，諷諫者多，童謠亦眾。

It means that the people did not want the move of the capital to Ōmi, and that many remonstrated indirectly and there were also many children’s songs about it.

授大織冠與大臣位。仍賜姓為藤原氏。

The record that the ailing Inner Minister was given the Great Woven Cap and the rank of Great Minister, and was granted the surname Fujiwara.

Deities and people, and the shrines that enshrine them

  • Emperor Tenji天智天皇Enshrined at: Ōmi Jingū
  • Emperor Tenji (天命開別天皇)Heir of Emperor Jomei (息長足日廣額天皇). After his mother’s death he governed without ascending the throne and pressed on with the aid to Paekche; he moved the capital to Ōmi and took the throne in the 7th year.
  • The Imperial Younger Brother (大皇弟)The emperor’s younger brother and Crown Prince (東宮). He was ordered to reform the cap ranks; at the emperor’s sickbed he declined to take charge of affairs after the emperor’s death, took holy orders, and entered Yoshino.
  • 豐璋*A Paekche prince who had been serving in Japan; he was given the Woven Cap and became king of Paekche, but he beheaded 福信* and fled to Koguryŏ after the defeat at Hakusukinoe.
  • 鬼室福信*A Paekche official of 佐平 rank. He welcomed 豐璋 and entrusted the government to him, but was suspected of rebellious intentions and beheaded by 豐璋.
  • 朴市田來津*A Japanese commander who escorted 豐璋*. He remonstrated against the move to 避城*, and was killed at Hakusukinoe after slaying dozens of men.
  • Inner Minister Fujiwara (藤原內大臣)A senior minister who, on his sickbed in the tenth month of the 8th year, was given the Great Woven Cap, the rank of Great Minister and the surname Fujiwara, and died the next day.
  • Prince Ōtomo (大友皇子)Son of 伊賀采女宅子娘*, also called Prince Iga (伊賀皇子); he became Grand Chancellor in the first month of the 10th year and, together with the senior ministers, swore to obey the emperor’s commands.
  • Soga no Akae (蘇我赤兄臣)After serving as Commander of Tsukushi (筑紫率), he became Minister of the Left in the first month of the 10th year and took the oath together with Prince Ōtomo.
  • 郭務悰*A man who came repeatedly, as an envoy of 劉仁願*, the garrison commander in Paekche, and as an envoy of the Tang state.
  • 倭姬王*Daughter of Prince Furuhito no Ōe; she was made empress in the second month of the 7th year.
