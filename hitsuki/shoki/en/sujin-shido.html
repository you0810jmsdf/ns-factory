Nihon Shoki, Book 5 — Emperor Sujin · page 14 of 50

The Generals of the Four Roads and the Rebellion of 武埴安彦*, up to Hashihaka and the Sacred Treasures of Izumo

四道将軍と武埴安彦の反乱（箸墓・出雲の神宝まで）

This page covers the period from the edict of the 10th year sending generals in the four directions, through the rebellion of 武埴安彦* that broke out before they departed and the story of Yamato-totohi-momoso-hime-no-Mikoto, Ōmononushi-no-Kami and Hashihaka (箸墓), to the census of the people in the 12th year and the title "御肇國天皇". It then records the dream divination to choose the crown prince, the quarrel between brothers over the sacred treasures of Izumo, the building of ponds, and tribute from Mimana, and the book ends with the emperor's death in the 68th year.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textIn the 9th month of the 10th year, Ōbiko-no-Mikoto (大彦命) was sent to Hokuriku (北陸), 武渟川別* to Tōkai (東海), 吉備津彦* to the western road (西道), and 丹波道主命* to Tanba, with orders to raise troops and attack any who did not follow the teachings; they were given seals and ribbons of office and made generals.
  2. Main textYamato-totohi-momoso-hime-no-Mikoto saw that the song of a girl heard by Ōbiko-no-Mikoto at 和珥坂* was a sign of rebellion by 武埴安彦*, and also told how his wife 吾田媛* had taken earth from Mount Kagu (香山) in Yamato and prayed, saying "倭國之物實". The rebellion was put down when 五十狹芹彦命* struck down 吾田媛 at Ōsaka (大坂), and 彦國葺* shot 武埴安彦 dead in Yamashiro (山背).
  3. Main textYamato-totohi-momoso-hime-no-Mikoto became the wife of Ōmononushi-no-Kami, but the deity came only at night. When she asked to see his form, the deity answered that in the morning he would be inside her comb box; when she looked, there was a beautiful small snake, and she cried out, and the deity, ashamed, returned to 御諸山*. The princess stabbed her genitals with a chopstick and died; she was buried at 大市*, and her tomb was called Hashihaka (箸墓).
  4. Main textIn the 9th month of the 12th year, the people were surveyed for the first time, and the bow-tip tribute (弭調) was imposed on men and the hand-end tribute (手末調) on women. Because the deities of heaven and earth were appeased and the realm became peaceful, the emperor was praised as "御肇國天皇".
  5. Compared with the KojikiThe Kojiki also records the first imposition of the bow-tip tribute on men and the hand-end tribute on women (男弓端之調, 女手末之調), and praises that reign with the title "所知初國之御眞木天皇". The way this title of praise is written differs from the Nihon Shoki's "御肇國天皇".
  6. CommentaryKokugakuin University's database of deity names states that it has been argued that the form of Ōmononushi-no-Kami is not clearly shown in the Kojiki, but that in the Nihon Shoki's entry for the 9th month of the 10th year of Emperor Sujin it is revealed to be a "small snake" (小蛇). What the main text says

In the 7th month of the 10th year, the emperor told his ministers that the basis of guiding the people lies in teaching and civilizing them, and that because he had revered the deities of heaven and earth the disasters had ceased, but that people in distant regions still did not follow the king's teachings; and he issued an edict to select ministers and send them in the four directions. In the 9th month he sent Ōbiko-no-Mikoto (大彦命) to Hokuriku (北陸), 武渟川別* to Tōkai (東海), 吉備津彦* to the western road (西道), and 丹波道主命* to Tanba, ordering them to raise troops and attack any who would not accept the teachings, and he gave them seals and ribbons of office and made them generals.

When Ōbiko-no-Mikoto reached the top of 和珥坂*, a girl was singing an enigmatic song. When he asked what she meant, the girl answered that she was only singing, repeated the same song, and vanished. When Ōbiko-no-Mikoto went back and reported this, Yamato-totohi-momoso-hime-no-Mikoto, the emperor's aunt, who was wise and able to know things before they happened, said that this was a sign that 武埴安彦* would rebel. She told him she had heard that 吾田媛*, the wife of 武埴安彦, had come secretly, taken earth from Mount Kagu (香山) in Yamato, prayed, saying, "This is the 物實 of the land of Yamato," and gone back, and she urged him to act quickly. While the generals were being held back for consultation, 武埴安彦 and 吾田媛 raised troops and tried to attack the capital, the husband from Yamashiro (山背) and the wife from Ōsaka (大坂).

The emperor sent 五十狹芹彦命*, who met the army of 吾田媛* at Ōsaka (大坂), crushed it, and killed 吾田媛. Ōbiko and 彦國葺*, a distant ancestor of the 和珥臣*, headed for Yamashiro; they set down a sacred jar (忌瓮) at the top of the 和珥* slope and then encamped on Mount Nara (那羅山) (it is called Mount Nara because the soldiers trampled the grass and trees flat). Facing 武埴安彦* across the river 輪韓河* (the origin of the name 挑河, the present 泉河; readings unconfirmed), 彦國葺 answered that his were righteous troops acting on the emperor's command. The arrow that 武埴安彦 shot first missed, while 彦國葺's arrow struck 武埴安彦 in the chest and killed him. It is said that from the places where the fleeing soldiers were pursued and cut down, where soldiers took off their armor, where they soiled their loincloths, and where they bowed their heads, came the place names 羽振苑, 伽和羅, 屎褌 (the present 樟葉) and 我君 (readings unconfirmed).

After that, Yamato-totohi-momoso-hime-no-Mikoto became the wife of Ōmononushi-no-Kami, but the deity was never seen by day and came only at night. The princess asked him, since she could not see his form in the daytime, to stay until the next morning and show her his beautiful form. The deity said this was reasonable, and answered that the next morning he would be inside her comb box, but asked her not to be startled by his form. When morning came and she looked in the comb box, there was a beautiful small snake about the size of a garment cord, and the princess was startled and cried out. The deity, ashamed, took human form and said, "You have put me to shame, so now I will put you to shame," and he trod the great sky and climbed to 御諸山*. The princess looked up after him in regret, sat down abruptly, stabbed her genitals with a chopstick and died. She was buried at 大市*, and her tomb was called Hashihaka (箸墓). It is said that the tomb was built by people by day and by the deity by night, and the text records that the people passed the stones of Mount Ōsaka (大坂山) from hand to hand from the mountain to the tomb; a song of the people of the time is added.

In the 10th month, the emperor issued an edict stating that the rebels had all been struck down and the home provinces were at peace, but that the unrest of the wild peoples beyond the sea had not yet ceased, and he ordered the generals of the four roads to set out quickly; on the day (丙子) the generals set out. In the 4th month of the 11th year, the generals of the four roads reported that they had pacified the barbarians (戎夷), and in that year many people of foreign customs submitted and the land was at peace. In the 3rd month of the 12th year, the emperor stated that at first his virtue had been insufficient and disasters such as epidemics had occurred, but that he had amended his offences, revered the deities of heaven and earth, spread his teachings and attacked those who would not obey, so that now even people beyond the sea had come to submit; and he issued an edict to survey the people anew and make known the order of their ages and the precedence of labor duties. In the 9th month the people were surveyed for the first time, and the bow-tip tribute (弭調) was imposed on men and the hand-end tribute (手末調) on women. The deities of heaven and earth were appeased, wind and rain came in their season, the hundred grains ripened and the realm became peaceful, so the emperor was praised as "御肇國天皇". In the 17th year, because the people of the coast were suffering for lack of boats, he had the provinces build boats, and in the 10th month ships were built for the first time.

In the 1st month of the 48th year, the emperor decided to determine by dream divination which of 豐城命 (Toyokiirihiko-no-Mikoto) and 活目尊* should be his heir. The elder brother, 豐城命, reported a dream in which he climbed 御諸山*, faced east, thrust a spear eight times and swung a sword eight times; the younger, 活目尊, reported a dream in which he climbed to the summit of 御諸山, stretched ropes in the four directions and drove away sparrows eating the millet. The emperor judged that since the elder had faced only east he should govern the eastern lands, and since the younger had looked out over all four directions he should succeed to the throne; in the 4th month he made 活目尊 crown prince and had 豐城命 govern the eastern lands. 豐城命 is regarded as the founding ancestor of the 上毛野君 and the 下毛野君 (readings unconfirmed).

In the 7th month of the 60th year, the emperor said he wished to see the sacred treasures that 武日照命* had brought from heaven and deposited in the great shrine of the Great Deity of Izumo (出雲大神宮), and sent 武諸隅*. Since 出雲振根*, who had charge of the sacred treasures, had gone to Tsukushi, his younger brother 飯入根* received the imperial command and presented them. When 振根 returned he resented his younger brother; he lured him to the pool of 止屋*, wore a fake sword made of wood himself, and while they bathed took his brother's real sword and wore it. The younger brother could not draw the wooden sword and was killed, and the people of the time composed a song. Receiving a complaint, the court sent 吉備津彦* and 武渟河別* to strike down 出雲振根. The Izumo no Omi and others were afraid and for a time did not worship the Great Deity, but 氷香戸邊* of 氷上* in Tanba conveyed the strange words of an infant to the crown prince, and the emperor had the worship performed. In the 62nd year, declaring that agriculture is the great foundation of the realm, he had ponds and channels dug, and the ponds 依網池, 苅坂池 and 反折池 (readings unconfirmed) were built. In the 7th month of the 65th year, the country of Mimana sent 蘇那曷叱知* to bring tribute, and in the 12th month of the 68th year the emperor died at the age of one hundred and twenty; in the 8th month of the following year he was buried in the 山邊道上陵* tomb.

Variant accounts

After its main text, the Nihon Shoki often sets out other versions, introduced with words such as "a certain book says" (一書曰).

One account (一云): where Ōbiko-no-Mikoto heard the girl's song

Whereas the main text says that Ōbiko-no-Mikoto heard the girl's song at the top of 和珥坂*, one account (一云) says that a young girl by the roadside sang when he arrived at 平坂* in Yamashiro.

One account (一云): another form of the girl's song

For the girl's song, one account (一云) gives a different form of the song, beginning with "於朋耆妬庸利".

One account (一云) and another account (又云): 武日照命*

Of 武日照命*, who brought the sacred treasures from heaven, one account (一云) gives the name as 武夷鳥 and another account (又云) as 天夷鳥 (readings unconfirmed).

Variant account (一書云): 武諸隅*

Of 武諸隅*, who was sent to have the sacred treasures presented, a variant account (一書) gives another name, 大母隅*.

One account (一云): the three ponds

Of the ponds 依網池, 苅坂池 and 反折池 (readings unconfirmed), one account (一云) says that the emperor built these three ponds while he was living at the 桑間宮* palace.

In the original text

是倭國之物實

The words of 吾田媛* as she wrapped up the earth of Mount Kagu (香山) and prayed: this is the 物實 of the land of Yamato (the thing that represents the land).

吾明旦入汝櫛笥而居。願無驚吾形

The words of Ōmononushi-no-Kami: tomorrow morning I will be inside your comb box, but please do not be startled by my form.

是墓者日也人作。夜也神作

This tomb (Hashihaka) was built by people by day and by the deity by night.

故稱謂御肇國天皇也

Therefore the emperor was praised as "御肇國天皇".

In the Kojiki

Kojiki, Part 2, section on Emperor Sujin (dispatch to the provinces)

In the Kojiki, Ōbiko-no-Mikoto (大毘古命) is sent to the 高志道*, his son Takenunakawawake-no-Mikoto (建沼河別命) to the twelve roads of the east (東方十二道), and 日子坐王* to the province of 旦波*; no dispatch to Kibi is recorded. The Nihon Shoki sends four men — Ōbiko-no-Mikoto (大彦命), 武渟川別*, 吉備津彦* and 丹波道主命* — to Hokuriku, Tōkai, the western road and Tanba.

大毘古命者。遣高志道
Kojiki, Part 2, section on Emperor Sujin (the rebellion of 建波邇安王)*

In the Kojiki, the emperor himself interprets the meaning of the girl's song as a sign of rebellion by "我之庶兄建波邇安王" (my elder half-brother 建波邇安王), and neither 吾田媛* nor Yamato-totohi-momoso-hime-no-Mikoto appears. The plot in which the two sides face each other across a river and 建波邇安王* dies by the arrow of 日子國夫玖命*, and the origin of the place name "屎褌" (which the Kojiki identifies with the present 久須婆 and the Nihon Shoki with the present 樟葉; readings unconfirmed), resemble the Nihon Shoki.

我之庶兄建波邇安王。起邪心之表耳
Kojiki, Part 2, section on Emperor Keikō (Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto's slaying of 出雲建)*

A song closely resembling the song of the people of the time when, in the Nihon Shoki, 出雲振根* kills his younger brother with a wooden sword appears in the Kojiki, in the section on Emperor Keikō, as a song of Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto (倭建命) after he killed 出雲建* using a fake sword (詐刀) of 赤檮* wood.
