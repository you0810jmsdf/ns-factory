Nihon Shoki, Book 10 — Emperor Ōjin · page 24 of 50

The immigrant peoples and the appointment of 菟道稚郎子* as Crown Prince

渡来の人々と菟道稚郎子の立太子

Covering the 14th to the 41st year, this section centers on entries about craftswomen, scholars and other people who came from Paekche, Wu (呉) and elsewhere, and also records the visit of the 国樔 people* of Yoshino, 兄媛* of Kibi, the koto made from the ship 枯野*, and other matters. In the 40th year the Emperor designates 菟道稚郎子* as his successor, and the book ends with his death at the 明宮 palace* in the 41st year; the revolt of 大山守命* is not recorded in this book.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textIn the 15th year the king of Paekche had 阿直岐* deliver two fine horses, and 阿直岐, who could read the classics, became tutor to the Crown Prince 菟道稚郎子*. Because 阿直岐 named 王仁* as a better scholar, 王仁 was summoned; he came in the 16th year, and the Crown Prince studied the various classics under him and mastered them all.
  2. Main textIn the 14th year 弓月君* came from Paekche and reported that the laborers of 120 districts he had brought were being held back by Silla and remained in Kara (加羅). 葛城襲津彦* did not return even after three years; in the 16th year, when 木菟宿禰* and others advanced troops to the border of Silla, the laborers of 弓月 came together with 襲津彦. In the 20th year 阿知使主*, ancestor of the 倭漢直*, and others came leading their people of seventeen districts.
  3. Main textIn the 40th year the Emperor asked 大山守命* and 大鷦鷯尊* which they held dearer, an older child or a younger one, and was pleased with 大鷦鷯尊, who answered the younger. The Emperor had long wished to make 菟道稚郎子* Crown Prince; he made 菟道稚郎子 his successor, gave 大山守命 charge of the mountains, rivers, forests and fields, and entrusted 大鷦鷯尊 with assisting the Crown Prince and handling the affairs of state.
  4. Main textIn the 28th year, because a memorial from the king of Koguryŏ (高麗) contained the words "高麗王教日本國也", the Crown Prince 菟道稚郎子*, angered at its discourtesy, reproached the envoy and tore up the memorial.
  5. Main textIn the 31st year the old government ship 枯野* was used as firewood to boil salt, and the salt was distributed to the provinces so that they would build ships. A koto made from the wood left unburned is said to have sounded clear and carried far.
  6. Compared with the KojikiThe exchange in which the successor is decided also appears in the Kojiki, but there 大山守命* is assigned the administration of the mountains and seas, 大雀命* the government of the realm (食国), and 宇遅能和紀郎子* is designated to rule as the heavenly successor (天津日継); after the Emperor's death the revolt of 大山守命 is related. For the Emperor's age at death, the Nihon Shoki gives 110 and the Kojiki 130.

What the main text says

In the 2nd month of the 14th year, the king of Paekche presented 真毛津*, a craftswoman who sewed garments; she is said to be the founding ancestor of the 来目衣縫*. In this year 弓月君* came from Paekche and reported that he had wished to come and settle with the laborers of 120 districts, but that they had been held back by the people of Silla and remained in the land of Kara (加羅). 葛城襲津彦* was therefore sent to Kara to bring them, but even after three years 襲津彦 did not return.

In the 8th month of the 15th year, the king of Paekche sent 阿直岐* to present two fine horses. They were kept in the stable on the slope of 軽*, and 阿直岐 was made to look after them, so the place is called 廐坂*. Because 阿直岐 could read the classics, he became tutor to the Crown Prince 菟道稚郎子*. When the Emperor asked whether there was a scholar better than he, he named 王仁*, so 荒田別*, ancestor of the 上毛野君*, and others were sent to Paekche to summon 王仁. In the 2nd month of the 16th year 王仁 came, and the Crown Prince studied the various classics with him and there was nothing he did not master. 王仁 is regarded as the founding ancestor of the 書首* and others. In this year King 阿花 (阿花王)* of Paekche died, and the Emperor sent King 直支 (直支王)* back to his country to succeed to the throne, granting him the land of 東韓*. In the 8th month, when 平群木菟宿禰* and 的戸田宿禰* were sent to Kara with picked troops, the king of Silla admitted his offense, and the laborers of 弓月 came together with 襲津彦.

In the 10th month of the 19th year, when the Emperor went to the Yoshino Palace, people of the 国樔* came, presented sweet sake (醴酒) and sang, and when they had finished singing they struck their mouths, looked up to the sky and laughed. It is said that the custom, still followed today on the days when the 国樔 present the products of their land, of striking their mouths and laughing after singing is a rule handed down from ancient times. The 国樔 people are very simple in their ways; they eat the nuts and fruits of the mountains, and boil frogs, which they regard as a fine delicacy and call 毛瀰*. They live in rugged land on the upper reaches of the Yoshino River, southeast of the capital beyond the mountains, and seldom came, but after this they came often and presented chestnuts, mushrooms, sweetfish and other products. In the 9th month of the 20th year, 阿知使主*, ancestor of the 倭漢直*, and his son 都加使主* came leading their people of seventeen districts.

In the 3rd month of the 22nd year, the Emperor resided at the 大隅宮 palace* in Naniwa. When he was gazing into the distance from a high tower, his consort 兄媛* looked toward the west, sighed deeply and asked to see her parents, so the Emperor had eighty fishermen of 淡路御原* serve as boatmen and send her to Kibi. In the 4th month, when 兄媛 set sail from 大津*, the Emperor watched her ship from the high tower and sang. In the 9th month the Emperor hunted on Awaji Island, then moved on to Kibi, visited the island of 小豆島*, and stayed at the 葦守宮 palace* at 葉田*. 兄媛's elder brother 御友別* had his brothers, children and grandchildren serve as stewards of the table (膳夫) and entertained him respectfully, so the Emperor, pleased, divided the land of Kibi: he enfeoffed 川嶋県* to the eldest son 稲速別* (founding ancestor of the 下道臣), 上道県* to 仲彦* (founding ancestor of the 上道臣 and 香屋臣), 三野県* to 弟彦* (founding ancestor of the 三野臣), 波区芸県* to 御友別's younger brother 鴨別* (ancestor of the 笠臣), and 苑県* to the elder brother 浦凝別* (founding ancestor of the 苑臣), and gave 織部県* to 兄媛.

In the 25th year King 直支 (直支王)* of Paekche died and his son 久爾辛* became king, but because he was young, 木満致* took charge of the government of the country; he had relations with the king's mother and repeatedly behaved without propriety, so the Emperor summoned 木満致. In the 9th month of the 28th year, an envoy of the king of Koguryŏ (高麗) presented a memorial, but because it said "The king of Koguryŏ instructs the land of Japan", the Crown Prince 菟道稚郎子*, angered at its discourtesy, tore it up. In the 8th month of the 31st year, the Emperor, not forgetting the long service of the government ship 枯野*, which had rotted, ordered that its name be passed down to later ages. The ministers used the ship's timber as firewood to boil salt and distributed five hundred baskets of salt to the provinces so that they would build ships. Many of the five hundred ships that gathered were burned at the harbor of 武庫水門* by a fire that started in the lodging of the Silla envoys, and the king of Silla, in fear, presented skilled craftsmen (the founding ancestors of the 猪名部* and others). A koto made from the unburned wood of 枯野 resounded far, and the Emperor composed a song.

In the 2nd month of the 37th year, 阿知使主* and 都加使主* were sent to Wu (呉) to seek seamstresses. The two crossed to Koguryŏ (高麗) but did not know the way, so the king of Koguryŏ gave them two guides, 久礼波 and 久礼志 (readings unconfirmed), and they reached Wu; the king of Wu gave them four craftswomen: 兄媛, 弟媛, 呉織 and 穴織 (readings unconfirmed). In the 2nd month of the 39th year, King 直支 (直支王)* of Paekche sent his younger sister 新斉都媛* to serve, and she came bringing seven women with her.

In the 1st month of the 40th year, the Emperor summoned 大山守命* and 大鷦鷯尊* and asked whether they loved their children, and which they held dearer, the older or the younger. 大山守命 answered the older child and displeased the Emperor. 大鷦鷯尊, reading the Emperor's expression, answered that an older child is already grown and gives no cause for worry, but a younger child is dear because it is not yet known how he will turn out; the Emperor was greatly pleased. The Emperor had wished to make 菟道稚郎子* Crown Prince, and asked this question to learn the minds of the two. On the day 甲子, he made 菟道稚郎子 his successor, had 大山守命 take charge of the mountains, rivers, forests and fields, and had 大鷦鷯尊 assist the Crown Prince and oversee the government of the country. In the 2nd month of the 41st year, the Emperor died at the 明宮 palace*. He was 110 years old. In the same month 阿知使主* and the others arrived in Tsukushi from Wu (呉); 胸形大神* asked for craftswomen, so 兄媛* was offered. They arrived with the remaining three at 武庫* in the province of 津国* but were too late for the Emperor, who had died, so they presented them to 大鷦鷯尊. Their descendants are said to be the present-day 呉衣縫 and 蚊屋衣縫 (readings unconfirmed).

A plain retelling of what the main text of the Nihon Shoki says (no published translation has been used).

Variant accounts

After its main text, the Nihon Shoki often sets out other versions, introduced with words such as "a certain book says" (一書曰).

One version (一云): the palace where the Emperor died

The main text says that the Emperor died at the 明宮 palace*, but a "one version says" (一云) in a small-character note says that he died at the 大隅宮 palace*.

In the original text

習諸典籍於王仁，莫不通達

The Crown Prince 菟道稚郎子* studied the various classics under 王仁*, and there was nothing he did not master.

歌訖即撃口仰咲者，蓋上古之遣則也

An explanation that the 国樔* striking their mouths, looking up to the sky and laughing after they finish singing is probably a rule handed down from ancient times.

高麗王教日本國也

The words of the memorial from the king of Koguryŏ (高麗). The Crown Prince regarded them as discourteous and tore up the memorial.

汝言寔合朕之心

The Emperor's words of pleasure at the answer of 大鷦鷯尊*: "Your words truly accord with my own heart."

In the Kojiki

Kojiki, Part 2, section on Emperor Ōjin (the division of duties among the three princes)

In the Kojiki, too, the Emperor asks 大山守命* and 大雀命* whether they love an elder child or a younger child more, and is pleased with the answer of 大雀命. The Kojiki then assigns to 大山守命 the administration of the mountains and seas, to 大雀命 the government of the realm (食国), and to 宇遅能和紀郎子* the heavenly succession (天津日継); after the Emperor's death it relates the revolt of 大山守命 and his end at the crossing of 宇遅*.

孰愛兄子與弟子
Kojiki, Part 2, section on Emperor Ōjin (the people who came from abroad)

The Kojiki records that 阿知吉師* brought two horses from King 照古 (照古王)* of Paekche, and that 和邇吉師* came bringing the Analects (論語) in ten scrolls and the Thousand Character Classic (千字文) in one scroll. In the Nihon Shoki main text, 王仁* is summoned on the recommendation of 阿直岐* and becomes the Crown Prince's tutor, but the titles of the books he brought are not recorded.

名和邇吉師。即論語十卷。千字文一卷。
Kojiki, Part 2, section on Emperor Ōjin (the song of the 国主* of Yoshino)

The Kojiki says that the 国主* of Yoshino brewed great sake (大御酒) in a 横臼 mortar* and presented it, singing while beating a mouth-drum (口鼓), and that this is the song still sung today when the great offerings (大贄) are presented. In the Nihon Shoki, in the 19th year, people of the 国樔* present sweet sake (醴酒) at the Yoshino Palace, sing, then strike their mouths and laugh.
