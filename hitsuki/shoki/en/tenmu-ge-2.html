Nihon Shoki, Book 29 — Emperor Tenmu (part 2) · page 48 of 50

Emperor Tenmu — Compiling the Codes and the Imperial Chronicles, and His Death

天武天皇の律令・帝紀の編纂と崩御

Gathers the records from the 10th year of Emperor Tenmu to his death in the 1st year of Shuchō (朱鳥). The making of codes (律令) and the recording and fixing of the Imperial Chronicles (帝紀) go forward, together with institutional measures such as the eight kabane (八色之姓) and new court ranks; the book ends with the emperor’s illness and death and the eulogies delivered at his temporary enshrinement.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textIn the second month of the 10th year, the emperor and empress, in the Great Audience Hall (大極殿), issued an edict to the imperial princes, princes and ministers declaring that codes (律令) would be established and the laws and regulations revised. On the same day Prince Kusakabe (草壁皇子尊) was made crown prince and put in charge of government affairs.
  2. Main textIn the third month of the 10th year, the emperor had twelve people, including Prince Kawashima (川嶋皇子) and Prince Osakabe (忍壁皇子), record and fix the Imperial Chronicles (帝紀) and the various matters of high antiquity. 中臣連大嶋 and 平群臣子首 (readings unconfirmed) took up the brush themselves and wrote the record.
  3. Main textIn the tenth month of the 13th year the clan titles (族姓) were reformed and the eight kabane were created: 真人, 朝臣, 宿禰, 忌寸, 道師, 臣, 連 and 稲置. After that, 真人 (thirteen clans), 朝臣 (fifty-two clans), 宿禰 (fifty clans) and 忌寸 (eleven clans) were granted one after another.
  4. Main textIn the 1st year of Shuchō, when divination was performed about the emperor’s illness, it was found to be a curse of the Kusanagi sword (草薙剣), so that same day the sword was sent to the Atsuta shrine (熱田社) in Owari province. In the seventh month it was ordered that all affairs of the realm, great or small, be reported to the empress and the crown prince; the era name was changed to Shuchō, and the palace was named the Asuka Kiyomihara Palace.
  5. Main textIn the ninth month of the 1st year of Shuchō, the emperor, his illness not cured, died in the main palace. A temporary enshrinement palace (殯宮) was built in the south court, and the text records that during the mourning Prince Ōtsu plotted rebellion against the crown prince.
  6. Main textIn the first month of the 14th year the names of the court ranks were changed and the number of grades increased: twelve grades of the 明 and 浄 ranks were established for princes, and forty-eight grades of 正, 直, 勤, 務, 追 and 進 for ministers. Prince Kusakabe was given the rank 浄広壱, Prince Ōtsu 浄大弐, and Prince Takechi 浄広弐.

What the main text says

In the first month of the 10th year, the Home Provinces and the other provinces were made to repair the shrines of the heavenly and earthly deities (天社, 地社). In the second month the emperor and empress declared in the Great Audience Hall that codes would be established, and Prince Kusakabe was made crown prince. In the third month the Imperial Chronicles and the various matters of high antiquity were recorded and fixed, and in the fourth month ninety-two articles of prohibitions (禁式) were laid down, fixing rules for what people could wear. In the fifth month the spirits of the imperial ancestors were worshipped, and in the seventh month a great purification was held throughout the realm. In the eighth month the envoys sent to 多禰 Island (多禰嶋)* presented a map of the land of 多禰 and reported on how its people lived.

In the third month of the 11th year, 三野王* and others were sent to 新城* to view the lay of the land, with the intention of placing the capital there. 境部連石積* and others were made to create a new script (新字), one work in forty-four scrolls. The sustenance households of imperial princes and below were abolished and returned to the state, and in the fourth month men and women alike were ordered to tie up their hair. In the seventh month many Hayato (隼人) came and presented local products, and the Ōsumi Hayato and the 阿多 Hayato* wrestled (相撲) at court, the Ōsumi Hayato winning. In the ninth month it was ordered that the practices of bowing on the knees and crawling be abolished and the standing bow of the Naniwa court (難波朝庭) be used.

In the first month of the 12th year a three-legged sparrow presented from Tsukushi was shown to the ministers; an edict declared that in the emperor’s reign auspicious signs from heaven appeared again and again every year, and an amnesty and a remission of taxes and labor followed. In the second month Prince Ōtsu took part in hearing state affairs for the first time. In the third month the monastic offices of 僧正, 僧都 and 律師 were filled; in the fourth month an edict ordered that copper coins be used and silver coins not be used, but a few days later another edict said the use of silver should not be stopped. In the twelfth month envoys were sent around to divide the boundaries of the provinces, but this was not settled that year; and an edict declared that capitals and palaces would be built in two places, and that first a capital would be placed at Naniwa.

In the second month of the 13th year, 広瀬王* and others were sent to the Home Provinces to divine a site suited to a capital, and 三野王* and others were sent to Shinano to view the lay of the land. In the third month the emperor toured the capital and fixed the site of the palace. In the intercalary fourth month, declaring that the essential thing in government is military affairs, he ordered civil and military officials to keep weapons and horses and to practice riding and the use of troops. In the tenth month the eight kabane were created, and on a night that month a great earthquake struck: mountains collapsed, the hot spring of Iyo was buried, and more than 500,000 頃 of fields in Tosa province sank into the sea. In the twelfth month 土師宿禰甥* and others who had stayed in Tang returned by way of Silla.

In the first month of the 14th year the names and grades of the court ranks were changed. In the third month an edict ordered every household in the provinces to make a Buddhist shrine (仏舎) and to place a Buddhist image and sutras in it for worship. In the seventh month the colors of court dress for each rank were fixed. In the ninth month, because the emperor was unwell, sutras were read for three days at Daikan Daiji (大官大寺), Kawara-dera and Asuka-dera. In the eleventh month a rite of summoning the soul (招魂) was performed for the emperor.

In the first month of the 1st year of Shuchō, a fire that started in the Treasury (大蔵省) at Naniwa burned down all the palace buildings. In the fifth month the emperor first became unwell, and the Medicine Buddha Sutra (薬師経) was expounded at Kawara-dera. In the sixth month divination about his illness pointed to a curse of the Kusanagi sword, and the sword was sent to the Atsuta shrine. In the seventh month affairs of the realm were made to be reported to the empress and the crown prince, the era name was changed to Shuchō, and the palace was named the Asuka Kiyomihara Palace. The princes and ministers made images of Kannon (観世音) for the emperor and had many monks ordained to pray for his recovery.

In the ninth month the imperial princes and those below gathered at Kawara-dera and made vows for the emperor’s illness, but the emperor died in the main palace without recovering. A temporary enshrinement palace was built in the south court, and the text records that during the mourning Prince Ōtsu plotted rebellion against the crown prince. Afterwards monks and nuns wailed in the courtyard of the temporary enshrinement, and ministers in turn delivered eulogies on the affairs of the 壬生, the princes, the palace household (宮内), the 大政官, the 法官 and others. Finally 百済王良虞* and the provincial chieftains (国造) also delivered eulogies, and various songs and dances were performed.

A plain retelling of what the main text of the Nihon Shoki says (no published translation has been used).

Variant accounts

After its main text, the Nihon Shoki often sets out other versions, introduced with words such as "a certain book says" (一書曰).

Some say (或曰)

The main text records that in the eighth month of the 11th year something fire-colored, like a consecration banner (灌頂幡), drifted north across the sky and was seen in every province. According to “some say,” it entered the sea of Koshi.

Some say (或曰)

The main text says that fire broke out from the Treasury at Naniwa and burned down all the palace buildings. According to “some say,” it was a fire from the house of 阿斗連薬* that spread to the palace, and only the Office of the Armory (兵庫職) did not burn.

Some say (或曰)

The main text says that lightning flashed and thunder sounded in the south, and the storehouse of the Ministry of Popular Affairs (民部省) holding the 庸 tax burned. According to “some say,” the fire started at the palace of Prince Osakabe and spread to the Ministry of Popular Affairs.

In the original text

朕今更欲定律令，改法式

The emperor’s words saying that he now wished to establish codes and revise the rules of law.

令記定帝紀及上古諸事

The record that he had the Imperial Chronicles and the various matters of high antiquity recorded and fixed.

作八色之姓，以混天下萬姓

The record that the eight kabane were created and the many clan titles of the realm were reorganized.

卜天皇病，祟草薙劍

When divination was performed about the emperor’s illness, it was found to be a curse of the Kusanagi sword.
