Nihon Shoki, Book 2 — Age of the Gods (part 2) · page 8 of 50

Sea-Luck and Mountain-Luck (Section 10)

海幸山幸（第十段）

The younger brother Hohodemi-no-Mikoto (火火出見尊), having lost the fishhook of his elder brother Honosusori-no-Mikoto (火闌降命), goes with the help of Shiotsuchi-no-Oji (鹽土老翁) to the palace of Watatsumi-no-Kami (海神), the sea deity, returns with the hook and with the tide-flowing jewel (潮滿瓊) and the tide-ebbing jewel (潮涸瓊), and makes his elder brother submit. The main text and four variant accounts transmit the story up to the point where Toyotama-hime (豐玉姫), the sea deity's daughter, departs into the sea after being seen in the act of giving birth, and the child left behind is named 波瀲武鸕鷀草葺不合尊 (Ugayafukiaezu-no-Mikoto).

Read in the Kojiki: Umisachi and Yamasachi (海幸山幸) ›

Key points to remember

  1. Main textIn the main text, the sea deity gathers the fish and questions them, and the hook is found in the mouth of 赤女 * (the name of a sea bream), which had not come because of an illness of the mouth. The sea deity instructed him to hand over the hook after secretly calling it a “poor hook” (貧鉤), gave him the tide-flowing jewel and the tide-ebbing jewel, and instructed him to drown his elder brother and to save him if he repented.
  2. Main textThe elder brother Honosusori-no-Mikoto, in distress, admitted his offense, vowed to become “a people of performers” (俳優之民), and was pardoned; he is regarded as the original ancestor of the 吾田君小橋* and others. In variant account 2 this is told as the origin of his descendants the Hayato serving beside the palace fence by barking in place of dogs, and in variant account 4 as the origin of a performance imitating the elder brother's drowning, which has not died out even today.
  3. Main textToyotama-hime's form when giving birth differs among the traditions: a “dragon” (龍) in the main text, an “eight-fathom great bear-wani” (八尋大熊鰐) in variant account 1, and an “eight-fathom great wani” (八尋大鰐) in variant account 3. In the main text, she wraps the child in grass, leaves it at the seashore, closes the way of the sea, and departs.
  4. Variant accountVariant account 4 alone reverses the brothers' luck, saying that the elder brother Honosuseri-no-Mikoto (火酢芹命) had the luck of the mountains and the younger brother 火折尊* the luck of the sea. It also has a scene in which the sea deity recognizes the Heavenly Grandson as the grandson of a heavenly deity by how he sits on the three couches in the sea deity's palace.
  5. Compared with the KojikiIn the Kojiki, the elder brother is 火照命 * (ancestor of the Hayato 阿多君) and the younger brother 火遠理命*; the fishhook is found in the throat of a red sea bream (赤海鯽魚), and Toyotama-hime (豊玉毘売) takes the form of an eight-fathom wani (八尋和迩). In the Nihon Shoki the elder brother is Honosusori-no-Mikoto or Honosuseri-no-Mikoto in every tradition, and the name of the elder brother made ancestor of the Hayato differs from the Kojiki.
  6. CommentaryThe deity-name database of Kokugakuin University treats the framework of this story as a tale of the “lost fishhook type,” in which someone goes to another world to look for a lost fishhook, and introduces the observation that very similar tales are distributed in Indonesia and Micronesia. Regarding the scene of peeping at Toyotama-hime giving birth, it states that the leading theory sees it as a tale of a supernatural wife of the “Melusine type,” in which a wife whose “prohibition against looking” is broken departs. What the main text says

The elder brother Honosusori-no-Mikoto originally had the luck of the sea, and the younger brother Hohodemi-no-Mikoto the luck of the mountains. The two tried exchanging their luck, but neither could get any catch. The elder brother, regretting it, returned the bow and arrows to the younger brother and asked for his own fishhook back, but the younger brother had already lost the hook. The younger brother forged his own cross-sword (横刀) into new hooks and gave them heaped in a winnowing basket, but the elder brother angrily said he would not accept them however many there were unless it was the original hook, and pressed him ever more harshly.

While Hohodemi-no-Mikoto was wandering by the seashore, deeply troubled, he met Shiotsuchi-no-Oji and told him the reason. The old man said there was no need to worry, made a basket without gaps, put him inside it, and sank it in the sea. He then arrived at a beautiful little beach, so he left the basket and went on, and at once arrived at the palace of the sea deity. The palace was well furnished with fences and towers; there was a well before the gate, and above it stood a many-branched 杜 tree (湯津杜樹) with thick branches and leaves.

As he stood lingering beneath that tree, a beautiful woman pushed open the door and came out, about to draw water with a jeweled vessel. Seeing him, the woman was startled and went back inside, and told her father and mother that there was an unusual guest beneath the tree before the gate. The sea deity spread out eightfold mats, welcomed him in, and asked why he had come. When he explained the circumstances in detail, the sea deity gathered the fish great and small and questioned them, and the fish answered that 赤女 * (the name of a sea bream) had lately not come because of an illness of the mouth. When 赤女 was summoned and its mouth searched, the lost hook was found.

He took the sea deity's daughter Toyotama-hime as wife and lived in the palace of the sea for three years, but there were times when he sighed, longing for his homeland. When Toyotama-hime heard this and told her father, the sea deity said to him that if he wished to return he would send him back, and gave him the hook that had been found. He then instructed him that when handing it to his elder brother he should first secretly call it a “poor hook” (貧鉤) and then hand it over; that if he soaked the tide-flowing jewel in water the tide would rise and he could drown his elder brother; and that if his elder brother repented and pleaded, he should soak the tide-ebbing jewel to make the tide recede and save him. At their parting, Toyotama-hime asked that, since she was pregnant and would come out to the seashore on a day of fierce wind and waves, he build a birthing hut and wait for her.

When Hohodemi-no-Mikoto, having returned to his palace, did as the sea deity had instructed, his elder brother Honosusori-no-Mikoto was tormented and admitted his offense, asking to be saved and saying that from now on he would be his people of performers. The younger brother granted his plea and pardoned his elder brother. Honosusori-no-Mikoto is regarded as the original ancestor of the 吾田君小橋* and others.

Later, as promised, Toyotama-hime braved the wind and waves and came to the seashore, bringing her younger sister Tamayori-hime (玉依姫). At the time of the birth, Toyotama-hime asked him not to look at her giving birth, but the Heavenly Grandson could not restrain himself and secretly peeped, and Toyotama-hime, in the midst of giving birth, had become a dragon. Toyotama-hime was deeply ashamed and said that, had she not been put to shame, she would have joined sea and land so that there was no barrier between them, but now that she had been shamed they could not form close ties; she wrapped the child in grass, left it at the seashore, closed the way of the sea, and departed. So the child was named 波瀲武鸕鷀草葺不合尊 (Ugayafukiaezu-no-Mikoto). Later Hohodemi-no-Mikoto died and was buried in the 高屋山上陵* mausoleum in Hyūga.

A plain retelling of what the main text of the Nihon Shoki says (no published translation has been used).

Variant accounts

After its main text, the Nihon Shoki often sets out other versions, introduced with words such as "a certain book says" (一書曰).

Variant account 1

The elder brother is Honosuseri-no-Mikoto. Shiotsuchi-no-Oji throws down a comb and makes it a bamboo grove, makes a basket from the bamboo and puts him into the sea, and at the sea deity's palace a beautiful woman attended by maidservants finds him. The sea deity instructs him to hand over the hook while reciting “貧窮之本。飢饉之始。困苦之根” (the root of poverty, the beginning of famine, the root of hardship); he returns riding on a great wani; Toyotama-hime, seen in the form of an eight-fathom great bear-wani, returns to the sea, leaving her younger sister Tamayori-hime to nurse the child; and the account makes the child's being born before the ridge of the birthing hut thatched with cormorant feathers was closed the origin of the child's name.

Variant account 2

He climbs the 杜 tree above the well, and Toyotama-hime, seeing his reflection in the well, drops her vessel and breaks it. The hook is found in the mouth of 口女 * (赤女), and the sea deity decrees that 口女 shall not be able to swallow bait and shall not be served at the Heavenly Grandson's table. The elder brother, after once surrendering, goes back on his promise, and at last submits when the tide presses up over the mountains and trees; this is regarded as the origin of his descendants the Hayato and others still serving beside the palace fence by barking in place of dogs.

Variant account 3

After the younger brother rescues a river goose caught in a snare by the seashore, he arrives at the sea deity's palace in the small boat 無目堅間* made by Shiotsuchi-no-Oji, and is entertained on eightfold mats of sea-lion skins. The sea deity obtains the hook from the mouth of 鯛女*, teaches him how to hand it over, has a one-fathom wani (一尋鰐魚), which declared it would take him in one day, carry him home, and instructs him that if his elder brother makes high fields he should make low-lying fields, and if his brother makes low-lying fields he should make high fields. Toyotama-hime comes riding a great turtle and is seen in the form of an eight-fathom great wani; she names the child herself and departs into the sea; and besides he and Toyotama-hime exchanging songs (擧歌), the origin of taking wet nurses to raise children is told.

Variant account 4

It holds that the elder brother Honosuseri-no-Mikoto had the luck of the mountains and the younger brother 火折尊* the luck of the sea; the one who helps is 鹽筒老翁*, and on the advice of the eight-fathom wani that the sea deity rides, he rides a one-fathom wani into the sea. The sea deity knows him to be the Heavenly Grandson by the way he sits on the three couches, and the hook comes out of the mouth of 口女 * (a grey mullet, 鯔魚). The younger brother whistles to raise a wind and drowns his elder brother; the elder brother puts on a loincloth, smears his face and palms with red earth, performs an imitation of drowning, and vows to be a performer forever; Toyotama-hime wraps the child in the coverlet 眞床覆衾* and grass, leaves it at the water's edge, and departs, and this is regarded as the origin of sea and land no longer being connected.

In the original text

從今以後。吾將爲汝俳優之民

The words of the elder brother Honosusori-no-Mikoto: “From now on, I shall be your people of performers.”

豐玉姫方產化爲龍

Toyotama-hime, just as she was giving birth, had become a dragon.

代吠狗而奉事者矣

Variant account 2: the Hayato and others serve (beside the palace fence) in place of barking dogs.

此海陸不相通之縁也

Variant account 4: this is the origin of sea and land not being connected with each other.

  • 鹽筒老翁*In variant account 4, the old man who helped the younger brother and introduced him to the wani that the sea deity rides.

  • 口女*In variant accounts 2 and 4, the fish that had the hook in its mouth. Variant account 4 says it is a grey mullet (鯔魚).
  • 一尋鰐*In variant accounts 3 and 4, the one-fathom wani that delivered the Heavenly Grandson home within a single day.

In the Kojiki

Kojiki, Part 1 — the marriage of Ninigi-no-Mikoto (the birth of the three children) and the visit to the land of the sea deity

In the Kojiki, the elder brother who, as 海佐知毘古 (the Sea-Luck Lad), quarrels with his younger brother is 火照命*, and he is made the ancestor of the Hayato 阿多君*. In the main text of the Nihon Shoki, the elder brother is Honosusori-no-Mikoto (in the variant accounts, Honosuseri-no-Mikoto), and he is regarded as the original ancestor of the 吾田君小橋* and others.

火照命〔此者隼人阿多君之祖〕
Kojiki, Part 1 — the visit to the land of the sea deity (the instructions of Watatsumi-no-Kami)

In the Kojiki, the hook was stuck in the throat of a “red sea bream” (赤海鯽魚). In the main text of the Nihon Shoki, it is found in the mouth of 赤女 * (the name of a sea bream), which had an illness of the mouth, and in variant accounts 2 and 4 the name 口女 also appears.

探赤海鯽魚之喉者，有鉤
Kojiki, Part 1 — the birth of Ugayafukiaezu-no-Mikoto

In the Kojiki, Toyotama-hime takes the form of an eight-fathom wani (八尋和迩) and crawls on her belly, and later they exchange songs. The main text of the Nihon Shoki says she became a dragon, and the exchange of songs appears in variant account 3.
