Nihon Shoki, Book 3 — Emperor Jinmu · page 11 of 50

The Pacification of Yamato and the Enthronement

大和の平定と即位

This page covers the period from the year 己未, when the emperor struck down the earth spiders (土蜘蛛) remaining in Yamato and declared that he would build a palace at Kashihara (橿原), through his accession at the Kashihara palace in the first month of the year 辛酉, until his death in his 76th year. The choice of a principal consort, the rewards to meritorious retainers, the worship of the imperial ancestors and heavenly deities at Mount 鳥見山*, the origin of the name 「秋津洲」*, and other matters are recorded year by year.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textIn a decree in the third month of the year 己未, the emperor declared that six years had passed since he began the eastern expedition and that the central land (中洲) was at peace, and that he would build a palace at Kashihara, southeast of Mount Unebi (畝傍山), and take the throne; he stated, “uniting the six directions, I will open a capital, and covering the eight cords (八紘), I will make them one roof (宇).”
  2. Main textOn the first day (庚辰) of the first month of spring in the year 辛酉, the emperor ascended the imperial throne at the Kashihara palace, and this year was made the first year of the emperor. He made Himetatara-isuzu-hime-no-Mikoto (媛蹈韛五十鈴媛命), who had been recommended as the child of Kotoshironushi-no-Kami and 玉櫛媛*, his empress, and 神八井命 and 神渟名川耳尊 (readings unconfirmed) were born.
  3. Main textIn the conferring of rewards for merit in the second month of the 2nd year, he gave 道臣命* a residence at the village of 築坂邑* and 大來目* land by the river west of Mount Unebi (the village of 來目邑)*; he made 珍彦* governor (國造) of Yamato (倭), 弟猾* district chief (縣主) of 猛田*, 弟磯城 (黒速)* district chief of 磯城*, and 釼根* governor of 葛城*, and he included the 頭八咫烏* crow among those rewarded as well.
  4. Main textIn the fourth month of the 31st year (「卅有一年」 in the original), the name 「秋津洲」* is said to have begun from the words of the emperor, who viewed the shape of the land from the hill 嗛間丘 at 腋上 (readings unconfirmed); the names by which Izanagi-no-Mikoto, Ōanamuchi-no-Ōkami (大己貴大神), and Nigihayahi-no-Mikoto called this land are also recorded.
  5. Compared with the KojikiIn the Kojiki, the empress is 伊須氣余理比賣*, daughter of Ōmononushi-no-Kami, and the emperor's age is given as 137 years. The Nihon Shoki makes Himetatara-isuzu-hime-no-Mikoto, daughter of Kotoshironushi-no-Kami, empress, and holds that he died at 127. Passages corresponding to the year of accession, the conferring of rewards for merit, the worship at Mount 鳥見山, and the naming of 秋津洲 could not be confirmed in the Kojiki.
  6. CommentaryAccording to the deity-name database of Kokugakuin University, the governors of Yamato (倭国造) are the clan who worship Yamato-Ōkunitama-no-Kami (倭大国魂神), and it has been pointed out that the Nihon Shoki story in which 椎根津彦 (珍彦)* fetches the earth of Mount Ame-no-Kagoyama (天香山), foreshadowing the pacification of Yamato, is a tale of meritorious deeds that legitimizes the worship conducted by the governors of Yamato. [source, Japanese]

What the main text says

In the second month of the year 己未, the emperor had his generals train their troops. Since the earth spiders in three places, namely 新城戸畔 at 波哆丘岬 in the district of 層富縣, 居勢祝 at 和珥坂下, and 猪祝 at 臍見長柄丘岬 (readings unconfirmed), would not submit, he sent separate detachments and executed them all. The earth spiders of the village of 高尾張邑* were short of stature with long arms and legs, so they were killed by throwing nets of kudzu (葛) over them, and the village's name was changed to 葛城*. The place that had been called 片居 or 片立 (readings unconfirmed) was renamed 磐余* because the imperial army gathered and filled it. Besides these, the origins of place names are told, such as 猛田, 城田, and 頬枕田 (readings unconfirmed), and the place where the clay of Mount Ame-no-Kagoyama was taken being called 埴安*.

In the third month, the emperor issued a decree stating that six years had passed since he began the eastern expedition, that through the might of imperial heaven (皇天) the wicked had been struck down, and that although the borderlands were not yet at peace, there was no longer strife in the central land. He held that though the people were still simple and lived in nests and caves, nothing should stand in the way of construction if it was for the benefit of the people, and declared that he would clear the mountain forests and build a palace, above responding to the virtue of the heavenly deities who had granted him the land, and below spreading the righteous heart of the imperial grandson. He then chose the land of Kashihara, southeast of Mount Unebi, as a good, secluded place in the land, and in that month ordered officials to begin building the imperial residence.

In the eighth month of the year 庚申, when the emperor sought widely for a principal consort, Himetatara-isuzu-hime-no-Mikoto, born to Kotoshironushi-no-Kami and 玉櫛媛*, daughter of 三嶋溝橛耳神*, was recommended as the most beautiful person in the land, and in the ninth month he made her principal consort. On the first day (庚辰) of the first month of spring in the year 辛酉, the emperor ascended the imperial throne at the Kashihara palace, made this year the first year of the emperor, and made the principal consort empress, and 神八井命 and 神渟名川耳尊 (readings unconfirmed) were born. In the old language (古語), he is praised as 神日本磐余彦火火出見天皇*, the emperor who firmly planted the pillars of his palace at Kashihara in Unebi (畝傍) and first ruled the realm. It is also said that on the day the emperor laid the foundations of the state, 道臣命*, leading the 大來目部*, dispelled evil influences with allusive songs (諷歌) and inverted words (倒語), and that this was the beginning of inverted words.

In the second month of the 2nd year, the emperor determined merits and conferred rewards. To 道臣命 he gave a residence at the village of 築坂邑*, and he had 大來目* live by the river west of Mount Unebi (the origin of the village of 來目邑)*. He made 珍彦 governor of Yamato, 弟猾 district chief of 猛田 (distant ancestor of the 菟田主水部)*, 黒速 of 弟磯城 (readings unconfirmed) district chief of 磯城, and 釼根* governor of 葛城; the 頭八咫烏 also received a reward, and its descendants are regarded as the 葛野主殿縣主部*. In the second month of the 4th year, the emperor issued an edict saying that since the spirits of the imperial ancestors had looked down from heaven and helped him, now that the rebels were subdued he should perform the suburban sacrifice (郊祀) to the heavenly deities and show his great filial piety; he set up a sacred ritual ground (靈畤) within Mount 鳥見山* and worshipped the imperial ancestors and heavenly deities. That place is called 上小野榛原 and 下小野榛原 (readings unconfirmed).

In the fourth month of the 31st year (卅有一年), the emperor made a royal tour and climbed the hill 嗛間丘 at 腋上 (readings unconfirmed), and, looking around at the shape of the land, said that though it was a land as narrow as the inner bark-cloth (内木綿), it was like the 臀呫 of dragonflies (蜻蛉之臀呫); this is said to be the beginning of the name 秋津洲*. It goes on to record that in the past Izanagi-no-Mikoto called this land 浦安國, 細戈千足國, and 磯輪上秀眞國 (readings unconfirmed), that Ōanamuchi-no-Ōkami called it 玉牆内國*, and that Nigihayahi-no-Mikoto, because he had flown through the great sky in a heavenly rock boat, seen this land, and descended, called it 虚空見日本國*. In the first month of the 32nd year (卅有二年), 神渟名川耳尊* was made crown prince. In the third month of the 76th year, the emperor died at the Kashihara palace, at the age of 127. In the ninth month of the following year he was buried in the mausoleum northeast of Mount Unebi (畝傍山東北陵).

A plain retelling of what the main text of the Nihon Shoki says (no published translation has been used).

Variant accounts

After its main text, the Nihon Shoki often sets out other versions, introduced with words such as "a certain book says" (一書曰).

Another account says (the origin of the name 磐余)

In contrast to the main text, which attributes the name 磐余* to the imperial army's gathering and filling the place, a different origin is recorded under 「或曰」: when the emperor ate the provisions of the sacred jars (嚴瓮) and set out to battle, the 八十梟帥 (Eighty Bandits) of 磯城* were massed there and were destroyed, so the place was named the village of 磐余邑.

In the original text

兼六合以開都。掩八紘而爲宇

A passage from the decree on building at Kashihara: uniting the four quarters of the realm, he will open a capital, and covering everything to the ends of the eight directions, he will make it one house.

天皇即帝位於橿原宮

Records that in the first month of the year 辛酉 the emperor ascended the imperial throne at the Kashihara palace.

由是始有秋津洲之號也

From this, the name 秋津洲* first came into being.

天皇崩于橿原宮。時年一百廿七歳

The emperor died at the Kashihara palace, at the age of 127.

In the Kojiki

Kojiki, Part 2 — section on Emperor Jinmu (the choice of the empress)

In the Kojiki, 伊須氣余理比賣*, who becomes empress, is said to be the daughter born when Ōmononushi-no-Kami of 美和* turned into a red-lacquered arrow (丹塗矢) and visited 勢夜陀多良比賣*. The empress of the Nihon Shoki, Himetatara-isuzu-hime-no-Mikoto, is said to be the child of Kotoshironushi-no-Kami and 玉櫛媛*.

美和之大物主神。見感而
Kojiki, Part 2 — end of the section on Emperor Jinmu (age at death and tomb)

The Kojiki gives the emperor's age as 137 years and places his tomb at 白檮尾上*, north of Mount Unebi (畝火山). The Nihon Shoki holds that he died at 127 and was buried in the mausoleum northeast of Mount Unebi (畝傍山東北陵).

御年。壹佰參拾漆歳
Kojiki, Part 2 — section on Emperor Jinmu (rule at the Kashihara palace)

The Kojiki records briefly that he subdued the unruly deities, drove away those who would not submit, and ruled the realm at the Kashihara palace (白檮原宮) in Unebi (畝火). Passages corresponding to the accession in the year 辛酉, the conferring of rewards for merit, the worship at Mount 鳥見山*, and the naming of 秋津洲 found in the Nihon Shoki could not be confirmed in the Kojiki.
