Nihon Shoki, Book 9 — Empress Jingū · page 22 of 50

The revolt of Oshikuma-no-Miko and the years of the regency

忍熊王の反乱と摂政の時代

This section records how, when the Empress returned from Silla, 麛坂王* and Oshikuma-no-Miko, fearing that the newly born prince would be made ruler, raised troops; how Takeuchi-no-Sukune and others put down the revolt; and how the Empress became regent. After that, it sets out year by year the exchanges with Silla and Paekche, the presentation of the Seven-Branched Sword, quotations from the Records of Wei (魏志) and the Jin court diary (晋起居注), and other matters, and the book ends with her death in the 69th year of the regency.

Read in the Kojiki: The Rebellion of Oshikuma-no-Miko (忍熊王) and Kehi-no-Ōkami (気比大神) ›

Key points to remember

  1. Main text麛坂王* and Oshikuma-no-Miko said, "How can we, the elder brothers, submit to a younger brother?" and gathered troops while pretending to build the late Emperor's tomb. At a divination hunt (祈狩) on 菟餓野*, 麛坂王 was eaten by a red boar, and Oshikuma-no-Miko, taking this as an ill omen, withdrew to Sumiyoshi.
  2. Main textTakeuchi-no-Sukune had his soldiers hide spare bowstrings in their hair and, claiming to act on the Empress's command, proposed a truce. He made Oshikuma-no-Miko's army cut their bowstrings and throw their swords into the river, then attacked, defeating them at 逢坂* and in the chestnut grove of 狭々浪*. Oshikuma-no-Miko exchanged songs with 五十狭茅宿禰*, and the two sank together at the crossing of 瀬田* and died.
  3. Main textWhen the Empress's ship could not advance toward Naniwa, divination was performed at the harbor of 務古*. Amaterasu-Ōmikami instructed that her rough spirit (荒魂) be enshrined in the land of 広田*, Wakahirume-no-Mikoto in the land of 活田長峡*, Kotoshironushi-no-Kami in the land of 長田*, and the three 筒男 deities* instructed that their gentle spirits (和魂) be enshrined at 大津渟中倉之長峡*. When this was done, the crossing by sea became possible.
  4. Main textIn the 10th month the ministers honored the Empress as Empress Dowager, and this year (辛巳) was made the first year of the regency. In the 3rd year Prince Homutawake (誉田別皇子) was made Crown Prince. In the entries for the 39th, 40th and 43rd years and the 66th year, passages from the Records of Wei (魏志) and the Jin court diary (晋起居注) recording exchanges with the queen of Wa and the land of Wa are quoted as small-character notes.
  5. Main textIn the 46th year contact with Paekche began through the land of 卓淳*. In the 49th year generals were sent who are said to have defeated Silla and subdued seven states, and in the 52nd year 久氐* and others from Paekche presented one Seven-Branched Sword (七枝刀), one Seven-Child Mirror (七子鏡) and other items.
  6. CommentaryThe Kokugakuin University deity-name database explains that "大津の渟中倉の長峽" (as quoted)*, where the gentle spirits of the three 筒男 deities* were settled following the oracle at the harbor of 務古*, is regarded as corresponding to Sumiyoshi Taisha. [source, Japanese]

What the main text says

In the 2nd month of the year after the attack on Silla, the Empress led her ministers and officials to the 穴門豊浦宮 palace*, gathered up the remains of the late Emperor, and set out for the capital by sea. 麛坂王* and Oshikuma-no-Miko heard of the Emperor's death, the Empress's western campaign and the birth of a prince. Thinking that the ministers would set up the young lord as ruler, they plotted, asking how elder brothers could submit to a younger brother. Pretending to build the Emperor's tomb, the two began a mausoleum at 赤石* in Harima, had stones carried from Awaji Island, armed the people, and waited for the Empress. 倉見別*, ancestor of the 犬上君*, and 五十狭茅宿禰*, ancestor of the 吉師*, sided with 麛坂王 and gathered troops from the eastern provinces. At 菟餓野* they held a divination hunt (祈狩), praying that if their enterprise was to succeed they would take good game. A red boar climbed onto the temporary platform (假庪) and ate 麛坂王, so Oshikuma-no-Miko decided that they should not wait for the enemy there and withdrew to Sumiyoshi.

The Empress had Takeuchi-no-Sukune hold the prince in his arms and go round by the southern sea to the harbor of Kii, while her own ship headed for Naniwa, but it circled in the sea and could not advance. She returned to the harbor of 務古* and performed divination. Amaterasu-Ōmikami instructed that her rough spirit (荒魂) should not be brought near the imperial residence but placed in the land of 広田*; Wakahirume-no-Mikoto asked to be enshrined in the land of 活田長峡*, Kotoshironushi-no-Kami in the land of 長田*, and the three 筒男 deities* asked that their gentle spirits (和魂) be enshrined at 大津渟中倉之長峡*. The Empress had 葉山媛*, 海上五十狭茅*, 長媛* and others perform these rites. Once the deities were settled as instructed, she was able to cross the sea safely, and Oshikuma-no-Miko withdrew further to 菟道*. The Empress met the Crown Prince at 日高* in Kii Province and moved to the 小竹宮 palace*.

At this time, days on which the daytime was as dark as night continued for many days. An old man said that this was the offense of 阿豆那比 (阿豆那比之罪)*. On investigation it was found that the priest (祝) of 小竹* and the priest of 天野* had been close friends; when one died of illness the other followed him in death, and the two had been buried in the same grave. When the grave was opened, the coffins were examined and the two were reburied in separate places, the sunlight returned and day and night could again be told apart.

In the 3rd month, Takeuchi-no-Sukune and 武振熊*, ancestor of the 和珥臣*, led tens of thousands of soldiers against Oshikuma-no-Miko and camped north of the 菟道 river*. When 熊之凝*, the vanguard of Oshikuma-no-Miko, sang to encourage his side, Takeuchi-no-Sukune had his soldiers tie up their hair with bowstrings hidden inside and wear wooden swords. Then, claiming to act on the Empress's command, he falsely proposed a truce, saying that she did not seek the realm but only to protect the young prince. He made Oshikuma-no-Miko's army cut their bowstrings and throw their swords into the river, after which his own men restrung their bows with the hidden strings, put on real swords, crossed the river and attacked. Deceived, Oshikuma-no-Miko retreated, was defeated at 逢坂*, and many of his men were cut down in the chestnut grove of 狭々浪*. Because blood flooded the chestnut grove, it is said that the chestnuts from there are still not offered to the palace. Oshikuma-no-Miko called 五十狭茅宿禰* and sang, and the two sank together at the crossing of 瀬田* and died. Takeuchi-no-Sukune also composed a song, and the body was found in the 菟道 river some days later.

In the 10th month the ministers honored the Empress as Empress Dowager, and this year (the Great Year being 辛巳; 太歳辛巳) was made the first year of the regency. In the 11th month of the 2nd year the Emperor was buried in the 長野陵 tomb* in Kawachi Province, and in the 1st month of the 3rd year Prince Homutawake (誉田別皇子) was made Crown Prince and the capital was placed at 磐余* (the 若桜宮 palace)*. In the 5th year, envoys of the king of Silla deceived the court by pretending to take home the hostage 微叱許智伐旱*: on Tsushima they laid a straw figure in a bed to look like a sick man, and let 微叱許智 escape to Silla. 葛城襲津彦*, realizing he had been tricked, put the three envoys in a cage and burned them to death, then stopped at the 蹈鞴津 harbor* in Silla, took the 草羅城 fortress* and returned. The people he brought back at this time are said to be the founding ancestors of the 漢人* and others of the four villages of 桑原, 佐糜, 高宮 and 忍海 (readings unconfirmed). In the 2nd month of the 13th year, the Crown Prince went with Takeuchi-no-Sukune to worship Kehi-no-Ōkami (笥飯大神) at 角鹿*. On his return the Empress Dowager offered him sake at a banquet and sang, and Takeuchi-no-Sukune answered with a song on the Crown Prince's behalf.

In the entries for the 39th, 40th and 43rd years, passages from the Records of Wei (魏志) recording envoys of the queen of Wa and other matters are quoted as small-character notes. In the 46th year 斯摩宿禰* was sent to the land of 卓淳*, and the king of 卓淳 told him that three men from Paekche, 久氐* and others, had come asking the way to Japan. When 爾波移*, an attendant of 斯摩宿禰, went to Paekche, King 肖古 (肖古王)* gave him silk, bows and arrows, and forty iron ingots. In the 47th year envoys from Paekche came together with envoys from Silla, but the tribute from Paekche was inferior. When questioned, the Paekche envoys answered that they had been seized by Silla on the way and their tribute had been switched, so, following the instruction of a heavenly deity, 千熊長彦* was sent to Silla to reproach it. In the 49th year 荒田別* and 鹿我別* were made generals and crossed to the land of 卓淳; together with the troops of 木羅斤資* and others they defeated Silla and are said to have subdued seven states, including 比自㶱*, and 忱彌多禮* was given to Paekche. 千熊長彦 climbed 辟支山 and 古沙山 (readings unconfirmed) with the king of Paekche and, on a great rock, received his oath that Paekche would pay tribute for ever. In the 50th and 51st years 久氐 and others from Paekche came to court again, and in the 52nd year they presented one Seven-Branched Sword (七枝刀), one Seven-Child Mirror (七子鏡) and other items.

In the 55th year King 肖古* of Paekche died, and in the 56th year the prince 貴須* became king. In the 62nd year, because Silla did not pay tribute, 襲津彦* was sent to attack it. In the 64th year King 貴須 died and 枕流王* became king. In the 65th year 枕流王 died, and because his son 阿花* was young, his uncle 辰斯* seized the throne. In the entry for the 66th year a passage from the Jin court diary (晋起居注) is quoted. In the 4th month of the 69th year the Empress Dowager died at the 稚桜宮 palace* (a small-character note says at the age of one hundred), and in the 10th month she was buried in the 狭城盾列陵 tomb*; on that day she was posthumously honored with the name 気長足姫尊*. This year was the Great Year 己丑 (太歳己丑).

Variant accounts

After its main text, the Nihon Shoki often sets out other versions, introduced with words such as "a certain book says" (一書曰).

One version (一云): the origin of 熊之凝*

A small-character note makes 熊之凝* the ancestor of the 葛野城首*, while a "one version says" (一云) makes him a distant ancestor of the 多呉吉師*.

One version (一云): the origin of 千熊長彦*

A small-character note says that the clan name of 千熊長彦* is unclear, and a "one version says" (一云) makes him a man of Musashi Province and the founding ancestor of the present-day 額田部槻本首* and others. It further asks whether he may be the 職麻那那加比跪* who appears in the Paekche Record (百済記).

Paekche Record (百済記) and one version (一云): 沙至比跪*

The main text only records that 襲津彦* was made to attack Silla, but the Paekche Record (百済記) quoted in a small-character note relates that 沙至比跪*, sent in the year 壬午, accepted beautiful women from Silla and instead destroyed the land of Kara (加羅), and that the Emperor in anger had 木羅斤資* restore Kara. A further "one version says" (一云) within it states that 沙至比跪, learning that the Emperor's anger had not abated, entered a stone cave and died.

In the original text

吾等何以兄從弟乎

The words of 麛坂王* and Oshikuma-no-Miko: how can we, who are elder brothers, submit to a younger brother (the newly born prince)?

我之荒魂、不可近皇居

The instruction of Amaterasu-Ōmikami: my rough spirit must not be brought near the imperial residence.

各以儲弦藏于髮中、且佩木刀

Takeuchi-no-Sukune's order: let each man hide spare bowstrings in his hair and wear a wooden sword.

則獻七枝刀一口・七子鏡一面

This lists the Seven-Branched Sword and the Seven-Child Mirror as items presented by the envoys of Paekche in the 52nd year.

In the Kojiki

Kojiki, Part 2, section on Emperor Chūai (the revolt of 香坂王* and Oshikuma-no-Miko)

In the Kojiki, the Empress, distrusting people's loyalty, put her son on a mourning ship and had it announced that the prince had already died, and so went up to the capital. 香坂王* climbs a 歴木 tree* and is eaten by a great boar. The Nihon Shoki main text has no mourning-ship stratagem; instead a red boar climbs onto a temporary platform and eats 麛坂王*.

一具喪船。御子載其喪船
Kojiki, Part 2, section on Emperor Chūai (the defeat and death of Oshikuma-no-Miko)

The bowstring stratagem also appears in the Kojiki, but there it is carried out by 建振熊命*, who falsely announces that 息長帯日売命* has already died, and Oshikuma-no-Miko's general is 伊佐比宿禰*. In the Nihon Shoki main text it is Takeuchi-no-Sukune who proposes a truce, claiming it as the Empress's command, and the one who dies with Oshikuma-no-Miko is 五十狭茅宿禰*.

爾建振熊命。權而令云
Kojiki, Part 2, section on Emperor Chūai (Kehi-no-Ōkami and the songs of the sake feast)

The Kojiki has a story in which Takeuchi-no-Sukune (建内宿禰) takes the Crown Prince to 角鹿* for purification and the prince exchanges names with 伊奢沙和気大神*; on their return the prince's mother brews welcoming sake and sings. The Nihon Shoki main text only records that in the 13th year the Crown Prince worshipped Kehi-no-Ōkami (笥飯大神) and that the Empress Dowager sang at a banquet; the name-exchange story appears in a "one version says" (一云) small-character note at the start of Book 10. No corresponding entries in the Kojiki could be confirmed for the exchanges with Paekche or the quotations from the Records of Wei.
