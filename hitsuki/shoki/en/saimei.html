Nihon Shoki, Book 26 — Empress Saimei · page 44 of 50

Empress Saimei — Her Second Reign, Prince Arima, and the Western Expedition to Aid Paekche

斉明天皇の重祚・有間皇子・百済救援の西征

This book records the seven years in which Empress Saimei (天豐財重日足姬天皇), who had once reigned as Empress Kōgyoku, took the throne again at the Itabuki Palace in Asuka after the death of Emperor Kōtoku, years marked by the building of palaces and canals, expeditions against the Emishi and the 肅慎*, and the affair of Prince Arima. When Paekche was overthrown by Tang and Silla in the 6th year, she decided to send aid and set out for Tsukushi herself; the book goes on to her death at the Asakura Palace in the seventh month of the 7th year.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textEmpress Saimei (天豐財重日足姬天皇) yielded the throne to Emperor Kōtoku (天萬豐日天皇) in the 4th year of her reign (改元四年) and was called “皇祖母尊”; after his death she took the throne again at the Itabuki Palace in Asuka in the first month of the 1st year.
  2. Main textIn the eleventh month of the 4th year, the caretaker official (留守官) Soga no Akae spoke to Prince Arima of the “three failings” of the empress’s government; the prince plotted, was then arrested, was questioned by the crown prince at the hot springs of Ki (紀温湯), and was strangled at 藤白坂*.
  3. Main textIn the ninth month of the 6th year a Paekche envoy reported the destruction of Paekche by Tang and Silla, and in the tenth month 鬼室福信* asked for aid and for the return of Prince 豐璋*, so the empress issued an edict deciding to send aid, and in the twelfth month she had weapons prepared at the Naniwa Palace.
  4. Main textIn the first month of the 7th year she set out westward in the imperial ship and, after the 石湯 temporary palace at 熟田津 and the 磐瀨 temporary palace at 娜大津 (readings unconfirmed), moved to the Asakura no Tachibana no Hironiwa Palace (朝倉橘廣庭宮), but in the seventh month she died at the Asakura Palace.
  5. Main textIt is recorded again and again that from the 4th to the 6th year the fleet of Abe no Omi (阿倍臣; Abe no Hikita no Hirafu, 阿倍引田臣比羅夫) subdued the Emishi of 齶田, 渟代, 津輕 (readings unconfirmed) and other places, and fought the 肅慎*.
  6. Variant accountThere are two “certain texts” (或本) concerning Prince Arima’s plot; they relate that the rebellion was divined by drawing lots (短籍), and that a plan was made to burn the palace and seize 牟婁津*.

What the main text says

Empress Saimei (天豐財重日足姬天皇) first married Prince 高向 (高向王)* and bore Prince 漢 (漢皇子)*, and later married Emperor Jomei (息長足日廣額天皇) and bore two sons and one daughter. She became empress and took the throne after her husband’s death, but in the sixth month of the 4th year of her reign (改元四年) she yielded the throne to Emperor Kōtoku (天萬豐日天皇) and was called 皇祖母尊. When Emperor Kōtoku died, she took the throne again at the Itabuki Palace in Asuka in the first month of the 1st year. In the fifth month of this year, it is said, a figure that looked like a Tang man, riding a dragon and wearing a blue oiled hat, flew from Mount Katsuragi (葛城嶺) to Mount Ikoma (膽駒山), and then from the pine ridge of Sumiyoshi (住吉松嶺) away to the west. In winter the Itabuki Palace burned and the empress moved to the Kawara Palace in Asuka (飛鳥川原宮).

In the 2nd year a palace site was chosen anew at Okamoto in Asuka and a palace was built, named the Later Okamoto Palace in Asuka (後飛鳥岡本宮). A wall was built around the peak of 田身嶺*, and beside two zelkova trees on its summit a tower (観) was built, called the Twin Zelkova Palace (兩槻宮), or the Heavenly Palace (天宮). She also had water engineers dig a canal from west of Mount Kagu (香山) to Mount Isonokami (石上山), and had stones carried on two hundred boats to build stone walls on the hill east of the palace, so people slandered it as “the Canal of the Mad Mind” (狂心渠) and lamented the great labor and expense. The Yoshino Palace was also built, but the Okamoto Palace suffered a fire. In the 3rd year an image of Mount Sumeru (須彌山) was made west of Asuka-dera and the Ullambana assembly (盂蘭瓮会) was held, and people of the country of 覩貨邏* who had drifted ashore in Tsukushi were entertained.

In the ninth month of the 3rd year, Prince Arima feigned madness, went to the 牟婁 hot springs (牟婁温湯)* and praised the place, so the empress wished to go there. When her grandson Prince 建 (建王)* died at the age of eight in the fifth month of the 4th year, the empress grieved deeply, composed poems, and ordered that he be buried together with her in her own tomb. In the tenth month, while she was away on an excursion to the hot springs of Ki, Soga no Akae told Prince Arima of three failings of the empress: building storehouses and gathering the people’s wealth, digging canals and wasting public provisions, and carrying stones by boat to build a hill. The prince plotted in the upper room of Akae’s house, but his armrest (脇息, called 夾膝) broke of its own accord, and taking this as an ill omen they swore to each other and stopped. That night Akae had 物部朴井連鮪* surround the prince’s house; the prince was sent to the hot springs of Ki, answered the crown prince’s questioning by saying that “only Heaven and Akae know,” and was strangled at 藤白坂*.

In the fourth month of the 4th year, Abe no Omi led a fleet of 180 ships against the Emishi, and the Emishi of 齶田 and 渟代 (readings unconfirmed) surrendered. 恩荷*, an Emishi of 齶田, swore that they carried bows and arrows to get meat to eat and not to turn them against the government army, and he received a rank and appointment as district chief. In the third month of the 5th year Abe no Omi again gathered and entertained the Emishi of 飽田, 渟代, 津輕 and 膽振鉏 (readings unconfirmed), and set up an administrative office at 後方羊蹄*. In the third month of the 6th year he attacked the land of the 肅慎* with two hundred ships and invited them to trade, but no peace was made; they fought at the stockade on 弊賂辨嶋*, and 能登臣馬身龍* was killed in battle. In the fifth month a Mount Sumeru was made beside the Isonokami pond (石上池) and forty-seven people of the 肅慎 were entertained.

In the seventh month of the 5th year, 坂合部連石布 and 津守連吉祥 (readings unconfirmed) were sent to Tang and showed Emishi men and women from Michinoku (道奥) to the Tang Son of Heaven. According to the Record of 伊吉連博德 (伊吉連博德書)* quoted in the main text, 石布’s ship was driven by contrary winds to an island in the southern sea called 爾加委* and destroyed by the islanders, while 吉祥’s ship reached 越州* and went up to the capital. The Son of Heaven asked the envoys about the well-being of the emperor (天皇) and about the kinds of Emishi and how they lived, and the envoys answered that, in order of distance, there were three kinds: the 都加留*, the “rough Emishi” (麤蝦夷) and the “tame Emishi” (熟蝦夷). Later they were nearly punished because of slander by a follower of 韓智興*, but escaped through a petition by 博德; the following year, on the grounds that there was to be “a matter of government east of the sea” (海東之政), they were confined in the Western Capital, and they were finally released only after Paekche had been subdued.

In the ninth month of the 6th year a Paekche envoy came and reported that in the seventh month Silla had brought in Tang forces and overthrown Paekche, that the king and his ministers had all been captured, and that 鬼室福信* and others had gathered the scattered troops and were holding the royal city. In the tenth month 福信 presented more than a hundred Tang prisoners and asked for aid, and for Prince 豐璋*, who was serving in Japan (日本), to be welcomed back as ruler of the country. Saying that helping a country in danger is a way of long standing, the empress ordered aid to be sent, and in the twelfth month she went to the Naniwa Palace and had weapons prepared. For this year the text records events that were taken as omens that the relief army would be defeated: the stern and bow of a ship built in Suruga province changed places of their own accord at night, and in Shinano province (科野国) swarms of flies flew toward the west.

In the first month of the 7th year the imperial ship headed west; on the 大伯 sea (大伯海)* Princess 大田 (大田姬皇女)* gave birth to a girl, who was named Princess 大伯 (大伯皇女). The empress stopped at the 石湯 temporary palace at 熟田津 in Iyo (readings unconfirmed), and in the third month entered the 磐瀨 temporary palace at 娜大津 (readings unconfirmed) and renamed the place 長津*. In the fifth month she moved to the Asakura no Tachibana no Hironiwa Palace, but because trees of the Asakura shrine (朝倉社) had been cut down to build the palace, the god was angered and destroyed the hall, will-o’-the-wisps (鬼火) were seen in the palace, and many of those serving close to her fell ill and died. In the seventh month the empress died at the Asakura Palace; in the eighth month, when the crown prince moved the mourning to the 磐瀨 palace, it is said that a demon (鬼) wearing a great hat watched the funeral rites from the top of Mount Asakura. The funeral party returned to Naniwa by sea, and in the eleventh month the temporary enshrinement was held at Kawara in Asuka (飛鳥川原).

A plain retelling of what the main text of the Nihon Shoki says (no published translation has been used).

Variant accounts

After its main text, the Nihon Shoki often sets out other versions, introduced with words such as "a certain book says" (一書曰).

A certain text says (或本云)

The main text says the plot was laid in the upper room of Akae’s house, but this certain text relates that Prince Arima drew lots (短籍) with Soga no Akae (蘇我臣赤兄), 鹽屋連小戈, 守君大石 and 坂合部連藥 (readings unconfirmed) to divine the matter of rebellion.

A certain text says (或本云)

It says that Prince Arima set out a specific plan: first burn the palace, seize 牟婁津* with five hundred men, and cut off Awaji province with a fleet; and that someone admonished him that at nineteen he was not yet an adult. It records that even when the leg of his table broke for no reason he did not abandon the plot, and that he was finally put to death.

A certain text says (或本云)

It relates that Abe no Hikita no Hirafu returned from fighting the 肅慎* and presented forty-nine captives (the main text’s entry for the third month of the 5th year records that he gathered and feasted the Emishi).

A certain text says (或本云)

The main text says the empress moved to the Asakura no Tachibana no Hironiwa Palace in the fifth month, but this certain text says she moved to the Asakura Palace in the fourth month.

A certain text says (或本云)

It places in the year 辛酉 (the 7th year) the settling, on newly cultivated fields in Ōmi province, of the 106 Tang prisoners presented by 福信*. The main text records the presentation in the tenth month of the 6th year and says they were placed in Mino province, and the compilers state that, since this had already been recorded under the year 庚申, they keep this only as a note.

In the original text

狂心渠。損費，功夫三萬餘矣。

The words with which people slandered the canal dug from Mount Kagu to Mount Isonokami, calling it “the canal of a mad mind” and saying that the labor of more than thirty thousand people had been wasted.

天與赤兄知，吾全不解。

Prince Arima’s answer when asked the reason for the rebellion. It means: “Heaven and Akae know. I understand nothing at all.”

乞師請救，聞之古昔。扶危繼絕，著自恒典。

A passage from the edict ordering aid for Paekche. It means: requests for troops and appeals for aid have been heard of since ancient times, and to support a country in danger and restore a line that has been cut off is the constant way.

皇太子初造漏剋，使民知時。

The record that the crown prince made a water clock (漏剋) for the first time and let the people know the time.

Deities and people, and the shrines that enshrine them

  • Emperor Tenji天智天皇Enshrined at: Ōmi Jingū
  • Empress Saimei (天豐財重日足姬天皇)Empress of Emperor Jomei (息長足日廣額天皇); after once yielding the throne, she took it again at the Itabuki Palace in Asuka. The sovereign of this book.
  • The crown prince (皇太子)The empress’s son (called 天命開別天皇 in the chronicle of Emperor Tenji); he questioned Prince Arima, made the water clock, and escorted the empress’s funeral to Naniwa.
  • Prince Arima (有間皇子)A prince who was arrested for plotting rebellion after being drawn in by Soga no Akae, and was strangled at 藤白坂*.
  • Soga no Akae (蘇我赤兄臣)As caretaker official he told Prince Arima of the empress’s three failings, and that same night had the prince’s house surrounded and reported to the empress.
  • Abe no Hikita no Hirafu (阿倍引田臣比羅夫)As governor of Koshi (越国守) he led a fleet, subdued the Emishi, and fought the 肅慎*.
  • 建王*The empress’s grandson, who died at the age of eight; the empress composed poems and grieved deeply.
  • 伊吉連博德*A member of the embassy to Tang; his record, the 伊吉連博德書, is quoted as a note in the main text, and through a petition he escaped punishment brought on by slander in Tang.
  • 鬼室福信*A Paekche official of 佐平 rank who, after Paekche’s fall, gathered troops and held the royal city, presented Tang prisoners, and asked for aid and for the return of Prince 豐璋*.
  • 大田姬皇女*Gave birth to a girl while the westbound imperial ship was on the 大伯 sea*; the child was named Princess 大伯 (大伯皇女).
