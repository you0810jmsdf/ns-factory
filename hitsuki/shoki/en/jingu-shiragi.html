Nihon Shoki, Book 9 — Empress Jingū · page 21 of 50

Empress Jingū's Oracle and the Expedition to Silla

神功皇后の神託と新羅遠征

This page records how, after the death of Emperor Chūai, 気長足姫尊 * (Empress Jingū) herself became the medium (神主) and asked the names of the deities, and how, following their instructions, she struck down the Kumaso, 羽白熊鷲* and the tsuchigumo (土蜘蛛), crossed the sea, and made Silla submit. After her return, 誉田天皇* is born in Tsukushi, and after the main text two "一云" (one account) passages give other traditions.

Key points to remember

Main textAt the abstinence palace (斎宮) in the village of 小山田邑*, the empress herself became the medium (神主), had Takeuchi-no-Sukune play the koto, made 中臣烏賊津使主* the interpreter of the oracle (審神者), and after seven days and seven nights drew out the names of the deities. Those who named themselves included 撞賢木厳之御魂天疎向津媛命*, who dwells at the Isuzu Shrine (五十鈴宮) in Ise, Kotoshironushi-no-Kami (事代主神), and 表筒男, 中筒男 and 底筒男 (readings unconfirmed).
  2. Main textAt the bay of 橿日浦*, the empress washed her hair in the sea and confirmed the deities' will when it parted in two; saying, "For a while I will borrow the form of a man," she announced the western campaign to her ministers. Before departure, since she had reached the month of childbirth, she tucked a stone at her waist and prayed that she would give birth in this land after she returned.
  3. Main textSetting out from 和珥津* in the 10th month, she reached Silla aided by the wind and by great fish. The king of Silla surrendered without fighting and swore to become her stable-keeper (飼部) and send tribute every year, and the empress set up the spear she had used as a staff at the gate of the king of Silla. The kings of Koguryŏ (高麗) and Paekche (百済) also promised tribute, and it is said that these are called the Three Han (三韓).
  4. Main textIn the 12th month, the empress gave birth to 誉田天皇* in Tsukushi, and that place was called 宇瀰*. Later, following the instruction of the three 筒男 deities (readings unconfirmed) who had accompanied the army, their rough spirit (荒魂) was worshipped at the village of 山田邑* in 穴門*, and 踐立*, ancestor of the 穴門直*, was made its priest (神主).

In the main text of the Nihon Shoki, more deities name themselves, and the rough spirit is worshipped at the village of 山田邑* in 穴門* after her return.

What the main text says

気長足姫尊* was a great-granddaughter of 稚日本根子彦大日々天皇* and a daughter of Prince 気長宿禰*; her mother was 葛城高顙媛*. She became empress in the 2nd year of 足仲彦天皇*; from her youth she was clever, and her appearance was beautiful. When the emperor died at the 橿日宮* palace in Tsukushi in the 2nd month of the 9th year, the empress grieved that the emperor had died early because he had not followed the deity's instruction, and resolved to learn which deity had cursed him and to seek the land of treasure. She therefore had the ministers and officials expiate their offences and correct their errors, and built an abstinence palace (斎宮) in the village of 小山田邑*.

In the 3rd month, the empress chose an auspicious day, entered the abstinence palace and herself became the medium (神主). She had Takeuchi-no-Sukune play the koto and made 中臣烏賊津使主* the interpreter of the oracle (審神者), and asked the names of the deities who had earlier instructed the emperor. After seven days and seven nights, 撞賢木厳之御魂天疎向津媛命*, who dwells at the Isuzu Shrine (五十鈴宮) in the district of 度逢県* in the province of Ise, named herself; then the deity dwelling in 淡郡 of 吾田節 in 尾田 (readings unconfirmed), Kotoshironushi-no-Kami (事代主神), and 表筒男, 中筒男 and 底筒男 (readings unconfirmed), who dwell in the depths of the water at 橘小門* in the province of Hyūga, named themselves. The empress worshipped them according to their instructions and had 鴨別*, ancestor of the 吉備臣*, strike down the Kumaso, who soon submitted. In the village of 荷持田村* there was also 羽白熊鷲*, who had wings, flew high, and plundered the people.

On her way from the 橿日宮* palace to the 松峡宮* palace, the empress's hat (笠) was blown off by a whirlwind, and that place was called 御笠*. She destroyed 羽白熊鷲* at 層増岐野*, and because she said that her heart was at ease, she named that place 安*. Further, at 山門県* she put to death 田油津媛* of the tsuchigumo (土蜘蛛), and her elder brother 夏羽*, who had raised troops and come to meet her, fled on hearing that his younger sister had been killed.

In the 4th month, by a small stream in the village of 玉嶋里 in the district of 松浦県 in the province of 火前国 (readings unconfirmed), the empress bent a needle into a fishhook, used grains of rice as bait and threads from her skirt as fishing line, and prayed, "If the undertaking of seeking the land of treasure in the west is to succeed, let a fish bite," and she caught a 細鱗魚*. Because she said "mezurashi" (how rare!), the land was called 梅豆羅国*, and it is said that the present 松浦 is a corruption of that name. It is said that the women of that land fish for sweetfish (年魚) in the river in the first part of the 4th month, and that when men fish they catch nothing. To make a sacred rice field (神田), the empress had a channel dug to draw water from the 儺河* river; when it was blocked by a great rock at 迹驚岡*, she had Takeuchi-no-Sukune offer up a sword and a mirror and pray, and thunder split the rock and the water passed through (the 裂田溝)*. Further, when she washed her hair in the sea at the bay of 橿日浦*, the hair parted of itself in two, so she tied it up into topknots (髻), and announced her resolve for the western campaign, saying that if it succeeded the credit would be the ministers', and if it failed the fault would be hers alone; and the ministers followed her.

In the 9th month she had ships gathered from the provinces and troops trained, but the troops did not assemble; when she set up the 大三輪社* shrine and offered swords and spears, the troops assembled. The fisherman 烏摩呂* reported that no land could be seen to the west, and 名草*, a fisherman of 磯鹿*, reported that there was a mountain with clouds over it to the northwest. The empress took up the axe of command (斧鉞) and set out the military rules, and the deity instructed, "The gentle spirit (和魂) will protect the ruler's body, and the rough spirit (荒魂) will lead the ships as the vanguard"; 依網吾彦男垂見* was made the priest (神主) for the worship. Since she had just reached the month of childbirth, the empress tucked a stone at her waist and prayed that she would give birth in this land on the day she returned when the undertaking was over. It is said that the stone is still by the roadside in 伊都県*.

In the 10th month, when she set out from 和珥津*, the wind rose and great fish held up the ships, so that they reached Silla without using oars, and the tide, carrying the ships, reached into the interior of the land. The king of Silla, fearing that these were the divine troops of the divine land in the east, surrendered of his own accord; he sealed up his maps and registers and surrendered before the royal ship, and swore again and again to become a stable-keeper (飼部) and send tribute every year. Some called for the king to be killed, but the empress, saying that one who surrenders of his own accord should not be killed, loosed his bonds, sealed the treasure storehouses of the land, and set up the spear she had used as a staff at the gate of the king of Silla as a mark for later ages. The king of Silla, 波沙寐錦*, gave 微叱己知波珍干岐* as a hostage, and had gold, silver and textiles loaded on eighty ships to accompany her. The kings of the two countries of Koguryŏ (高麗) and Paekche (百済), knowing they could not win, also swore to bring tribute, and the royal granaries (屯倉) of the 内官家* were established. This is regarded as what are called the Three Han (三韓), and the empress returned from Silla.

In the 12th month, the empress gave birth to 誉田天皇* in Tsukushi, and the people called the place where she gave birth 宇瀰*. The three deities 表筒男, 中筒男 and 底筒男 (readings unconfirmed), who had accompanied the army, also instructed that their rough spirit be worshipped at the village of 山田邑* in 穴門*. 踐立*, ancestor of the 穴門直*, and 田裳見宿禰*, ancestor of the 津守連*, said that the place the deities desired should be determined, and 踐立 was made the priest (神主) to worship the rough spirit, and a shrine was set up at the village of 山田邑 in 穴門.

A plain retelling of what the main text of the Nihon Shoki says (no published translation has been used).

Variant accounts

After its main text, the Nihon Shoki often sets out other versions, introduced with words such as "a certain book says" (一書曰).

One account (一云), the first

The deity first possessed 内避高国避高松屋種*, ancestor of the 沙麼県主*, and instructed the emperor, and next possessed the empress, who was playing the koto. The deity named itself with the names 表筒雄, 中筒雄 and 底筒雄 (readings unconfirmed) and with the name 向匱男聞襲大歴五御魂速狭騰尊*, and the emperor, who did not believe, fell ill and died that night. The empress, in men's attire, conquered Silla, and this account gives the name of the king of Silla who surrendered as 宇流助富利智干*.

One account (一云), the second

It says that the king of Silla was captured, killed on the beach and buried in the sand, and that one man was left behind as governor (宰) when they returned. The king's wife tricked the governor into telling her where the king was buried, then killed the governor and buried him beneath the king's grave. When the emperor heard of this and sent a great army, the people of Silla killed the king's wife and apologized.

In the original text

名撞賢木嚴之御魂天疎向津媛命焉

The name of the deity dwelling at the Isuzu Shrine (五十鈴宮) in Ise, who was the first to name herself during the interpretation of the oracle.

然暫假男貌、强起雄略。

The empress's words: though I am a woman, for a while I will borrow the form of a man and undertake a great plan.

和魂服王身而守壽命。荒魂爲先鋒而導師船。

The deity's instruction: the gentle spirit will attach itself to the ruler's body and protect her life, and the rough spirit will become the vanguard and lead the army's ships.

以皇后所杖矛、樹於新羅王門

The spear the empress had used as a staff was set up at the gate of the king of Silla as a mark for later ages.

In the Kojiki

Kojiki, Part 2, section on Emperor Chūai (the oracle and the naming of the deities)

In the Kojiki, the deities name themselves as the will of Amaterasu-Ōmikami and as the three great deities 底筒男, 中筒男 and 上筒男 (readings unconfirmed). In the main text of the Nihon Shoki, the deity of the Isuzu Shrine (五十鈴宮) in Ise, the deity of 淡郡*, Kotoshironushi-no-Kami (事代主神) and the three 筒男 deities name themselves in turn, and the name of Amaterasu-Ōmikami does not appear.

是天照大神之御心者
Kojiki, Part 2, section on Emperor Chūai (Empress Jingū's personal expedition to Silla)

Setting up the staff at the gate of the king of Silla is common to both. In the Kojiki she then worships the rough spirit (荒御魂) of the Great Deity of 墨江 (墨江大神)* as the deity protecting the land and returns, whereas in the main text of the Nihon Shoki the rough spirit is worshipped at the village of 山田邑* in 穴門* after her return.

以其御杖。衝立新羅國主之門
Kojiki, Part 2, section on Emperor Chūai (the birth of the child and the fishing at 玉嶋里)*

The story of fishing for sweetfish (年魚) at 玉嶋里* is also in the Kojiki, but there it is an event after she has returned from Silla and her child has been born in Tsukushi, whereas the main text of the Nihon Shoki tells it, before the expedition, as fishing to divine whether the undertaking would succeed. The stone she had at her waist is said in the Kojiki to be at the village of 伊斗村*, and in the Nihon Shoki to be by the roadside in 伊都県*.
