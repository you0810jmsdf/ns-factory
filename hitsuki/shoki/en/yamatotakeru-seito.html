Nihon Shoki, Book 7 — Emperor Keikō · page 18 of 50

Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto's Campaign against the Kumaso and His Departure on the Eastern Campaign

日本武尊の熊襲征伐と東征への出発

This page records how, in the 27th year of Emperor Keikō, the sixteen-year-old Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto was sent to strike down the Kumaso, who had rebelled again, how he stabbed 川上梟帥* while disguised as a young girl, and how, on being presented with a name by him, he came to call himself "日本武" (Yamato Takeru). When the eastern barbarians rebel in the 40th year, Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto receives the axe of command (斧鉞) in place of Prince 大碓*, who has fled and hidden, and in Ise he is given the sword Kusanagi (草薙剣) by 倭姫命* and sets out.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textIn the 10th month of the 27th year, the sixteen-year-old Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto was sent to strike down the Kumaso. In the 12th month, he let down his hair to take the appearance of a young girl, hid a sword in his clothing, slipped into the banquet of the Kumaso chieftain 川上梟帥 (also 取石鹿文; readings unconfirmed), and stabbed the drunken 梟帥 in the chest.
  2. Main textThe dying 川上梟帥*, saying that he had never met anyone of such martial power, presented the name "日本武皇子" (Prince Yamato Takeru), and it is said that from then on the prince came to be called Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto.
  3. Main textIn the 40th year, when the eastern barbarians rebelled, Prince 大碓* was afraid and hid in the grass, and was enfeoffed in Mino. The emperor gave Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto the axe of command (斧鉞), and, saying, "In form he is my son; in truth he is a divine person," entrusted him with the pacification of the eastern lands; he had 吉備武彦* and 大伴武日連* accompany him, and made 七掬脛* his steward of meals (膳夫).
  4. Main textIn the 10th month, Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto turned aside from his road to worship at the Ise Shrine, and was given the sword Kusanagi (草薙剣) by 倭姫命*, who told him, "Be careful, and do not be negligent."
  5. Compared with the KojikiIn the Kojiki, 小碓命* is sent to the west because he killed his elder brother 大碓命* and the emperor feared his violent nature; in the Nihon Shoki he is sent because the Kumaso rebelled again, and Prince 大碓 later hides out of fear of the eastern campaign and is enfeoffed in Mino.
  6. CommentaryOn his way back from the Kumaso, Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto killed the evil deities of the 穴海* in Kibi and of the 柏 crossing* in Naniwa. Concerning the "穴戸神"*, who is brought to submission by words in the corresponding scene of the Kojiki, the database of deity names lists the theory that it is the 穴門* of Nagato, the theory that it is the 穴海 of Kibi, and the theory that it refers to straits in general. [source, Japanese]

What the main text says

In the 8th month of the 27th year, the Kumaso rebelled again and kept invading the frontier. In the 10th month, the emperor sent the sixteen-year-old Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto to strike down the Kumaso. When the prince sought a skilled archer, he was told that 弟彦公* of the province of Mino would be good, and he had 宮戸彦* of Katsuragi (葛城) go to summon him. 弟彦公 followed the prince, bringing with him 石占横立* and the 稲置 of 田子 and the 稲置 of 乳近 (readings unconfirmed) of Owari.

In the 12th month, the prince arrived in the land of the Kumaso and observed the situation and the lie of the land. Hearing that the Kumaso chieftain 取石鹿文, also called 川上梟帥 (readings unconfirmed), was gathering his kin for a banquet, the prince let down his hair to take the appearance of a young girl, hid a sword in his clothing, and went in among the women at the banquet. 梟帥 was charmed by his appearance, took him by the hand and seated him beside him, offered him the cup and dallied with him. When the night grew late and 梟帥 was drunk, the prince drew his sword and stabbed him in the chest.

Before dying, 梟帥* asked him to wait a moment and asked who he was. When the prince named himself 日本童男*, son of 大足彦天皇*, 梟帥 said that he himself was the strongest man in the land and had met many men of martial power, but none had equaled the prince; he presented the name 日本武皇子 (Prince Yamato Takeru), and when he had finished speaking he was stabbed to death by the prince. After 弟彦* and the others had cut down all of 梟帥's followers, the prince, on his way back to Yamato by sea, killed the evil deity at the place where one crosses the 穴海* in Kibi, and in Naniwa also killed the evil deity of the 柏 crossing*.

In the 2nd month of the 28th year, Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto reported the pacification of the Kumaso, saying that thanks to the emperor's divine spirit he had struck down their chieftain and the western lands were calm. He also reported that the deity of the 穴済* in Kibi and the deity of the 柏済* in Naniwa had been giving off poisonous vapors and afflicting travelers, so he had killed them both and opened the routes by water and by land. The emperor praised the prince's achievement and loved him especially.

In the 6th month of the 40th year, many of the eastern barbarians rebelled, and the frontier was in turmoil. In the 7th month, when the emperor asked his ministers whom to send, Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto said that he had labored in the western campaign, so this time it was the task of Prince 大碓*. Prince 大碓 was startled and fled and hid in the grass, and the emperor reproached him and enfeoffed him in Mino. He is the founding ancestor of the 身毛津君 and the 守君 (readings unconfirmed). When Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto came forward to go himself to pacify them, the emperor gave him the axe of command (斧鉞), spoke of the fierceness of the eastern barbarians, especially the Emishi (蝦夷), praised the prince, saying, "In form he is my son; in truth he is a divine person," and ordered him to make them submit by displaying his might and winning them over with virtue. 吉備武彦* and 大伴武日連* accompanied him, and 七掬脛* became his steward of meals (膳夫).

On the day (癸丑) of the 10th month, Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto set out. On the day (戊午), he turned aside from his road to worship at the Ise Shrine, and told 倭姫命* that he was setting out on the eastern campaign. 倭姫命 took the sword Kusanagi (草薙剣), gave it to the prince, and told him to be careful and never be negligent.

A plain retelling of what the main text of the Nihon Shoki says (no published translation has been used).

In the original text

吾多遇武力矣。未有若皇子者。

The words of 川上梟帥*: I have met many men of martial power, but there has been none like the prince.

自今以後號皇子。應稱日本武皇子。

The words with which 川上梟帥* presented the name: from now on the prince should be called 日本武皇子 (Prince Yamato Takeru).

形則我子。實則神人。

The emperor's words describing Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto: in form he is my son, but in truth he is a person like a deity.

倭姫命取草薙釼。授日本武尊曰。愼之莫怠也。

倭姫命* gave the sword Kusanagi (草薙剣) to Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto and told him to be careful and not be negligent.

In the Kojiki

Kojiki, Part 2, section on Emperor Keikō (the western campaign of 小碓命)*

In the Kojiki, the 熊曾建* are two brothers; 小碓命* stabs the elder through the chest and the fleeing younger through the buttocks and kills them, and is presented with the name 倭建 (Yamato Takeru) by the younger brother. In the Nihon Shoki the chieftain is a single man, 川上梟帥*. The Kojiki further includes the story of his killing 出雲建* by treachery on the way back, but this is not found in this entry of the Nihon Shoki.

熊曾建兄弟二人
Kojiki, Part 2, section on Emperor Keikō (beginning of the western campaign of 小碓命)*

In the Kojiki, when his elder brother 大碓命* did not come out for the morning and evening meals, 小碓命* caught him in the privy, tore off his limbs, wrapped him in a straw mat (薦) and threw him away. In the Nihon Shoki, Prince 大碓 is alive, hides out of fear of the eastern campaign, and is enfeoffed in Mino.

引闕其枝。裹薦投棄。
Kojiki, Part 2, section on Emperor Keikō (the eastern campaign of Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto)

In the Kojiki, Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto (倭建命), in Ise, laments to his aunt 倭比売命*, asking whether the emperor wishes him dead, and 倭比売命 gives him the sword Kusanagi (草那芸剣) and a bag (御嚢). In the Nihon Shoki, the emperor praises the prince as a divine person and gives him the axe of command, and 倭姫命* only gives him the sword Kusanagi (草薙剣) and says, "Be careful, and do not be negligent."
