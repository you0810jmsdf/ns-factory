Nihon Shoki, Book 13 — Emperor Ingyō and Emperor Ankō · page 27 of 50

Emperor Ingyō's oath ordeal and 衣通郎姫*, and the death of Emperor Ankō

允恭天皇の盟神探湯と衣通郎姫・安康天皇の最期

Emperor 雄朝津間稚子宿禰 (雄朝津間稚子宿禰天皇; Ingyō)* kept declining the throne on account of a serious illness, but ascended it at the plea of his consort 忍坂大中姫*, and corrected the confusion of clan names and titles through the oath ordeal by boiling water (盟神探湯). After his favor toward the Empress's younger sister 衣通郎姫* and the affair of the Crown Prince 木梨輕 (木梨輕太子)*, the next ruler, Emperor 穴穗 (穴穗天皇; Ankō)*, believed a slander and killed 大草香皇子*, and was killed by that prince's son 眉輪王*.

Read in the Kojiki: Emperor Ingyō, Crown Prince 軽 and 軽大郎女 (readings unconfirmed) ›Read in the Kojiki: Emperor Ankō and the Incident of 目弱王* ›

Key points to remember

  1. Main textPrince 雄朝津間稚子宿禰 (雄朝津間稚子宿禰皇子)* kept refusing the ministers' nomination on account of a serious illness, but in the 12th month of the first year his consort 忍坂大中姫* kept waiting in the cold wind holding out water for washing his hands, until the water in the metal bowl (鋺) overflowed and froze onto her arms, and at last he ascended the throne.
  2. Main textIn the 4th year, saying that clan names and titles had fallen into confusion and that some people were making false claims, the Emperor had people undergo the oath ordeal by boiling water (盟神探湯; a reading note gives "區訶陀智") at the 味橿 hill*. They wore sashes of bark-cloth (木綿) and were made to reach into the boiling water in cauldrons; those who told the truth were unharmed and those who lied were all injured, and the text records that from then on clan names and titles were settled.
  3. Main textHearing at a banquet in the 7th year the name of the Empress's younger sister 弟姫 (衣通郎姫) (readings unconfirmed), the Emperor sent 中臣烏賦津使主* to bring her. Because of the Empress's jealousy he built a residence for her at Fujiwara (藤原), and later at 茅渟* in Kawachi; after the Empress remonstrated with him in the 10th year, citing the hardship of the people, his visits to 茅渟 became rare.
  4. Main text木梨輕皇子*, who became Crown Prince in the 23rd year, had relations with his full sister 輕大娘皇女*; in the 24th year this came to light when divination was performed about the strange freezing of the soup at the Emperor's meal. The Crown Prince, being heir apparent, was not punished, and 大娘皇女 was exiled to Iyo; after the Emperor's death the Crown Prince, deserted by the ministers, took his own life in the house of 物部大前宿禰*.
  5. Main textIn his first year Emperor 穴穗 (穴穗天皇)* believed the slander of 根使主* and killed 大草香皇子*, took his wife 中蒂姫* into the palace, and made her Empress in the 2nd year. In the 8th month of the 3rd year, the Emperor was killed by 眉輪王*, whom 中蒂姫 had borne to 大草香皇子 (a note says that the details are in the annals of Emperor 大泊瀬)*.
  6. Compared with the KojikiIn the Kojiki, the affair of Crown Prince 軽 (軽太子) and 軽大郎女 (readings unconfirmed) is placed after the death of Emperor Ingyō and before the accession; the one exiled is the Crown Prince (to the Iyo hot springs, 伊余湯), and 軽大郎女 later follows him and the two die together by their own hand. In the Kojiki another name of 軽大郎女 is 衣通郎女*, but in the Nihon Shoki 衣通郎姫* is portrayed separately, as 弟姫*, the Empress's younger sister.

What the main text says

Emperor 雄朝津間稚子宿禰 (雄朝津間稚子宿禰天皇)* was the full younger brother of Emperor 瑞齒別 (瑞齒別天皇)*. From childhood he was compassionate and modest, but as an adult he fell seriously ill and could not move about freely. When the previous emperor died in the 1st month of the 5th year of Emperor 瑞齒別, the ministers chose this prince, the eldest and most benevolent and filial of the sons of Emperor 大鷦鷯 (大鷦鷯天皇)*, and offered him the imperial seals. The prince declined, citing how he had secretly injured his own body trying to cure his illness and had been reproached by the former emperor as unfilial, and how he had been slighted by his two elder brothers, the emperors; and even when the ministers pleaded again and again, he would not agree. In the 12th month of the first year, his consort 忍坂大中姫命* came before the prince holding out water for washing his hands and waited in the cold wind for four or five 剋 without withdrawing, so that the water in the metal bowl (鋺) overflowed and froze onto her arms and she was near to freezing to death. The prince, startled, said that the ministers' plea was plainly reasonable, and at last accepted the seals of office (璽符) and ascended the throne.

In the 2nd month of the 2nd year, 忍坂大中姫* was made Empress, and the 刑部* were established. The Empress bore nine children, including 木梨輕皇子*, Emperor 穴穗 (穴穗天皇)*, 輕大娘皇女* and Emperor 大泊瀬稚武 (大泊瀬稚武天皇)*. Once, while the Empress was still living at home with her mother, the 闘鷄国造*, passing by, spoke rudely to her from his horse and asked for an orchid; after she became Empress she sought him out, charged him with his offense, spared him the death penalty, and reduced his title to 稻置*. In the 1st month of the 3rd year an envoy was sent to Silla to seek a good physician, and the physician who came in the 8th month cured the Emperor's illness, so the Emperor rewarded him generously and sent him home.

In the 9th month of the 4th year, the Emperor issued an edict to correct clan names and titles, saying that the cause of disorder was that high and low were contending, and that some people had lost their own titles while others falsely claimed high clans. Since people were saying, one after another, that they were descendants of emperors or descendants of those who had descended from heaven, pots for the boiling-water ordeal (探湯瓮) were set up at the 味橿 hill* and the oath ordeal by boiling water (盟神探湯) was carried out; those who were lying grew afraid and did not come forward, and from then on clan names and titles were settled. On the night of an earthquake in the 7th month of the 5th year, it became known that 玉田宿禰*, who had been charged with the temporary enshrinement (殯) of Emperor 瑞齒別 (瑞齒別天皇)*, had left the mortuary palace (殯宮) and was holding a drinking party. 玉田宿禰 killed 尾張連吾襲*, who had come to see how things were, and fled into the grave precinct of Takeuchi-no-Sukune. When 玉田宿禰 was summoned, a court lady (采女) saw that he was wearing armor under his robe; he fled to his house, where he was surrounded and put to death. In the 11th month, Emperor 瑞齒別 was buried in the 耳原陵 tomb*.

In the 12th month of the 7th year, at a banquet for a new house, the Emperor played the koto and the Empress danced, but after the dance the Empress did not say the words required by the etiquette of the time, offering a maiden; urged by the Emperor, she said she would offer her younger sister 弟姫*. 弟姫 was so beautiful that her beauty shone through her clothing, and she was called 衣通郎姫*. 弟姫, who lived at 坂田* in Ōmi, out of consideration for the Empress's feelings did not come though summoned seven times, but when 中臣烏賦津使主* lay prostrate in her courtyard for seven days, enduring on nothing but the dried rice in the fold of his robe, she at last came up to the capital. Because the Empress was displeased, the Emperor built a hall at Fujiwara (藤原) and had her live there. On the night the Empress was giving birth to Emperor 大泊瀬 (大泊瀬天皇)*, the Emperor went to Fujiwara; the Empress set fire to the birthing hall intending to die, and the Emperor apologized, saying it was his fault. In the 8th year, 衣通郎姫 asked to live far away to escape the Empress's resentment, and the Emperor built a palace at 茅渟* in Kawachi; but in the 10th year the Empress remonstrated, citing the hardship of the people, so his visits to 茅渟 became rare, and in the 11th year the 藤原部* was established for 衣通郎姫.

In the 9th month of the 14th year, the Emperor hunted on Awaji Island but caught no game all day. When divination was performed, the deity of the island declared that if the pearl at the bottom of the sea of Akashi were offered to it, game would be caught. Among the fishermen, only 男狹磯* of 長邑* in Awa Province dived to the deep bottom; he came up holding a large 蝮 (a creature from the seabed) that held something shining, but died then and there. A pearl the size of a peach came out of its body, and when it was offered to the deity much game was caught; it is said that 男狹磯's grave was built with great care and still remains today. In the 3rd month of the 23rd year, 木梨輕皇子* was made Crown Prince. The Crown Prince had relations with his full sister 輕大娘皇女*; in the 6th month of the 24th year, when divination was performed because the soup at the Emperor's meal had frozen, it indicated disorder within the family, and the affair came to light. The Crown Prince, being heir apparent, was not punished, and 大娘皇女 was exiled to Iyo.

In the 1st month of the 42nd year, the Emperor died. The king of Silla, hearing this, sent eighty tribute ships and eighty musicians; the party wept greatly at Tsushima and Tsukushi, and from Naniwa they went to the mortuary palace in mourning clothes, playing their instruments. In the winter, in the 10th month, the Emperor was buried in the 長野原陵 tomb* in Kawachi. In the 11th month, the Silla mourning envoys, on their way home, called out to Mount Unebi (畝傍山) and Mount Miminashi (耳成山) with longing in their accented speech; the horse-keepers (飼部) of Yamato suspected them of having had relations with court ladies (采女) and accused them to Prince 大泊瀬 (大泊瀬皇子)*, and the envoys were arrested and questioned. The suspicion was cleared, but it is said that the people of Silla deeply resented this and reduced the kinds of their tribute and the number of ships.

Emperor 穴穗 (穴穗天皇)* was the second son of Emperor 雄朝津間稚子宿禰*, and his mother was 忍坂大中姫命*. After the funeral of the previous emperor, the Crown Prince 木梨輕 (木梨輕太子)* was censured by the people for his violence and his dissoluteness with women, and the ministers followed Prince 穴穗. The Crown Prince prepared troops but, isolated, fled to the house of 物部大前宿禰*; when Prince 穴穗 surrounded the house, after 大前宿禰's mediation, the Crown Prince took his own life in that house. In the 12th month Prince 穴穗 ascended the throne and placed the capital at the 穴穗宮 palace* in 石上*. In the 2nd month of the first year he sought 幡梭皇女*, younger sister of 大草香皇子*, for his younger brother Prince 大泊瀬*; but the messenger 根使主* kept for himself the 押木珠縵 jeweled headdress* that 大草香皇子 had presented and slandered him, so the Emperor in anger killed 大草香皇子. 難波吉師日香蛟*, who had served 大草香皇子, took his own life with his two sons beside their lord's body, and the Emperor took 中蒂姫*, the wife of 大草香皇子, into the palace and made her Empress in the 2nd year. In the 8th month of the 3rd year, the Emperor was killed by 眉輪王*, the child 中蒂姫 had brought with her, and three years later he was buried in the 菅原伏見陵 tomb*.

Variant accounts

After its main text, the Nihon Shoki often sets out other versions, introduced with words such as "a certain book says" (一書曰).

One version (一云)

The main text makes Emperor 穴穗 (穴穗天皇)* the second son of Emperor 雄朝津間稚子宿禰*, but this "one version says" (一云) makes him the third son.

One version (一云)

The main text says that the Crown Prince 木梨輕 (木梨輕太子)* took his own life in the house of 物部大前宿禰*, but this "one version says" (一云) says that he was exiled to Iyo Province.

One version (一云) / Another version (又云)

Concerning the treasure presented by 大草香皇子*, the 押木珠縵 jeweled headdress*, these give other names for it: 立縵 and also 磐木縵 (readings unconfirmed).

In the original text

得實則全。僞者必害。

The words declared at the oath ordeal by boiling water: those who tell the truth will be unharmed, and those who lie will surely be injured.

其艶色徹衣而晃之。是以。時人號曰衣通郎姫也。

Her beauty shone through her clothing, so the people of the time called her 衣通郎姫*.

大中姫所捧鋺水溢而腕凝。

The water in the metal bowl that 大中姫* was holding out overflowed and froze onto her arms.

吾君無罪以死之。悲乎。

The lament of 日香蛟* and his sons: our lord has died though guilty of no crime; how sad!

In the Kojiki

Kojiki, Part 3, beginning of the section on Emperor Ingyō (G58)

In the Kojiki, the one who cures the Emperor's illness is 金波鎭漢紀武*, chief envoy of the tribute mission from Silla (新良国); it is not, as in the Nihon Shoki, a matter of sending an envoy to seek a physician. The story of settling clan names and titles is also given in a single sentence, saying only that they were settled by setting up 玖訶瓮 pots* at 言八十禍津日前*.

名云金波鎭漢紀武。此人深知藥方。
Kojiki, Part 3, section on Emperor Ingyō, Crown Prince 軽 and 軽大郎女 (readings unconfirmed) (G59–G83)

In the Kojiki, Crown Prince 軽 (軽太子)* has relations with his full sister after Emperor Ingyō's death and before his own accession; deserted by the officials, he is captured by 大前小前宿禰* and exiled to the Iyo hot springs (伊余湯). 軽大郎女* (also called 衣通郎女)* follows him, and the two are said to have died together by their own hand. In the Nihon Shoki the affair comes to light during the Emperor's reign, the one exiled is 輕大娘皇女*, and the Crown Prince takes his own life in the house of 大前宿禰*.

故其輕太子者。流於伊余湯也。
Kojiki, Part 3, section on Emperor Ankō (G84)

In the Kojiki, the prince who is killed is 大日下王*, and the one sought as wife for Prince 大長谷 (大長谷王子)* is his younger sister 若日下王*; the Emperor makes 長田大郎女*, the principal wife of 大日下王, his Empress, and 目弱王*, aged seven, overhears the Emperor's words and cuts him down while he is taking a nap. In the Nihon Shoki the wife of 大草香皇子* is 中蒂姫*; it records that 日香蛟* and his sons died following their lord, and leaves the details of the killing to the annals of Emperor Yūryaku (雄略紀).

其王之嫡妻。長田大郎女。爲皇后。
