Nihon Shoki, Book 10 — Emperor Ōjin · page 23 of 50

The birth and accession of Emperor Ōjin and the first half of his reign

応神天皇の誕生・即位と前半の治世

This section records the birth of Emperor Homuta (誉田天皇; Emperor Ōjin) and the origin of his name, his accession, and his consorts and children, and then describes the events from the 3rd to the 13th year, year by year. It includes the replacement of King 辰斯* of Paekche, the ship 枯野*, the ordeal by boiling water (探湯) between Takeuchi-no-Sukune and 甘美内宿禰*, and the songs of the Emperor and 大鷦鷯尊* concerning 髪長媛*.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textThe Emperor was born at 蚊田* in Tsukushi in the 12th month of the year (庚辰) in which the Empress attacked Silla. Because there was flesh on his arm shaped like an archer's wrist guard (鞆), he is said to have been called Emperor Homuta (誉田天皇), from the ancient custom of calling the wrist guard "褒武多".
  2. Main textIn the 9th year, the younger brother 甘美内宿禰* slandered Takeuchi-no-Sukune, saying, "Takeuchi-no-Sukune intends to split off Tsukushi and take the realm." 真根子*, ancestor of the 壱伎直*, who resembled Takeuchi-no-Sukune, killed himself in his place. Takeuchi-no-Sukune pleaded his innocence at court, and by the Emperor's command the two underwent the ordeal by boiling water (探湯) beside the 磯城 river*; Takeuchi-no-Sukune won.
  3. Main textIn the 13th year, the Emperor, learning that his son 大鷦鷯尊* had set his heart on 髪長媛*, whom the Emperor had summoned from Hyūga, gave her to 大鷦鷯尊 by means of a song at a banquet, and 大鷦鷯尊 answered with a song of joy.
  4. Main textIn the 3rd year, because King 辰斯* of Paekche had been discourteous to the Emperor, 紀角宿禰* and three others were sent to reproach him. Paekche killed King 辰斯 and apologized, and 紀角宿禰 and the others set up 阿菟 (as written in the original)* as king and returned.
  5. Compared with the KojikiThe song the Emperor composed at 菟道野上* in the 6th year also appears in the Kojiki, in almost the same words, as a song composed at 宇遅野上* while looking out over 葛野*.
  6. CommentaryOn the origin of the name, a "one version says" (一云) in a small-character note gives a story in which the Crown Prince exchanged names with Kehi-no-Ōkami (笥飯大神) of 角鹿*, and says it is "unclear" (未詳) because there is no record to confirm it. The Kokugakuin University deity-name database notes that there is one view that this note was a later insertion and another that it was there from the time of compilation. [source, Japanese]

What the main text says

Emperor Homuta (誉田天皇) was the fourth son of Emperor 足仲彦 (足仲彦天皇)*, and his mother was 気長足姫尊*. He was born at 蚊田* in Tsukushi in the winter, in the 12th month of the year 庚辰, the year the Empress attacked Silla. From childhood he was intelligent, and his conduct was outstanding. In the 3rd year of the Empress Dowager's regency, at the age of three, he was made Crown Prince. It is said that while he was in his mother's womb the heavenly and earthly deities granted him the Three Han (三韓). When he was born there was flesh on his arm like an archer's wrist guard (鞆), resembling the wrist guard the Empress Dowager had worn when she dressed as a man, so he was named Emperor Homuta. In the 4th month of the 69th year of the regency the Empress Dowager died (a small-character note says at the age of one hundred).

In the 1st month of the first year, the Crown Prince ascended the throne. This year was the Great Year 庚寅 (太歳庚寅). In the 3rd month of the 2nd year, Nakatsuhime-no-Mikoto (仲姫) was made Empress, and she bore 荒田皇女*, Emperor 大鷦鷯 (大鷦鷯天皇)* and 根鳥皇子*. Before that, the Empress's elder sister 高城入姫* had borne 額田大中彦皇子, 大山守皇子 and 去来真稚皇子 (readings unconfirmed) and others, and the other consorts bore 菟道稚郎子皇子, 稚野毛二派皇子 and 隼総別皇子 (readings unconfirmed) and others; the Emperor's children, sons and daughters together, numbered twenty. 根鳥皇子 is regarded as the founding ancestor of the 大田君*, 大山守皇子 of the 土形君 and 榛原君 (readings unconfirmed), and 去来真稚皇子 of the 深河別*.

In the 10th month of the 3rd year, all the Emishi of the east brought tribute, so the Emishi were used to build the 廐坂 road*. In the 11th month, the fishermen (海人) of various places made disturbances and did not obey orders, so 大浜宿禰*, ancestor of the 阿曇連*, was sent to pacify them and was made chief of the fishermen. In this year, because King 辰斯* of Paekche had been discourteous, 紀角宿禰, 羽田矢代宿禰, 石川宿禰 and 木菟宿禰 (readings unconfirmed) were sent to reproach him. Paekche killed King 辰斯 and apologized, and 紀角宿禰 and the others set up 阿菟* as king and returned. In the 8th month of the 5th year, the provinces were ordered to establish fishermen's groups and mountain wardens (山守部). In the 10th month, a ship ten jō (十丈) long was built in Izu Province; because it ran light and fast it was named 枯野* (a small-character note suggests this is a corruption of "軽野").

In the 2nd month of the 6th year, the Emperor went to Ōmi Province and composed a song at 菟道野上*. In the 9th month of the 7th year, people from Koguryŏ (高麗), Paekche, Mimana and Silla came to court; Takeuchi-no-Sukune was made to lead the various Korean people (韓人) in building a pond, which was named the 韓人池 pond*. In the 3rd month of the 8th year, people from Paekche came to court. The Paekche Record (百済記), quoted in a small-character note, records that because King 阿花 (阿花王)* had been discourteous, lands such as 枕彌多禮* were taken from him, and he sent the prince 直支* to the heavenly court to renew the friendship of the former king.

In the 4th month of the 9th year, Takeuchi-no-Sukune was sent to Tsukushi to inspect the people. His younger brother 甘美内宿禰*, wishing to set his elder brother aside, slandered him, saying that Takeuchi-no-Sukune intended to split off Tsukushi, bring the Three Han (三韓) under him and take the realm; the Emperor sent a messenger to kill Takeuchi-no-Sukune. 真根子*, ancestor of the 壱伎直*, who closely resembled Takeuchi-no-Sukune in appearance, said that he would take his place and so make plain the minister's loyalty, and fell on his sword and died. Takeuchi-no-Sukune secretly left Tsukushi, went round by the southern sea, reached the harbor of 紀水門*, and pleaded his innocence at court. Since the claims of the two could not be decided, the Emperor petitioned the deities of heaven and earth and had them undergo the ordeal by boiling water (探湯); beside the 磯城 river*, Takeuchi-no-Sukune won. Takeuchi-no-Sukune knocked 甘美内宿禰 down and was about to kill him, but the Emperor had him spared and gave him to the ancestors of the 紀伊直* and others.

In the 10th month of the 11th year, the ponds of 剣池, 軽池, 鹿垣池 and 廐坂池 (readings unconfirmed) were made. In this year the Emperor heard that in Hyūga Province there was a beautiful girl named 髪長媛*, daughter of 諸県君牛諸井*, and wished to summon her. In the 3rd month of the 13th year he sent a messenger, and in the 9th month 髪長媛 came from Hyūga and was lodged at the village of 桑津*. The Emperor's son 大鷦鷯尊* saw her and set his heart on her for her beauty. Learning of this, the Emperor, on the day of a banquet in the inner palace, had 髪長媛 brought to the banquet seats, called 大鷦鷯尊 and sang while pointing to her. 大鷦鷯尊, understanding that she was being given to him, rejoiced and answered with a song, and after becoming intimate with her he composed two more songs.

A plain retelling of what the main text of the Nihon Shoki says (no published translation has been used).

Variant accounts

After its main text, the Nihon Shoki often sets out other versions, introduced with words such as "a certain book says" (一書曰).

One version (一云): the origin of the name Homuta (誉田)

A "one version says" (一云) in a small-character note states that when the Emperor was Crown Prince he went to the land of 越*, worshipped Kehi-no-Ōkami (笥飯大神) of 角鹿* and exchanged names with the great deity, so that the deity is called Izasawake-no-Kami (去来紗別神) and the Crown Prince Homutawake-no-Mikoto (誉田別尊). If so, the deity's original name would have been Homutawake-no-Kami (誉田別神) and the Crown Prince's original name Izasawake-no-Mikoto (去来紗別尊); but it records that, since there is no record, this is unclear (未詳).

One version (一云): the presentation of 髪長媛*

It says that when 諸県君牛*, grown old, retired from service at court, he presented his daughter 髪長媛*. While the Emperor was hunting on Awaji Island, he saw a herd of several dozen deer coming across the sea, but they were in fact people wearing deerskins with the antlers attached. It records that the place where they landed came to be called the harbor of 鹿子水門*, and that the practice of calling boatmen 鹿子 began at this time.

In the original text

上古時俗，號鞆謂褒武多焉

An explanation of the origin of the name: by the custom of ancient times, the archer's wrist guard (鞆) was called "褒武多".

故名其舩曰枯野

Because the ship ran light and fast, it was named 枯野*.

天皇勅之，令請神祇探湯

Since it could not be decided who was in the right, the Emperor petitioned the deities of heaven and earth and had the ordeal by boiling water (探湯) performed.

感其形之美麗，常有戀情

大鷦鷯尊* was moved by the beauty of 髪長媛* and constantly longed for her.

In the Kojiki

Kojiki, Part 2, section on Emperor Ōjin (the land-viewing song at 宇遅野上*)

The land-viewing song looking out over 葛野* also appears in the Kojiki in almost the same words. In the Kojiki it is followed by the story of the Emperor meeting 矢河枝比売* at the village of 木幡* and the birth of 宇遅能和紀郎子*.

御立宇遲野上。望葛野。歌曰。
Kojiki, Part 2, section on Emperor Ōjin (髪長比売*)

In the Kojiki, 大雀命* asks Takeuchi-no-Sukune (建内宿禰) to make the request to the Emperor on his behalf, and on the day of the court banquet (豊明) the Emperor has 髪長比売* hold the oak-leaf cup of great sake (大御酒柏) and gives her to the prince. In the Nihon Shoki main text, the Emperor notices the feelings of 大鷦鷯尊* by himself and gives her to him at a banquet.

即誂告。建内宿禰大臣
Kojiki, Part 2, section on Emperor Ōjin (the achievements of this reign)

The establishment of the fishermen's groups (海部) and mountain wardens (山守部) and the making of the 剣池 pond* are recorded together in the Kojiki as events of "this reign". No corresponding entry in Part 2 of the Kojiki could be confirmed for the story of the ordeal by boiling water between Takeuchi-no-Sukune and 甘美内宿禰*.

定賜。海部。山部。山守部。伊勢部也。亦作劍池。
