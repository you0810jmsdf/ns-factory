Nihon Shoki, Book 25 — Emperor Kōtoku · page 43 of 50

Emperor Kōtoku — The Cap-Rank System, the Death of Ishikawa no Maro, the Hakuchi Era and the Naniwa Palace

孝徳天皇 ― 冠位の制・石川麻呂の死・白雉改元と難波の宮

Covers the period from the 3rd year of Taika to the 5th year of Hakuchi: the organization of cap ranks and of the official system, the death of the Minister of the Right Soga no Kurayamada no Ishikawa no Maro, the change of era name after a white pheasant was presented, the completion of the Nagara-Toyosaki Palace in Naniwa, and the dispatch of envoys to Tang. In the 4th year of Hakuchi the crown prince moved with the 皇祖母尊, Empress Hashihito and others to the riverside temporary palace at Asuka (飛鳥河辺行宮) in Yamato (倭), and in the 5th year the emperor died at the Naniwa Palace.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textIn the third month of the 5th year of Taika, 蘇我日向* falsely told the crown prince that his half-brother, the Minister of the Right Soga no Kurayamada no Ishikawa no Maro, harbored rebellious intentions, and the minister took his own life at Yamada-dera (山田寺) after swearing that he “would not resent his lord.” Later, among his property were found items labeled “the crown prince’s documents” and “the crown prince’s things,” and the crown prince, realizing that the minister’s heart had been pure, regretted what had happened.
  2. Main textIn the second month of the 1st year of Hakuchi, the governor of 穴戸* presented a white pheasant, and after the Lord of Paekche (百済君) and monks were asked about its origins it was judged an auspicious omen. The emperor issued an edict proclaiming a general amnesty throughout the realm and changing the era name to Hakuchi.
  3. Main textIn the 3rd year of Taika a system of caps in seven colors and thirteen grades was established; in the second month of the 5th year it was changed to nineteen grades of caps, from 大織 to 立身, and the scholars 高向玄理 and 僧旻 (readings unconfirmed) were made to establish the eight ministries and the hundred offices.
  4. Main textIn the 4th year of Hakuchi the crown prince asked to move to the Yamato capital (倭京), but the emperor did not allow it. The crown prince took the 皇祖母尊, Empress Hashihito, the emperor’s younger brother (皇弟) and others and moved to the riverside temporary palace at Asuka in Yamato, and the high officials and all the hundred officials followed. The emperor, resentful, wished to abandon the throne and sent a poem to Empress Hashihito.
  5. Main textIn the fifth month of the 4th year of Hakuchi, the ambassador 吉士長丹* and his party and the ambassador 高田首根麻呂* and his party were sent to Great Tang in two ships, and in the second month of the 5th year the supervising envoy (押使) 高向玄理* and others were also sent. 玄理 died in Tang, and 吉士長丹 and his party returned in the seventh month of the 5th year and were rewarded.
  6. Main textIn the tenth month of the 5th year of Hakuchi, the crown prince, hearing of the emperor’s illness, went to the Naniwa Palace with the 皇祖母尊, Empress Hashihito and others, and the emperor died in his main chamber (正寝). In the twelfth month he was buried at the 大坂磯長陵*, and the crown prince, escorting the 皇祖母尊, moved to the riverside temporary palace in Yamato (倭河辺行宮).

What the main text says

In the 3rd year of Taika the 小郡* was demolished and a palace built there, and rules of etiquette were set: holders of rank were to line up outside the south gate at the hour of the tiger (寅), bow twice in the courtyard at sunrise, and withdraw on hearing the bell at the hour of the horse (午). Someone remonstrated that the craftsman 倭漢直荒田井比羅夫* had mistakenly had ditches dug and had exhausted the people; the emperor said this was his own fault and stopped the work that same day. In the twelfth month the crown prince’s palace burned. In this year a system of caps in seven colors and thirteen grades was established; 金春秋* of Silla came escorting 高向黒麻呂* and others and became a hostage; and the 渟足 stockade (渟足柵)* was built and stockade households (柵戸) were placed there.

In the second month of the 4th year of Taika, student monks were sent to Koguryŏ, Paekche and Silla, and Great Minister Abe invited the fourfold Buddhist assembly (四衆) to Shitennō-ji (四天王寺) and enshrined four Buddhist images inside the pagoda. In the fourth month the old caps were abolished, but the Ministers of the Left and Right still wore the old caps. In this year the 磐舟 stockade (磐舟柵)* was put in order to guard against the Emishi, and people from Koshi and Shinano were selected and for the first time placed there as stockade households.

In the second month of the 5th year of Taika, nineteen grades of caps were established, and 高向玄理 and 僧旻 (readings unconfirmed) were made to establish the eight ministries and the hundred offices. In the third month the Minister of the Left, Great Minister Abe, died. 蘇我日向* falsely told the crown prince that the Minister of the Right Soga no Kurayamada no Ishikawa no Maro was plotting rebellion. When the emperor sent troops against him, the minister fled to Yamada-dera in Yamato, refused the plan of his eldest son 興志* to meet the attack, swore before the Buddha hall that he would not resent his lord, and took his own life; his wife, children and others died with him. Afterwards, items marked as being for the crown prince were found among his property and the crown prince regretted what had happened; 日向 was made Governor-General of Tsukushi (筑紫大宰帥), and people of the time asked whether this was a hidden banishment.

The minister’s daughter 造媛*, a consort of the crown prince, grieved that her father had been cut down by 物部二田造塩*, and died; when the crown prince lamented, 野中川原史満* presented two poems to him. In the fourth month 巨勢徳陀古臣* was made Minister of the Left and 大伴長徳連* Minister of the Right. In this year the king of Silla sent 金多遂* as a hostage.

In the second month of the 1st year of Hakuchi, 草壁連醜経*, governor of 穴戸*, presented a white pheasant. The Lord of Paekche and the monks cited ancient precedents from China and the example of a white deer in Koguryŏ, among others, and judged it an auspicious omen; at court, with the Ministers of the Left and Right and the hundred officials lined up, the palanquin bearing the pheasant was brought forward before the throne. The emperor delivered an edict saying that auspicious omens are the result of subjects serving with sincerity, proclaimed a general amnesty throughout the realm, and changed the era name to Hakuchi. In the tenth month, gifts were given to those whose tombs had been destroyed and to those who had been moved to make room for the palace site, and work began on sixteen-foot (丈六) embroidered images and other works.

In the third month of the 2nd year of Hakuchi the sixteen-foot embroidered images and other works were completed. On the last day of the twelfth month more than 2,100 monks and nuns were invited to the 味経宮* to read the complete Buddhist canon (一切経), and the emperor moved from the 大郡* to the new palace, which was named the Nagara-Toyosaki Palace in Naniwa (難波長柄豊碕宮). In this year Silla’s tribute envoys arrived in Tsukushi wearing Tang clothing and were sent back, and the Great Minister 巨勢* petitioned that Silla should be attacked. In the 3rd year the allotment of rice land was completed, household registers were made with fifty households forming one village, and in the ninth month construction of the palace was finished.

In the fifth month of the 4th year of Hakuchi, the ambassador 吉士長丹 and his party and the ambassador 高田首根麻呂 and his party (readings unconfirmed) were sent to Great Tang in two ships, and the emperor visited the sick monk 旻*. In this year the crown prince asked to move the capital to the Yamato capital but was refused; he took the 皇祖母尊, Empress Hashihito and others to the riverside temporary palace at Asuka, and the hundred officials followed, so the emperor, resentful, wished to abandon the throne and sent a poem to Empress Hashihito. In the first month of the 5th year Nakatomi no Kamatari (中臣鎌足連) was given the purple cap, and in the second month the supervising envoy 高向玄理* and others were sent to Tang, but 玄理 died there. In the tenth month the emperor died in his main chamber; in the twelfth month he was buried at the 大坂磯長陵*, and the crown prince, escorting the 皇祖母尊, moved to the riverside temporary palace in Yamato.

Variant accounts

After its main text, the Nihon Shoki often sets out other versions, introduced with words such as "a certain book says" (一書曰).

A certain text (或本)

A certain text records that in the seventh month of the 5th year of Hakuchi the monk 旻* lay ill at 阿曇寺*, and that the emperor visited him, took his hand and said, “If the master dies today, I too will die tomorrow.” The main text says that the emperor visited 旻’s quarters in the fifth month of the 4th year.

A certain text (或本)

Concerning the first ship of the embassy to Tang in the 4th year of Hakuchi, a certain text adds the student monks 知辨 and 義徳 and the student 坂合部連磐積 (readings unconfirmed).

A certain text says (或本云)

The main text says that in the second month the supervising envoy 高向史玄理*, of 大錦上 rank, was sent, but a certain text says that in summer, in the fifth month, the supervising envoy 高向玄理, of 大花下 rank, was sent, and it also gives the rank of the secretary (判官) 書直麻呂* as 小山下.

A certain text says (或本云)

A certain text records that in this emperor’s reign the three kingdoms of Koguryŏ, Paekche and Silla sent envoys every year to present tribute.

In the original text

妄聽比羅夫所詐，而空穿瀆。朕之過也。

The emperor said that it was his own fault for listening to the deception of 比羅夫* and having useless ditches dug, and he stopped the work that same day.

願我生生世世，不怨君王。

The vow Great Minister Ishikawa no Maro made before the Buddha hall at the end. It means: however many times I am reborn, I will not resent my lord.

所以大赦天下，改元白雉。

Words of the edict: because the auspicious omen of a white pheasant has appeared in the province of 穴戸*, a general amnesty is proclaimed throughout the realm and the era name is changed to Hakuchi.

是隱流乎。

When 蘇我日向*, who had made the false accusation, was appointed Governor-General of Tsukushi, people of the time said, “Is this a hidden banishment?”
