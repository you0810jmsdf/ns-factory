Nihon Shoki, Book 1 — Age of the Gods (part 1) · page 3 of 50

The Birth of the Three Noble Children, the Birth of the Deities, and Yomi (Section 5)

三貴子の誕生と神生み・黄泉（第五段）

In the main text, Izanagi-no-Mikoto and Izanami-no-Mikoto together give birth to the sea, rivers, mountains, and trees and grasses, and then to the Sun Deity, the Moon Deity, Hiruko, and Susanoo-no-Mikoto. The birth of the fire deity and the death of Izanami-no-Mikoto, the visit to the land of Yomi, the birth of the three deities through purification, and the story of Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto and Ukemochi-no-Kami are transmitted in the eleven variant accounts.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textIn the main text, after the two deities together gave birth to the sea, rivers, mountains, the ancestor of trees, and the ancestor of grasses, they sought to produce a ruler of all under heaven and gave birth to the Sun Deity (Ōhirume-no-Muchi, 大日孁貴), whom they sent up to heaven. They next sent the Moon Deity to heaven as well; Hiruko, who could not stand on its legs even at the age of three, was set adrift in a boat; and Susanoo-no-Mikoto was driven away to the Root Land (根国) for his lawlessness.
  2. Variant accountThe main text of section 5 does not record the birth of the fire deity or the death of Izanami-no-Mikoto. The story of Izanami-no-Mikoto dying after giving birth to the fire deity (Kagutsuchi, 軻遇突智; Homusubi, 火産霊) is transmitted in variant accounts 2, 3, 5, and 6, each in a slightly different form, and variant account 4 relates that Kanayamahiko and others were born from her suffering in giving birth to the fire deity.
  3. Variant accountVariant account 6 relates how Izanagi-no-Mikoto saw Izanami-no-Mikoto's appearance in Yomi and fled back, blocked 泉津平坂* with a rock and exchanged vows of separation, and purified himself at 檍原* in Hyūga. When he washed his left eye Amaterasu-Ōmikami was born, from his right eye Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto (月読尊), and from his nose Susanoo-no-Mikoto, and they were entrusted with ruling the High Plain of Heaven (高天原), the sea, and the realm under heaven, respectively.
  4. Variant accountIn variant account 11, Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto (月夜見尊) visits Ukemochi-no-Kami (保食神) on Amaterasu-Ōmikami's orders but, angered at being entertained with food brought out of the deity's mouth, kills it. Amaterasu-Ōmikami, angered, came to live separated from Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto by one day and one night, and the account holds that the millet, barnyard millet, rice, wheat, beans, and silkworms that arose from Ukemochi-no-Kami's body became the origin of the seeds for fields and of sericulture.
  5. Compared with the KojikiIn the Kojiki, the three noble children are born when Izanagi-no-Mikoto (伊耶那岐命) performs purification after returning from Yomi, and Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto (月読命) is entrusted with the realm of the night (夜之食國) and Susanoo-no-Mikoto with the sea plain. In the Nihon Shoki the traditions are divided: in the main text they are born of the two deities, in variant account 1 by means of white copper mirrors, and in variant account 6 through purification.
  6. CommentaryThe deity-name database of Kokugakuin University explains that the Kojiki's story of the fire deity is structured as though it had gathered and organized the various traditions of the Nihon Shoki. For example, it states that deities coming into being from what Izanami-no-Mikoto (伊耶那美神) vomited and excreted corresponds to variant account 4, 泣沢女神* coming into being from tears to variant account 6, the burial to variant account 5, and deities coming into being when the fire deity is cut down to variant accounts 6 to 8. [source, Japanese]

What the main text says

Izanagi-no-Mikoto and Izanami-no-Mikoto next gave birth to the sea, then to the rivers, then to the mountains. They further gave birth to 句句迺馳*, the ancestor of trees, and to 草野姫 * (also called 野槌), the ancestor of grasses. The two deities then consulted together, saying, “We have already given birth to the Land of the Great Eight Islands (大八洲国) and to the mountains, rivers, grasses, and trees. How can we not give birth to one who will be ruler of all under heaven?”

So the two deities together gave birth to the Sun Deity and named it Ōhirume-no-Muchi (大日孁貴) (according to a small note, one writing also calls it Amaterasu-Ōmikami (天照大神) or 天照大日孁尊*). This child shone with beautiful, radiant light and illuminated everything within heaven, earth, and the four directions. The two deities rejoiced and said, “Though we have many children, none is as wondrous as this one. It should not be kept long in this land; let us quickly send it to heaven and entrust it with the affairs of heaven.” It is said that because heaven and earth were not yet far apart at this time, they raised the child up to heaven by way of the pillar of heaven (天柱).

Next they gave birth to the Moon Deity (a small note says it is also called 月弓尊*, 月夜見尊, or 月読尊, that is, Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto). Its light was second only to the sun's, and since it was fit to rule alongside the sun, it too was sent to heaven. Next they gave birth to Hiruko, but since the child could not stand on its legs even at the age of three, they placed it in the heavenly rock-camphor boat (天磐櫲樟船) and cast it away to drift as the wind took it.

Next they gave birth to Susanoo-no-Mikoto (a small note says he is also called 神素戔嗚尊 or 速素戔嗚尊, readings unconfirmed). This deity was brave and fierce, did cruel things without hesitation, and was always crying and wailing. Because of this, many of the people of the land died young, and the green mountains withered. The two parent deities commanded, “You are utterly lawless, so you cannot reign over the universe. Go far away to the Root Land,” and drove Susanoo-no-Mikoto away.

A plain retelling of what the main text of the Nihon Shoki says (no published translation has been used).

Variant accounts

After its main text, the Nihon Shoki often sets out other versions, introduced with words such as "a certain book says" (一書曰).

Variant account 1

Izanagi-no-Mikoto, alone, wishing to produce a noble child to rule the world, took a white copper mirror in his left hand, whereupon Ōhirume-no-Mikoto (大日孁尊) appeared; when he took one in his right hand, 月弓尊* appeared; and while he turned his head to look back, Susanoo-no-Mikoto (素戔鳴尊) appeared. The two deities of sun and moon were given the role of illuminating heaven and earth, and Susanoo-no-Mikoto, cruel by nature, was given the role of ruling the Root Land.

Variant account 2

It explains that Hiruko, born after the sun and moon, could not stand even at the age of three because, when the deities went around the pillar, the female deity spoke words of joy first and so went against the principle of yin and yang; the child was placed in the 鳥磐櫲樟橡船 (a rock-camphor boat) and set adrift. Next Izanami-no-Mikoto gave birth to the fire deity Kagutsuchi, was burned and died, and as she was dying gave birth to the earth deity Haniyamahime (埴山姫) and the water deity 罔象女*. Kagutsuchi took Haniyamahime as wife and 稚産霊* was born, and the account holds that silkworms and mulberry arose on top of this deity's head and the five grains within its navel.

Variant account 3

When Izanami-no-Mikoto gave birth to Homusubi (火産霊), she was burned by the child and “withdrew as a deity” (神退; also written 神避). The account holds that as she withdrew she gave birth to the water deity 罔象女*, the earth deity Haniyamahime, and further to 天吉葛*.

Variant account 4

The account holds that when Izanami-no-Mikoto was giving birth to Kagutsuchi, suffering from the heat, what she vomited became Kanayamahiko (金山彦), her urine became 罔象女*, and her feces became Haniyamahime.

Variant account 5

Izanami-no-Mikoto gave birth to the fire deity, was burned and died, and was buried at the village of 有馬村* in Kumano, in the province of Kii. It is said that the people of that place worship this deity's spirit with flowers in the flowering season, and worship it with singing and dancing, using drums, flutes, and banners.

Variant account 6

Izanagi-no-Mikoto gave birth to many deities, among them the wind deity 級長戸辺命*, born from the breath with which he blew away the morning mist; after this Izanami-no-Mikoto died through the birth of the fire deity. From his tears Izanagi-no-Mikoto produced 啼沢女命*, and he cut Kagutsuchi into three pieces with his ten-span sword, and from the blood arose deities such as 甕速日神* and Takemikazuchi (武甕槌神). Visiting Yomi, Izanagi-no-Mikoto peered at the form he had been told not to look at and fled; pursued by the Yomotsu-shikome (泉津醜女), he blocked 泉津平坂* with a rock that would take a thousand people to pull, and when Izanami-no-Mikoto said she would kill a thousand people a day, he answered that he would cause fifteen hundred to be born. He purified himself at 筑紫日向小戸橘之檍原* and produced 八十枉津日神*, the Sumiyoshi Great Deities (住吉大神), and others; Amaterasu-Ōmikami was born from his left eye, Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto (月読尊) from his right eye, and Susanoo-no-Mikoto from his nose, and they were entrusted with the High Plain of Heaven, the sea plain, and the realm under heaven, respectively; but Susanoo-no-Mikoto wept, wishing to go to the Root Land where his mother was, and was driven out.

Variant account 7

The account holds that when Izanagi-no-Mikoto cut Kagutsuchi into three pieces, they became, respectively, a thunder deity, Ōyamatsumi-no-Kami (大山祇神), and Takaokami (高龗). The blood from the cutting also dyed the five hundred rocks of the 天八十河*, giving rise to deities such as 磐裂神 and 根裂神 (readings unconfirmed), and that lineage leads down to Futsunushi-no-Kami (経津主神). In the latter half, glosses showing the readings of words in variant account 6 are placed together.

Variant account 8

The account holds that when Izanagi-no-Mikoto cut Kagutsuchi into five pieces, the head, trunk, hands, waist, and feet became five mountain deities: Ōyamatsumi (大山祇), 中山祇, 麓山祇, 正勝山祇, and 䨄山祇 (readings unconfirmed), respectively. It is said that the blood that spattered at that time dyed the rocks, grasses, and trees, and that this is why grasses, trees, and rocks came to contain fire.

Variant account 9

Izanagi-no-Mikoto went to the place of temporary enshrinement (殯斂) to see his wife; he was told not to look, but he lit a light and looked, and Izanami-no-Mikoto's body was swollen, with eight kinds of thunder upon it. Fleeing, Izanagi-no-Mikoto hid beneath a large peach tree and drove off the thunders by throwing peaches, and the account holds that this is the origin of warding off demons with peaches. He threw down his staff, saying, “Beyond here the thunders cannot come,” and it became Funato-no-Kami (岐神; originally named 来名戸之祖神)*.

Variant account 10

Izanami-no-Mikoto, whose form had been seen when she had said not to look, said resentfully, “You have seen my heart, so I too have seen yours.” From the spittle that the departing Izanagi-no-Mikoto spat out as he swore was born Hayatama-no-O (速玉之男), and from what he swept away was born Yomotsu-Kotosaka-no-O (泉津事解之男). At 泉平坂*, 泉守道者* conveyed Izanami-no-Mikoto's words, and Kukuri-hime-no-Kami (菊理媛神) also said something, which Izanagi-no-Mikoto praised before leaving; he then purified himself at 橘之小門* and produced 磐土命, 大直日神, 底土命, 大綾津日神, 赤土命 (readings unconfirmed), and others.

Variant account 11

Izanagi-no-Mikoto appointed Amaterasu-Ōmikami (天照太神) to rule 高天之原 (the High Plain of Heaven), Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto (月夜見尊) to rule the affairs of heaven alongside the sun, and Susanoo-no-Mikoto to rule the sea plain. Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto killed Ukemochi-no-Kami, who had entertained him with food brought out of its mouth, and came to live separated by one day and one night from the angered Amaterasu-Ōmikami. Cattle and horses, millet, silkworms, barnyard millet, rice, wheat, and beans arose from Ukemochi-no-Kami's body; the account holds that Amaterasu-Ōmikami made these the food of people, designating them as seeds for dry fields and paddy fields, planted rice in 天狭田 and 長田 (readings unconfirmed), and, drawing thread from a cocoon held in her mouth, began the way of sericulture.

In the original text

何不生天下之主者歟

How can we not give birth to one who will be ruler of all under heaven? (the two deities' consultation).

此子光華明彩，照徹於六合之內

This child shone with beautiful, radiant light and illuminated everything within heaven, earth, and the four directions (the birth of the Sun Deity).

吾當縊殺汝所治國民日將千頭

I will strangle to death a thousand of the people of the land you rule each day (Izanami-no-Mikoto's words in variant account 6).

是物者則顯見蒼生可食而活之也

These are things that the people of this world should eat in order to live (Amaterasu-Ōmikami's words in variant account 11).

In the Kojiki

Kojiki, Part 1 (purification and the birth of the three noble children)

In the Kojiki, Izanagi-no-Mikoto returns from Yomi and purifies himself; when he washes his left eye Amaterasu-Ōmikami comes into being, from his right eye Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto, and from his nose Susanoo-no-Mikoto. In the main text of the Nihon Shoki, the two deities, as husband and wife, give birth to the Sun Deity, the Moon Deity, and Susanoo-no-Mikoto; birth through purification is the tradition of variant account 6.

於是洗左御目時，所成神名，天照大御神
Kojiki, Part 1 (the division of rule among the three noble children)

In the Kojiki, Amaterasu-Ōmikami is entrusted with the High Plain of Heaven, Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto with the realm of the night (夜之食國), and Susanoo-no-Mikoto with the sea plain. In the Nihon Shoki, variant account 6 entrusts Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto (月読尊) with the sea plain and Susanoo-no-Mikoto with the realm under heaven, while variant account 11 entrusts Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto (月夜見尊) with the affairs of heaven alongside the sun and Susanoo-no-Mikoto with the sea plain.

汝命者，所知夜之食國矣
Kojiki, Part 1 (the banishment of Susanoo-no-Mikoto and Ōgetsuhime-no-Kami)

In the Kojiki, it is Susanoo-no-Mikoto who kills Ōgetsuhime-no-Kami (大宜津比売神), who had entertained him with food brought out of her mouth and rear, and this story comes after the episode of the Heavenly Rock Cave (天石屋戸). In the Nihon Shoki, in variant account 11 Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto (月夜見尊) kills Ukemochi-no-Kami, and for that reason the sun and moon came to be separated by one day and one night.
