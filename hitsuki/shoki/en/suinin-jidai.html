Nihon Shoki, Book 6 — Emperor Suinin · page 16 of 50

倭姫命* and the Worship at Ise, the Origin of Haniwa, the Sacred Treasures of Ame-no-Hiboko, and 田道間守*

倭姫命と伊勢の祭祀、埴輪の起こり、天日槍の神宝、田道間守

This page covers the entries from the 25th year of Emperor Suinin onward, recording that 倭姫命* worshipped Amaterasu-Ōmikami at Ise, that the practice of following the dead in death was stopped and haniwa were set up instead, the development of ponds and channels, the sacred treasures of Isonokami Shrine (石上神宮), and the presentation of the sacred treasures of Ame-no-Hiboko (天日槍). The emperor died in the 99th year, and 田道間守*, who brought back the 非時香菓 from the Land of Tokoyo (常世国), wept and cried out at the emperor's tomb and died.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textIn the 3rd month of the 25th year, Amaterasu-Ōmikami was taken from 豊耜入姫命* and entrusted to 倭姫命*. 倭姫命 traveled through 菟田*, Ōmi and Mino and reached Ise; following the Great Deity's instruction, she built a shrine in Ise and established the abstinence palace (斎宮) by the Isuzu River.
  2. Main textIn the 28th year, when attendants were buried alive at the tomb of the emperor's younger brother by the same mother, 倭彦命*, they wept and cried out for several days and then died. The emperor grieved over this and ordered that the practice of following the dead in death be stopped.
  3. Main textWhen the empress 日葉酢媛命* died in the 32nd year, 野見宿禰* summoned one hundred clay workers (土部) from Izumo and proposed making figures of people, horses and other things out of clay and setting them up at the tomb in place of living people. These clay objects were called haniwa (埴輪), or "standing objects" (立物), and 野見宿禰 was granted the title 土部臣* and is regarded as the founding ancestor of the 土部連*.
  4. Main textIn the 90th year, the emperor sent 田道間守* to the Land of Tokoyo (常世国) to seek the 非時香菓 (what is now the tachibana, 橘). 田道間守 returned in the following year (the 1st year of Emperor Keikō), but the emperor had already died, and he wept and cried out toward the tomb and died.
  5. Main textIn the 39th year, 五十瓊敷命* made one thousand swords, deposited them at Isonokami Shrine (石上神宮), and took charge of the sacred treasures. In the 87th year, 五十瓊敷命, now old, wished to entrust them to his younger sister 大中姫命*, but 大中姫命 had 物部十千根大連* manage them; this is regarded as the origin of the Mononobe no Muraji managing the sacred treasures of Isonokami to this day.
  6. CommentaryIn the 88th year, 清彦*, the great-grandson of Ame-no-Hiboko (天日槍), presented the sacred treasures but tried to hide only the small sword called 出石*; even after it had been presented, the small sword returned of its own accord to 清彦's house, and it is said that it then moved to Awaji Island and was worshipped in a shrine. The database of deity names (entry: Izushi-Yamae-no-Ōkami, 伊豆志之八前大神) introduces a theory that draws attention to the point that, whereas in the Kojiki the treasures remain at 出石, in the Nihon Shoki they are presented and deposited in the divine storehouse (神府), and takes this to show that the sacred treasures of 出石 were absorbed into the rituals of the royal court. [source, Japanese]

What the main text says

In the 2nd month of the 25th year, the emperor told five high officials, distant ancestors of the Abe no Omi, the 和珥臣*, the Nakatomi no Muraji, the Mononobe no Muraji and the Ōtomo no Muraji, that because the previous emperor, Emperor Sujin, had reverently worshipped the deities, the realm had been at peace, and that in his own reign too the worship of the deities must not be neglected. In the 3rd month, Amaterasu-Ōmikami was taken from 豊耜入姫命* and entrusted to 倭姫命*. Seeking a place where the Great Deity would settle, 倭姫命 went to 筱幡 in 菟田 (readings unconfirmed), and passing through the province of Ōmi and the province of Mino, reached the province of Ise. The Great Deity instructed her that Ise was a beautiful land where the waves of Tokoyo (常世) roll in, and that she wished to stay there; so a shrine was set up in the province of Ise and the abstinence palace (斎宮) was established by the Isuzu River. In the 26th year, 物部十千根大連* went to Izumo to examine the sacred treasures and was entrusted with their management. In the 27th year, divination about making weapons into offerings to the deities proved auspicious, so bows and arrows and swords (横刀) were deposited at the various shrines, and divine lands and shrine households (神地, 神戸) were established. Worshipping the deities with weapons is regarded as having begun at this time. In this year a royal granary (屯倉) was established at the village of 来目邑*.

In the 28th year, the emperor's younger brother by the same mother, 倭彦命*, died, and his attendants were buried alive around his tomb. They did not die for several days but wept and cried out day and night, and the emperor, hearing their voices, grieved and had the ending of following the dead in death deliberated. In the 30th year, the emperor asked two princes what they wished for; the elder, 五十瓊敷命*, wished for a bow and arrows, and the younger, 大足彦尊*, wished for the throne, so he granted each his wish.

In the 32nd year, the empress 日葉酢媛命* died. The emperor knew that following the dead in death was not good, but was uncertain how to conduct the burial; 野見宿禰* offered advice, summoned one hundred clay workers (土部) from the province of Izumo, had them make figures of people, horses and other things from clay (埴土), and presented them. The emperor was pleased and set these up for the first time at the tomb of 日葉酢媛命, named them haniwa (埴輪) or "standing objects" (立物), and ordered that from then on these clay objects must always be set up at tombs. 野見宿禰 was given the title 土部臣* and the office of the clay workers, and this is regarded as the origin of the 土部連* and others presiding over the funerals of emperors.

In the 34th year, the emperor went to Yamashiro, took the beautiful 綺戸辺* into the women's quarters of the palace, and 磐衝別命* was born. It is said that when the emperor prayed for a sign, a large turtle came out of the river as he was arriving at the temporary palace, and when he stabbed it with a spear it turned into a white stone. In the 35th year, he had 五十瓊敷命* build the ponds 高石池 and 茅渟池 (readings unconfirmed) in the province of Kawachi, and further the ponds 狭城池 and 迹見池 (readings unconfirmed) in Yamato, and had more than eight hundred ponds and channels dug in the provinces to promote agriculture, so that the people became prosperous. In the 37th year, 大足彦尊* was made crown prince.

In the 39th year, 五十瓊敷命* made one thousand swords at the 菟砥川上宮* palace in 茅渟* and deposited them at Isonokami Shrine, and thereafter he took charge of the sacred treasures of Isonokami Shrine. In the 87th year, 五十瓊敷命, now old, wished to entrust the sacred treasures to his younger sister 大中姫命*, but 大中姫命 had 物部十千根大連* manage them. The text also records the story that when the dog of 甕襲* in the province of Tanba killed a wild beast, a curved jewel (八尺瓊勾玉) came out of the beast's belly, and that it is kept at Isonokami Shrine.

In the 88th year, the emperor said he wished to see the treasures that Ame-no-Hiboko (天日槍) had brought, and had his great-grandson 清彦* present them. 清彦 hid only the small sword called 出石* in his clothing, but it was discovered and he presented it, and the treasures were deposited in the divine storehouse (神府). However, the small sword disappeared and returned to 清彦's house, and it is said that it then moved to Awaji Island and was worshipped by the people of the island. Next the text records that Ame-no-Hiboko stayed in Tajima, married the daughter of 前津耳*, and fathered 但馬諸助*.

In the 90th year, the emperor sent 田道間守* to the Land of Tokoyo (常世国) to seek the 非時香菓 (what is now the tachibana, 橘). In the 7th month of the 99th year, the emperor died at the 纒向宮* palace at the age of one hundred and forty, and in the 12th month he was buried in the 菅原伏見陵* tomb. In the 3rd month of the following year, 田道間守 returned bringing the 非時香菓, eight on stalks and eight on strings (八竿八縵), but the emperor was already dead; lamenting the hardship of the ten years spent going and returning, he wept and cried out toward the tomb and died of his own accord. 田道間守 is regarded as the founding ancestor of the 三宅連*.

A plain retelling of what the main text of the Nihon Shoki says (no published translation has been used).

Variant accounts

After its main text, the Nihon Shoki often sets out other versions, introduced with words such as "a certain book says" (一書曰).

One account (一云): variant tradition in the entry for the 3rd month of the 25th year

It says that 倭姫命* worshipped Amaterasu-Ōmikami at the foot of the sacred oak (厳橿) of 磯城*, and afterwards, in the 10th month of the year (丁巳), moved her to the 渡遇宮* shrine in the province of Ise. It also records that the Great Deity of Yamato (倭大神) possessed 大水口宿禰*, a distant ancestor of the 穂積臣*, and declared that the previous emperor had been short-lived because he had not searched out the root of the worship of the deities; that 渟名城稚姫命* was chosen by divination but was too weak in body to perform the worship; and that 長尾市宿禰*, ancestor of the 倭直*, was made to perform the worship.

One account (一云): small note in the entry for the 7th month of the 32nd year

It gives the name of the empress 日葉酢媛命* as 日葉酢根命*.

One account (一云): variant tradition in the entry for the 10th month of the 39th year

It says that Prince 五十瓊敷* had a smith named 河上* make one thousand swords (大刀), and was granted ten occupational groups (品部), including the 楯部 and the 倭文部 (readings unconfirmed). It says that the swords were first deposited at the village of 忍坂邑* and later moved to Isonokami Shrine, and that at the deity's request 市河*, of the kin of the 春日臣*, was made to manage them, and it makes him the founding ancestor of the 物部首*.

One account (一云): small note in the entry for the 7th month of the 88th year

It gives 前津耳*, the father of the woman Ame-no-Hiboko (天日槍) married, as 前津見 or 太耳 (readings unconfirmed).

In the original text

是神風伊勢國。則常世之浪重浪歸國也。

The words of Amaterasu-Ōmikami to 倭姫命*: Ise is a land where the waves of Tokoyo (常世) roll in, wave upon wave.

夫以生所愛令殉亡者。是甚傷矣。

The emperor's words: to make those one loved in life follow the dead in death is a very painful thing.

仍號是土物謂埴輪。亦名立物也。

These objects made of clay were named haniwa (埴輪), and were also called "standing objects" (立物).

天皇命田道間守。遣常世國。令求非時香菓。

The emperor sent 田道間守* to the Land of Tokoyo (常世国) and had him seek the 非時香菓.

In the Kojiki

Kojiki, Part 2, section on Emperor Suinin (多遅摩毛理)*

In the Kojiki, 多遅摩毛理* presents half of the eight strings and eight spears (縵八縵, 矛八矛) he brought back to the empress, places the rest at the emperor's tomb, and weeps and cries out and dies. In the Nihon Shoki he brings back "八竿八縵" and dies before the tomb lamenting his ten years of hardship, and no presentation to the empress is recorded.

分縵四縵。矛四矛。獻于大后。
Kojiki, Part 2, section on Emperor Sujin (small note in the genealogy of his children) and the end of the section on Emperor Suinin

The Kojiki records, in a small note in the section on Emperor Sujin, that a "human hedge" (人垣) was first set up at a tomb at the time of 倭日子命*, and in the section on Emperor Suinin says that the 土師部* was established at the time of the empress 比婆須比売命*. A story like that of the Nihon Shoki — that the emperor, grieving over the following in death for 倭彦命*, stopped the practice, and that haniwa were set up on the proposal of 野見宿禰* — could not be confirmed.

始而於陵立人垣
Kojiki, Part 2, section on Emperor Ōjin (Ame-no-Hiboko, 天之日矛)

The Kojiki places the story of Ame-no-Hiboko (天之日矛) in the section on Emperor Ōjin as an event of "long ago" (昔); the treasures are eight kinds — two strings of jewels, four kinds of scarves (比礼) and two kinds of mirrors — and are regarded as Izushi-Yamae-no-Ōkami (伊豆志之八前大神). In the genealogy too, 清日子* is made a brother of 多遅摩毛理*, which differs from the Nihon Shoki's one account (一云), which makes 清彦* the father of 田道間守*.

珠二貫。又振浪比禮。
