Nihon Shoki, Book 28 — Emperor Tenmu (part 1) · page 46 of 50

Prince Ōama’s Retreat to Yoshino and the Jinshin War

大海人皇子の吉野入りと壬申の乱

This book gathers into one account the year in which Prince Ōama, younger brother of Emperor Tenji (天命開別天皇) by the same mother, declined at his brother’s sickbed to take charge of affairs after his death, took holy orders and entered Yoshino, and then, in the sixth month of the 1st year, went out to the eastern provinces, secured Fuwa (不破), and fought the army of the Ōmi court. It describes events up to Prince Ōtomo hanging himself after his defeat at Seta in the seventh month, and the emperor, back in the Yamato capital (倭京) in the ninth month, moving to the Asuka Kiyomihara Palace.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textThe Crown Prince (Prince Ōama), whom Emperor Tenji (天命開別天皇) on his sickbed asked to take charge of affairs after his death, declined, asking that the realm be entrusted to the empress and that Prince Ōtomo be made heir; that same day he took holy orders, handed over his private weapons to the government, and entered Yoshino.
  2. Main textIn the fifth month of the 1st year, the emperor heard that the Ōmi court, claiming to be building an imperial tomb, was having laborers in Mino and Owari carry weapons; in the sixth month he sent 村國連男依* and others to Mino to block the Fuwa road (不破道), and he himself left Yoshino and entered the eastern provinces.
  3. Main textBeside the 跡太川 river* in 朝明 district (朝明郡)*, the emperor bowed toward Amaterasu-Ōmikami (天照大神) from afar; he sent Prince Takechi (高市皇子) to Fuwa to oversee military affairs, and at the temporary palace at 野上* he handed over all military affairs to Prince Takechi.
  4. Main textIn the seventh month, the army of 村國連男依* and others advanced by way of 息長横河, 鳥籠山 and 安河 (readings unconfirmed) and broke the Ōmi lines at Seta; Prince Ōtomo hid at 山前* and hanged himself, and his head was presented before the camp at the Fuwa palace.
  5. Main textIn Yamato, 大伴連吹負* seized the camp west of Asuka-dera and became a general; he was once defeated at Nara Hill (乃楽山), but after the battles at Taima (当麻) and at the 箸陵 tomb* he drove back the Ōmi army. During this time Kotoshironushi-no-Kami (事代主神) and other deities possessed the chief of Takechi district (高市郡大領) and announced that they would protect the government army, and after the war these deities were raised in rank (品).
  6. CommentaryKokugakuin University’s database of kami names introduces the view that the status of Kotoshironushi-no-Kami was established in the reign of Emperor Tenmu, or took shape around that reign, on grounds that include the resemblance between the words spoken by Kotoshironushi-no-Kami in this possession in the seventh month of the 1st year of the Tenmu chronicle and the words of Ōkuninushi-no-Kami (大国主神) in the transfer of the land in the Kojiki. [source, Japanese]

What the main text says

Emperor Tenmu (天渟中原瀛真人天皇) was the younger brother of Emperor Tenji (天命開別天皇) by the same mother; his childhood name was Prince Ōama (大海人皇子). As he grew up he was brave, and he was versed in astronomy and in 遁甲. He took Princess Uno (菟野皇女), daughter of Emperor Tenji, as his principal consort, and became Crown Prince in the 1st year of Emperor Tenji. In winter, in the tenth month of the 4th year (the tenth month of the 10th year in the chronicle of Emperor Tenji), when he was summoned by the gravely ill emperor, the messenger 蘇賀臣安麻侶* secretly told him to be careful what he said, so the Crown Prince suspected a hidden plot. When the emperor tried to hand the great task over to him, the Crown Prince declined on the grounds of his frequent illness, asked that the realm be entrusted to the empress and that Prince Ōtomo be made heir, and that same day took holy orders and handed his private weapons over to the government. When he went into the Yoshino Palace, the Ministers of the Left and Right and others saw him off as far as Uji (菟道), and someone said, “They have put wings on a tiger and let it loose.” At Yoshino the Crown Prince told his attendants (舎人) that those who wished to serve and make a name for themselves should go back, and half of them stayed.

In the twelfth month Emperor Tenji died. In the third month of the 1st year, the emperor’s death was announced to 郭務悰* and the other Tang envoys in Tsukushi; they put on mourning clothes, raised their voices in lament three times, and bowed toward the east. In the fifth month 朴井連雄君* reported that in Mino and Owari laborers had been assigned on the pretext of building an imperial tomb and that each had been made to carry weapons; there were also reports that lookouts had been posted at various places from the Ōmi capital to the Yamato capital, and that the bridge-keepers at Uji were stopping the transport of food for the attendants of the Imperial Younger Brother (皇大弟). The emperor had it looked into and learned that it was true, and said that he had given up the throne to keep himself safe, but that he could not silently let himself be destroyed. In the sixth month the emperor sent 村國連男依, 和珥部臣君手 and 身毛君廣 (readings unconfirmed) hurrying to Mino and ordered 多臣品治*, steward of the bath-estate (湯沐令) in 安八磨 district*, to raise troops and block the Fuwa road.

On the day 甲申, the emperor asked 高坂王*, one of the caretaker officials, for the post-station bells (駅鈴) but could not get them; he sent 大分君惠尺* to Ōmi to summon Prince Takechi and Prince Ōtsu (大津皇子), and he himself set out for the eastern provinces without even waiting for a carriage. The empress followed in a palanquin, and Prince Kusakabe (草壁皇子), Prince Osakabe (忍壁皇子), some twenty attendants and some ten ladies-in-waiting (女孺) accompanied them from the start. The party passed through 菟田 and 甘羅村 (readings unconfirmed) and at midnight burned the post station at 隱*; before reaching 横河* they saw a black cloud spreading across the sky, and the emperor himself took up a divination board (式) and divined, saying that it was a sign that the realm would be split in two, but that he would win the realm. At 積殖山口* in Iga, Prince Takechi joined them, and at Suzuka the governors of Ise and others came to him and blocked the Suzuka mountain road with five hundred soldiers. Through thunder and rain they reached the Mie district office (三重郡家), where they burned one house to warm those who were freezing.

On the morning of the day 丙戌, beside the 跡太川 river* in 朝明 district*, the emperor bowed toward Amaterasu-Ōmikami from afar. Prince Ōtsu arrived, and word came from 村國連男依* that the Fuwa road had been blocked with three thousand soldiers from Mino. The emperor sent Prince Takechi to Fuwa to oversee military affairs and had him raise the armies of the Tōkai and Tōsan regions (東海, 東山). At the Ōmi court the ministers were alarmed, and Prince Ōtomo sent messengers to the eastern provinces, the Yamato capital, Tsukushi and Kibi to raise troops, but 當摩公廣嶋*, governor of Kibi, was killed, and 栗隈王*, the governor (大宰) of Tsukushi, did not move his troops, giving as his reason the need to guard against foreign enemies. On the day 丁亥 the emperor entered Fuwa, and the governor of Owari came over to his side, bringing twenty thousand soldiers. At 野上* Prince Takechi told the emperor that, relying on the emperor’s spirit (霊), he would lead the commanders and attack; the emperor took his hand, stroked his back, and handed over all military affairs to him.

In the seventh month the emperor sent 紀臣阿閉麻呂* and others with tens of thousands of men from Ise toward Yamato, and 村國連男依* and others with tens of thousands from Fuwa toward Ōmi, and had them put red markings on their clothing so they could tell friend from foe. On the Ōmi side, beside the 犬上川 river*, 山部王* was killed by his own side and the army fell into disorder; 蘇賀臣果安* stabbed himself in the neck and died, and 羽田公矢國* surrendered with his kin. 男依 and the others won at 息長横河, at 鳥籠山 and on the shore of 安河 (readings unconfirmed), and on the day 辛亥 they reached Seta. West of the bridge lay the great camp of Prince Ōtomo and his ministers; the commander 智尊* had cut the middle of the bridge and laid a plank across it, but 大分君稚臣*, wearing two layers of armor, crossed the plank, cut the rope, and charged into the camp, and the Ōmi army collapsed. Prince Ōtomo fled, then hid at 山前* and hanged himself, and on the day 乙卯 his head was presented before the camp at the Fuwa palace.

In Yamato, 大伴連吹負* conspired with the caretaker official 坂上直熊毛* and others; pretending that Prince Takechi had arrived, he scattered the troops in the camp west of Asuka-dera and cut down 穗積臣百足*. 吹負 was appointed general, but he was defeated at Nara Hill (乃樂山) by the Ōmi commander 大野君果安*, and at 倉歷*, 田中臣足麻呂*, attacked at night by 田邊小隅*, barely escaped by means of the password “金” (metal). After being pressed by the army of 壹伎史韓國*, which came from Kawachi, 吹負 met the army of 置始連菟* at 墨坂* and regained his strength; he defeated 韓國’s army beside the 葦池 pond* at Taima, and ordered that, since the aim was to strike down the chief culprits, the common people were not to be killed indiscriminately. On the Upper Road (上道), 三輪君高市麻呂 and 置始連菟 (readings unconfirmed) inflicted a great defeat on the Ōmi army at the 箸陵 tomb*, and after that the Ōmi army no longer came.

Before this, 高市縣主許梅*, chief of Takechi district, suddenly became unable to speak; three days later he was possessed by deities who named themselves Kotoshironushi-no-Kami of the Takechi shrine (高市社) and the 生靈神 of the 身狹社 shrine (readings unconfirmed), and they told him to offer horses and weapons at the tomb of Emperor Jinmu (神日本磐余彥天皇) and said they would protect the government army. The god of 村屋* also announced that an army would come by the Middle Road (中道), and since everything happened as foretold, the ranks of the three deities were raised after the war. In the eighth month Prince Takechi pronounced the crimes of the Ōmi ministers: eight who had committed grave crimes were given the ultimate penalty, the Minister of the Right 中臣連金* was beheaded at 淺井田根*, the Minister of the Left Soga no Akae (蘇我臣赤兄) and others were banished, and the rest were pardoned. In the ninth month the emperor passed through Kuwana, Suzuka, 阿閉* and Nabari, entered the 嶋宮 palace* in the Yamato capital, and moved to the Okamoto Palace. In this year a palace was built south of the Okamoto Palace, and in winter he moved into it; this is called the Asuka Kiyomihara Palace (飛鳥淨御原宮).

A plain retelling of what the main text of the Nihon Shoki says (no published translation has been used).

In the original text

臣今日出家，為陛下欲修功德。

The words of the Crown Prince when he declined to take charge of affairs. It means: “Today I will take holy orders, and I wish to perform meritorious deeds for Your Majesty.”

虎著翼放之。

Words someone spoke on seeing off the Crown Prince on his way to Yoshino. It means: “They have put wings on a tiger and let it loose.”

於朝明郡跡太川邊，望拜天照大神。

The record that on the way to the eastern provinces, beside the 跡太川 river* in 朝明 district*, he bowed toward Amaterasu-Ōmikami from afar.

其發兵之元意，非殺百姓，是為元兇。

An order issued to the army by 大伴連吹負*. It means that the troops were raised not to kill the common people but to strike down the chief culprits.

  • Prince Ōtsu (大津皇子)Summoned from Ōmi by 大分君惠尺*, he passed through the Suzuka barrier and joined the emperor in 朝明 district*.
