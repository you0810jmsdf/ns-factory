Nihon Shoki, Book 23 — Emperor Jomei · page 40 of 50

Emperor Jomei — The Ministers’ Debate over the Succession, and the Great Palace and Great Temple of Kudara

舒明天皇 ― 皇位をめぐる群臣の議と百済大宮・大寺

Empress Suiko died without naming a successor, and Soga no Emishi and the ministers debated whether to enthrone Prince Tamura or Prince Yamashiro no Ōe; Prince Tamura took the throne. His reign records the coming and going of envoys with Tang, Paekche and Koguryŏ, a campaign against the Emishi, and the building of a great palace and a great temple beside the Kudara River (百済川), and he died at the Kudara Palace (百済宮) in his 13th year.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textIn the 36th year of Empress Suiko the empress died, but no successor had been decided. Soga no Emishi gathered the ministers at his house, presented her last words, and asked whether Prince Tamura or Prince Yamashiro no Ōe should be enthroned. The ministers were divided between those who backed Prince Tamura and those who backed Prince Yamashiro no Ōe.
  2. Main textPrince Yamashiro no Ōe gave a detailed account of the words he had heard directly from the empress on her sickbed and protested that they differed from the last words reported by the ministers. 境部摩理勢*, who backed Prince Yamashiro no Ōe, refused to follow Emishi; troops were sent against him, and he was killed together with his son.
  3. Main textIn the first month of the 1st year, the Great Minister and the high officials presented the imperial seal to Prince Tamura; the prince declined once and then took the throne that same day. The text records that in the first month of the 2nd year he made Princess Takara (宝皇女) his empress, and that she bore Prince Katsuragi (葛城皇子; the emperor who reigned at the Ōmi Ōtsu Palace), Princess Hashihito (間人皇女) and Prince Ōama (大海皇子; the emperor who reigned at the Kiyomihara Palace).
  4. Main textIn the eighth month of the 2nd year, 犬上君三田耜 and 薬師恵日 (readings unconfirmed) were sent to Great Tang. In the eighth month of the 4th year the Tang envoy 高表仁* came escorting 三田耜, and in the tenth month he was welcomed at Naniwa Harbor (難波津) with thirty-two ships. 高表仁 and his party went home in the first month of the 5th year.
  5. Main textIn the seventh month of the 11th year the emperor ordered a great palace and a great temple to be built beside the Kudara River (百済川); the people of the west were made to build the palace and the people of the east the temple, and in the twelfth month a nine-storied pagoda was erected beside the Kudara River. The emperor moved to the Kudara Palace (百済宮) in the tenth month of the 12th year and died there in the tenth month of the 13th year.
  6. Main textThe text records that the place of temporary enshrinement (mogari, 殯) was north of the palace and was called the “Great Mogari of Kudara” (百済大殯), and that on this occasion the crown prince, Prince 開別 (開別皇子)*, then sixteen years old, delivered the eulogy (shinobigoto, 誄).

What the main text says

Emperor Jomei (息長足日廣額天皇) was a grandson of Emperor Bidatsu (渟中倉太珠敷天皇) and a son of Prince 彦人大兄 (彦人大兄皇子)*; his mother was Princess 糠手姫 (糠手姫皇女)*. After the crown prince 豊聡耳尊* died in the 29th year of Empress Suiko (豊御食炊屋姫天皇), no crown prince had been appointed; the empress died in the third month of her 36th year, and even after the funeral rites were completed in the ninth month the succession had not been settled.

The Great Minister Soga no Emishi feared that if he decided alone the ministers would not follow him, so after consulting 阿倍麻呂臣* he invited the ministers to his house. 阿倍臣 presented the late sovereign’s last words, according to which the sovereign on the sickbed had spoken to Prince Tamura and to Prince Yamashiro no Ōe in turn, and asked whom they should make sovereign. 大伴鯨連* and others backed Prince Tamura, 許勢臣大摩呂* and others backed Prince Yamashiro no Ōe, and 蘇我倉摩呂臣* withheld his answer. Because the ministers could not agree, Emishi withdrew without being able to settle the matter.

Prince Yamashiro no Ōe, who was at the Ikaruga Palace (斑鳩宮), heard of this debate and sent messengers to ask the views of Emishi, his uncle. Emishi had them answer that he had only told the ministers of the late sovereign’s testament and that it was not his own private opinion. Prince Yamashiro no Ōe stated that he had been called to the sick sovereign’s bedside and had received words on great matters of state directly, and that the sovereign had told him that Emishi was concerned about him; he repeatedly asked to learn the true content of the testament.

境部摩理勢* backed Prince Yamashiro no Ōe and would not give way. He tore down the huts at the site where the tomb of the 嶋大臣* was being built, withdrew to the Soga estate house (田家), and then moved to the palace of Prince 泊瀬 (泊瀬王)* at Ikaruga. Prince Yamashiro no Ōe admonished 摩理勢 to go along with the ministers. When Prince 泊瀬 died of illness, 摩理勢 lost his refuge, and he was strangled together with his son 阿椰* by troops that Emishi sent against him. 毛津* also hid in a nunnery, then fled into Mount Unebi (畝傍山) and took his own life in the mountains.

In the first month of the 1st year, the Great Minister and the high officials presented the imperial seal to Prince Tamura; the prince declined, but because the ministers pressed him strongly he took the throne that same day. In the first month of the 2nd year he made Princess Takara his empress, and 法提郎媛*, daughter of the Soga 嶋大臣*, bore Prince Furuhito (古人皇子). In this year envoys came from Koguryŏ and Paekche; in the eighth month 犬上君三田耜 and 薬師恵日 (readings unconfirmed) were sent to Great Tang, and in the tenth month the emperor moved to the Okamoto Palace (岡本宮) beside the hill of Asuka.

In the 3rd year people of 掖玖* came and submitted, and King 義慈* of Paekche sent Prince 豊章* as a hostage. In the 4th year the Tang envoy 高表仁* came escorting 三田耜, and the student monks 霊雲 and 僧旻 (readings unconfirmed) and others returned with him. In the 8th year all those who had violated palace women (采女) were punished, and in the sixth month the Okamoto Palace burned and the emperor moved to the 田中宮*. In the 9th year, in the campaign against the rebellious Emishi, the general 上毛野君形名* was surrounded, but his wife gave him wine to drink and encouraged him, and had the women twang their bowstrings, so the soldiers rallied and defeated the Emishi.

In the seventh month of the 11th year the emperor ordered a great palace and a great temple to be built beside the Kudara River; in the twelfth month a nine-storied pagoda was erected, and he went to the hot-spring palace (温湯宮) in Iyo. In the 12th year he had 恵隠* expound the Sutra of Immeasurable Life (無量寿経), welcomed 清安 and 高向漢人玄理 (readings unconfirmed), who had returned from Tang, and in the tenth month moved to the Kudara Palace. In the tenth month of the 13th year the emperor died at the Kudara Palace; the temporary enshrinement was held north of the palace, and the crown prince, Prince 開別*, then sixteen years old, delivered the eulogy.

A plain retelling of what the main text of the Nihon Shoki says (no published translation has been used).

In the original text

宗廟，重事矣。寡人不賢，何敢當乎？

The words with which Prince Tamura, presented with the imperial seal, declined once, saying that he did not have the ability to bear the great affairs of the state.

諸惡莫作，諸善奉行。

Words that Prince Yamashiro no Ōe cites as an admonition the former sovereign left to his children before dying. It means: do nothing evil, and practice what is good.

非流星，是天狗也。其吠聲似雷耳。

In the 9th year, when a large star streaked across the sky with a sound, 僧旻* said, “It is not a shooting star but a heavenly dog (天狗); its barking voice is like thunder.”

是以西民造宮，東民作寺。

The record that, in building the great palace and the great temple beside the Kudara River, the people of the west were made to build the palace and the people of the east the temple.

Deities and people, and the shrines that enshrine them

  • Emperor Tenji天智天皇Enshrined at: Ōmi Jingū
  • Emperor Jomei (息長足日廣額天皇; Prince Tamura, 田村皇子)Son of Prince 彦人大兄*; the emperor who took the throne after the ministers’ debate.
  • Soga no Emishi (蘇我蝦夷)As Great Minister he gathered the ministers and steered the debate toward making Prince Tamura the successor.
  • Prince Yamashiro no Ōe (山背大兄王)A prince living at the Ikaruga Palace who set out the last words he himself had heard and pressed Emishi for an answer.
  • 境部摩理勢*Backed Prince Yamashiro no Ōe and defied Emishi; he was killed together with his son.
  • Princess Takara (寶皇女)Became empress in the 2nd year and bore Prince Katsuragi, Princess Hashihito and Prince Ōama.
  • 犬上君三田耜*Sent to Great Tang in the 2nd year; he returned in the 4th year, escorted by the Tang envoy 高表仁*.
  • 高表仁*An envoy of Great Tang who escorted 三田耜 to Naniwa Harbor and went home in the 5th year.
  • 上毛野君形名*General in the campaign against the Emishi in the 9th year; with his wife’s encouragement he turned a losing situation around.
  • 僧旻*A student monk who returned from Tang and gave his opinions on shooting stars and comets.
  • 開別皇子 * (also Prince Katsuragi, 葛城皇子)Son of the emperor and Princess Takara; at sixteen he delivered the eulogy at his father’s temporary enshrinement.
