Nihon Shoki, Book 8 — Emperor Chūai · page 20 of 50

Emperor Chūai: the White Birds, the Campaign against the Kumaso, the Oracle, and His Death

仲哀天皇（白鳥・熊襲征討と神託・崩御）

This book records the period from the accession of 足仲彦天皇 * (Emperor Chūai) to his death after he entered Tsukushi to strike down the Kumaso without believing the words of the deity that had possessed the empress. It ends by saying that the emperor's death was concealed by the empress and Takeuchi-no-Sukune, and that he was not buried in that year because of the expedition to Silla.

Read in the Kojiki: The Death of Emperor Chūai and the Silla Expedition of 息長帯日売命* ›

Key points to remember

  1. Main textIn the 9th month of the 8th year, when the campaign against the Kumaso was being deliberated at the 橿日宮* palace in Tsukushi, a deity possessed the empress and instructed: "The Kumaso are not a land worth attacking. If you worship me, I will make the land of Silla across the sea, rich in gold and silver, submit without fighting." The emperor did not believe these words, saying that even when he climbed a high hill he could see only the sea.
  2. Main textStill not believing, the emperor attacked the Kumaso but could not win, and in the 2nd month of the 9th year he suddenly felt pain in his body and died the next day (aged fifty-two). The main text states that "he died early because he did not heed the words of the deity," while one account (一云) in a small note says that he went himself to strike down the Kumaso, was hit by a bandit's arrow, and died.
  3. Main textIn the 11th month of the 1st year, the emperor, longing for his father Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto, who had become a white bird and ascended to heaven, had the provinces present white birds. His half-brother 蘆髮蒲見別王*, who seized the white birds from Koshi on their way, was put to death for having committed a discourtesy against the late prince.
  4. Main textWhen the emperor entered Tsukushi in the 1st month of the 8th year, 熊鰐*, ancestor of the 岡県主*, and 五十迹手*, ancestor of the 伊覩県主*, came out to meet him with a white copper mirror, a ten-fist sword (十握剣) and a jewel (八尺瓊) hung on sakaki trees (賢木) set up on their boats. 五十迹手 praised the emperor's reign by likening it to these three objects, and it is said that the name 伊蘇国 (the present 伊覩; readings unconfirmed) arose from the word "伊蘇志" with which the emperor praised him.
  5. Compared with the KojikiIn the Kojiki, the emperor, thinking the deity a false deity, pushes the koto (琴) away; urged by 建内宿禰 (Takeuchi-no-Sukune), he plays it, but the sound of the koto stops, and when a light is lit and they look, he has already died. In both works his age at death is fifty-two.
  6. CommentaryConcerning the temporary palace "笥飯宮" that the emperor built at 角鹿 in the 2nd year, Kokugakuin University's database of deity names explains it by noting the view that Kehi is a place name of 角鹿 (Tsuruga, 敦賀), and by connecting it with Kehi-no-Ōkami (気比大神; written 笥飯大神 in the Nihon Shoki), who dwells in that place. [source, Japanese]

What the main text says

足仲彦天皇* was the second son of Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto, and his mother was 両道入姫命*, daughter of 活目入彦五十狹茅天皇*. He was handsome in appearance, and is said to have been ten shaku (十尺) tall. Because 稚足彦天皇* had no son, he was made heir apparent in that emperor's 48th year. When 稚足彦天皇 died in his 60th year, he was buried in the 9th month of the following year in the 狭城盾列陵* tomb in the land of Yamato. The heir apparent acceded to the throne in the 1st month of the 1st year, and in the 9th month made his mother empress dowager.

In the 11th month of the 1st year, the emperor told his ministers that his longing for the spirit of his father the prince, which had become a white bird and ascended to heaven, would not cease, and that he wished to keep white birds in the pond at the tomb to console his heart; and he had the provinces present white birds. In the intercalary 11th month, the province of Koshi presented four white birds, but when the messengers stopped for the night beside the Uji River (菟道河), 蘆髮蒲見別王* said, "Even a white bird, if you burn it, becomes a black bird," seized the white birds and went off with them. The emperor, hating this discourtesy toward the late prince, sent troops and put to death 蒲見別王, his half-brother.

In the 1st month of the 2nd year, he made 気長足姫尊* empress. Before this, he had taken as consort 大中姫*, daughter of his uncle 彦人大兄*, and had by her Prince ■坂 (one character is missing; in Book 9 the name is 麛坂王)* and Prince Oshikuma (忍熊皇子); and he had taken as consort 弟媛*, daughter of 大酒主*, and had by her Prince 誉屋別*. In the 2nd month he went to 角鹿 (Tsuruga) and built a temporary palace, which was called the Kehi Palace (笥飯宮), and in the same month he established the royal granary (屯倉) of Awaji. In the 3rd month, leaving the empress and the officials behind, he toured the southern provinces, and while he was at the 徳勒津宮* palace in the province of Kii, the Kumaso rebelled and ceased to bring tribute, so he headed by sea for 穴門*, and ordered the empress to set out from 角鹿 and meet him at 穴門.

In the 6th month, the emperor anchored at 豊浦津*. The empress set out from 角鹿 (Tsuruga) and reached 渟田門*; when she took a meal on the boat and poured sake on the fish that gathered beside the boat (the original text has "海鰤魚"), the fish became drunk and floated up, and the fishermen caught many and rejoiced. It is said that this is why the fish of that place float as if drunk when the 6th month comes. In the 7th month the empress also anchored at 豊浦津, and on that day she obtained a wish-granting jewel (如意珠) in the sea. In the 9th month a palace was built at 穴門* and they lived there; it is called the 穴門豊浦宮*.

In the 1st month of the 8th year, the emperor set out for Tsukushi. 熊鰐*, ancestor of the 岡県主*, hung a mirror, a sword and a jewel on a sakaki tree (賢木) and came out to meet him at the bay of 沙麼 in 周芳 (readings unconfirmed), presented lands for taking fish and salt, and guided him along the sea route. When the boat would not move at the harbor mouth of 岡浦*, 熊鰐 answered that it was probably the will of the male deity 大倉主 and the female deity 菟夫羅媛 (readings unconfirmed) at the mouth of the bay; when the emperor prayed and had 伊賀彦*, a man of 菟田*, worship them as priest (祝), the boat moved. The empress's boat, which had entered from 洞海* in a separate boat, could not move because the tide had ebbed; 熊鰐 made a pool for fish and a pond for birds and gathered fish and birds there, and on seeing them the empress's anger subsided somewhat. 五十迹手*, ancestor of the 伊覩県主*, likewise hung a jewel, a mirror and a sword on a sakaki tree and met him at 引嶋* in 穴門, and the emperor praised him. After that the emperor arrived at 儺県* and lived at the 橿日宮* palace.

In the 9th month, when the campaign against the Kumaso was being deliberated, a deity possessed the empress and instructed that, rather than the Kumaso, he should obtain, by worshipping the deity, the land of Silla across the sea, rich in gold, silver and bright colors; and it asked that the emperor's boat and the paddy field (大田) presented by 穴門直踐立* be made offerings. The emperor retorted that even looking out from a high hill he could see no land, and asked what deity was deceiving him. The deity told him again that if he did not believe, he would not obtain that land, and that the child the empress had now conceived for the first time would obtain it; but the emperor still did not believe, attacked the Kumaso, could not win, and returned.

In the 2nd month of the 9th year, the emperor suddenly felt pain in his body and died the next day. He was fifty-two years old. The empress and the Great Minister Takeuchi-no-Sukune, fearing that if the people of the realm learned of it they would grow slack, concealed the death, and had four high officials — 中臣烏賊津連, 大三輪大友主君, 物部膽咋連 and 大伴武以連 (readings unconfirmed) — lead the officials and guard the palace. The emperor's body was secretly entrusted to Takeuchi-no-Sukune and moved by sea to 穴門*, and a temporary enshrinement without fires (无火殯斂) was held at the 豊浦宮* palace. On the day (甲子), Takeuchi-no-Sukune returned from 穴門 and reported to the empress. In this year the emperor could not be buried because of the expedition to Silla.

A plain retelling of what the main text of the Nihon Shoki says (no published translation has been used).

Variant accounts

After its main text, the Nihon Shoki often sets out other versions, introduced with words such as "a certain book says" (一書曰).

One account (一云): the manner of the emperor's death

The main text says that he died early of a sudden illness because he did not heed the words of the deity, but one account (一云) in a small note relates that the emperor went himself to strike down the Kumaso, was hit by a bandit's arrow, and died.

In the original text

雖白鳥而燒之則爲黒鳥

The words with which 蒲見別王* seized the white birds being presented: even a white bird, if burned, becomes a black bird.

是謂栲衾新羅國焉

The words with which the deity named the land of Silla as the "land of treasure" it was teaching about.

即知。不用神言而早崩。

The explanation in the main text: it was understood that he had died early because he did not heed the words of the deity.

天皇即美五十迹手曰伊蘇志

The emperor praised 五十迹手*, saying "伊蘇志", and this is regarded as the origin of the place name 伊蘇国*.

In the Kojiki

Kojiki, Part 2, section on Emperor Chūai (the divine possession and the emperor's death)

In the Kojiki, the emperor, thinking the deity's words false, pushes the koto away; urged by 建内宿禰 (Takeuchi-no-Sukune), he reluctantly plays it, but the sound stops, and when a light is lit he has already died. The main text of the Nihon Shoki has a sudden death on the following day, and one account (一云) a death from an arrow wound.

不聞御琴之音。即擧火見者。既崩訖。
Kojiki, Part 2, section on Emperor Chūai (palaces, consorts and children)

The establishment of the royal granary of Awaji also appears in the Kojiki, as something of "this reign"; the Nihon Shoki places it in the 2nd month of the 2nd year. For the story of the white birds and 蒲見別王*, and for the stories of the welcome by 熊鰐 and 五十迹手 (readings unconfirmed), no corresponding entries in the Kojiki could be confirmed.

此之御世。定淡道之屯家也。
