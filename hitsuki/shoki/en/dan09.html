Nihon Shoki, Book 2 — Age of the Gods (part 2) · page 7 of 50

The Pacification of the Central Land of Reed Plains and the Descent of the Heavenly Grandson (Section 9)

葦原中国の平定と天孫降臨（第九段）

Takamimusubi-no-Mikoto, wishing to make his grandson Ninigi-no-Mikoto (天津火瓊瓊杵尊) lord of the Central Land of Reed Plains, sends one envoy after another; at the demand of Futsunushi-no-Kami and Takemikazuchi-no-Kami, Ōanamuchi-no-Kami (大己貴神) withdraws from the land, and the imperial grandson descends to the peak of Takachiho in Hyūga. The main text and eight variant accounts transmit the story up to the point where the descended imperial grandson takes 鹿葦津姫 * (Konohanasakuya-hime, 木花之開耶姫) as wife and three children are born in a birth amid fire undertaken to clear his suspicion.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textIn the main text, the one who decides on sending the envoys and who covers the imperial grandson with the coverlet 眞床追衾* and sends him down is “the imperial ancestor” (皇祖) Takamimusubi-no-Mikoto; Amaterasu-Ōmikami (天照太神) appears only by name in the genealogy at the beginning. In variant account 1, it is Amaterasu-Ōmikami who both gives the command to Ame-no-Wakahiko (天稚) and has the imperial grandson descend.
  2. Main textThe envoys of the main text, Ame-no-Hohi-no-Mikoto, his son 大背飯三熊之大人*, and Ame-no-Wakahiko, in that order, all fail to report back, and finally Futsunushi-no-Kami and Takemikazuchi-no-Kami descend to the small beach of 五十田狹* in Izumo. Ōanamuchi-no-Kami, receiving the reply of his son Kotoshironushi-no-Kami, withdraws from the land, hands the two deities the broad spear (廣矛) he used when he pacified the land, and goes into hiding.
  3. Variant accountThe three treasures (the Yasakani curved jewel, the Yata mirror, and the Kusanagi Sword), the accompaniment of the deities of the five groups (五部神), the command “coeternal with heaven and earth” (天壌無窮), and the exchange between the deity of the crossroads (衢神; Sarutahiko-no-Ōkami) and Ame-no-Uzume appear in variant account 1; the main text has no passage on the bestowal of the treasures. In variant account 2, Amaterasu-Ōmikami gives the treasured mirror to Ame-no-Oshihomimi-no-Mikoto (天忍穗耳尊) and commands that it be worshipped “on the same couch and in the same hall” (同床共殿).
  4. Variant accountIn variant account 2, the transfer of the land is concluded when Takamimusubi-no-Mikoto shows Ōanamuchi-no-Kami that the imperial grandson will govern visible matters (顯露之事) while Ōanamuchi-no-Kami governs divine matters, and that the palace 天日隅宮* will be built and Ame-no-Hohi-no-Mikoto made to worship him there. The same variant account contains the origin of short lifespans arising from the rejection of Iwanagahime (磐長姫) (Iwanagahime does not appear in the main text).
  5. Compared with the KojikiIn the Kojiki, Amaterasu-Ōmikami and 高木神 * (Takamimusubi-no-Kami) give the command, Takemikazuchi-no-Kami (建御雷神) descends together with Ame-no-Torifune-no-Kami, and a contest of strength with Takeminakata-no-Kami is also told. In section 9 of the Nihon Shoki, the two deities who descend are Futsunushi-no-Kami and Takemikazuchi-no-Kami, and Takeminakata-no-Kami appears in neither the main text nor any variant account.
  6. CommentaryThe deity-name database of Kokugakuin University notes that the story of the origin of short lifespans through Iwanagahime appears only in the Kojiki and in variant account 2 of the Nihon Shoki, and that the theory regarding it as the same type as the southern “banana type” tales, in which a choice is made between a stone and a banana, has become the established view. It also notes that whereas the Kojiki makes it a story about the lifespan of the emperors, an alternative version (一云) within variant account 2 makes it the origin of the short lives of humans in general. What the main text says

正哉吾勝勝速日天忍穗耳尊*, the child of Amaterasu-Ōmikami (天照太神), took as wife Takuhatachijihime (栲幡千千姫), daughter of Takamimusubi-no-Mikoto, and Ninigi-no-Mikoto (天津火瓊瓊杵尊) was born. The imperial ancestor Takamimusubi-no-Mikoto was especially fond of this grandson and thought to make him lord of the Central Land of Reed Plains. In that land, however, there were many deities that shone like the light of fireflies and evil deities as noisy as the buzzing of flies, and even the grasses and trees all spoke. Takamimusubi-no-Mikoto gathered the eighty deities and asked whom he should send, and he sent Ame-no-Hohi-no-Mikoto, whom they all recommended, but Ame-no-Hohi-no-Mikoto fawned on Ōanamuchi-no-Kami and did not report back even after three years. His son 大背飯三熊之大人 (also called 武三熊之大人; readings unconfirmed), who was sent next, also followed his father and likewise did not report back.

Next Ame-no-Wakahiko (天稚), son of 天國玉*, was chosen, and he received the heavenly deer-bow (天鹿兒弓) and the heavenly feathered arrows (天羽羽矢) and descended. Ame-no-Wakahiko took as wife Shitateru-hime (下照姫), daughter of Utsushikunitama (顯國玉), and lived there, and, saying that he too wished to govern the Central Land of Reed Plains, did not report back. When Takamimusubi-no-Mikoto sent a nameless pheasant to find out how matters stood, the pheasant perched in the top of the many-branched 杜木 tree (湯津杜木) before Ame-no-Wakahiko's gate, and Ama-no-Sagume (天探女), seeing it, told Ame-no-Wakahiko. Ame-no-Wakahiko shot the pheasant dead with the bow and arrows he had received, and the arrow passed through the pheasant's breast and reached the place before Takamimusubi-no-Mikoto's seat. Takamimusubi-no-Mikoto saw the bloodied arrow and, thinking it might be because Ame-no-Wakahiko had fought with the earthly deities, threw it down; the arrow struck the breast of Ame-no-Wakahiko, who was lying asleep after the first-fruits festival, and Ame-no-Wakahiko died. This is said to be the origin of the saying people have, 「反矢可畏」 (“a returned arrow is to be feared”).

The sound of Shitateru-hime's weeping reached heaven, and 天國玉*, learning that Ame-no-Wakahiko had died, had a swift wind carry the body up to heaven, built a mourning house, and held a temporary enshrinement (殯). He assigned the roles of the funeral to birds such as river geese and sparrows, and they wept and lamented for eight days and eight nights (a different arrangement of the birds' roles is also recorded in an “alternative version” (一云) in a small note). When Ajisukitakahikone-no-Kami (味耜高根神), who had been close to Ame-no-Wakahiko, ascended to heaven to mourn, his appearance was the very image of Ame-no-Wakahiko, so the relatives, wife, and children clung to him, saying 「吾君猶在」 (“our lord is still alive”). Ajisukitakahikone-no-Kami, angry at being mistaken for the dead, cut down the mourning house with the sword he wore, 大葉刈 * (also 神戶劔). The mourning house fell and became a mountain, and it is said that this is 喪山* at the upper reaches of the 藍見川* in the province of Mino.

When Takamimusubi-no-Mikoto again consulted the deities, they all recommended Futsunushi-no-Kami. Then Takemikazuchi-no-Kami, the child of 熯速日神, who was the child of 甕速日神, who was the child of 稜威雄走神 (readings unconfirmed) dwelling in the Heavenly Rock Cave, came forward and asked whether Futsunushi-no-Kami alone was a brave warrior and he himself was not; so it was decided to have the two deities pacify the Central Land of Reed Plains. The two deities descended to the small beach of 五十田狹* in the province of Izumo, thrust a ten-span sword upside down into the ground, squatted on its point, and asked Ōanamuchi-no-Kami whether he would withdraw from the land. Ōanamuchi-no-Kami said he would answer after asking his son, and sent the messenger 稻背脛* in the 熊野諸手船 (a many-oared boat of Kumano) to Kotoshironushi-no-Kami, who was enjoying fishing at the cape of 三穗*. Kotoshironushi-no-Kami answered that his father should withdraw and that he himself would not resist; he built an eightfold fence of green brushwood (八重蒼柴籬) in the sea, stepped on the side of the boat (枻), and departed.

Ōanamuchi-no-Kami conveyed his son's words to the two deities and stated that he too would withdraw: if he resisted, the deities within the land would all resist with him, but if he withdrew, no one would oppose them. He then gave the two deities the broad spear he had used as a staff when he pacified the land, saying that if the Heavenly Grandson governed the land with this spear it would surely be at peace, and he went into hiding in 「百不足之八十隅」 (“the eighty corners, short of a hundred”). The two deities executed the demon-deities who would not submit and reported back. An “alternative version” (一云) in a small note holds that 香香背男*, a star deity who refused to submit to the very end, was brought to submission by sending Takehazuchi-no-Mikoto (建葉槌命), the deity of 倭文*.

Takamimusubi-no-Mikoto covered the imperial grandson Ninigi-no-Mikoto with the coverlet 眞床追衾* and sent him down. The imperial grandson left the heavenly rock seat (天磐座), pushed apart the eightfold clouds of heaven, and descended to the peak of Takachiho in 襲* in Hyūga. From there, by way of the Floating Bridge of Heaven (天浮橋) of 二上*, he stood on a level place on a floating sandbank (浮渚在平處), and he proceeded along the hills through the barren, empty land in search of a country, arriving at the cape of 笠狹 at 長屋 in 吾田 (readings unconfirmed). A person there named 事勝國勝長狹* answered that there was a land here and that he should pass his time as he pleased, and the imperial grandson stayed there.

The imperial grandson met a beautiful woman of that land, 鹿葦津姫 * (also 神吾田津姫 and Konohanasakuya-hime, 木花之開耶姫), who declared that she was the child born when a heavenly deity took Ōyamatsumi-no-Kami (大山祇神) as wife. When the imperial grandson summoned her, she conceived in a single night, but the imperial grandson doubted, saying that he could not have made her conceive in one night and that the child was probably not his. Angered, she built a chamber without a door and entered it, vowed that if the child were not the Heavenly Grandson's it would be burned up, and if it were the Heavenly Grandson's the fire could not harm it, and set it on fire. When the smoke first rose, Honosusori-no-Mikoto (火闌降命; first ancestor of the Hayato and others) was born; while she was avoiding the heat, Hohodemi-no-Mikoto (火火出見尊) was born; and next Hoakari-no-Mikoto (火明命; first ancestor of the Owari no Muraji (尾張連) and others) was born. The children were three. Later Ninigi-no-Mikoto died and was buried in the 可愛之山陵* mausoleum in Hyūga in Tsukushi.

A plain retelling of what the main text of the Nihon Shoki says (no published translation has been used).

Variant accounts

After its main text, the Nihon Shoki often sets out other versions, introduced with words such as "a certain book says" (一書曰).

Variant account 1

It is Amaterasu-Ōmikami who gives the command; Ame-no-Wakahiko takes as wives many daughters of the earthly deities and does not report back for eight years, and a pheasant is sent on the plan of Omoikane-no-Kami. The heavenly deity throws the arrow back with the curse, “If he shot with an evil heart, let it strike Ame-no-Wakahiko,” and in the funeral scene two songs (rustic songs, 夷曲) by those gathered to mourn (in an “alternative version” (或云), by Shitateru-hime (下照媛), younger sister of Ajisukitakahikone-no-Kami) are added. Ame-no-Oshihomimi-no-Mikoto looks down on the lower world from the Floating Bridge of Heaven and gives up descending; after the pacification by Takemikazuchi-no-Kami and Futsunushi-no-Kami, the imperial grandson who had been born descends in his place, accompanied by the three treasures and the deities of the five groups and having received the command “coeternal with heaven and earth”; on the way there is an exchange between the deity of the crossroads, Sarutahiko-no-Ōkami (猿田彦大神), and Ame-no-Uzume, and Ame-no-Uzume is given the title 猿女君*.

Variant account 2

The two deities first say they wish to execute the evil deity of heaven 天津甕星 (天香香背男)*. Ōanamuchi-no-Kami at first refuses, but after receiving a decree on such matters as the division between visible matters and divine matters, the building of the palace 天日隅宮*, and Ame-no-Hohi-no-Mikoto's being in charge of the worship, he complies, recommends Funato-no-Kami (岐神) as guide, and goes into hiding; Ōmononushi-no-Kami and Kotoshironushi-no-Kami submit, and Mihotsu-hime (三穗津姫) is given to Ōmononushi-no-Kami as wife. Amaterasu-Ōmikami bestows the treasured mirror and the rice ears of the sacred garden (齋庭之穂), the imperial grandson is born in mid-air and descends instead, and after Iwanagahime is rejected and the origin of short lifespans is told, Honosuseri-no-Mikoto (火酢芹命), Hoakari-no-Mikoto, and Hohodemi-no-Mikoto (火折尊)* are born in the birth amid fire.

Variant account 3

It gives the order of birth in the fire as Hoakari-no-Mikoto, when the fire first began to brighten; Honosusumi-no-Mikoto (火進命; Honosuseri-no-Mikoto), when the fire was at its height; and 火折火火出見尊*, when avoiding the fire; and holds that mother and children were all unharmed. It adds that the umbilical cord was cut with a bamboo knife and that the discarded bamboo knife became a bamboo grove, so that the place was called 竹屋*, and that heavenly sweet sake (天甜酒) was brewed from the rice of the 狹名田* fields that 神吾田鹿葦津姫* determined by divination.

Variant account 4

Takamimusubi-no-Mikoto wraps 天津國光火瓊瓊杵尊 (Ninigi-no-Mikoto) in the coverlet 眞床覆衾*, opens the heavenly rock door (天磐戶), and sends him down. A point not in the main text is that Ame-no-Oshihi-no-Mikoto (天忍日命), distant ancestor of the Ōtomo no Muraji (大伴連), leading 天槵津大來目*, distant ancestor of the 來目部*, went before him as vanguard, bearing arms. 事勝國勝長狹* is made a child of Izanagi-no-Mikoto, with the other name Shiotsuchi-no-Oji (鹽土老翁).

Variant account 5

It makes the children born four, and from within the fire Hoakari-no-Mikoto, Honosusumi-no-Mikoto, 火折尊*, and Hohodemi-no-Mikoto come out in turn, announcing their names. The Heavenly Grandson at first mocks the children, but later explains that, since there were those who doubted that she had conceived in a single night, he had done it to make everyone know that they were his children and that a heavenly deity can cause conception in one night, and that mother and children possess miraculous power.

Variant account 6

It makes the children of 天忍穗根尊* 天火明命* and 天津根火瓊瓊杵根尊*, and makes Ame-no-Kagoyama (天香山), child of 天火明命, the distant ancestor of the Owari no Muraji and others. It transmits that the first nameless male pheasant stayed in the millet and bean fields and did not return (the origin of 「雉頓」), and that the next one, a female pheasant, was shot by Ame-no-Wakahiko; it gives the place of descent as the 添山 peak* of Takachiho in 襲* in Hyūga, and the children born as Honosuseri-no-Mikoto and 火折尊 (Hohodemi-no-Mikoto). Because 豐吾田津姫* resented that the imperial grandson would not exchange words with her, the imperial grandson, troubled, composes a song.

Variant account 7

It has no story, and only lists variant genealogies. There are traditions such as one making 天之杵火火置瀨尊* the child of 天忍骨命 and 天萬栲幡千幡姫 (readings unconfirmed), and, as an “alternative version” (一云), a tradition making Hoakari-no-Mikoto, 火夜織命*, and Hohodemi-no-Mikoto the children of 天杵瀨命 and 吾田津姫 (readings unconfirmed).

Variant account 8

It makes the children of 正哉吾勝勝速日天忍穗耳尊* and 天萬栲幡千幡姫* two: 天照國照火明命 * (distant ancestor of the Owari no Muraji and others) and 天饒石國饒石天津火瓊瓊杵尊*. It holds that Ninigi-no-Mikoto (天津火瓊瓊杵尊) took Konohanasakuya-hime-no-Mikoto (木花開耶姫命) as wife and Honosuseri-no-Mikoto and Hohodemi-no-Mikoto were born, making the children two.

In the original text

此世人所謂反矢可畏之縁也

This is the origin of people's saying that “a returned arrow is to be feared.”

今我當於百不足之八十隅將隱去矣

Ōanamuchi-no-Kami's words as he withdraws from the land: “I shall now go into hiding in the eighty corners, short of a hundred (the distant recesses).”

寶祚之隆當與天壌無窮者矣

Amaterasu-Ōmikami's command in variant account 1: the prosperity of the imperial throne shall be without end, together with heaven and earth.

吾兒視此寶鏡當猶視吾

Variant account 2: “My child, regard looking upon this treasured mirror as the same as looking upon me.”

  • Ame-no-Oshihi-no-Mikoto (天忍日命)In variant account 4, the deity who, as distant ancestor of the Ōtomo no Muraji, went before the Heavenly Grandson as armed vanguard.

In the Kojiki

Kojiki, Part 1 — the sending of Takemikazuchi-no-Kami and Ōkuninushi-no-Kami's transfer of the land

In the Kojiki, Ame-no-Torifune-no-Kami is attached to Takemikazuchi-no-Kami (建御雷神) and they descend to the small beach of 伊耶佐*; after Kotoshironushi-no-Kami, Takeminakata-no-Kami challenges them to a contest of strength and flees as far as the sea of 州羽 in the province of 科野 (readings unconfirmed). In the main text of the Nihon Shoki, the ones who descend are Futsunushi-no-Kami and Takemikazuchi-no-Kami, and there is no story of Takeminakata-no-Kami.

尒天鳥船神副建御雷神而遣
Kojiki, Part 1 — the sending of Ame-no-Wakahiko (the funeral scene)

In the Kojiki, the father, wife, and children of Ame-no-Wakahiko (天若日子) come down from heaven and build the mourning house on that spot (on earth). In the main text of the Nihon Shoki, the body is carried to heaven by a swift wind, and the mourning house is built and the temporary enshrinement held in heaven.

乃於其處作喪屋
Kojiki, Part 1 — the marriage of Ninigi-no-Mikoto

In the Kojiki, Ōyamatsumi-no-Kami offers Iwanagahime (石長比売) in addition, and Ōyamatsumi-no-Kami himself states that because Iwanagahime was sent back, the lifespans of the children of the heavenly deities became short. In the main text of the Nihon Shoki Iwanagahime does not appear, and in variant account 2 it is Iwanagahime herself who, ashamed, utters the curse.
