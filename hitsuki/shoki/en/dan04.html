Nihon Shoki, Book 1 — Age of the Gods (part 1) · page 2 of 50

The Birth of the Land (section 4)

国生み（第四段）

Izanagi-no-Mikoto (伊弉諾尊) and Izanami-no-Mikoto (伊弉冉尊) probe the sea with a spear from the Floating Bridge of Heaven (天浮橋) and obtain Onogoro Island (磤馭慮島), where they descend and become husband and wife. Taking Awaji Island (淡路洲) as the afterbirth (胞), the two deities give birth to 大日本豊秋津洲* and other islands, and the name “Land of the Great Eight Islands” (大八洲国) arises.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textIn the main text, Izanagi-no-Mikoto (伊弉諾尊) and Izanami-no-Mikoto (伊弉冉尊) stand on the Floating Bridge of Heaven (天浮橋) and consult together, “Might there not be a land below?”; when they probe with the Jewelled Spear of Heaven (天之瓊矛), the brine that drips from the spear’s tip hardens and becomes Onogoro Island (磤馭慮島). The main text does not record any command from the heavenly deities (天神).
  2. Main textWhen the two deities went round the island, taken as the central pillar of the land, in opposite directions, the female deity (陰神) spoke first, so the male deity (陽神), saying that the man ought to speak first, had them go round again; the second time the male deity spoke first, and they became husband and wife.
  3. Main textWhen it came to giving birth, they first took Awaji Island (淡路洲) as the afterbirth (胞, e), and then gave birth to 大日本豊秋津洲, 伊予二名洲 (readings unconfirmed), Tsukushi Island (筑紫洲), Oki Island (隠岐洲) and Sado Island (佐度洲) (as twins), Koshi (越洲), 大洲 and 吉備子洲 (readings unconfirmed), and so the name “Land of the Great Eight Islands” (大八洲国) arose. Tsushima (対馬島), Iki (壱岐島) and the small islands are said to have formed from hardened foam of the brine.
  4. Variant accountIn variant account 1, the heavenly deities (天神) give the two deities the Jewelled Spear of Heaven (天瓊戈) with a command; their first child, 蛭児 (Hiruko), is set adrift in a reed boat, and 淡洲* too is not counted among their children. When the two deities return to heaven and report, the heavenly deities divine by 太占* and tell them that it happened because the woman’s words came first, and have them do it again.
  5. Variant accountThere are ten variant accounts, and their traditions of how the islands were born vary. Some take Awaji Island (淡路洲) and 淡洲* as the afterbirth (variant account 6), and one takes 磤馭慮嶋 (Onogoro Island) as the afterbirth and gives birth to Awaji Island (variant account 8); variant account 7 counts Iki (壱岐洲) and Tsushima (対馬洲) among the islands born.
  6. CommentaryKokugakuin University’s database of deity names notes that there is a tradition in which an island forms from drops from the spear’s tip hardening, as in the main text and variant account 1, and a tradition in which the island is obtained by probing with the spear, as in variant accounts 2 to 4, and that there is a view that the latter is the older form. It also cites a view that old elements of Awaji traditions survive in variant account 8, in which Awaji Island is born first with 磤馭慮島 (Onogoro Island) as the afterbirth. What the main text says

Izanagi-no-Mikoto (伊弉諾尊) and Izanami-no-Mikoto (伊弉冉尊) stood on the Floating Bridge of Heaven (天浮橋) and consulted together, “How could there be no land below this?” So they thrust the Jewelled Spear of Heaven (天之瓊矛; 瓊 means jewel) downward and probed, and struck the blue sea. The brine that dripped from the tip of the spear hardened into an island, which was named Onogoro Island (磤馭慮島).

The two deities descended to the island and dwelt there, and thought to become husband and wife and give birth to lands. Taking Onogoro Island as the central pillar of the land, the male deity (陽神) went round the pillar from the left and the female deity (陰神) from the right, and they met at the same place. Then the female deity spoke first: “Oh, how delightful! I have met a lovely young man.” The male deity was displeased and said, “I am a man, so it is right that I should speak first. It is not good that the woman spoke first, so let us go round again.”

The two deities went round once more and met, and this time the male deity spoke first: “Oh, how delightful! I have met a lovely young woman.” The male deity and the female deity examined each other’s bodies, and here for the first time they were joined as husband and wife.

When the time came to give birth, they first took Awaji Island (淡路洲) as the afterbirth (胞, e), but because it did not satisfy them, the original text records, they named it Awaji Island. Then they gave birth to 大日本豊秋津洲, 伊予二名洲 (readings unconfirmed) and Tsukushi Island (筑紫洲), and gave birth to Oki Island (隠岐洲) and Sado Island (佐度洲) as twins. The original text adds that the twins sometimes born among people follow this precedent. They further gave birth to Koshi (越洲), 大洲 and 吉備子洲 (readings unconfirmed).

In this way the name “Land of the Great Eight Islands” (大八洲国) first arose. Tsushima (対馬島), Iki (壱岐島) and the small islands in various places are all said to have formed from hardened foam of the brine (or foam of the water).

A plain retelling of what the main text of the Nihon Shoki says (no published translation has been used).

Variant accounts

After its main text, the Nihon Shoki often sets out other versions, introduced with words such as "a certain book says" (一書曰).

Variant account 1

The heavenly deities (天神) command the two deities to go to “豊葦原千五百秋瑞穂之地”* and bestow on them the Jewelled Spear of Heaven (天瓊戈), and the two deities set up a hall eight fathoms long (八尋之殿) and a heavenly pillar (天柱) on the island. At first the female deity goes round from the left and the male deity from the right, and the female deity speaks first; 蛭児 (Hiruko; set adrift in a reed boat) and 淡洲* are born, but they are not counted as children. Following the teaching of the heavenly deities, obtained by divination with 太占*, the male deity goes round from the left and speaks first again. The islands born are 大日本豊秋津洲*, Awaji Island (淡路洲), 伊予二名洲*, Tsukushi Island (筑紫洲), 隠岐三子洲*, Sado Island (佐度洲), Koshi (越洲) and 吉備子洲*, and these are called the Land of the Great Eight Islands (大八洲国).

Variant account 2

The two deities stood in the mist of heaven (天霧) and said, “We wish to obtain a land,” and thrusting down the Jewelled Spear of Heaven (天瓊矛) they probed and obtained Onogoro Island (磤馭慮島). It says that they drew out the spear and rejoiced, “How good, there was a land,” and there is no story of drops hardening.

Variant account 3

The two deities, who were in Takamagahara (高天原), said, “There must be a land,” and with the Jewelled Spear of Heaven (天瓊矛) produced Onogoro Island (磤馭慮島) as if drawing it.

Variant account 4

The two deities talked together: “There is a thing like floating oil. Might there not be a land within it?” They probed with the Jewelled Spear of Heaven (天瓊矛), made an island, and named it Onogoro Island (磤馭慮嶋).

Variant account 5

That the female deity spoke first, which was taken as inauspicious, and that they went round again, is the same as in the main text; but afterwards the two deities did not know how to unite. Then a wagtail (鶺鴒) flew up and shook its head and tail, and by copying it the two deities learned the way of union, according to this account.

Variant account 6

It says that, taking Awaji Island (淡路洲) and 淡洲* as the afterbirth, they gave birth to 大日本豊秋津洲*, and then to 伊予洲*, Tsukushi Island (筑紫洲), Oki Island (隠岐洲) and Sado Island (佐度洲) (twins), Koshi (越洲), 大洲 and 子洲 (readings unconfirmed).

Variant account 7

There is no story of the afterbirth; it says that they first gave birth to Awaji Island (淡路洲), and then to 大日本豊秋津洲, 伊予二名洲 (readings unconfirmed), Oki Island (隠岐洲), Sado Island (佐度洲), Tsukushi Island (筑紫洲), Iki (壱岐洲) and Tsushima (対馬洲). Iki and Tsushima, which in the main text formed from the foam of the brine, are included among the islands born.

Variant account 8

It says that, taking Onogoro Island (磤馭慮嶋) as the afterbirth, they gave birth to Awaji Island (淡路洲), and then to 大日本豊秋津洲, 伊予二名洲 (readings unconfirmed), Tsukushi Island (筑紫洲), 吉備子洲*, Oki Island (隠岐洲) and Sado Island (佐度洲) (twins), and Koshi (越洲).

Variant account 9

It says that, taking Awaji Island (淡路洲) as the afterbirth, they gave birth to 大日本豊秋津洲*, and then to 淡洲, 伊予二名洲, 隠岐三子洲 (readings unconfirmed), Sado Island (佐度洲), Tsukushi Island (筑紫洲), 吉備子洲 and 大洲 (readings unconfirmed).

Variant account 10

It says that the female deity spoke first, “Ah, what a dear young man!”, took the male deity’s hand, and they became husband and wife, and gave birth to Awaji Island (淡路洲) and next to 蛭児 (Hiruko). There is no story of going round again.

In the original text

底下豈無國歟

How could there be no land below this? (the words of the two deities as they consulted)

吾是男子，理當先唱，如何婦人反先言乎

I am a man, so it is right that I should speak first; why did the woman speak first? (the words of the male deity)

由是始起大八洲國之號焉

By this the name “Land of the Great Eight Islands” (大八洲国) first arose.

時有鶺鴒飛來，搖其首尾

Then a wagtail (鶺鴒) flew up and shook its head and tail (variant account 5).

  • 大日本豐秋津洲*In the main text, the first island born after Awaji Island, the afterbirth (a reading note says that “日本” is read 耶麻騰).
  • 淡洲*An island that in variant account 1 is not counted among the children, and in variant account 6 is taken as the afterbirth together with Awaji Island.
  • Wagtail (鶺鴒)The bird in variant account 5 that, by shaking its head and tail, taught the two deities the way of union.

In the Kojiki

Kojiki, Part 1 (the section on 淤能碁呂島* and the making firm of the land)

In the Kojiki, the heavenly deities command the two deities to put the land in order and make it firm, and bestow on them the Jewelled Spear of Heaven (天沼矛). In the main text of the Nihon Shoki there is no command from the heavenly deities, and the two deities lower the spear after consulting between themselves (the command of the heavenly deities is in variant account 1).

賜天沼矛而言依賜也
Kojiki, Part 1 (水蛭子 and 淡嶋 (readings unconfirmed); consulting the heavenly deities)

In the Kojiki, the female deity speaks first and 水蛭子* is born, who is put in a reed boat and set adrift; 淡嶋* too is not counted among their children, and they do it again following the heavenly deities’ divination by 太占*. In the main text of the Nihon Shoki, 蛭児 (Hiruko) does not appear in section 4, and it is the male deity himself who proposes going round again, so the account closest to the Kojiki is variant account 1.

此子者入葦船而流去
Kojiki, Part 1 (the formation of the Land of the Great Eight Islands, 大八嶋国)

The Kojiki begins the births with 淡道之穂之狭別嶋* and calls the eight islands, including 伊伎嶋* and 津嶋 (Tsushima), the Land of the Great Eight Islands (大八嶋国), after which six more islands, such as 吉備児嶋*, are born. The main text of the Nihon Shoki takes Awaji Island (淡路洲) as the afterbirth, begins the births with 大日本豊秋津洲*, and says that Tsushima and Iki were formed from hardened foam of the brine.

故因此八嶋先所生，謂大八嶋國。
