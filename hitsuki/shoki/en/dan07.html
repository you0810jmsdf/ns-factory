Nihon Shoki, Book 1 — Age of the Gods (part 1) · page 5 of 50

The Heavenly Rock Cave (Section 7)

天の石窟（第七段）

Amaterasu-Ōmikami, alarmed and wounded by Susanoo-no-Mikoto's violent deeds, shuts herself in the Heavenly Rock Cave, and the world falls into a darkness with neither day nor night. The eight hundred thousand deities hold a ritual following the plan of Omoikane-no-Kami and bring Amaterasu-Ōmikami out, and they make Susanoo-no-Mikoto bear restitution for his offenses and banish him from heaven.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textIn the main text, Susanoo-no-Mikoto sows seed over seed in Amaterasu-Ōmikami's fields, breaks down the ridges between them, defecates in the new palace at the time of the first-fruits festival (新嘗), and makes a hole in the roof of the hall of sacred garments (斎服殿) and throws in a flayed heavenly piebald colt (天斑駒). Amaterasu-Ōmikami, startled, wounds herself with a shuttle (梭, hi), and in anger shuts herself in the Heavenly Rock Cave (天石窟).
  2. Main textWhen Amaterasu-Ōmikami closes the rock door, everything “within the six directions” (六合之内) is always dark, and there is no longer any distinction between day and night. The deities gather on the bank of the 天安河*; Omoikane-no-Kami has the long-crying birds cry, Ame-no-Koyane-no-Mikoto and Futodama-no-Mikoto (太玉命) hang a jewel string (御統), the Yata mirror (八咫鏡), and nikite offerings (和幣) on a sakaki tree (真坂樹) of Mount Ame-no-Kagoyama (天香山) and pray, and Ame-no-Uzume-no-Mikoto performs (wazaoki, 俳優) before the door.
  3. Main textWhen Amaterasu-Ōmikami opens the door a crack and peers out, Tajikarao-no-Kami (手力雄神) takes her hand and draws her out, and the Nakatomi deity (中臣神) and the 忌部* deity stretch a shirikume rope (端出之縄) and beg her, “Please do not go back again.” Afterward the deities made Susanoo-no-Mikoto bear restitution in the form of a thousand tables of offerings (千座置戸), had his hair (or the nails of his hands and feet) pulled out, and banished him.
  4. Variant accountThe story of the mirror differs from one variant account to another. In variant account 1, Ishikoridome (石凝姥) makes an image of a deity from the metal of Mount Ame-no-Kagoyama, and it is regarded as the Hinokuma deity (日前神) of the province of Kii. In variant account 2, Ame-no-Nukado (天糠戸) makes a mirror, and the mirror placed in the cave, which still bears a small flaw from touching the door, is regarded as the 伊勢崇秘之大神*. In variant account 3, 石凝戸辺* makes the Yata mirror. [source, Japanese]
  5. Variant accountVariant account 3 presents the making of restitution with the nails of the hands and feet as “nails of good riddance and nails of ill riddance” (吉爪棄物, 凶爪棄物) as the reason why people dispose of their own nails with care, and presents the banished Susanoo-no-Mikoto being refused lodging while wearing a hat and straw cape as the reason why entering another's house in a hat and straw cape is shunned. It also includes a story in which Susanoo-no-Mikoto ascends to heaven once more, makes a pledge, and produces six male deities.
  6. CommentaryThe deity-name database of Kokugakuin University cites a theory that, based on the Nihon Shoki's spelling 「思兼神」, interprets the name of Omoikane-no-Kami as “a deity combining many thoughts,” and also notes that the Nihon Shoki describes him with the phrases “deeply plotting and thinking far ahead” and “wisdom of deliberation.” Variant account 1 makes Omoikane-no-Kami the child of Takamimusubi (高皇産霊). [source, Japanese]

What the main text says

After the pledge, Susanoo-no-Mikoto's conduct became extremely violent. Amaterasu-Ōmikami had made 天狭田 and 長田 (readings unconfirmed) her sacred fields, but in spring Susanoo-no-Mikoto sowed seed over seed and broke down the ridges between the fields, and in autumn he let loose the heavenly piebald colt and made it lie down in the fields. When Amaterasu-Ōmikami was holding the first-fruits festival, he secretly defecated in the new palace, and when Amaterasu-Ōmikami was weaving the garments of the deities in the hall of sacred garments, he flayed the heavenly piebald colt, made a hole in the roof of the hall, and threw it in.

Amaterasu-Ōmikami, startled, wounded herself with the shuttle, and in anger entered the Heavenly Rock Cave, closed the rock door, and shut herself in. Because of this, everything within the six directions was always dark, and the alternation of day and night could no longer be told.

The eight hundred thousand deities gathered on the bank of the 天安河* and discussed how they should pray. Omoikane-no-Kami, plotting deeply, gathered the long-crying birds of the Eternal Land (常世) and made them cry at length, and had Tajikarao-no-Kami stand beside the rock door. Ame-no-Koyane-no-Mikoto, distant ancestor of the Nakatomi no Muraji (中臣連), and Futodama-no-Mikoto, distant ancestor of the 忌部*, dug up by the roots five hundred sakaki trees (五百箇真坂樹) of Mount Ame-no-Kagoyama, hung the string of five hundred Yasakani jewels (八坂瓊之五百箇御統) on the upper branches, the Yata mirror on the middle branches, and blue and white nikite offerings (青和幣, 白和幣) on the lower branches, and prayed together.

Ame-no-Uzume-no-Mikoto, distant ancestor of the 猿女君*, held in her hand a spear wound with miscanthus grass, stood before the door of the Heavenly Rock Cave, and performed skillfully. She made a headdress of the sakaki of Mount Ame-no-Kagoyama and a sash (tasuki, 手繦) of hikage moss (蘿), lit fires, set an overturned tub (覆槽, uke) in place, and spoke words of divine possession.

Hearing this, Amaterasu-Ōmikami said that since she had shut herself away, the Central Land of Plentiful Reed Plains (豊葦原中国) should be in long night, so why was Ame-no-Uzume-no-Mikoto making merry like this? She opened the rock door a crack with her hand and peered out. At that moment Tajikarao-no-Kami took her hand and drew her out. The Nakatomi deity and the 忌部* deity stretched the shirikume rope (端出之縄) all around and begged her not to go back again.

Afterward the deities laid the guilt on Susanoo-no-Mikoto, imposed on him a thousand tables of restitution (千座置戸), and had his hair pulled out to atone for his offenses. It is also said that they had the nails of his hands and feet pulled out. Thus Susanoo-no-Mikoto was at last driven out of heaven.

A plain retelling of what the main text of the Nihon Shoki says (no published translation has been used).

Variant accounts

After its main text, the Nihon Shoki often sets out other versions, introduced with words such as "a certain book says" (一書曰).

Variant account 1

It was Wakahirume-no-Mikoto (稚日女尊) who was weaving the garments of the deities in the hall of sacred garments; startled by the piebald colt thrown in, she fell from the loom, wounded her body with the shuttle, and died (神退). Amaterasu-Ōmikami tells Susanoo-no-Mikoto, “You still have a black heart,” and shuts herself in the cave. The deities gather at 天高市*, and on the proposal of Omoikane-no-Kami, child of Takamimusubi, Ishikoridome (石凝姥) makes an image of a deity using the metal of Mount Ame-no-Kagoyama and a bellows of deerskin. This is said to be the Hinokuma deity (日前神) of the province of Kii; the scene in which Amaterasu-Ōmikami comes out is not recorded.

Variant account 2

The Sun Deity had forgiven Susanoo-no-Mikoto's ravaging of the fields and his throwing in of the piebald colt, without anger or resentment, but at the first-fruits festival he secretly placed feces beneath her seat; she sat down without knowing it, fell ill, and shut herself in the cave. Ame-no-Nukado (天糠戸), distant ancestor of the mirror-makers' guild (鏡作部), makes a mirror; Futodama (太玉), distant ancestor of the 忌部*, makes offerings; 豊玉*, distant ancestor of the jewel-makers' guild (玉作部), makes jewels; and when Ame-no-Koyane-no-Mikoto recites divine blessings, the Sun Deity comes out. The flaw made when the mirror placed in the cave touched the door remains, and this is said to be the 伊勢崇秘之大神*. Susanoo-no-Mikoto is made to give up things from his hands and feet and his spittle and nasal mucus as articles of purification, and is banished.

Variant account 3

Through the contrast between the Sun Deity's good fields and Susanoo-no-Mikoto's poor fields, Susanoo-no-Mikoto, jealous, ravages the fields. Ame-no-Koyane-no-Mikoto hangs on a sakaki tree (真坂木) the Yata mirror of 石凝戸辺*, the curved jewels of 天明玉*, and the bark-cloth of 天日鷲*; when Futodama-no-Mikoto holds it up and prays, the Sun Deity says, “There have never been words as beautiful as these,” and opens the door, and Ame-no-Tajikarao-no-Kami (天手力雄神) pulls it open and light fills everything. The account tells the purification by nails and the story of being refused when asking for lodging in a hat and straw cape as the origins of customs, and continues on to a story in which Susanoo-no-Mikoto ascends to heaven again, makes a pledge, produces six male deities and presents them to his elder sister, and goes down to the Root Land.

In the original text

閉磐戶而幽居焉。故六合之內常闇而不知晝夜之相代。

Because she closed the rock door and shut herself in, the whole world was always dark, and the alternation of day and night could no longer be told.

時手力雄神則奉承天照大神之手引而奉出。

At that moment Tajikarao-no-Kami took Amaterasu-Ōmikami's hand and drew her out.

然後諸神歸罪過於素戔鳴尊。而科之以千座置戶。

Afterward the deities laid the guilt on Susanoo-no-Mikoto and imposed on him a thousand tables of restitution (千座置戸, a great quantity of articles of atonement).

頃者人雖多請。未有若此言之麗美者也。

Lately many have made petitions, but there have never been words as beautiful as these: the Sun Deity's words in variant account 3.

  • Amaterasu-Ōmikami (天照大神)Startled and wounded by Susanoo-no-Mikoto's deeds, she shuts herself in the Heavenly Rock Cave, but, drawn by the deities' ritual, opens the door.

  • Ame-no-Uzume-no-Mikoto (天鈿女命)Distant ancestor of the 猿女君*. She performed before the door of the cave and spoke words of divine possession.
  • Wakahirume-no-Mikoto (稚日女尊)In variant account 1, the deity who was weaving the garments of the deities in the hall of sacred garments and died, startled by the piebald colt thrown in.
  • Ishikoridome (石凝姥)In variant account 1, the smith who used the metal of Mount Ame-no-Kagoyama to make the image that became the Hinokuma deity (gloss: 「伊之居梨度咩」).
  • Ame-no-Nukado (天糠戶)In variant account 2, the distant ancestor of the mirror-makers' guild, who made the mirror.
  • 天明玉*In variant account 3, the distant ancestor of the jewel-makers who made the Yasakani curved jewels; regarded as a child of Izanagi-no-Mikoto.
  • Kogotomusubi (興台產靈)In variant account 3, regarded as the father of Ame-no-Koyane-no-Mikoto (gloss: 「許語等武須毘」).

In the Kojiki

Kojiki, Part 1 — Susanoo-no-Mikoto's misdeeds (after the pledge)

In the Kojiki, the one who dies in the incident at the hall of sacred garments is a heavenly weaving maiden (天衣織女), who strikes her genitals on a shuttle and dies. In the main text of the Nihon Shoki, Amaterasu-Ōmikami herself wounds herself with the shuttle, and in variant account 1 Wakahirume-no-Mikoto dies.

天衣織女見驚而於梭衝陰上而死
Kojiki, Part 1 — the Heavenly Rock Cave (天石屋)

In the Kojiki, the eight million deities laugh, and Ame-no-Koyane-no-Mikoto and Futodama-no-Mikoto (布刀玉命) hold out the mirror to show it to Amaterasu-Ōmikami; when she comes out wondering, Tajikarao-no-Kami (手力男神) draws her out. The main text of the Nihon Shoki has neither the scene of showing the mirror nor the laughter of the deities; Amaterasu-Ōmikami opens the door herself after hearing the merry voice of Ame-no-Uzume-no-Mikoto.

天兒屋命、布刀玉命指出其鏡
Kojiki, Part 1 — the banishment of Susanoo-no-Mikoto and Ōgetsuhime

In the Kojiki, right after the banishment comes the story of Susanoo-no-Mikoto killing Ōgetsuhime-no-Kami (大気津比売神), from whose body the five grains and other things arise, but no corresponding passage could be confirmed in the main text or the variant accounts of section 7 of the Nihon Shoki (a similar story appears in section 5, variant account 11, as the story of Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto (月夜見尊) and Ukemochi-no-Kami).

又，食物乞大氣津比賣神。
