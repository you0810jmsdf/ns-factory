Emperor Tenmu’s Accession and the Yoshino Covenant

天武天皇の即位と吉野の盟約

Gathers the records from Emperor Tenmu’s accession at the Asuka Kiyomihara Palace in his 2nd year through his 9th year. Year by year it records the posthumous granting of ranks to those who served with merit in the year 壬申, the worship of the gods and Buddhist ceremonies, exchanges with Silla, Koguryŏ and 耽羅*, the princes’ covenant at Yoshino, and the vow to found Yakushi-ji.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textIn the second month of the 2nd year, the emperor had officials set up an altar platform (壇場) and took the throne at the Asuka Kiyomihara Palace. He made his principal consort empress; the text records that the empress bore Prince Kusakabe (草壁皇子尊), and then lists his consorts and wives and their princes and princesses.
  2. Main textIn the fifth month of the 8th year, at the Yoshino Palace, the emperor had the empress and Prince Kusakabe, Prince Ōtsu, Prince Takechi, Prince Kawashima (河嶋皇子), Prince Osakabe (忍壁皇子) and Prince 芝基 (芝基皇子)* swear to help one another and not to quarrel. Saying that he would cherish these children of different mothers as though they had been born of one mother, the emperor embraced the six princes.
  3. Main textRecords recur in which, when people who had served in the war of the year 壬申 died, they were posthumously given ranks for their merit (坂本財臣, 紀臣阿閉麻呂, 物部雄君連, 村国連雄依 and others; readings unconfirmed).
  4. Main textWorship of the gods: in the 2nd year Princess 大来 (大来皇女)* was made to live in the abstinence palace (斎宮) at 泊瀬* so that she could serve at the shrine of Amaterasu-Ōmikami (天照大神宮), and in the tenth month of the 3rd year she was sent to the Ise shrine (伊勢神宮). From the fourth month of the 4th year, records of worship of the wind god of Tatsuta (龍田) and the 大忌 deity of Hirose (広瀬) appear almost every year.
  5. Main textBuddhist ceremonies: in the 2nd year the copying of the complete Buddhist canon (一切経) began at Kawara-dera (川原寺), and in the 6th year a great vegetarian feast (大斎) was held at Asuka-dera and the complete canon was read. In the eleventh month of the 9th year Yakushi-ji was founded for the first time for the sake of the empress’s illness, and a hundred monks were ordained.
  6. Main textIn the second month of the 4th year an edict declared that the 部曲 granted to the clans in the year 甲子 were abolished, and that the mountains, marshes, forests and fields that had been granted to imperial princes, princes, ministers and temples were taken back. In the fourth month of the same year, fishing and hunting devices were restricted and the eating of the meat of cattle, horses, dogs, monkeys and chickens was forbidden.

What the main text says

In the first month of the 2nd year a banquet was given for the ministers. In the second month the emperor took the throne at the Asuka Kiyomihara Palace and made his principal consort empress. In the third month a white pheasant was presented from Bingo province and a general amnesty was proclaimed throughout the realm, and at Kawara-dera scribes were gathered and the copying of the complete canon began. In the fourth month, so that Princess 大来* could serve at the shrine of Amaterasu-Ōmikami, she was first made to live in the abstinence palace at 泊瀬*. In the fifth month an edict declared that those entering service for the first time should first serve as great attendants (大舎人), and that women too would be allowed to serve if they wished.

In the intercalary sixth month of the 2nd year, Silla sent envoys to congratulate the emperor on his accession and envoys to offer condolences on the death of the previous emperor. A prince of 耽羅* also came with tribute, and in the eighth month the 耽羅 envoys were given court rank for the first time. In the twelfth month gifts were given to the Nakatomi (中臣), the 忌部*, the shrine officials, and the district officials of Harima and Tanba and others who had served at the Great Thanksgiving (大嘗), and the head of the office for building the Great Temple of Takechi (高市大寺) was appointed.

In the third month of the 3rd year it was reported from Tsushima that silver had been produced for the first time, and the governor was given rank. In the eighth month Prince Osakabe was sent to Isonokami Shrine (石上神宮) to polish the sacred treasures with oil, and it was ordered that the treasures that the various houses had deposited in the divine storehouse (神府) be returned to their descendants. In the tenth month Princess 大来* set out from the abstinence palace at 泊瀬 for the Ise shrine.

In the first month of the 4th year a star-observation platform (占星台) was built for the first time. In the second month the abolition of the 部曲 and other measures were proclaimed, and in the fourth month the emperor had the wind god of Tatsuta and the 大忌 deity of Hirose worshipped, and fixed restrictions on fishing, hunting and eating meat. In the 5th year there was a great drought in summer; though prayers were offered to the gods and to the Buddha, no rain fell and the people starved. In the eighth month a great purification (大祓) was held in all four directions and an amnesty reducing punishments was issued. In this year the emperor intended to place the capital at 新城* and let the fields there go to waste, but in the end he did not make it the capital.

In the sixth month of the 6th year the emperor issued an edict to the 東漢直* and others in which he reproached them for the crimes they had committed in the past, but pardoned them out of great mercy so that their clan would not die out. In the eighth month a great vegetarian feast was held at Asuka-dera and the complete canon was read, and imperial princes, princes and high officials were each permitted to have one person enter holy orders. In spring of the 7th year an abstinence palace was built on the upper reaches of the river at 倉梯* to worship the gods of heaven and earth, but just before the departure in the fourth month Princess 十市 (十市皇女)* died suddenly in the palace, the excursion was cancelled, and the gods were not worshipped. In the twelfth month of the 7th year a great earthquake struck Tsukushi; the ground split and many houses collapsed.

In the third month of the 8th year the emperor paid homage at the tomb of the Later Okamoto Emperor (後岡本天皇) at 越智*. In the fifth month, at the Yoshino Palace, he made a covenant with the empress and the six princes, and when they returned to the palace the six princes together bowed to the emperor. In the tenth month, saying that bad people were numerous in the towns because princes and high officials overlooked them, he issued an edict that high and low should correct one another, and he also fixed the dress of monks and nuns. In the eleventh month barriers were set up for the first time on Mount Tatsuta and on Mount 大坂 (大坂山)*, and an outer wall (羅城) was built at Naniwa.

In the fourth month of the 9th year it was decided that, except for two or three great state temples, government offices would not administer temples. In the fifth month the Golden Light Sutra (金光明経) was expounded for the first time in the palace and at the temples. In the seventh month a branch of the zelkova tree west of Asuka-dera broke off of its own accord, and Prince 舎人 (舎人王)* died. In the eleventh month, when the empress fell ill, the emperor made a vow for her sake and founded Yakushi-ji for the first time, and had a hundred monks ordained, and the empress recovered. In the same month the emperor also fell ill, but as soon as a hundred monks were ordained he recovered.

A plain retelling of what the main text of the Nihon Shoki says (no published translation has been used).

Variant accounts

After its main text, the Nihon Shoki often sets out other versions, introduced with words such as "a certain book says" (一書曰).

One version says (一云)

The main text records 金薩儒* and others of Silla as envoys sent to offer condolences on the death of the previous emperor. A small note attached there reads “一云，調使” (“one version says: tribute envoys”), which shows that there was also a tradition that regarded these envoys as envoys bringing tribute (調).

In the original text

即帝位於飛鳥淨御原宮

The accession record: he took the imperial throne at the Asuka Kiyomihara Palace.

朕男等各異腹而生，然今如一母同產慈之

The emperor’s words at the Yoshino covenant. He says that he will cherish the princes born of different mothers as though they were children of the same mother.

凡銀有倭國，初出于此時

The record of silver produced in Tsushima, which states that this was the first time silver had been produced in the land of Yamato (倭国).

爲皇后誓願之，初興藥師寺

The record that a vow was made for the empress’s illness and Yakushi-ji was founded for the first time.

Deities and people, and the shrines that enshrine them

  • Amaterasu-Ōmikami天照大御神Enshrined at: Ise Jingū (Naikū, Inner Shrine), Atsuta Jingū, Kumano Hongū Taisha, Sumiyoshi Jinja (Hakata), Komagata Jinja, Hirota Jinja, Minashi Jinja, Hinomisaki Jinja, Izawa-no-Miya, Ōhirumemuchi Jinja, Kono Jinja, Kumano Nachi Taisha, Kamikawa Jinja, Mononobe Jinja, Nitta Jinja, Tamaki Jinja
  • Prince Kusakabe (草壁皇子尊)Son of the empress. At the Yoshino covenant he was the first to step forward and take the oath.
  • Prince Ōtsu (大津皇子)Son of Princess 大田 (大田皇女)*. One of the six princes who joined the Yoshino covenant.
  • Prince Takechi (高市皇子)Son of 尼子娘*. He joined the Yoshino covenant, and in the 9th year was sent to offer condolences on the death of a monk of Asuka-dera and to visit the ailing Prince 舎人*.
  • 大來皇女*A princess who purified herself at the abstinence palace at 泊瀬* in order to serve at the shrine of Amaterasu-Ōmikami, and set out for the Ise shrine in the tenth month of the 3rd year.
  • 十市皇女*Daughter of Nukata no Ōkimi (額田姫王). In the fourth month of the 7th year, just before the excursion to the abstinence palace, she died suddenly in the palace.
  • Prince Osakabe (忍壁皇子)A prince who was sent to Isonokami Shrine in the 3rd year and polished the sacred treasures.
