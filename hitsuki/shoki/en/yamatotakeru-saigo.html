Nihon Shoki, Book 7 — Emperor Keikō and Emperor Seimu · page 19 of 50

Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto's Eastern Campaign and Death, the Last Years of Emperor Keikō, and Emperor Seimu

日本武尊の東征と最期、景行天皇の晩年と成務天皇

Setting out on the eastern campaign, Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto passes through the grass fire in Suruga, the entry of 弟橘媛* into the sea, the submission of the Emishi, and the mountain deity of Shinano, and returns to Owari; but, led astray by the deity of Mount Ibuki (膽吹山), he falls ill, dies at the age of thirty at 能褒野*, and flies away as a white bird. The page goes on to record Emperor Keikō's later tour of the eastern lands and his death, and Emperor Seimu's appointment of heads for the provinces and districts (国郡) and for the 県 and 邑 (県邑).

Key points to remember

  1. Main textIn Suruga, deceived by bandits who set fire to the plain, Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto escaped danger by lighting a counter-fire with a fire-striker, and named the place Yaizu (焼津). When they met a violent storm on the sea crossing from Sagami to Kazusa, his concubine 弟橘媛* entered the sea in place of the prince, the wind dropped, and the boat reached the shore.
  2. Main textIn Owari, the prince married Miyazu-hime (宮簀媛), left his sword at Miyazu-hime's house, and went to strike down the wild deity of Mount Ibuki (膽吹山); but, taking the mountain deity, who had become a great serpent, for a messenger, he stepped over it and went on, was led astray by clouds and icy rain, and fell ill. He died at the age of thirty at 能褒野*, became a white bird and flew to 琴弾原* in Yamato and to the village of 旧市邑* in Kawachi, and the three tombs were called the White Bird Tombs (白鳥陵).
  3. Main textThe text records that the sword Kusanagi (草薙横刀), which Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto wore, is now at the Atsuta Shrine (熱田社) in the district of 年魚市郡* in the province of Owari. It also says that the Emishi presented to the Ise Shrine were noisy, so they were moved, by way of the vicinity of 御諸山*, to five provinces outside the home provinces, and became the ancestors of the 佐伯部*.
  4. Main textEmperor Seimu, troubled in the 4th year that the provinces and districts (国郡) and the 県 and 邑 (県邑) had no heads, in the 9th month of the 5th year placed 造長 over the provinces and districts and 稲置 over the 県 and 邑 (readings unconfirmed), giving them shields and spears as tokens. He divided the provinces and 県 by mountains and rivers and fixed the villages by roads, and called east-west "日縦", north-south "日横", the south side of a mountain "影面", and the north side "背面".
  5. CommentaryAccording to the database of deity names (entry: 伊服岐能山之神)*, the deity of Mount Ibuki, who appears as a white boar in the Kojiki, appears as a great serpent in the Nihon Shoki. The same database also notes that the Engishiki register of deities (延喜式神名帳) lists the 伊夫伎神社* shrine in the district of 坂田郡* in the province of Ōmi and the 伊富岐神社* shrine in the district of 不破郡* in the province of Mino. [source, Japanese]
  6. CommentaryRegarding the story in the Nihon Shoki in which the mountain deity of Shinano, transformed into a white deer, is killed with wild garlic (蒜), the database of deity names (entry: 科野之坂神)* points out that the Kojiki records only that the deity of the slope of 科野 was brought to submission by words, and that the plot of killing a white deer with wild garlic closely resembles the Kojiki's story of the deity of the slope of Ashigara (足柄). [source, Japanese]

What the main text says

In this year, Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto reached Suruga. Bandits, pretending to submit, invited the prince to hunt, saying there were many beasts on the plain, and set fire to the plain. Realizing he had been deceived, the prince produced fire with a fire-striker and escaped by a counter-fire, and burned all the bandits to death, so that place is called Yaizu (焼津). When he was about to cross from Sagami to Kazusa and said, "It is a small sea; one could even jump across it," a violent storm arose in the middle of the sea. His concubine 弟橘媛*, who was accompanying him, said that this was the will of the sea deity, and entered the sea in place of the prince's life; the wind dropped, and the boat reached the shore. That sea was called 馳水*.

From Kazusa the prince entered the province of Mutsu, hung a large mirror on his boat, proceeded by sea, and reached the border of the Emishi. The Emishi leaders, who were about to meet him in battle at 竹水門*, saw the prince's boat and, awed by its majesty, threw away their bows and arrows and asked his name. When the prince answered, "I am the son of a god manifest in human form (現人神)," the Emishi drew the boat to the shore and surrendered. Having pacified the Emishi, he returned from the land of 日高見国*, passed through Hitachi, and entered the 酒折宮* palace in the province of Kai, where the prince asked his attendants a question in song; a man tending the fires continued the end of the song, so the prince praised him and gave him a reward.

As Shinano and Koshi had not yet submitted, the prince went round through Musashi and Kōzuke and reached the 碓日坂* slope, where, longing for 弟橘媛*, he looked to the southeast and lamented three times, "吾嬬者耶" ("Oh, my wife!"). For this reason the provinces east of the mountains are called the land of Azuma (吾嬬国). He sent 吉備武彦* to the province of Koshi, and the prince himself entered rugged Shinano. When the mountain deity appeared as a white deer, he flicked a piece of wild garlic (蒜) into its eye and killed it; then he lost his way, but was guided out to Mino by a white dog. It is said that ever since then, anyone crossing this slope who chews wild garlic and smears it on people, oxen and horses is not affected by the deity's vapor.

Returning to Owari, the prince married Miyazu-hime (宮簀媛) and stayed past the month. Hearing that there was a wild deity on Mount Ibuki (膽吹山) in Ōmi, he left his sword at Miyazu-hime's house and went there on foot. The mountain deity became a great serpent and blocked the road, but the prince, thinking it was the deity's messenger, stepped over it and went on. The mountain deity raised clouds and made ice fall, so the prince forced his way through the fog and barely got out; he became as if he had lost his senses, and came to himself by drinking the water of a spring. That spring is called 居醒泉*. It was then that the prince first fell ill; he returned to Owari but did not enter Miyazu-hime's house, and at 尾津* in Ise, seeing that the sword he had earlier left behind under a pine tree was still there, he composed a song.

When he reached 能褒野*, his illness grew severe; the prince presented the captured Emishi to the Shrine (神宮), sent 吉備武彦* to convey to the emperor his regret at being unable to report back on his mission, and died at 能褒野 at the age of thirty. The emperor lamented and grieved, and had him buried in the 能褒野陵 tomb. But the prince became a white bird, came out of the tomb and flew toward the land of Yamato, and only his clothes were left in the coffin. The white bird stopped at 琴弾原* in Yamato and then at the village of 旧市邑* in Kawachi, so tombs were built at each place, and these three tombs are called the White Bird Tombs (白鳥陵). The white bird at last ascended to heaven, and the 武部* was established to hand down the prince's renown.

In the 51st year, 稚足彦尊* and Takeuchi-no-Sukune, who had not attended a banquet, answered that they had been guarding the gate against emergencies, and the emperor held them in high regard. In the 8th month, he made 稚足彦尊 crown prince and Takeuchi-no-Sukune the chief minister (棟梁之臣). The text also records that the sword Kusanagi (草薙横刀) is at the Atsuta Shrine (熱田社), that the Emishi presented to the Shrine became the ancestors of the 佐伯部*, and the consorts and children of Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto (including 足仲彦天皇)*. In the 52nd year the empress died, and 八坂入媛命* became empress. In the 53rd year, wishing to tour the lands that Prince 小碓 (小碓王)* had pacified, the emperor entered the Tōkai region from Ise, and in Kazusa, because 磐鹿六鴈* made white clams into namasu (膾) and presented them, he granted him the 膳大伴部*. In the 55th year, 彦狭嶋王*, who had been made governor-general (都督) of the fifteen provinces of the Tōsandō (東山道), died before reaching his post, and in the 56th year his son 御諸別王* governed the eastern lands and made the Emishi submit. In the 58th year the emperor moved to the 高穴穂宮* palace in Ōmi, and in the 11th month of the 60th year he died at the age of one hundred and six.

稚足彦天皇 * (Emperor Seimu) was the fourth son of Emperor Keikō, and his mother was the empress 八坂入姫命*. He acceded to the throne in the 1st month of his 1st year, and in the 11th month of the 2nd year buried Emperor Keikō in the 山辺道上陵* tomb in the land of Yamato. In the 3rd year he made Takeuchi-no-Sukune, who had been born on the same day as the emperor, Great Minister (大臣). In the 4th year he issued an edict lamenting that the provinces and districts had no lords and heads, and in the 9th month of the 5th year he placed 造長 over the provinces and districts and 稲置 over the 県 and 邑 (readings unconfirmed) and fixed their boundaries, and it is said that the people were at peace. In the 48th year he made his nephew 足仲彦尊* crown prince, and in the 6th month of the 60th year he died at the age of one hundred and seven.

Variant accounts

After its main text, the Nihon Shoki often sets out other versions, introduced with words such as "a certain book says" (一書曰).

One account (一云): small note in the "this year" entry of the 40th year

In the scene of the grass fire in Suruga, the main text says that the prince escaped by lighting a counter-fire with a fire-striker, but one account (一云) says that he was able to escape because the sword 叢雲* that the prince wore drew itself and mowed down the grass beside the prince, and that this is why the sword is called Kusanagi (草薙).

In the original text

願以妾之身。贖王之命而入海。

The words of 弟橘媛*: I wish to enter the sea, giving my body in place of the prince's life.

三歎曰。吾嬬者耶

The words the prince spoke three times in lament on the pass of 碓日*, longing for 弟橘媛*. They are regarded as the origin of the name of the land of Azuma (吾嬬国).

明衣空留而屍骨無之。

When the coffin was opened, only the clothes remained, and there was no body.

以國郡立造長。縣邑置稻置。

Emperor Seimu placed 造長 over the provinces and districts and 稲置 over the 県 and 邑 (readings unconfirmed).

In the Kojiki

Kojiki, Part 2, section on Emperor Keikō (the death of Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto; the deity of Mount 伊服岐能山)*

In the Kojiki, the deity of Mount Ibuki appears as a white boar as big as an ox, and Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto (倭建命), because he declares aloud that it is the deity's messenger, is struck by great icy rain. In the Nihon Shoki it appears as a great serpent, and the prince, thinking it the deity's messenger, steps over it and goes on.

白猪逢于山邊。其大如牛。
Kojiki, Part 2, section on Emperor Keikō (the death of Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto; the white bird)

In the Kojiki, Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto (倭建命) becomes a great white bird eight fathoms long (八尋白智鳥) and flies off; his consorts and children follow it singing, and it stops at 志幾* in the province of Kawachi, where the White Bird Tomb (白鳥御陵) is built. In the Nihon Shoki, the three tombs at 能褒野*, at 琴弾原* in Yamato, and at the village of 旧市邑* in Kawachi are called the White Bird Tombs (白鳥陵).

留河内國之志幾。故於其地作御陵。
Kojiki, Part 2, section on Emperor Seimu

On Emperor Seimu, the Kojiki records briefly that he made 建内宿禰 (Takeuchi-no-Sukune) Great Minister and established the 国造 of the provinces large and small, the boundaries of the provinces, and the 県主 of the 県 large and small (readings unconfirmed), and gives his age as ninety-five. The Nihon Shoki says that he placed 造長 and 稲置 and that he died at the age of one hundred and seven.
