Nihon Shoki, Book 17 — Emperor Keitai · page 32 of 50

The Enthronement of Emperor Keitai and the Rebellion of Iwai, Kuni-no-Miyatsuko of Tsukushi

継体天皇の擁立と筑紫国造磐井の反乱

After 小泊瀬天皇* (Emperor Buretsu) died without an heir, the Great Chieftain Ōtomo no Kanamura and others brought 男大迹王*, a fifth-generation descendant of Emperor Ōjin, from Mikuni and had him ascend the throne. During his reign, negotiations with Paekche, Silla and Kara over Mimana continued, and in the 21st year Iwai, Kuni-no-Miyatsuko (provincial chief) of Tsukushi, rose in rebellion and was struck down by 物部麁鹿火*.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textWhen 小泊瀬天皇* died without any children, the Great Chieftain Ōtomo no Kanamura and others brought 男大迹王*, a fifth-generation descendant of 譽田天皇* (Emperor Ōjin), from Mikuni. After declining many times, 男大迹王 received the imperial seal in the 2nd month of the 1st year and ascended the throne.
  2. Main textIn the 21st year, Iwai, Kuni-no-Miyatsuko of Tsukushi, took bribes from Silla, based himself in the two provinces of Hi and Toyo, and blocked the army of 近江毛野臣*, which was heading for Mimana. In the 11th month of the 22nd year, the Great Chieftain 物部麁鹿火* beheaded Iwai in 御井郡* in Tsukushi, and Iwai's son 筑紫君葛子* presented the 糟屋屯倉* estate in an effort to escape the death penalty.
  3. Main textIn the 6th year, in response to a request from Paekche, the four districts of Mimana — 上哆唎*, 下哆唎*, 娑陀* and 牟婁* — were given to Paekche. It is written that at this time a rumor arose that Ōtomo no Kanamura and others had received bribes from Paekche.
  4. Main textThe main text records in advance that 天國排開廣庭尊* (later Emperor Kinmei), born to the empress 手白香皇女*, was the legitimate heir but was young, and that he ruled the realm after his two elder brothers (Prince 勾大兄* and Prince 檜隈高田*) had ruled.
  5. Compared with the KojikiThe Kojiki gives the emperor's age as forty-three, while the Nihon Shoki gives it as eighty-two. Also, as to who struck down Iwai (written "竺紫君石井" in the Kojiki), the Kojiki names two men, 物部荒甲之大連* and 大伴之金村連*, while the Nihon Shoki says that 物部麁鹿火* was sent as commander-in-chief.
  6. Variant accountAs for the year of his death, the Nihon Shoki gives the 25th year (辛亥), but "a certain text" gives the 28th year (甲寅). The main text notes that it adopted the 25th year on the basis of an entry in the Kudara Hongi.

What the main text says

男大迹天皇* (also called 彦太尊*) was a fifth-generation descendant of 譽田天皇* and the son of 彦主人王*; his mother, 振媛*, was a seventh-generation descendant of 活目天皇*. His father sent messengers from his country residence at 三尾* in 高嶋郡*, Ōmi Province, to 坂中井* in Mikuni and took 振媛 as his consort, but he died while the future emperor was still young. 振媛 returned to her home of 高向* in Echizen Province and raised him. When 小泊瀬天皇* died without children in the 12th month of his 8th year, the Great Chieftain Ōtomo no Kanamura first tried to bring 倭彦王*, a fifth-generation descendant of 足仲彦天皇*, from 桑田郡* in Tanba Province; but 倭彦王, seeing the soldiers sent to escort him, became afraid, fled into the mountains, and his whereabouts became unknown.

In the 1st month of the 1st year, the Great Chieftain Ōtomo no Kanamura put forward 男大迹王*; the Great Chieftain 物部麁鹿火* and the Great Minister 許勢男人* agreed, and envoys were sent to Mikuni. 男大迹王 sat calmly on a folding chair but still had doubts and did not respond. When 河內馬飼首荒籠* secretly sent a messenger to convey the true intentions of the Great Minister, the Great Chieftains and the others, he at last set out, and he later treated 荒籠* generously. In the 2nd month, when Kanamura presented the mirror, the sword and the imperial seal, the emperor declined three times facing west and twice facing south before accepting them; he ascended the throne at the 樟葉宮* palace, and the Great Minister and Great Chieftains were kept in their former posts.

On Kanamura's advice, 手白香皇女* was made empress in the 3rd month, and she gave birth to 天國排開廣庭尊*. In the same month, citing the precedent that the sovereign himself tills the fields and the empress and consorts themselves raise silkworms, the emperor issued an edict that the realm should be told that neither officials nor common people were to neglect farming and weaving. He also took eight consorts; his first consort, 目子媛*, gave birth to Prince 勾大兄* and Prince 檜隈高田*. In the 10th month of the 2nd year, 小泊瀬稚鷦鷯天皇* was buried in the 傍丘磐杯丘陵* tomb. The capital was moved to 筒城* in Yamashiro in the 5th year, to 弟国* in the 12th year, and to 磐余玉穂* in the 20th year.

In the 6th year, Paekche asked for four districts of Mimana; 穂積臣押山*, governor of the province of 哆唎*, and Ōtomo no Kanamura agreed to this, and the four districts were given to Paekche. 物部麁鹿火*, who was to deliver the imperial command, was told by his wife of the teachings of the great deity of Sumiyoshi and of the history going back to Empress Jingū, so he claimed illness and withdrew; later Prince 大兄* tried to revoke the decision, but the Paekche envoy did not comply. In the 7th year, Paekche sent the Doctor of the Five Classics 段楊爾* and asked for the return of the land of 己汶*, which had been taken by the country of 伴跛*; in the 11th month, 己汶 and 帯沙* were given to Paekche. In the same year, Prince 勾大兄* took Princess 春日* as his consort, and in the 12th month the emperor installed 勾大兄 as Crown Prince. In the 1st month of the 8th year, because Princess 春日 lamented that she had no heir, the 匝布屯倉* estate was given to preserve her name.

In the 6th month of the 21st year, 近江毛野臣* led an army of sixty thousand and was about to head for Mimana to restore 南加羅* and 喙己呑*, which had been defeated by Silla. Iwai, Kuni-no-Miyatsuko of Tsukushi, had long been plotting rebellion; taking bribes from Silla, he based himself in the two provinces of Hi and Toyo, drew in the tribute ships of the various countries, and obstructed the army of 毛野臣*. The emperor made 物部麁鹿火* general, and in the 8th month gave him the axe of command, ordering that he himself would rule east of Nagato, while 麁鹿火* should rule west of Tsukushi. In the 11th month of the 22nd year, 麁鹿火 fought Iwai in 御井郡* in Tsukushi and beheaded him; in the 12th month, Iwai's son 筑紫君葛子* presented the 糟屋屯倉* estate and asked to atone for his death penalty.

In the 23rd year, at Paekche's request, 多沙津* in Kara was given to Paekche; Kara, resenting this, allied itself with Silla. 毛野臣* was sent to Ara and summoned the kings of Silla and Paekche to 熊川*, but the kings did not come, and a high minister of Silla came with troops and seized four villages. In the 9th month of the 24th year, envoys from Mimana complained that 毛野臣 had built a house at 久斯牟羅* and neglected his duties, and had caused many people to die by judging them through ordeal by hot water. 毛野臣 did not respond when recalled and provoked fighting with the troops of Paekche and Silla; later he was summoned, died of illness on Tsushima, and his wife composed a song.

In the 2nd month of the 24th year, citing how emperors of old had relied on wise ministers, the emperor issued an edict that people of integrity be recommended. In the 2nd month of the 25th year, the emperor fell gravely ill and died on the 丁未 day at the 磐余玉穂宮* palace. He was eighty-two years old. In the 12th month he was buried in the 藍野陵* tomb.

A plain retelling of what the main text of the Nihon Shoki says (no published translation has been used).

Variant accounts

After its main text, the Nihon Shoki often sets out other versions, introduced with words such as "a certain book says" (一書曰).

A certain text says (或本云)

The main text places the emperor's death in the 25th year (辛亥), but a certain text places it in the 28th year (甲寅). The main text notes that it adopted the 25th year because the Kudara Hongi says that in the year 辛亥 it was heard that the emperor, the crown prince and the princes of Japan had all died together.

One text says (一本云)

The main text dates the move of the capital to 磐余玉穂* to the 9th month of the 20th year, but one text places it in the 7th year.

One text says (一本云)

The main text says that 毛野臣* stayed at 熊川*, but one text says he stayed at 久斯牟羅* in Mimana. Also, for the four villages seized by the high minister of Silla, the main text gives 金官*, 背伐*, 安多* and 委陀*, while one text gives 多多羅*, 須那羅*, 和多* and 費智*.

Some say (或曰)

It adds a view that the seizure of 多々羅* and the other three villages was due to the fault of 毛野臣*.

The Kudara Hongi says (百濟本記云)

Regarding the entry on sending an envoy to Paekche in the 3rd year, the Kudara Hongi records that "久羅麻致支彌" came from Japan, but the main text notes that the details are not known.

In the original text

西向讓者三。南向讓者再

男大迹天皇* declined the throne three times facing west and twice facing south.

世云、勿論貴賤。但重其心

It is said in the world: do not ask whether a person is of high or low status, but value only his heart (words praising what 荒籠* had done).

長門以東朕制之。筑紫以西汝制之

East of Nagato I will rule. West of Tsukushi you (麁鹿火*) shall rule.

天皇崩于磐余玉穗宮。時年八十二

The emperor died at the 磐余玉穂宮* palace. He was eighty-two years old.

In the Kojiki

Kojiki, Part 3 — section on Emperor Keitai (genealogy and death)

The Kojiki gives the emperor's age as forty-three and places his tomb at 藍* in 三嶋*. The Nihon Shoki says that he died at eighty-two and was buried in the 藍野陵* tomb.

天皇御年。肆拾參歲
Kojiki, Part 3 — section on Emperor Keitai (the rebellion of 石井)

The Kojiki writes Iwai as "竺紫君石井" and says that two men, 物部荒甲之大連* and 大伴之金村連*, were sent to kill him. The Nihon Shoki writes "筑紫國造磐井" and says that 物部麁鹿火*, as commander-in-chief, beheaded him in 御井郡* in Tsukushi.

竺紫君石井。不從天皇之命而。多无禮
Kojiki, Part 3 — end of the section on Emperor Buretsu (bringing in Emperor Keitai)

The Kojiki records briefly that 袁本杼命* was brought up from the land of 近淡海* and, joined with 手白髮命*, was given the realm. The Nihon Shoki says he was raised at 高向* in Echizen and brought from Mikuni, and describes in detail how he declined and then ascended the throne.
