Nihon Shoki Archive

The Nihon Shoki, completed in 720, records Japan from the age of the gods to Empress Jitō in thirty books. Here it is divided into 50 pages. The Age of the Gods is arranged by section, with the main text and its variant accounts side by side; from Book 3 onward, each reign has its own page. Up to Empress Suiko, the pages link to the same events in the Kojiki Archive; from Emperor Jomei onward, only the Nihon Shoki records the period.

How this archive is written

A small * after a name in kanji means that its reading could not be confirmed from the sources used here, so the name is left in its original characters.

Everything here has been checked against the original text of the Nihon Shoki. Years are given as reign years, as in the original, and are not converted to the Western calendar. Where a later interpretation is given, it is marked "Commentary" and its source is a public institution's explanation.

The Age of the Gods (Books 1–2)

Nihon Shoki, Book 1 — Age of the Gods (part 1)

  1. 1The Opening of Heaven and Earth and the Seven Generations of the Age of the Gods (sections 1–3)The main text of section 1 says that out of an egg-like chaos, what was clear and light became heaven and what was heavy and turbid became earth, and that something like a reed shoot that arose between heaven and earth became a deity, and Kuni-no-Tokotachi-no-Mikoto (国常立尊) was born. Then Kuni-no-Sazuchi-no-Mikoto (国狭槌尊) and 豊斟渟尊* were born, and these three deities are described as purely male deities (純男).
  2. 2The Birth of the Land (section 4)In the main text, Izanagi-no-Mikoto (伊弉諾尊) and Izanami-no-Mikoto (伊弉冉尊) stand on the Floating Bridge of Heaven (天浮橋) and consult together, “Might there not be a land below?”; when they probe with the Jewelled Spear of Heaven (天之瓊矛), the brine that drips from the spear’s tip hardens and becomes Onogoro Island (磤馭慮島). The main text does not record any command from the heavenly deities (天神).
  3. 3The Birth of the Three Noble Children, the Birth of the Deities, and Yomi (Section 5)In the main text, after the two deities together gave birth to the sea, rivers, mountains, the ancestor of trees, and the ancestor of grasses, they sought to produce a ruler of all under heaven and gave birth to the Sun Deity (Ōhirume-no-Muchi, 大日孁貴), whom they sent up to heaven. They next sent the Moon Deity to heaven as well; Hiruko, who could not stand on its legs even at the age of three, was set adrift in a boat; and Susanoo-no-Mikoto was driven away to the Root Land (根国) for his lawlessness.
  4. 4Susanoo-no-Mikoto's Ascent to Heaven and the Pledge (Section 6)In the main text, Susanoo-no-Mikoto himself proposes the terms of the pledge, “if a female is born, an impure heart; if a male is born, a pure heart,” and Amaterasu-Ōmikami produces three goddesses from Susanoo-no-Mikoto's ten-span sword, while Susanoo-no-Mikoto produces five male deities from Amaterasu-Ōmikami's string of Yasakani jewels (八坂瓊之御統).
  5. 5The Heavenly Rock Cave (Section 7)In the main text, Susanoo-no-Mikoto sows seed over seed in Amaterasu-Ōmikami's fields, breaks down the ridges between them, defecates in the new palace at the time of the first-fruits festival (新嘗), and makes a hole in the roof of the hall of sacred garments (斎服殿) and throws in a flayed heavenly piebald colt (天斑駒). Amaterasu-Ōmikami, startled, wounds herself with a shuttle (梭, hi), and in anger shuts herself in the Heavenly Rock Cave (天石窟).
  6. 6Yamata-no-Orochi and Ōanamuchi-no-Kami (Section 8)In the main text, Susanoo-no-Mikoto meets the earthly deities Ashinazuchi (脚摩乳) and Tenazuchi (手摩乳) and their daughter Inada-hime upstream on the 簸 River* in the province of Izumo. Since their daughters had been swallowed by Yamata-no-Orochi year after year, Susanoo-no-Mikoto changed the maiden into a many-toothed comb (湯津爪櫛) and stuck it in his topknot, filled eight vats with eight-times-brewed sake (八醞酒) to make the serpent drunk, and cut it down with his ten-span sword.

Nihon Shoki, Book 2 — Age of the Gods (part 2)

  1. 7The Pacification of the Central Land of Reed Plains and the Descent of the Heavenly Grandson (Section 9)In the main text, the one who decides on sending the envoys and who covers the imperial grandson with the coverlet 眞床追衾* and sends him down is “the imperial ancestor” (皇祖) Takamimusubi-no-Mikoto; Amaterasu-Ōmikami (天照太神) appears only by name in the genealogy at the beginning. In variant account 1, it is Amaterasu-Ōmikami who both gives the command to Ame-no-Wakahiko (天稚) and has the imperial grandson descend.
  2. 8Sea-Luck and Mountain-Luck (Section 10)In the main text, the sea deity gathers the fish and questions them, and the hook is found in the mouth of 赤女 * (the name of a sea bream), which had not come because of an illness of the mouth. The sea deity instructed him to hand over the hook after secretly calling it a “poor hook” (貧鉤), gave him the tide-flowing jewel and the tide-ebbing jewel, and instructed him to drown his elder brother and to save him if he repented.
  3. 9The Birth of 神日本磐余尊* (Section 11)Ugayafukiaezu-no-Mikoto took as consort his aunt (his mother's younger sister) Tamayori-hime, and four sons were born: Itsuse-no-Mikoto, 稻飯命*, Mikeirino-no-Mikoto, and 神日本磐余尊*. In the main text, 神日本磐余尊 is the youngest child.

Emperor Jinmu to Emperor Ōjin (Books 3–10)

Nihon Shoki, Book 3 — Emperor Jinmu

  1. 10Emperor Jinmu's Eastern ExpeditionIn the year 甲寅, the emperor, aged forty-five, stated that since the descent of the heavenly ancestor they had governed this western region, but that distant lands had not yet received the blessings of royal rule and each village had its own lord or chief contending with the others, and he decided to establish his capital in the “beautiful land in the east” of which he had heard from Shiotsuchi-no-Oji (鹽土老翁).
  2. 11The Pacification of Yamato and the EnthronementIn a decree in the third month of the year 己未, the emperor declared that six years had passed since he began the eastern expedition and that the central land (中洲) was at peace, and that he would build a palace at Kashihara, southeast of Mount Unebi (畝傍山), and take the throne; he stated, “uniting the six directions, I will open a capital, and covering the eight cords (八紘), I will make them one roof (宇).”

Nihon Shoki, Book 4 — Emperor Suizei to Emperor Kaika

  1. 12From Emperor Suizei to Emperor Kaika (Including the Rebellion of 手研耳命)After the death of Emperor Jinmu, his elder half-brother 手研耳命* seized power at will during the mourning period and sought to harm his two younger brothers. 神渟名川耳尊*, together with his elder brother Kamuyaimimi-no-Mikoto (神八井耳命), attacked 手研耳命 as he slept in a chamber at 片丘*, and, taking the place of his elder brother, whose hands and feet trembled so that he could not shoot, shot him dead himself.

Nihon Shoki, Book 5 — Emperor Sujin

  1. 13Emperor Sujin and the Deity of Miwa: 大田田根子*In the 5th year an epidemic killed more than half of the people, and in the 6th year the peasants wandered from their homes and some rebelled. The emperor, in awe of the divine power of the two deities Amaterasu-Ōmikami and Yamato-Ōkunitama (倭大國魂), whom he had been worshipping side by side inside the great hall of the palace, moved them outside. He entrusted Amaterasu-Ōmikami to 豐鍬入姫命* to be worshipped at 笠縫邑* in Yamato, and entrusted Yamato-Ōkunitama-no-Kami (日本大國魂神) to 渟名城入姫命*, but 渟名城入姫命 lost her hair, grew thin, and was unable to perform the worship.
  2. 14The Generals of the Four Roads and the Rebellion of 武埴安彦*, up to Hashihaka and the Sacred Treasures of IzumoIn the 9th month of the 10th year, Ōbiko-no-Mikoto (大彦命) was sent to Hokuriku (北陸), 武渟川別* to Tōkai (東海), 吉備津彦* to the western road (西道), and 丹波道主命* to Tanba, with orders to raise troops and attack any who did not follow the teachings; they were given seals and ribbons of office and made generals.

Nihon Shoki, Book 6 — Emperor Suinin

  1. 15The Accession of Emperor Suinin, the Rebellion of 狭穂彦王, and 誉津別命 (readings unconfirmed)In the 9th month of the 4th year, 狭穂彦王* asked his younger sister the empress, "Which do you love more, your brother or your husband?" and gave her a dagger with which to stab the emperor. In the 10th month of the 5th year, the empress could not bring herself to stab the emperor as he slept with his head on her lap; her tears fell on the emperor's face, and the emperor's dream of "a small brocade-colored snake, and heavy rain from 狭穂*" led her to confess the plot.
  2. 16倭姫命* and the Worship at Ise, the Origin of Haniwa, the Sacred Treasures of Ame-no-Hiboko, and 田道間守*In the 3rd month of the 25th year, Amaterasu-Ōmikami was taken from 豊耜入姫命* and entrusted to 倭姫命*. 倭姫命 traveled through 菟田*, Ōmi and Mino and reached Ise; following the Great Deity's instruction, she built a shrine in Ise and established the abstinence palace (斎宮) by the Isuzu River.

Nihon Shoki, Book 7 — Emperor Keikō

  1. 17The Accession of Emperor Keikō and His Tour of KyūshūIn the 2nd year, the empress 播磨稲日大郎姫* bore Prince 大碓 and 小碓尊 (readings unconfirmed) as twins on a single day, and these names are said to have arisen because the emperor cried out toward a mortar (碓). 小碓尊 was also called 日本童男* and Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto (日本武尊); he was one jō (一丈) tall and strong enough to lift a cauldron (鼎).
  2. 18Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto's Campaign against the Kumaso and His Departure on the Eastern CampaignIn the 10th month of the 27th year, the sixteen-year-old Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto was sent to strike down the Kumaso. In the 12th month, he let down his hair to take the appearance of a young girl, hid a sword in his clothing, slipped into the banquet of the Kumaso chieftain 川上梟帥 (also 取石鹿文; readings unconfirmed), and stabbed the drunken 梟帥 in the chest.

Nihon Shoki, Book 7 — Emperor Keikō and Emperor Seimu

  1. 19Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto's Eastern Campaign and Death, the Last Years of Emperor Keikō, and Emperor SeimuIn Suruga, deceived by bandits who set fire to the plain, Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto escaped danger by lighting a counter-fire with a fire-striker, and named the place Yaizu (焼津). When they met a violent storm on the sea crossing from Sagami to Kazusa, his concubine 弟橘媛* entered the sea in place of the prince, the wind dropped, and the boat reached the shore.

Nihon Shoki, Book 8 — Emperor Chūai

  1. 20Emperor Chūai: the White Birds, the Campaign against the Kumaso, the Oracle, and His DeathIn the 9th month of the 8th year, when the campaign against the Kumaso was being deliberated at the 橿日宮* palace in Tsukushi, a deity possessed the empress and instructed: "The Kumaso are not a land worth attacking. If you worship me, I will make the land of Silla across the sea, rich in gold and silver, submit without fighting." The emperor did not believe these words, saying that even when he climbed a high hill he could see only the sea.

Nihon Shoki, Book 9 — Empress Jingū

  1. 21Empress Jingū's Oracle and the Expedition to SillaAt the abstinence palace (斎宮) in the village of 小山田邑*, the empress herself became the medium (神主), had Takeuchi-no-Sukune play the koto, made 中臣烏賊津使主* the interpreter of the oracle (審神者), and after seven days and seven nights drew out the names of the deities. Those who named themselves included 撞賢木厳之御魂天疎向津媛命*, who dwells at the Isuzu Shrine (五十鈴宮) in Ise, Kotoshironushi-no-Kami (事代主神), and 表筒男, 中筒男 and 底筒男 (readings unconfirmed).
  2. 22The revolt of Oshikuma-no-Miko and the years of the regency麛坂王* and Oshikuma-no-Miko said, "How can we, the elder brothers, submit to a younger brother?" and gathered troops while pretending to build the late Emperor's tomb. At a divination hunt (祈狩) on 菟餓野*, 麛坂王 was eaten by a red boar, and Oshikuma-no-Miko, taking this as an ill omen, withdrew to Sumiyoshi.

Nihon Shoki, Book 10 — Emperor Ōjin

  1. 23The birth and accession of Emperor Ōjin and the first half of his reignThe Emperor was born at 蚊田* in Tsukushi in the 12th month of the year (庚辰) in which the Empress attacked Silla. Because there was flesh on his arm shaped like an archer's wrist guard (鞆), he is said to have been called Emperor Homuta (誉田天皇), from the ancient custom of calling the wrist guard "褒武多".
  2. 24The immigrant peoples and the appointment of 菟道稚郎子* as Crown PrinceIn the 15th year the king of Paekche had 阿直岐* deliver two fine horses, and 阿直岐, who could read the classics, became tutor to the Crown Prince 菟道稚郎子*. Because 阿直岐 named 王仁* as a better scholar, 王仁 was summoned; he came in the 16th year, and the Crown Prince studied the various classics under him and mastered them all.

Emperor Nintoku to Empress Suiko (Books 11–22)

Nihon Shoki, Book 11 — Emperor Nintoku

  1. 25Emperor Nintoku, the people's cooking fires, and Empress 磐之媛*In the 2nd month of the 4th year, the Emperor looked out over the land from a high tower and, seeing that no cooking smoke was rising, realized that the people were poor; in the 3rd month he exempted them from all taxes and labor for three years. Even when the palace fences fell down and the rain leaked in, he did not repair them, and in the 4th month of the 7th year, seeing smoke filling the land, he told the Empress, "When the people are rich, I am rich."

Nihon Shoki, Book 12 — Emperor Richū and Emperor Hanzei

  1. 26Emperor Richū, Emperor Hanzei and the revolt of 住吉仲皇子*After the death of Emperor 大鷦鷯 (大鷦鷯天皇)*, when the Crown Prince was about to take 黒媛* as his consort, 住吉仲皇子*, sent as messenger, used the Crown Prince's name to sleep with 黒媛. When this was discovered from the hand bell he left behind, he raised troops, surrounded the Crown Prince's palace and burned it. The Crown Prince, helped by 平群木菟宿禰, 物部大前宿禰 and 阿知使主 (readings unconfirmed), escaped on horseback, and woke up at the 埴生坂 slope* in Kawachi to see the fire at Naniwa.

Nihon Shoki, Book 13 — Emperor Ingyō and Emperor Ankō

  1. 27Emperor Ingyō's oath ordeal and 衣通郎姫*, and the death of Emperor AnkōPrince 雄朝津間稚子宿禰 (雄朝津間稚子宿禰皇子)* kept refusing the ministers' nomination on account of a serious illness, but in the 12th month of the first year his consort 忍坂大中姫* kept waiting in the cold wind holding out water for washing his hands, until the water in the metal bowl (鋺) overflowed and froze onto her arms, and at last he ascended the throne.

Nihon Shoki, Book 14 — Emperor Yūryaku

  1. 28The accession of Emperor Yūryaku and Hitokotonushi-no-Kami (一事主神) of 葛城山*In the 8th month of the 3rd year of Emperor Ankō, 眉輪王* stabbed Emperor 穴穗 (穴穗天皇)* to death as the enemy of his father. Prince 大泊瀬* cut down Prince 八釣白彦 (八釣白彦皇子)*, burned the house of the Great Minister 圓 (圓大臣)*, who was sheltering Prince 坂合黒彦 (坂合黒彦皇子)* and 眉輪王, killing all three, and in the 10th month lured Prince 市邊押磐 (市邊押磐皇子)* on a hunt and shot him dead; then in the 11th month he ascended the throne at 泊瀬朝倉*.
  2. 29Emperor Yūryaku's expeditions to Silla, the craftsmen of Wu, and his testamentary edictIn the 8th year, Silla, attacked by Koguryŏ (高麗), asked for help through the king of Mimana, and 膳臣斑鳩, 吉備臣小梨 and 難波吉士赤目子 (readings unconfirmed) defeated the Koguryŏ army with a stratagem of tunnels and ambushes. In the 9th year the Emperor intended to attack Silla himself, but was warned by a deity, "Do not go", and sent 紀小弓宿禰* and others as commanders. 小弓 died of illness, and the remaining commanders quarreled among themselves and turned back without reaching the royal palace of Paekche.

Nihon Shoki, Book 15 — Emperor Seinei, Emperor Kenzō and Emperor Ninken

  1. 30Emperors Seinei, Kenzō and Ninken, and the brothers 億計 and 弘計 (readings unconfirmed)In the 11th month of the 2nd year of Emperor Seinei, 來目部小楯*, governor of Harima (播磨國司), discovered the brothers 億計 and 弘計 (readings unconfirmed), sons of Prince 市邊押磐 (市邊押磐皇子)*, at a banquet for the new house of the head of the 縮見 granary (縮見屯倉首)* in 赤石郡*. Emperor 白髪 (白髪天皇)*, who had no children, rejoiced at this, and in the 3rd year welcomed the two to the palace, making Prince 億計 Crown Prince and Prince 弘計 an imperial prince.

Nihon Shoki, Book 16 — Emperor Buretsu

  1. 31Emperor Buretsu and the Fall of the Heguri ClanThe Nihon Shoki describes this emperor as one who was versed in punishments and trials and made the laws clear, yet who also "repeatedly did evil and did not do a single good thing"; it writes that he watched cruel punishments in person, so that everyone in the country trembled with fear.

Nihon Shoki, Book 17 — Emperor Keitai

  1. 32The Enthronement of Emperor Keitai and the Rebellion of Iwai, Kuni-no-Miyatsuko of TsukushiWhen 小泊瀬天皇* died without any children, the Great Chieftain Ōtomo no Kanamura and others brought 男大迹王*, a fifth-generation descendant of 譽田天皇* (Emperor Ōjin), from Mikuni. After declining many times, 男大迹王 received the imperial seal in the 2nd month of the 1st year and ascended the throne.

Nihon Shoki, Book 18 — Emperor Ankan and Emperor Senka

  1. 33Emperor Ankan, Emperor Senka and the Establishment of Miyake EstatesBecause Emperor Ankan had no children even after taking four wives, on the advice of Ōtomo no Kanamura he established miyake estates (小墾田屯倉*, 櫻井屯倉* and 難波屯倉*) to preserve the names of his empress and consorts for later generations.

Nihon Shoki, Book 19 — Emperor Kinmei (part 1)

  1. 34The Accession of Emperor Kinmei, the Talks on Restoring Mimana, and the Presentation of a Buddha ImageIn the 10th month of the 13th year, King Sŏngmyŏng of Paekche presented one gilt-bronze image of the Buddha Śākyamuni, together with banners and canopies and sutras and treatises, and submitted a memorial saying that this Dharma was excellent. The emperor was pleased but did not decide for himself; he asked the assembled ministers. Soga no Iname said it should be worshiped, while Mononobe no Okoshi and Nakatomi no Kamako opposed it, saying it would bring on the anger of the gods of the land.
  2. 35The Death of King Sŏngmyŏng in Battle and the Fall of MimanaIn the 12th month of the 15th year, when 餘昌*, a prince of Paekche, built the fort of 久陀牟羅塞* in Silla territory, his father King Sŏngmyŏng set out to reward his son for his labors, but his road was cut off by the Silla army and he was beheaded by 苦都*, a slave who kept horses in the village of 佐知村*.

Nihon Shoki, Book 20 — Emperor Bidatsu

  1. 36Emperor Bidatsu, the Koguryŏ Letter, and the Beginnings of the Buddhist DharmaAn entry before his accession says that the emperor did not believe in the Buddhist Dharma and loved literature and history. The conflict over the Buddhist Dharma in this book takes place in this emperor's reign.

Nihon Shoki, Book 21 — Emperor Yōmei and Emperor Sushun

  1. 37Emperor Yōmei, Emperor Sushun and the Fall of Mononobe no MoriyaIn the 4th month of the 2nd year, Emperor Yōmei, having fallen ill, consulted his ministers, saying, "I wish to take refuge in the Three Treasures." Moriya and Nakatomi no Katsumi objected, asking whether he would turn his back on the gods of the land and revere other gods, while Umako answered that they should follow the edict and assist him.

Nihon Shoki, Book 22 — Empress Suiko (part 1)

  1. 38The Accession of Empress Suiko, the Twelve Cap Ranks and the Seventeen-Article ConstitutionIn the 4th month of the 1st year, Prince Umayado no Toyotomimi was made Crown Prince and, as regent, was entrusted with all affairs of state. It is written that the prince was born upon coming up against the door of a stable of the horse office, and that he could hear the petitions of ten people at once without error.
  2. 39Empress Suiko, the Envoys to the Great Tang, and the Death of the Crown Prince of the Upper PalaceIn the 7th month of the 15th year, Ono no Omi Imoko, of the rank Greater Propriety, was sent to the Great Tang, and in the 4th month of the 16th year the Tang envoy Pei Shiqing came to Tsukushi together with Imoko. Imoko reported that the letter of the Tang emperor had been stolen from him in Paekche; the ministers said he should be exiled, but the empress pardoned him.

Emperor Jomei to Empress Jitō (Books 23–30)

Nihon Shoki, Book 23 — Emperor Jomei

  1. 40Emperor Jomei — The Ministers’ Debate over the Succession, and the Great Palace and Great Temple of KudaraIn the 36th year of Empress Suiko the empress died, but no successor had been decided. Soga no Emishi gathered the ministers at his house, presented her last words, and asked whether Prince Tamura or Prince Yamashiro no Ōe should be enthroned. The ministers were divided between those who backed Prince Tamura and those who backed Prince Yamashiro no Ōe.

Nihon Shoki, Book 24 — Empress Kōgyoku

  1. 41Empress Kōgyoku — The Fall of the Royal House of the Upper Palace and the Killing of Soga no IrukaIn the eleventh month of the 2nd year, Soga no Iruka sent troops to attack Prince Yamashiro no Ōe and others at Ikaruga. The prince rejected a plan to raise troops in the eastern provinces, saying he did not want the people to suffer for his sake alone, and took his own life at Ikaruga-dera (斑鳩寺) together with his consorts, children and younger kinsmen.

Nihon Shoki, Book 25 — Emperor Kōtoku

  1. 42Emperor Kōtoku’s Accession and the Taika Reform EdictIn the first month of the 2nd year of Taika the Reform Edict was proclaimed, setting out four articles: abolishing the 子代 people, the 屯倉 granaries, the 部曲 people and the 田荘 estates, and granting sustenance fiefs (食封) instead; establishing the capital, the Home Provinces (畿内), provincial governors, district officials, post horses and so on; beginning household registers, tax registers and the law for allotting and reclaiming rice land (班田収授); and abolishing the old labor levies and introducing a levy on rice land (田之調).
  2. 43Emperor Kōtoku — The Cap-Rank System, the Death of Ishikawa no Maro, the Hakuchi Era and the Naniwa PalaceIn the third month of the 5th year of Taika, 蘇我日向* falsely told the crown prince that his half-brother, the Minister of the Right Soga no Kurayamada no Ishikawa no Maro, harbored rebellious intentions, and the minister took his own life at Yamada-dera (山田寺) after swearing that he “would not resent his lord.” Later, among his property were found items labeled “the crown prince’s documents” and “the crown prince’s things,” and the crown prince, realizing that the minister’s heart had been pure, regretted what had happened.

Nihon Shoki, Book 26 — Empress Saimei

  1. 44Empress Saimei — Her Second Reign, Prince Arima, and the Western Expedition to Aid PaekcheEmpress Saimei (天豐財重日足姬天皇) yielded the throne to Emperor Kōtoku (天萬豐日天皇) in the 4th year of her reign (改元四年) and was called “皇祖母尊”; after his death she took the throne again at the Itabuki Palace in Asuka in the first month of the 1st year.

Nihon Shoki, Book 27 — Emperor Tenji

  1. 45Emperor Tenji — Hakusukinoe and the Move of the Capital to ŌmiIn the eighth month of the 2nd year, the Japanese (日本) fleet fought the Tang fleet at Hakusukinoe; attacking without regard to the conditions, it was caught from left and right, many drowned, and it was defeated. King 豐璋* of Paekche fled to Koguryŏ, and in the ninth month the fortress of 州柔 (州柔城)* surrendered to Tang.

Nihon Shoki, Book 28 — Emperor Tenmu (part 1)

  1. 46Prince Ōama’s Retreat to Yoshino and the Jinshin WarThe Crown Prince (Prince Ōama), whom Emperor Tenji (天命開別天皇) on his sickbed asked to take charge of affairs after his death, declined, asking that the realm be entrusted to the empress and that Prince Ōtomo be made heir; that same day he took holy orders, handed over his private weapons to the government, and entered Yoshino.

Nihon Shoki, Book 29 — Emperor Tenmu (part 2)

  1. 47Emperor Tenmu’s Accession and the Yoshino CovenantIn the second month of the 2nd year, the emperor had officials set up an altar platform (壇場) and took the throne at the Asuka Kiyomihara Palace. He made his principal consort empress; the text records that the empress bore Prince Kusakabe (草壁皇子尊), and then lists his consorts and wives and their princes and princesses.
  2. 48Emperor Tenmu — Compiling the Codes and the Imperial Chronicles, and His DeathIn the second month of the 10th year, the emperor and empress, in the Great Audience Hall (大極殿), issued an edict to the imperial princes, princes and ministers declaring that codes (律令) would be established and the laws and regulations revised. On the same day Prince Kusakabe (草壁皇子尊) was made crown prince and put in charge of government affairs.

Nihon Shoki, Book 30 — Empress Jitō

  1. 49Empress Jitō — Rule before Enthronement, and AccessionWhen Emperor Tenmu (天渟中原瀛真人天皇) died in the ninth month of the 1st year of Shuchō, the empress came to court and took charge of government (臨朝称制). In the first month of the 4th year, 物部麻呂朝臣* set up the great shield, 中臣大嶋朝臣* read the words of blessing of the heavenly deities (天神寿詞), and 忌部宿禰色夫知* presented the sacred sword and mirror (神璽), and the empress took the imperial throne.
  2. 50Empress Jitō — The Fujiwara Palace and Her AbdicationIn the second month of the 6th year, when the empress announced that she would make an excursion to Ise in the third month, the Middle Counselor (中納言) 三輪朝臣高市麻呂* remonstrated that it would interfere with farm work. In the third month he remonstrated again, taking off his cap of rank and offering it up to the court, but the empress did not listen and went to Ise.

Key points of every page

For review: the first two key points of each page.

Nihon Shoki, Book 1 — Age of the Gods (part 1): The Opening of Heaven and Earth and the Seven Generations of the Age of the Gods (sections 1–3)

  • The main text of section 1 says that out of an egg-like chaos, what was clear and light became heaven and what was heavy and turbid became earth, and that something like a reed shoot that arose between heaven and earth became a deity, and Kuni-no-Tokotachi-no-Mikoto (国常立尊) was born. Then Kuni-no-Sazuchi-no-Mikoto (国狭槌尊) and 豊斟渟尊* were born, and these three deities are described as purely male deities (純男).
  • In the main text of section 2, four pairs of male and female deities appear (埿土煑尊 and 沙土煑尊, 大戸之道尊 and 大苫辺尊, 面足尊 and 惶根尊 (readings unconfirmed), and Izanagi-no-Mikoto (伊弉諾尊) and Izanami-no-Mikoto (伊弉冉尊)), and the main text of section 3 sums them up as “eight deities”. The generations from Kuni-no-Tokotachi-no-Mikoto to Izanagi-no-Mikoto and Izanami-no-Mikoto are called the Seven Generations of the Age of the Gods (神世七代).

Nihon Shoki, Book 1 — Age of the Gods (part 1): The Birth of the Land (section 4)

  • In the main text, Izanagi-no-Mikoto (伊弉諾尊) and Izanami-no-Mikoto (伊弉冉尊) stand on the Floating Bridge of Heaven (天浮橋) and consult together, “Might there not be a land below?”; when they probe with the Jewelled Spear of Heaven (天之瓊矛), the brine that drips from the spear’s tip hardens and becomes Onogoro Island (磤馭慮島). The main text does not record any command from the heavenly deities (天神).
  • When the two deities went round the island, taken as the central pillar of the land, in opposite directions, the female deity (陰神) spoke first, so the male deity (陽神), saying that the man ought to speak first, had them go round again; the second time the male deity spoke first, and they became husband and wife.

Nihon Shoki, Book 1 — Age of the Gods (part 1): The Birth of the Three Noble Children, the Birth of the Deities, and Yomi (Section 5)

  • In the main text, after the two deities together gave birth to the sea, rivers, mountains, the ancestor of trees, and the ancestor of grasses, they sought to produce a ruler of all under heaven and gave birth to the Sun Deity (Ōhirume-no-Muchi, 大日孁貴), whom they sent up to heaven. They next sent the Moon Deity to heaven as well; Hiruko, who could not stand on its legs even at the age of three, was set adrift in a boat; and Susanoo-no-Mikoto was driven away to the Root Land (根国) for his lawlessness.
  • The main text of section 5 does not record the birth of the fire deity or the death of Izanami-no-Mikoto. The story of Izanami-no-Mikoto dying after giving birth to the fire deity (Kagutsuchi, 軻遇突智; Homusubi, 火産霊) is transmitted in variant accounts 2, 3, 5, and 6, each in a slightly different form, and variant account 4 relates that Kanayamahiko and others were born from her suffering in giving birth to the fire deity.

Nihon Shoki, Book 1 — Age of the Gods (part 1): Susanoo-no-Mikoto's Ascent to Heaven and the Pledge (Section 6)

  • In the main text, Susanoo-no-Mikoto himself proposes the terms of the pledge, “if a female is born, an impure heart; if a male is born, a pure heart,” and Amaterasu-Ōmikami produces three goddesses from Susanoo-no-Mikoto's ten-span sword, while Susanoo-no-Mikoto produces five male deities from Amaterasu-Ōmikami's string of Yasakani jewels (八坂瓊之御統).
  • The children's affiliation is determined by the owner of the “original object” (物根). Since the jewels were Amaterasu-Ōmikami's, the five male deities are regarded as Amaterasu-Ōmikami's children, and since the sword was Susanoo-no-Mikoto's, the three goddesses are regarded as Susanoo-no-Mikoto's children; the three goddesses are recorded as the deities worshipped by the 胸肩君* and others of Tsukushi.

Nihon Shoki, Book 1 — Age of the Gods (part 1): The Heavenly Rock Cave (Section 7)

  • In the main text, Susanoo-no-Mikoto sows seed over seed in Amaterasu-Ōmikami's fields, breaks down the ridges between them, defecates in the new palace at the time of the first-fruits festival (新嘗), and makes a hole in the roof of the hall of sacred garments (斎服殿) and throws in a flayed heavenly piebald colt (天斑駒). Amaterasu-Ōmikami, startled, wounds herself with a shuttle (梭, hi), and in anger shuts herself in the Heavenly Rock Cave (天石窟).
  • When Amaterasu-Ōmikami closes the rock door, everything “within the six directions” (六合之内) is always dark, and there is no longer any distinction between day and night. The deities gather on the bank of the 天安河*; Omoikane-no-Kami has the long-crying birds cry, Ame-no-Koyane-no-Mikoto and Futodama-no-Mikoto (太玉命) hang a jewel string (御統), the Yata mirror (八咫鏡), and nikite offerings (和幣) on a sakaki tree (真坂樹) of Mount Ame-no-Kagoyama (天香山) and pray, and Ame-no-Uzume-no-Mikoto performs (wazaoki, 俳優) before the door.

Nihon Shoki, Book 1 — Age of the Gods (part 1): Yamata-no-Orochi and Ōanamuchi-no-Kami (Section 8)

  • In the main text, Susanoo-no-Mikoto meets the earthly deities Ashinazuchi (脚摩乳) and Tenazuchi (手摩乳) and their daughter Inada-hime upstream on the 簸 River* in the province of Izumo. Since their daughters had been swallowed by Yamata-no-Orochi year after year, Susanoo-no-Mikoto changed the maiden into a many-toothed comb (湯津爪櫛) and stuck it in his topknot, filled eight vats with eight-times-brewed sake (八醞酒) to make the serpent drunk, and cut it down with his ten-span sword.
  • When he cut the serpent's tail, the edge of his sword was slightly chipped, and when he split the tail open there was a sword inside. This is the Kusanagi Sword (草薙剣), and a writing quoted in a small note records that its original name was 天叢雲剣* and that it was renamed the Kusanagi Sword in the time of Prince Yamato Takeru (日本武皇子). Susanoo-no-Mikoto said, “I cannot make this my own,” and presented it to the heavenly deities.

Nihon Shoki, Book 2 — Age of the Gods (part 2): The Pacification of the Central Land of Reed Plains and the Descent of the Heavenly Grandson (Section 9)

  • In the main text, the one who decides on sending the envoys and who covers the imperial grandson with the coverlet 眞床追衾* and sends him down is “the imperial ancestor” (皇祖) Takamimusubi-no-Mikoto; Amaterasu-Ōmikami (天照太神) appears only by name in the genealogy at the beginning. In variant account 1, it is Amaterasu-Ōmikami who both gives the command to Ame-no-Wakahiko (天稚) and has the imperial grandson descend.
  • The envoys of the main text, Ame-no-Hohi-no-Mikoto, his son 大背飯三熊之大人*, and Ame-no-Wakahiko, in that order, all fail to report back, and finally Futsunushi-no-Kami and Takemikazuchi-no-Kami descend to the small beach of 五十田狹* in Izumo. Ōanamuchi-no-Kami, receiving the reply of his son Kotoshironushi-no-Kami, withdraws from the land, hands the two deities the broad spear (廣矛) he used when he pacified the land, and goes into hiding.

Nihon Shoki, Book 2 — Age of the Gods (part 2): Sea-Luck and Mountain-Luck (Section 10)

  • In the main text, the sea deity gathers the fish and questions them, and the hook is found in the mouth of 赤女 * (the name of a sea bream), which had not come because of an illness of the mouth. The sea deity instructed him to hand over the hook after secretly calling it a “poor hook” (貧鉤), gave him the tide-flowing jewel and the tide-ebbing jewel, and instructed him to drown his elder brother and to save him if he repented.
  • The elder brother Honosusori-no-Mikoto, in distress, admitted his offense, vowed to become “a people of performers” (俳優之民), and was pardoned; he is regarded as the original ancestor of the 吾田君小橋* and others. In variant account 2 this is told as the origin of his descendants the Hayato serving beside the palace fence by barking in place of dogs, and in variant account 4 as the origin of a performance imitating the elder brother's drowning, which has not died out even today.

Nihon Shoki, Book 2 — Age of the Gods (part 2): The Birth of 神日本磐余尊* (Section 11)

  • Ugayafukiaezu-no-Mikoto took as consort his aunt (his mother's younger sister) Tamayori-hime, and four sons were born: Itsuse-no-Mikoto, 稻飯命*, Mikeirino-no-Mikoto, and 神日本磐余尊*. In the main text, 神日本磐余尊 is the youngest child.
  • The main text records that Ugayafukiaezu-no-Mikoto died at the 西洲之宮* palace and was buried in the 吾平山上陵* mausoleum in Hyūga.

Nihon Shoki, Book 3 — Emperor Jinmu: Emperor Jinmu's Eastern Expedition

  • In the year 甲寅, the emperor, aged forty-five, stated that since the descent of the heavenly ancestor they had governed this western region, but that distant lands had not yet received the blessings of royal rule and each village had its own lord or chief contending with the others, and he decided to establish his capital in the “beautiful land in the east” of which he had heard from Shiotsuchi-no-Oji (鹽土老翁).
  • When he was defeated by Nagasunebiko at 孔舍衞坂* and Itsuse-no-Mikoto was wounded by an arrow, the emperor, thinking that “for a descendant of the Sun Deity to attack facing the sun is to go against the way of heaven,” withdrew and changed his plan to attacking with the sun at his back. Itsuse-no-Mikoto died at 竃山* in the province of Kii.

Nihon Shoki, Book 3 — Emperor Jinmu: The Pacification of Yamato and the Enthronement

  • In a decree in the third month of the year 己未, the emperor declared that six years had passed since he began the eastern expedition and that the central land (中洲) was at peace, and that he would build a palace at Kashihara, southeast of Mount Unebi (畝傍山), and take the throne; he stated, “uniting the six directions, I will open a capital, and covering the eight cords (八紘), I will make them one roof (宇).”
  • On the first day (庚辰) of the first month of spring in the year 辛酉, the emperor ascended the imperial throne at the Kashihara palace, and this year was made the first year of the emperor. He made Himetatara-isuzu-hime-no-Mikoto (媛蹈韛五十鈴媛命), who had been recommended as the child of Kotoshironushi-no-Kami and 玉櫛媛*, his empress, and 神八井命 and 神渟名川耳尊 (readings unconfirmed) were born.

Nihon Shoki, Book 4 — Emperor Suizei to Emperor Kaika: From Emperor Suizei to Emperor Kaika (Including the Rebellion of 手研耳命)

  • After the death of Emperor Jinmu, his elder half-brother 手研耳命* seized power at will during the mourning period and sought to harm his two younger brothers. 神渟名川耳尊*, together with his elder brother Kamuyaimimi-no-Mikoto (神八井耳命), attacked 手研耳命 as he slept in a chamber at 片丘*, and, taking the place of his elder brother, whose hands and feet trembled so that he could not shoot, shot him dead himself.
  • Kamuyaimimi-no-Mikoto, saying, “Though I am the elder brother, I was cowardly and could not carry the matter through,” yielded the heavenly throne to his younger brother, and stated that he himself would take the role of worshipping the deities of heaven and earth to help his younger brother. Kamuyaimimi-no-Mikoto is regarded as the first ancestor of the 多臣*.

Nihon Shoki, Book 5 — Emperor Sujin: Emperor Sujin and the Deity of Miwa: 大田田根子*

  • In the 5th year an epidemic killed more than half of the people, and in the 6th year the peasants wandered from their homes and some rebelled. The emperor, in awe of the divine power of the two deities Amaterasu-Ōmikami and Yamato-Ōkunitama (倭大國魂), whom he had been worshipping side by side inside the great hall of the palace, moved them outside. He entrusted Amaterasu-Ōmikami to 豐鍬入姫命* to be worshipped at 笠縫邑* in Yamato, and entrusted Yamato-Ōkunitama-no-Kami (日本大國魂神) to 渟名城入姫命*, but 渟名城入姫命 lost her hair, grew thin, and was unable to perform the worship.
  • In the 2nd month of the 7th year, the emperor gathered the eighty myriad deities at 神淺茅原* and performed divination, and Ōmononushi-no-Kami possessed Yamato-totohi-momoso-hime-no-Mikoto and declared that he should be worshipped. When this still had no effect, the emperor purified himself and prayed; Ōmononushi-no-Kami then appeared in a dream and told him that if he had his son 大田田根子* worship him, the land would at once be at peace and the countries beyond the sea would submit of their own accord.

Nihon Shoki, Book 5 — Emperor Sujin: The Generals of the Four Roads and the Rebellion of 武埴安彦*, up to Hashihaka and the Sacred Treasures of Izumo

  • In the 9th month of the 10th year, Ōbiko-no-Mikoto (大彦命) was sent to Hokuriku (北陸), 武渟川別* to Tōkai (東海), 吉備津彦* to the western road (西道), and 丹波道主命* to Tanba, with orders to raise troops and attack any who did not follow the teachings; they were given seals and ribbons of office and made generals.
  • Yamato-totohi-momoso-hime-no-Mikoto saw that the song of a girl heard by Ōbiko-no-Mikoto at 和珥坂* was a sign of rebellion by 武埴安彦*, and also told how his wife 吾田媛* had taken earth from Mount Kagu (香山) in Yamato and prayed, saying "倭國之物實". The rebellion was put down when 五十狹芹彦命* struck down 吾田媛 at Ōsaka (大坂), and 彦國葺* shot 武埴安彦 dead in Yamashiro (山背).

Nihon Shoki, Book 6 — Emperor Suinin: The Accession of Emperor Suinin, the Rebellion of 狭穂彦王, and 誉津別命 (readings unconfirmed)

  • In the 9th month of the 4th year, 狭穂彦王* asked his younger sister the empress, "Which do you love more, your brother or your husband?" and gave her a dagger with which to stab the emperor. In the 10th month of the 5th year, the empress could not bring herself to stab the emperor as he slept with his head on her lap; her tears fell on the emperor's face, and the emperor's dream of "a small brocade-colored snake, and heavy rain from 狭穂*" led her to confess the plot.
  • 狭穂彦王* piled up rice to make a "rice-stack fortress" (稲城) and resisted. The empress entered the fortress holding the prince 誉津別命*, but when it was attacked with fire she sent the prince out of the fortress, asked that the women's quarters of the palace be entrusted to the five daughters of 丹波道主王*, and died in the fortress together with her brother.

Nihon Shoki, Book 6 — Emperor Suinin: 倭姫命* and the Worship at Ise, the Origin of Haniwa, the Sacred Treasures of Ame-no-Hiboko, and 田道間守*

  • In the 3rd month of the 25th year, Amaterasu-Ōmikami was taken from 豊耜入姫命* and entrusted to 倭姫命*. 倭姫命 traveled through 菟田*, Ōmi and Mino and reached Ise; following the Great Deity's instruction, she built a shrine in Ise and established the abstinence palace (斎宮) by the Isuzu River.
  • In the 28th year, when attendants were buried alive at the tomb of the emperor's younger brother by the same mother, 倭彦命*, they wept and cried out for several days and then died. The emperor grieved over this and ordered that the practice of following the dead in death be stopped.

Nihon Shoki, Book 7 — Emperor Keikō: The Accession of Emperor Keikō and His Tour of Kyūshū

  • In the 2nd year, the empress 播磨稲日大郎姫* bore Prince 大碓 and 小碓尊 (readings unconfirmed) as twins on a single day, and these names are said to have arisen because the emperor cried out toward a mortar (碓). 小碓尊 was also called 日本童男* and Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto (日本武尊); he was one jō (一丈) tall and strong enough to lift a cauldron (鼎).
  • In the 12th year, because the Kumaso neglected their tribute, the emperor set out for Tsukushi. The text records that at 娑麼 in 周芳 (readings unconfirmed), a female chieftain, 神夏磯媛*, hung a sword, a mirror and a jewel (瓊) on a sakaki tree (賢木), raised a white flag and submitted, and that at 碩田* in Bungo the tsuchigumo were struck down with mallets made of camellia wood (海石榴), giving rise to the place names 海石榴市 and 血田 (readings unconfirmed).

Nihon Shoki, Book 7 — Emperor Keikō: Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto's Campaign against the Kumaso and His Departure on the Eastern Campaign

  • In the 10th month of the 27th year, the sixteen-year-old Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto was sent to strike down the Kumaso. In the 12th month, he let down his hair to take the appearance of a young girl, hid a sword in his clothing, slipped into the banquet of the Kumaso chieftain 川上梟帥 (also 取石鹿文; readings unconfirmed), and stabbed the drunken 梟帥 in the chest.
  • The dying 川上梟帥*, saying that he had never met anyone of such martial power, presented the name "日本武皇子" (Prince Yamato Takeru), and it is said that from then on the prince came to be called Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto.

Nihon Shoki, Book 7 — Emperor Keikō and Emperor Seimu: Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto's Eastern Campaign and Death, the Last Years of Emperor Keikō, and Emperor Seimu

  • In Suruga, deceived by bandits who set fire to the plain, Yamato Takeru-no-Mikoto escaped danger by lighting a counter-fire with a fire-striker, and named the place Yaizu (焼津). When they met a violent storm on the sea crossing from Sagami to Kazusa, his concubine 弟橘媛* entered the sea in place of the prince, the wind dropped, and the boat reached the shore.
  • In Owari, the prince married Miyazu-hime (宮簀媛), left his sword at Miyazu-hime's house, and went to strike down the wild deity of Mount Ibuki (膽吹山); but, taking the mountain deity, who had become a great serpent, for a messenger, he stepped over it and went on, was led astray by clouds and icy rain, and fell ill. He died at the age of thirty at 能褒野*, became a white bird and flew to 琴弾原* in Yamato and to the village of 旧市邑* in Kawachi, and the three tombs were called the White Bird Tombs (白鳥陵).

Nihon Shoki, Book 8 — Emperor Chūai: Emperor Chūai: the White Birds, the Campaign against the Kumaso, the Oracle, and His Death

  • In the 9th month of the 8th year, when the campaign against the Kumaso was being deliberated at the 橿日宮* palace in Tsukushi, a deity possessed the empress and instructed: "The Kumaso are not a land worth attacking. If you worship me, I will make the land of Silla across the sea, rich in gold and silver, submit without fighting." The emperor did not believe these words, saying that even when he climbed a high hill he could see only the sea.
  • Still not believing, the emperor attacked the Kumaso but could not win, and in the 2nd month of the 9th year he suddenly felt pain in his body and died the next day (aged fifty-two). The main text states that "he died early because he did not heed the words of the deity," while one account (一云) in a small note says that he went himself to strike down the Kumaso, was hit by a bandit's arrow, and died.

Nihon Shoki, Book 9 — Empress Jingū: Empress Jingū's Oracle and the Expedition to Silla

  • At the abstinence palace (斎宮) in the village of 小山田邑*, the empress herself became the medium (神主), had Takeuchi-no-Sukune play the koto, made 中臣烏賊津使主* the interpreter of the oracle (審神者), and after seven days and seven nights drew out the names of the deities. Those who named themselves included 撞賢木厳之御魂天疎向津媛命*, who dwells at the Isuzu Shrine (五十鈴宮) in Ise, Kotoshironushi-no-Kami (事代主神), and 表筒男, 中筒男 and 底筒男 (readings unconfirmed).
  • At the bay of 橿日浦*, the empress washed her hair in the sea and confirmed the deities' will when it parted in two; saying, "For a while I will borrow the form of a man," she announced the western campaign to her ministers. Before departure, since she had reached the month of childbirth, she tucked a stone at her waist and prayed that she would give birth in this land after she returned.

Nihon Shoki, Book 9 — Empress Jingū: The revolt of Oshikuma-no-Miko and the years of the regency

  • 麛坂王* and Oshikuma-no-Miko said, "How can we, the elder brothers, submit to a younger brother?" and gathered troops while pretending to build the late Emperor's tomb. At a divination hunt (祈狩) on 菟餓野*, 麛坂王 was eaten by a red boar, and Oshikuma-no-Miko, taking this as an ill omen, withdrew to Sumiyoshi.
  • Takeuchi-no-Sukune had his soldiers hide spare bowstrings in their hair and, claiming to act on the Empress's command, proposed a truce. He made Oshikuma-no-Miko's army cut their bowstrings and throw their swords into the river, then attacked, defeating them at 逢坂* and in the chestnut grove of 狭々浪*. Oshikuma-no-Miko exchanged songs with 五十狭茅宿禰*, and the two sank together at the crossing of 瀬田* and died.

Nihon Shoki, Book 10 — Emperor Ōjin: The birth and accession of Emperor Ōjin and the first half of his reign

  • The Emperor was born at 蚊田* in Tsukushi in the 12th month of the year (庚辰) in which the Empress attacked Silla. Because there was flesh on his arm shaped like an archer's wrist guard (鞆), he is said to have been called Emperor Homuta (誉田天皇), from the ancient custom of calling the wrist guard "褒武多".
  • In the 9th year, the younger brother 甘美内宿禰* slandered Takeuchi-no-Sukune, saying, "Takeuchi-no-Sukune intends to split off Tsukushi and take the realm." 真根子*, ancestor of the 壱伎直*, who resembled Takeuchi-no-Sukune, killed himself in his place. Takeuchi-no-Sukune pleaded his innocence at court, and by the Emperor's command the two underwent the ordeal by boiling water (探湯) beside the 磯城 river*; Takeuchi-no-Sukune won.

Nihon Shoki, Book 10 — Emperor Ōjin: The immigrant peoples and the appointment of 菟道稚郎子* as Crown Prince

  • In the 15th year the king of Paekche had 阿直岐* deliver two fine horses, and 阿直岐, who could read the classics, became tutor to the Crown Prince 菟道稚郎子*. Because 阿直岐 named 王仁* as a better scholar, 王仁 was summoned; he came in the 16th year, and the Crown Prince studied the various classics under him and mastered them all.
  • In the 14th year 弓月君* came from Paekche and reported that the laborers of 120 districts he had brought were being held back by Silla and remained in Kara (加羅). 葛城襲津彦* did not return even after three years; in the 16th year, when 木菟宿禰* and others advanced troops to the border of Silla, the laborers of 弓月 came together with 襲津彦. In the 20th year 阿知使主*, ancestor of the 倭漢直*, and others came leading their people of seventeen districts.

Nihon Shoki, Book 11 — Emperor Nintoku: Emperor Nintoku, the people's cooking fires, and Empress 磐之媛*

  • In the 2nd month of the 4th year, the Emperor looked out over the land from a high tower and, seeing that no cooking smoke was rising, realized that the people were poor; in the 3rd month he exempted them from all taxes and labor for three years. Even when the palace fences fell down and the rain leaked in, he did not repair them, and in the 4th month of the 7th year, seeing smoke filling the land, he told the Empress, "When the people are rich, I am rich."
  • In the 9th year, although the provinces asked to pay taxes and repair the palace, the Emperor did not allow it. When he reimposed taxes and labor in the 10th month of the 10th year, the people, helping the old and bringing their small children, carried timber of their own accord, and the palace buildings were soon completed. The original text records that for this reason "he is called the sage emperor (聖帝) to this day".

Nihon Shoki, Book 12 — Emperor Richū and Emperor Hanzei: Emperor Richū, Emperor Hanzei and the revolt of 住吉仲皇子*

  • After the death of Emperor 大鷦鷯 (大鷦鷯天皇)*, when the Crown Prince was about to take 黒媛* as his consort, 住吉仲皇子*, sent as messenger, used the Crown Prince's name to sleep with 黒媛. When this was discovered from the hand bell he left behind, he raised troops, surrounded the Crown Prince's palace and burned it. The Crown Prince, helped by 平群木菟宿禰, 物部大前宿禰 and 阿知使主 (readings unconfirmed), escaped on horseback, and woke up at the 埴生坂 slope* in Kawachi to see the fire at Naniwa.
  • The Crown Prince, having entered the 石上振神宮 shrine*, suspected even his younger brother 瑞齒別皇子* and ordered him to go back to Naniwa, kill 仲皇子*, and only then come to him. 瑞齒別皇子 had 刺領巾*, a Hayato attendant of 仲皇子, stab 仲皇子 in the privy, but 木菟宿禰*, saying that one who had killed his own lord could not be allowed to live, killed 刺領巾.

Nihon Shoki, Book 13 — Emperor Ingyō and Emperor Ankō: Emperor Ingyō's oath ordeal and 衣通郎姫*, and the death of Emperor Ankō

  • Prince 雄朝津間稚子宿禰 (雄朝津間稚子宿禰皇子)* kept refusing the ministers' nomination on account of a serious illness, but in the 12th month of the first year his consort 忍坂大中姫* kept waiting in the cold wind holding out water for washing his hands, until the water in the metal bowl (鋺) overflowed and froze onto her arms, and at last he ascended the throne.
  • In the 4th year, saying that clan names and titles had fallen into confusion and that some people were making false claims, the Emperor had people undergo the oath ordeal by boiling water (盟神探湯; a reading note gives "區訶陀智") at the 味橿 hill*. They wore sashes of bark-cloth (木綿) and were made to reach into the boiling water in cauldrons; those who told the truth were unharmed and those who lied were all injured, and the text records that from then on clan names and titles were settled.

Nihon Shoki, Book 14 — Emperor Yūryaku: The accession of Emperor Yūryaku and Hitokotonushi-no-Kami (一事主神) of 葛城山*

  • In the 8th month of the 3rd year of Emperor Ankō, 眉輪王* stabbed Emperor 穴穗 (穴穗天皇)* to death as the enemy of his father. Prince 大泊瀬* cut down Prince 八釣白彦 (八釣白彦皇子)*, burned the house of the Great Minister 圓 (圓大臣)*, who was sheltering Prince 坂合黒彦 (坂合黒彦皇子)* and 眉輪王, killing all three, and in the 10th month lured Prince 市邊押磐 (市邊押磐皇子)* on a hunt and shot him dead; then in the 11th month he ascended the throne at 泊瀬朝倉*.
  • In the 2nd month of the 4th year, on 葛城山*, the Emperor met a tall person who closely resembled him, and that person named himself "Hitokotonushi-no-Kami (一事主神)". The two hunted side by side, chasing deer, and the deity escorted the Emperor as far as 來目水*. Seeing this, the people said, "A virtuous emperor" (有徳天皇).

Nihon Shoki, Book 14 — Emperor Yūryaku: Emperor Yūryaku's expeditions to Silla, the craftsmen of Wu, and his testamentary edict

  • In the 8th year, Silla, attacked by Koguryŏ (高麗), asked for help through the king of Mimana, and 膳臣斑鳩, 吉備臣小梨 and 難波吉士赤目子 (readings unconfirmed) defeated the Koguryŏ army with a stratagem of tunnels and ambushes. In the 9th year the Emperor intended to attack Silla himself, but was warned by a deity, "Do not go", and sent 紀小弓宿禰* and others as commanders. 小弓 died of illness, and the remaining commanders quarreled among themselves and turned back without reaching the royal palace of Paekche.
  • In the 14th year, 身狹村主青* and others arrived at the port of Sumiyoshi (住吉津) together with envoys from Wu (呉), bringing craftswomen including the weavers 漢織 and 呉織 and the seamstresses 兄媛 and 弟媛 (readings unconfirmed). The road for the guests from Wu was named 呉坂*, and 桧隈野*, where the people of Wu were settled, was named 呉原*; 漢織, 呉織 and the seamstresses are regarded as the ancestors of the Asuka seamstress group (飛鳥衣縫部) and the Ise seamstresses (伊勢衣縫).

Nihon Shoki, Book 15 — Emperor Seinei, Emperor Kenzō and Emperor Ninken: Emperors Seinei, Kenzō and Ninken, and the brothers 億計 and 弘計 (readings unconfirmed)

  • In the 11th month of the 2nd year of Emperor Seinei, 來目部小楯*, governor of Harima (播磨國司), discovered the brothers 億計 and 弘計 (readings unconfirmed), sons of Prince 市邊押磐 (市邊押磐皇子)*, at a banquet for the new house of the head of the 縮見 granary (縮見屯倉首)* in 赤石郡*. Emperor 白髪 (白髪天皇)*, who had no children, rejoiced at this, and in the 3rd year welcomed the two to the palace, making Prince 億計 Crown Prince and Prince 弘計 an imperial prince.
  • At the banquet for the new house, the younger brother, Prince 弘計 (弘計王)*, after reciting the words and song of house-blessing (室壽), declared that he was a descendant of 押磐尊*, who had ruled the realm from the 市邊宮 palace*. 小楯*, astonished, left his seat and bowed twice, gathered the people of the district and built a temporary palace.

Nihon Shoki, Book 16 — Emperor Buretsu: Emperor Buretsu and the Fall of the Heguri Clan

  • The Nihon Shoki describes this emperor as one who was versed in punishments and trials and made the laws clear, yet who also "repeatedly did evil and did not do a single good thing"; it writes that he watched cruel punishments in person, so that everyone in the country trembled with fear.
  • When 億計天皇* died in the 11th year of Emperor Ninken, the Great Minister 平群眞鳥* monopolized the government of the country and sought to become king of Japan. The Crown Prince wished to marry 影媛*, daughter of the Great Chieftain 物部麁鹿火*, but 影媛 was already involved with Shibi, son of 眞鳥, and at the song-gathering place at 海柘榴市* the Crown Prince and Shibi exchanged songs.

Nihon Shoki, Book 17 — Emperor Keitai: The Enthronement of Emperor Keitai and the Rebellion of Iwai, Kuni-no-Miyatsuko of Tsukushi

  • When 小泊瀬天皇* died without any children, the Great Chieftain Ōtomo no Kanamura and others brought 男大迹王*, a fifth-generation descendant of 譽田天皇* (Emperor Ōjin), from Mikuni. After declining many times, 男大迹王 received the imperial seal in the 2nd month of the 1st year and ascended the throne.
  • In the 21st year, Iwai, Kuni-no-Miyatsuko of Tsukushi, took bribes from Silla, based himself in the two provinces of Hi and Toyo, and blocked the army of 近江毛野臣*, which was heading for Mimana. In the 11th month of the 22nd year, the Great Chieftain 物部麁鹿火* beheaded Iwai in 御井郡* in Tsukushi, and Iwai's son 筑紫君葛子* presented the 糟屋屯倉* estate in an effort to escape the death penalty.

Nihon Shoki, Book 18 — Emperor Ankan and Emperor Senka: Emperor Ankan, Emperor Senka and the Establishment of Miyake Estates

  • Because Emperor Ankan had no children even after taking four wives, on the advice of Ōtomo no Kanamura he established miyake estates (小墾田屯倉*, 櫻井屯倉* and 難波屯倉*) to preserve the names of his empress and consorts for later generations.
  • In the 5th month of the 2nd year of Emperor Ankan, more than twenty miyake estates were established at once in provinces including Tsukushi, Toyo, Hi, Harima, Bingo, Awa, Kii, Tanba, Ōmi, Owari, Kamitsukeno and Suruga.

Nihon Shoki, Book 19 — Emperor Kinmei (part 1): The Accession of Emperor Kinmei, the Talks on Restoring Mimana, and the Presentation of a Buddha Image

  • In the 10th month of the 13th year, King Sŏngmyŏng of Paekche presented one gilt-bronze image of the Buddha Śākyamuni, together with banners and canopies and sutras and treatises, and submitted a memorial saying that this Dharma was excellent. The emperor was pleased but did not decide for himself; he asked the assembled ministers. Soga no Iname said it should be worshiped, while Mononobe no Okoshi and Nakatomi no Kamako opposed it, saying it would bring on the anger of the gods of the land.
  • The emperor entrusted the image to Iname and let him worship it as a trial; Iname enshrined it in his house at 小墾田* and made his house at 向原* into a temple. Later, when an epidemic spread, on the advice of Okoshi and Kamako the image was thrown away into the canal (堀江) at Naniwa and the temple was burned; at that time, though there was neither wind nor cloud, the great hall caught fire.

Nihon Shoki, Book 19 — Emperor Kinmei (part 2): The Death of King Sŏngmyŏng in Battle and the Fall of Mimana

  • In the 12th month of the 15th year, when 餘昌*, a prince of Paekche, built the fort of 久陀牟羅塞* in Silla territory, his father King Sŏngmyŏng set out to reward his son for his labors, but his road was cut off by the Silla army and he was beheaded by 苦都*, a slave who kept horses in the village of 佐知村*.
  • In the 1st month of the 23rd year, Silla attacked and destroyed the government post (官家) of Mimana. In the 6th month the emperor issued an edict condemning Silla, and in the 7th month he sent 紀男麻呂宿禰* and others; but the deputy general 河邊臣瓊缶* was defeated and captured, and 調吉士伊企儺* refused to surrender and was killed.

Nihon Shoki, Book 20 — Emperor Bidatsu: Emperor Bidatsu, the Koguryŏ Letter, and the Beginnings of the Buddhist Dharma

  • An entry before his accession says that the emperor did not believe in the Buddhist Dharma and loved literature and history. The conflict over the Buddhist Dharma in this book takes place in this emperor's reign.
  • In the 5th month of the 1st year, none of the scribes could read a memorial from Koguryŏ even after three days, and only 王辰爾*, ancestor of the 船史*, deciphered it. It says that the characters written on crow feathers were copied onto silk by steaming the feathers over the steam of cooking rice.

Nihon Shoki, Book 21 — Emperor Yōmei and Emperor Sushun: Emperor Yōmei, Emperor Sushun and the Fall of Mononobe no Moriya

  • In the 4th month of the 2nd year, Emperor Yōmei, having fallen ill, consulted his ministers, saying, "I wish to take refuge in the Three Treasures." Moriya and Nakatomi no Katsumi objected, asking whether he would turn his back on the gods of the land and revere other gods, while Umako answered that they should follow the edict and assist him.
  • In the 7th month before Emperor Sushun's accession, Umako, together with the princes and ministers, attacked Moriya at 渋河*. Prince Umayado made images of the Four Heavenly Kings from the wood of a 白膠木* tree and vowed to build a temple and pagoda if they won, and 迹見首赤梼* shot Moriya down.

Nihon Shoki, Book 22 — Empress Suiko (part 1): The Accession of Empress Suiko, the Twelve Cap Ranks and the Seventeen-Article Constitution

  • In the 4th month of the 1st year, Prince Umayado no Toyotomimi was made Crown Prince and, as regent, was entrusted with all affairs of state. It is written that the prince was born upon coming up against the door of a stable of the horse office, and that he could hear the petitions of ten people at once without error.
  • In the 4th month of the 12th year, the Crown Prince himself composed the Seventeen-Article Constitution. Article 1 teaches that harmony is to be valued; Article 2, that the Three Treasures (the Buddha, the Dharma and the Sangha) are to be sincerely revered; and Article 3, that when an imperial command is received it must be obeyed with care.

Nihon Shoki, Book 22 — Empress Suiko (part 2): Empress Suiko, the Envoys to the Great Tang, and the Death of the Crown Prince of the Upper Palace

  • In the 7th month of the 15th year, Ono no Omi Imoko, of the rank Greater Propriety, was sent to the Great Tang, and in the 4th month of the 16th year the Tang envoy Pei Shiqing came to Tsukushi together with Imoko. Imoko reported that the letter of the Tang emperor had been stolen from him in Paekche; the ministers said he should be exiled, but the empress pardoned him.
  • In the 9th month of the 16th year, when Pei Shiqing returned home, Imoko was again sent with him as ambassador, and the empress sent a letter beginning "東天皇敬白西皇帝". At this time eight students and student monks in all were also sent to Tang.

Nihon Shoki, Book 23 — Emperor Jomei: Emperor Jomei — The Ministers’ Debate over the Succession, and the Great Palace and Great Temple of Kudara

  • In the 36th year of Empress Suiko the empress died, but no successor had been decided. Soga no Emishi gathered the ministers at his house, presented her last words, and asked whether Prince Tamura or Prince Yamashiro no Ōe should be enthroned. The ministers were divided between those who backed Prince Tamura and those who backed Prince Yamashiro no Ōe.
  • Prince Yamashiro no Ōe gave a detailed account of the words he had heard directly from the empress on her sickbed and protested that they differed from the last words reported by the ministers. 境部摩理勢*, who backed Prince Yamashiro no Ōe, refused to follow Emishi; troops were sent against him, and he was killed together with his son.

Nihon Shoki, Book 24 — Empress Kōgyoku: Empress Kōgyoku — The Fall of the Royal House of the Upper Palace and the Killing of Soga no Iruka

  • In the eleventh month of the 2nd year, Soga no Iruka sent troops to attack Prince Yamashiro no Ōe and others at Ikaruga. The prince rejected a plan to raise troops in the eastern provinces, saying he did not want the people to suffer for his sake alone, and took his own life at Ikaruga-dera (斑鳩寺) together with his consorts, children and younger kinsmen.
  • In the sixth month of the 4th year, at the ceremony where the memorial of the Three Han (三韓) was read aloud, Prince Naka-no-Ōe, together with 佐伯連子麻呂 and 葛城稚犬養連網田 (readings unconfirmed), cut down Iruka before the empress in the Great Audience Hall. Prince Naka-no-Ōe told the empress that Iruka had been destroying the imperial family and was trying to overturn the imperial throne.

Nihon Shoki, Book 25 — Emperor Kōtoku: Emperor Kōtoku’s Accession and the Taika Reform Edict

  • In the first month of the 2nd year of Taika the Reform Edict was proclaimed, setting out four articles: abolishing the 子代 people, the 屯倉 granaries, the 部曲 people and the 田荘 estates, and granting sustenance fiefs (食封) instead; establishing the capital, the Home Provinces (畿内), provincial governors, district officials, post horses and so on; beginning household registers, tax registers and the law for allotting and reclaiming rice land (班田収授); and abolishing the old labor levies and introducing a levy on rice land (田之調).
  • In the sixth month of the 4th year of Empress Kōgyoku, the empress wished to pass the throne to Prince Naka-no-Ōe, but on Nakatomi no Kamako’s advice Prince Naka-no-Ōe put forward his uncle, Prince Karu. Prince Karu tried to yield to Furuhito no Ōe, but Furuhito no Ōe said he would become a monk and enter Yoshino, and shaved his head at Hōkō-ji, so Prince Karu took the throne.

Nihon Shoki, Book 25 — Emperor Kōtoku: Emperor Kōtoku — The Cap-Rank System, the Death of Ishikawa no Maro, the Hakuchi Era and the Naniwa Palace

  • In the third month of the 5th year of Taika, 蘇我日向* falsely told the crown prince that his half-brother, the Minister of the Right Soga no Kurayamada no Ishikawa no Maro, harbored rebellious intentions, and the minister took his own life at Yamada-dera (山田寺) after swearing that he “would not resent his lord.” Later, among his property were found items labeled “the crown prince’s documents” and “the crown prince’s things,” and the crown prince, realizing that the minister’s heart had been pure, regretted what had happened.
  • In the second month of the 1st year of Hakuchi, the governor of 穴戸* presented a white pheasant, and after the Lord of Paekche (百済君) and monks were asked about its origins it was judged an auspicious omen. The emperor issued an edict proclaiming a general amnesty throughout the realm and changing the era name to Hakuchi.

Nihon Shoki, Book 26 — Empress Saimei: Empress Saimei — Her Second Reign, Prince Arima, and the Western Expedition to Aid Paekche

  • Empress Saimei (天豐財重日足姬天皇) yielded the throne to Emperor Kōtoku (天萬豐日天皇) in the 4th year of her reign (改元四年) and was called “皇祖母尊”; after his death she took the throne again at the Itabuki Palace in Asuka in the first month of the 1st year.
  • In the eleventh month of the 4th year, the caretaker official (留守官) Soga no Akae spoke to Prince Arima of the “three failings” of the empress’s government; the prince plotted, was then arrested, was questioned by the crown prince at the hot springs of Ki (紀温湯), and was strangled at 藤白坂*.

Nihon Shoki, Book 27 — Emperor Tenji: Emperor Tenji — Hakusukinoe and the Move of the Capital to Ōmi

  • In the eighth month of the 2nd year, the Japanese (日本) fleet fought the Tang fleet at Hakusukinoe; attacking without regard to the conditions, it was caught from left and right, many drowned, and it was defeated. King 豐璋* of Paekche fled to Koguryŏ, and in the ninth month the fortress of 州柔 (州柔城)* surrendered to Tang.
  • After the defeat, in this year (the 3rd year), guards (防) and beacons (烽) were placed on Tsushima and Iki and in Tsukushi, and in Tsukushi a great embankment was built to hold water and was named the “Water Fortress” (水城). In the 4th year castles were built in Nagato province, and two castles, 大野 and 椽 (readings unconfirmed), in Tsukushi.

Nihon Shoki, Book 28 — Emperor Tenmu (part 1): Prince Ōama’s Retreat to Yoshino and the Jinshin War

  • The Crown Prince (Prince Ōama), whom Emperor Tenji (天命開別天皇) on his sickbed asked to take charge of affairs after his death, declined, asking that the realm be entrusted to the empress and that Prince Ōtomo be made heir; that same day he took holy orders, handed over his private weapons to the government, and entered Yoshino.
  • In the fifth month of the 1st year, the emperor heard that the Ōmi court, claiming to be building an imperial tomb, was having laborers in Mino and Owari carry weapons; in the sixth month he sent 村國連男依* and others to Mino to block the Fuwa road (不破道), and he himself left Yoshino and entered the eastern provinces.

Nihon Shoki, Book 29 — Emperor Tenmu (part 2): Emperor Tenmu’s Accession and the Yoshino Covenant

  • In the second month of the 2nd year, the emperor had officials set up an altar platform (壇場) and took the throne at the Asuka Kiyomihara Palace. He made his principal consort empress; the text records that the empress bore Prince Kusakabe (草壁皇子尊), and then lists his consorts and wives and their princes and princesses.
  • In the fifth month of the 8th year, at the Yoshino Palace, the emperor had the empress and Prince Kusakabe, Prince Ōtsu, Prince Takechi, Prince Kawashima (河嶋皇子), Prince Osakabe (忍壁皇子) and Prince 芝基 (芝基皇子)* swear to help one another and not to quarrel. Saying that he would cherish these children of different mothers as though they had been born of one mother, the emperor embraced the six princes.

Nihon Shoki, Book 29 — Emperor Tenmu (part 2): Emperor Tenmu — Compiling the Codes and the Imperial Chronicles, and His Death

  • In the second month of the 10th year, the emperor and empress, in the Great Audience Hall (大極殿), issued an edict to the imperial princes, princes and ministers declaring that codes (律令) would be established and the laws and regulations revised. On the same day Prince Kusakabe (草壁皇子尊) was made crown prince and put in charge of government affairs.
  • In the third month of the 10th year, the emperor had twelve people, including Prince Kawashima (川嶋皇子) and Prince Osakabe (忍壁皇子), record and fix the Imperial Chronicles (帝紀) and the various matters of high antiquity. 中臣連大嶋 and 平群臣子首 (readings unconfirmed) took up the brush themselves and wrote the record.

Nihon Shoki, Book 30 — Empress Jitō: Empress Jitō — Rule before Enthronement, and Accession

  • When Emperor Tenmu (天渟中原瀛真人天皇) died in the ninth month of the 1st year of Shuchō, the empress came to court and took charge of government (臨朝称制). In the first month of the 4th year, 物部麻呂朝臣* set up the great shield, 中臣大嶋朝臣* read the words of blessing of the heavenly deities (天神寿詞), and 忌部宿禰色夫知* presented the sacred sword and mirror (神璽), and the empress took the imperial throne.
  • In the tenth month of the 1st year of Shuchō, Prince Ōtsu’s rebellion was discovered, and the prince was ordered to die at his house at 訳語田* (he was twenty-four). His consort, Princess 山辺 (山辺皇女)*, ran there with her hair disheveled and her feet bare and died with him, and it is said that all who saw it wept.

Nihon Shoki, Book 30 — Empress Jitō: Empress Jitō — The Fujiwara Palace and Her Abdication

  • In the second month of the 6th year, when the empress announced that she would make an excursion to Ise in the third month, the Middle Counselor (中納言) 三輪朝臣高市麻呂* remonstrated that it would interfere with farm work. In the third month he remonstrated again, taking off his cap of rank and offering it up to the court, but the empress did not listen and went to Ise.
  • In the tenth month of the 5th year the 新益京 capital* was ritually pacified, and in the fifth month of the 6th year the palace site at Fujiwara was ritually pacified, and the new palace was announced at four places: Ise, 大倭*, Sumiyoshi and Kii. In the twelfth month of the 8th year the empress moved to and took up residence in the Fujiwara Palace.
