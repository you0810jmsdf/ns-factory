Nihon Shoki, Book 14 — Emperor Yūryaku · page 29 of 50

Emperor Yūryaku's expeditions to Silla, the craftsmen of Wu, and his testamentary edict

雄略天皇の新羅出兵・呉の工人と遺詔

For the 8th to the 23rd years of Emperor Yūryaku, the text records year by year the fighting with Koguryŏ (高麗) over Silla, the expedition to Silla by 紀小弓宿禰* and others, the settling of the craftsmen who came from Wu (呉), the crime of 根使主* concerning the jeweled headdress, the establishment of groups (部) for the 秦, 漢 and 土師 clans (readings unconfirmed), and other matters. In his last years the Emperor was involved in the fall and restoration of Paekche, and in the 8th month of the 23rd year he died, leaving a testamentary edict warning against Prince 星川 (星川王)*.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textIn the 8th year, Silla, attacked by Koguryŏ (高麗), asked for help through the king of Mimana, and 膳臣斑鳩, 吉備臣小梨 and 難波吉士赤目子 (readings unconfirmed) defeated the Koguryŏ army with a stratagem of tunnels and ambushes. In the 9th year the Emperor intended to attack Silla himself, but was warned by a deity, "Do not go", and sent 紀小弓宿禰* and others as commanders. 小弓 died of illness, and the remaining commanders quarreled among themselves and turned back without reaching the royal palace of Paekche.
  2. Main textIn the 14th year, 身狹村主青* and others arrived at the port of Sumiyoshi (住吉津) together with envoys from Wu (呉), bringing craftswomen including the weavers 漢織 and 呉織 and the seamstresses 兄媛 and 弟媛 (readings unconfirmed). The road for the guests from Wu was named 呉坂*, and 桧隈野*, where the people of Wu were settled, was named 呉原*; 漢織, 呉織 and the seamstresses are regarded as the ancestors of the Asuka seamstress group (飛鳥衣縫部) and the Ise seamstresses (伊勢衣縫).
  3. Main textIn the 4th month of the 14th year, at a banquet to entertain the people of Wu, the Empress saw the jeweled headdress (玉縵) worn by 根使主*, recognized it as the item her elder brother 大草香皇子* had presented on the occasion of her marriage, and wept. 根使主 admitted his crime and fled to 日根*, where he built a rice-bale fortification (稲城) and fought, but was killed; his descendants were divided between the people of the 大草香部* and the bag carriers (負嚢者).
  4. Main textThe text says that in the winter of the 20th year Koguryŏ (高麗) attacked and destroyed Paekche, but that the king of Koguryŏ stopped the attack on the survivors, saying, "Paekche is an official domain (官家) of Japan." In the 21st year the Emperor gave 久麻那利* to King ■洲 (■洲王; one character is missing in the original)* to restore the country, and in the 23rd year he had 末多王* escorted by five hundred soldiers of Tsukushi and installed as king, as King 東城 (東城王)*.
  5. Main textIn the 8th month of the 23rd year, the Emperor died in the great hall, leaving a testamentary edict to the Ōmuraji 大伴室屋 (大伴室屋大連)* and to 東漢掬直*. In it, after describing how he had striven to give the people peace, he ordered them to beware of the wicked heart of Prince 星川 (星川王)* and to govern the realm together with the Crown Prince (Prince 白髪, 白髪皇子)*.
  6. Main textFor the 7th month of the 22nd year, it is briefly recorded that a great turtle caught while fishing by 水江浦嶋子* of 餘社郡* in Tanba Province turned into a woman, and that the two entered the sea and reached Mount Hōrai (蓬莱山). The detailed story is not given in the main text, which says that it is in a "separate scroll" (別卷).

What the main text says

In the 2nd month of the 8th year, 身狹村主青* and 桧隈民使博徳* were sent to Wu (呉). Silla had not paid tribute for eight years since the Emperor's accession and had allied itself with Koguryŏ (高麗), but when it learned Koguryŏ's true intentions from the words of a Koguryŏ soldier, it killed the Koguryŏ people within its borders. When Koguryŏ, enraged, invaded Silla, the king of Silla asked the generals of the Japanese headquarters (日本府) for help through the king of Mimana. 膳臣斑鳩* and the others faced the Koguryŏ army for more than ten days, then at night dug tunnels and placed troops in ambush, caught the pursuing Koguryŏ army between them, and defeated it soundly.

In the 2nd month of the 9th year, because 凡河内直香賜*, who had been sent to worship 胸方神*, violated a court lady (采女), the Emperor sent men to cut him down at 藍原* in 三嶋郡*. In the 3rd month the Emperor intended to attack Silla himself, but gave this up when warned by a deity, and sent 紀小弓宿禰, 蘇我韓子宿禰, 大伴談連 and 小鹿火宿禰 (readings unconfirmed) as commanders. 紀小弓宿禰 subdued the land of 喙* in Silla, but 大伴談連 and others were killed in battle, and 小弓宿禰 also died of illness. In the 5th month, when 小弓's son 大磐宿禰* came and took command, quarrels arose among the commanders; 韓子宿禰 was shot from his horse by 大磐宿禰 and died, and the army turned back without reaching the royal palace of Paekche. 小弓宿禰 was buried at the village of 田身輪*, and 小鹿火宿禰 stayed in the land of 角國* and became the ancestor of the 角臣*. For the 7th month the text records a story in which 田邊史伯孫* exchanged his own horse for a swift red horse beside the tomb of Homuta (譽田陵), and the next morning that horse had turned into a clay horse.

In the 9th month of the 10th year, a goose presented from Wu (呉) was bitten to death in Tsukushi by a dog belonging to 水間君*, and 水間君 atoned for the offense by presenting swans and bird keepers. In the 10th month of the 11th year, when a bird of the Bird Office (鳥官) was bitten to death by the dog of a man of 菟田*, the Emperor had the man's face tattooed and made him one of the bird keepers' group (鳥養部). Conscript laborers (直丁) from Shinano Province and Musashi Province who said to one another that this was "the deed of an unreasonable, wicked master" were ordered to gather birds and pile them up; they could not do so, and they too were made bird keepers. In the 10th month of the 12th year, the carpenter 鷄御田*, who had built a tower, was suspected of having violated a court lady, but the Emperor came to understand through the koto and song of 秦酒公*, and pardoned him. In the 13th year, the Emperor made 齒田根命*, who had violated a court lady, pay for his offense; had 文石小麻呂* of Harima attacked; and was about to execute the carpenter 猪名部眞根*, but on hearing his companions' song he regretted it and sent a messenger galloping on a black horse of Kai to stop the execution.

In the 1st month of the 14th year, 身狹村主青* and others arrived at the port of Sumiyoshi (住吉津) together with envoys from Wu (呉), bringing the weavers 漢織 and 呉織 and the seamstresses 兄媛 and 弟媛 (readings unconfirmed), presented by Wu. In the 3rd month, the people of Wu were settled at 桧隈野*, and that place was named 呉原*; 兄媛 was offered to the Great Deity of Miwa (大三輪神), and 弟媛 was made part of the 漢衣縫部*. In the 4th month, at a banquet to entertain the people of Wu, it was found that the jeweled headdress (玉縵) of 根使主* had belonged to the Empress's elder brother 大草香皇子*, and 根使主 was killed by government troops at 日根*. Later his son 小根使主* was also killed, because he had said that his father's fortification was stronger than the Emperor's, and the descendants of 根使主 became the 坂本臣*.

In the 15th year, the people of 秦*, who had been dispersed and used by various ministers and chiefs, were gathered and given to 秦酒公*; 酒公 piled up a great quantity of silk at court as tribute and was granted the title 禹豆麻佐*. In the 16th year, the provinces and districts suited to mulberry were made to plant mulberry, and the 漢部* were gathered and their chief (伴造) was given the title 直*. In the 17th year, 吾笥*, ancestor of the 土師連*, put forward people who made vessels, and they became the 贄土師部*. In the 18th year, 朝日郎* of Ise held off the government troops with a powerful bow, but 物部目連* and 筑紫聞物部大斧手* killed him, and the 猪名部* of 物部菟代宿禰*, who had refused to advance, were given to 目連. In the 3rd month of the 19th year, the 穴穗部* was established.

In the winter of the 20th year, Koguryŏ (高麗) attacked and destroyed Paekche, and the few remaining people gathered at 倉下*. The Koguryŏ generals urged that the rest be destroyed as well, but the king of Koguryŏ stopped them, on the grounds that Paekche was an official domain (官家) of Japan. In the 3rd month of the 21st year, the Emperor gave 久麻那利* to King ■洲 (■洲王)* and had him restore Paekche. In the 1st month of the 22nd year, Prince 白髪 (白髪皇子)* was made Crown Prince, and for the 7th month the story of 水江浦嶋子* reaching Mount Hōrai (蓬莱山) is recorded.

In the 4th month of the 23rd year, when King 文斤 (文斤王)* of Paekche died, the Emperor invited 末多王*, the second of the five sons of 昆攴王*, to the palace, encouraged him, and had five hundred soldiers of Tsukushi escort him to become king. This was King 東城 (東城王)*. In the 7th month the Emperor fell ill and entrusted the affairs of government to the Crown Prince, and in the 8th month he died in the great hall. In his testamentary edict he ordered the Ōmuraji 大伴室屋 (大伴室屋大連)* and others to beware of Prince 星川 (星川王)*, who had a wicked heart, and to govern the realm together with the Crown Prince. At this time five hundred Emishi, led by 吉備臣尾代*, a general for the expedition against Silla, heard of the Emperor's death and attacked the surrounding districts; 尾代 fought them at the harbor of 娑婆水門*, pursued them as far as the harbor of 浦掛水門* in Tanba Province, and killed them all.

A plain retelling of what the main text of the Nihon Shoki says (no published translation has been used).

Variant accounts

After its main text, the Nihon Shoki often sets out other versions, introduced with words such as "a certain book says" (一書曰).

The Paekche Record (百濟記) says

It says that in the winter of the year 乙卯 of 蓋鹵王*, a great army of 狛 (Koguryŏ, 高麗) came and attacked the great fortress for seven days and seven nights; the royal fortress fell, the land of 尉禮國* was lost, and the king, the queen dowager, the princes and others all died at the hands of the enemy. The main text centers on the story of the king of Koguryŏ stopping the destruction of the survivors.

The Old Records of Japan (日本舊記) says

It makes 末多王* the one who was given 久麻那利*. The Nihon Shoki's note says that this is probably a mistake, and records that King ■洲 (■洲王)* of the main text was the younger brother by the same mother of 蓋鹵王*.

One text says (一本云)

As another form of the testamentary edict, it says that Prince 星川 (星川王)* is known throughout the realm to be ill-natured and rough of heart, and will surely harm the Crown Prince after the Emperor's death, so those who hold many people's groups (民部) should join forces and help the Crown Prince.

Another text says (別本云)

It says that the dog that bit the goose from Wu (呉) to death was not the dog of 水間君* but that of 筑紫嶺縣主泥麻呂*.

One text says (一本云)

It says that 吉備臣尾代*, after pursuing as far as 浦掛*, sent men to kill all the Emishi. In the main text, 尾代 himself pursues them to the harbor of 浦掛水門* and kills them.

In the original text

百濟國者。爲日本國之官家。所由來遠久矣

Written as the words of the king of Koguryŏ (高麗), meaning: "Paekche is an official domain of Japan, and its origins go back a long way."

漢織。呉織。衣縫。是飛鳥衣縫部。伊勢衣縫之先也

An entry saying that the weaving and sewing craftswomen who came from Wu (呉) are the ancestors of the Asuka seamstress group (飛鳥衣縫部) and the Ise seamstresses (伊勢衣縫).

相逐入海。到蓬莱山歴覩仙衆。語在別卷

Meaning that 浦嶋子* entered the sea with the woman and met the immortals on Mount Hōrai (蓬莱山), and that the details are in a separate scroll.

今星川王。心懷悖惡。行闕友于

A passage of the testamentary edict, meaning: "Now Prince 星川 (星川王)* harbors a perverse and wicked heart, and lacks conduct that values the bond between brothers."

Deities and people, and the shrines that enshrine them

  • 身狹村主青 and 桧隈民使博徳 (readings unconfirmed)Members of the scribes (史部) favored by the Emperor. They were repeatedly sent to Wu (呉), and in the 14th year brought back craftswomen from Wu.
  • 膳臣斑鳩*In the 8th year, at the urging of the king of Mimana, he went to the aid of Silla and defeated the Koguryŏ army with a stratagem of tunnels and ambushes. A reading note says "斑鳩。此云伊柯屡餓".
  • 紀小弓宿禰*In the 9th year he was sent as commander of the expedition against Silla and subdued the land of 喙*, but died of illness in camp. He was buried at the village of 田身輪*.
  • 大伴談連*Sent to Silla together with 紀小弓宿禰*, he was killed in battle. A reading note says "談。此云箇陀利".
  • 紀大磐宿禰*Son of 小弓宿禰*. After his father's death he went to Silla and took command, quarreled with 蘇我韓子宿禰*, and shot him dead.
  • 根使主*In the 14th year it came to light that he had kept for himself the jeweled headdress of 大草香皇子*, and he was killed by government troops at 日根*. His descendants became the 坂本臣*.
  • 秦酒公*With koto and song he brought the Emperor to understanding and saved a carpenter; in the 15th year he led the gathered people of 秦*, presented silk, and was granted the title 禹豆麻佐*.
  • 物部目連*In the 18th year he killed 朝日郎* of Ise and was given the 猪名部* of 菟代宿禰*.
  • 末多王 (東城王) (readings unconfirmed)Second son of 昆攴王*. In the 23rd year he was escorted back to Paekche by five hundred soldiers of Tsukushi and became king.
  • 星川王 (星川稚宮皇子) (readings unconfirmed)Son of 稚媛*. In the Emperor's testamentary edict he was named as one to be guarded against for his wicked heart.
  • 水江浦嶋子*A man of 管川* in 餘社郡*, Tanba Province. A great turtle he caught turned into a woman, and they entered the sea together and reached Mount Hōrai (蓬莱山).

In the Kojiki

Kojiki, Part 3, section on Emperor Yūryaku (consorts, name-bearing groups (御名代) and the people of Wu (呉))

At the beginning of the section on Emperor Yūryaku, the Kojiki records only in a single line that because people of Wu (呉人) came across, they were settled at 呉原* and the place was named 呉原. The Nihon Shoki gives details such as the names of the envoys, the craftswomen 漢織 and 呉織 and the seamstresses 兄媛 and 弟媛 (readings unconfirmed), the origin of the name 呉坂*, and the offering of 兄媛 to the Great Deity of Miwa (大三輪神).

其呉人。安置於呉原
Kojiki, Part 3, section on Emperor Ankō (the jeweled headdress of 大日下王*)

In the Kojiki, the story of 根臣* stealing the 押木 jeweled headdress (押木玉縵)* presented by 大日下王* and slandering the prince is told in the section on Emperor Ankō, but no story could be confirmed in the section on Emperor Yūryaku in which the crime later comes to light. In the Nihon Shoki, the crime of 根使主* comes to light in the 14th year when the Empress recognizes the headdress.

令持押木之玉縵而貢獻
Kojiki, Part 3, end of the section on Emperor Yūryaku (age at death and tomb)

The Kojiki gives the Emperor's age as 124 and his tomb as 高鸇* at 多治比* in Kawachi, but has no entry for a testamentary edict. The Nihon Shoki records his death in the 8th month of the 23rd year and a long testamentary edict, and in this book does not give his age.
