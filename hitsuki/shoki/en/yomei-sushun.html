Nihon Shoki, Book 21 — Emperor Yōmei and Emperor Sushun · page 37 of 50

Emperor Yōmei, Emperor Sushun and the Fall of Mononobe no Moriya

用明天皇・崇峻天皇と物部守屋の滅亡

It is recorded that Emperor Yōmei believed in the Buddhist Dharma and revered the way of the gods (神道); in his 2nd year he fell ill and consulted his ministers, saying he wished to take refuge in the Three Treasures, but he died within that month. Before the accession of the next ruler, Emperor Sushun, Soga no Umako, together with the princes and ministers, destroyed Mononobe no Moriya, and construction of Hōkō-ji began; but in his 5th year the emperor was killed by 東漢直駒*, who had been sent by Umako.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textIn the 4th month of the 2nd year, Emperor Yōmei, having fallen ill, consulted his ministers, saying, "I wish to take refuge in the Three Treasures." Moriya and Nakatomi no Katsumi objected, asking whether he would turn his back on the gods of the land and revere other gods, while Umako answered that they should follow the edict and assist him.
  2. Main textIn the 7th month before Emperor Sushun's accession, Umako, together with the princes and ministers, attacked Moriya at 渋河*. Prince Umayado made images of the Four Heavenly Kings from the wood of a 白膠木* tree and vowed to build a temple and pagoda if they won, and 迹見首赤梼* shot Moriya down.
  3. Main textAfter the conflict, Shitennō-ji was built in Settsu Province, and Umako, as he had vowed, founded Hōkō-ji in Asuka. In the 1st year of Emperor Sushun, monks, temple builders, a 鑪盤* (pagoda-finial) master, tile masters and a painter came from Paekche, and construction of Hōkō-ji began at 飛鳥眞神原*.
  4. Main textIn the 10th month of the 5th year, Umako heard that the emperor, pointing at a boar that had been presented, had said, "Someday I would like to cut off the head of someone I dislike, just as one cuts off the head of this boar." Fearing that he was the one disliked, Umako had 東漢直駒* kill the emperor in the 11th month.
  5. Variant accountA certain text says that the one who told Umako of the boar remark and of the weapons being prepared in the palace was 大伴嬪小手子*, who resented the waning of the emperor's favor.
  6. Compared with the KojikiThe Kojiki gives Emperor Yōmei's palace as the 池辺宮* and his reign as three years, and Emperor Sushun's palace as the 倉椅柴垣宮* and his reign as four years, and records only their empresses and consorts, children and tombs; entries on the attack on Moriya or the killing of the emperor could not be confirmed there.

What the main text says

Emperor Yōmei (橘豐日天皇*) was the fourth son of 天國排開廣庭天皇*, and his mother was 堅塩媛*. When Emperor Bidatsu died in the 8th month of his 14th year, he ascended the throne in the 9th month and lived at the 池辺雙槻宮* palace in 磐余*, making Umako Great Minister and Moriya Great Chieftain. He sent Princess 酢香手姫* to the Ise Shrine to serve in the worship of the Sun Deity. In the 1st month of the 1st year, Princess Anahobe no Hashihito was made empress, and Prince Umayado, Prince Kume, Prince 殖栗* and Prince 茨田* were born. It is recorded that Prince Umayado at first lived at the 上宮* and later moved to Ikaruga.

In the 5th month of the 1st year, Prince Anahobe, intending to violate Empress 炊屋姫*, tried to force his way into the temporary enshrinement palace, but 三輪君逆* had the guards secure the gate and did not let him in. The prince demanded that 逆* be executed for his insolence, and together with Moriya led troops to surround 磐余* 池辺*. 逆 hid on 三諸岳* and then at the 海石榴市宮*, a country residence of Empress 炊屋姫, but 白堤* and 横山*, men of his own clan, revealed where he was. Moriya led troops and executed 逆 and the others, and Umako lamented that disorder in the realm was not far off. A note records that because of this, Empress 炊屋姫 and Umako both came to resent Prince Anahobe.

In the 4th month of the 2nd year, on the day of the Niiname harvest rite, the emperor fell ill and consulted his ministers, saying he wished to take refuge in the Three Treasures. Moriya and Nakatomi no Katsumi objected, and Umako said they should follow the edict; but when Prince Anahobe brought the 豊国法師* into the palace, Moriya grew furious, withdrew to his country residence at 阿都*, and gathered men. Nakatomi no Katsumi made images of the Crown Prince 彦人皇子* and of Prince Takeda and cursed them, but later submitted to 彦人皇子, and on his way back he was cut down by 迹見赤梼*. When the emperor's sores grew worse, 鞍部多須奈* offered to take holy orders for the emperor's sake and to make a jōroku (sixteen-foot) Buddha image and a temple. The emperor died soon afterward and was buried in the 磐余池上陵* tomb in the 7th month.

Emperor Sushun (泊瀬部天皇*) was the twelfth son of 天國排開廣庭天皇*, and his mother was 小姉君*. In the 6th month after Emperor Yōmei's death, Umako and the others, acting under 炊屋姫尊*, had Prince Anahobe — whom Moriya had intended to make emperor — struck down, and the next day also killed Prince 宅部*, who had been close to him. In the 7th month, Umako led princes including Prince 泊瀬部*, Prince Takeda and Prince Umayado, together with the ministers, and attacked Moriya at his house at 渋河*; but because Moriya built a rice-stalk fortress (稲城) and shot arrows like rain from up in a tree, the princes' army fell back three times. Prince Umayado made images of the Four Heavenly Kings from the wood of a 白膠木* tree, placed them in his hair, and vowed that if they granted him victory he would build a temple and pagoda for the Four World-Protecting Kings; Umako, too, vowed to build a temple and pagoda for the heavenly kings and great divine kings and to spread the Three Treasures. 迹見首赤梼* shot Moriya down from the branches and executed Moriya and his sons, and Moriya's army collapsed. After the conflict, Shitennō-ji was built in Settsu Province, and Umako also founded Hōkō-ji in Asuka.

捕鳥部萬*, an attendant of Moriya, was guarding the house at Naniwa, but on hearing of Moriya's fall he fled to the mountains of the village of 有真香邑* in the district of 茅渟県*. When he was surrounded by guardsmen, he shook bamboo with a rope to mislead them about where he was, killed more than thirty men, and then stabbed himself in the neck with a small knife and died. The court ordered his body to be cut into eight pieces and displayed in eight provinces, but the white dog that 萬* had kept took his head in its mouth, placed it in an old mound, lay down beside it, and starved to death. The court, moved to pity, had 萬's kin build a grave and bury 萬 and the dog at 有真香邑.

In the 8th month, at the urging of 炊屋姫尊* and the ministers, Prince 泊瀬部* ascended the throne and set up his palace at 倉梯*. In the 1st year, a Buddha relic and monks, temple builders, a 鑪盤* (pagoda-finial) master, tile masters and a painter were sent from Paekche; the nun Zenshin and the others were entrusted to the Paekche envoys and sent abroad to study; and the house of 樹葉*, ancestor of the 飛鳥衣縫造*, was torn down and construction of Hōkō-ji began. In the 3rd month of the 3rd year, the nun Zenshin and the others returned from Paekche and lived at 桜井寺*, and in this year 善徳*, daughter of 大伴狭手彦連*, and others, as well as 鞍部多須奈*, took holy orders. In the 8th month of the 4th year, when the emperor consulted his ministers about rebuilding Mimana, the ministers agreed, and in the 11th month an army of more than twenty thousand under 紀男麻呂宿禰* and others as commanders-in-chief was sent out to Tsukushi.

In the 10th month of the 5th year, the emperor, pointing at a boar that had been presented, said that someday he would like to cut off the head of someone he disliked, just as one cuts off the head of this boar, and had more weapons prepared than usual. Umako, hearing of this, feared that he was the one disliked, gathered his associates, and plotted to kill the emperor. In the 11th month, Umako deceived the ministers by saying that tribute from the eastern provinces was being presented, had 東漢直駒* kill the emperor, and buried the emperor in the 倉梯岡陵* tomb that same day. Afterward, it came to light that 駒* had secretly taken as his wife 河上娘*, Umako's daughter and a consort (嬪) of the emperor, and 駒 was killed by Umako.

Variant accounts

After its main text, the Nihon Shoki often sets out other versions, introduced with words such as "a certain book says" (一書曰).

A certain text says (或本云)

A note in the main text says that Princess 酢香手姫* served in the worship of the Sun Deity from the time of Emperor Yōmei until the reign of 炊屋姫天皇*, but a certain text says that she served for thirty-seven years, then withdrew of her own accord and died.

A certain text says (或本云)

It says that Moriya was sent to strike down 三輪君逆* after Prince Anahobe had consulted with Prince 泊瀬部*.

A certain text says (或本云)

In the main text it is reported that Moriya executed 逆* and the others, but a certain text says that Prince Anahobe shot him dead himself.

A certain text says (或本云)

It says that 東漢直駒*, who killed the emperor, was the son of 東漢直磐井*.

A certain text says (或本云)

The main text says only that Umako heard the emperor's words, but a certain text says that 大伴嬪小手子*, who resented the waning of the emperor's favor, sent a messenger to tell Umako of the boar remark and of the weapons being prepared in the palace.

In the original text

天皇信佛法尊神道

The emperor believed in the Buddhist Dharma and revered the way of the gods (神道).

朕思欲歸三寶。卿等議之

I wish to take refuge in the Three Treasures. You are to discuss it among yourselves.

何背國神敬他神也

Why would you turn your back on the gods of the land and revere other gods? (words of objection by Moriya and Nakatomi no Katsumi).

何時如斷此猪之頚。斷朕所嫌之人

When will I be able to cut down the person I dislike, just as one cuts off the head of this boar?

Deities and people, and the shrines that enshrine them

  • 橘豐日天皇*Emperor Yōmei. He is regarded as having believed in the Buddhist Dharma and revered the way of the gods, and while ill he consulted his ministers about taking refuge in the Three Treasures.
  • 泊瀬部天皇*Emperor Sushun. After taking part in the attack on Moriya he ascended the throne, and in his 5th year he was killed by 東漢直駒*.
  • Soga no Umako no Sukune (蘇我馬子宿禰)Great Minister. He destroyed Moriya, founded Hōkō-ji, and had Emperor Sushun killed.
  • The Great Chieftain Mononobe no Moriya (物部守屋大連)Great Chieftain. He opposed the Buddhist Dharma and tried to put Prince Anahobe on the throne, but was destroyed at 渋河*.
  • Prince Anahobe (穴穗部皇子)A prince who, desiring the realm, had 三輪君逆* struck down, and was later executed by Umako and the others.
  • 三輪君逆*A minister favored by Emperor Bidatsu. He guarded the gate of the temporary enshrinement palace and kept Prince Anahobe out, and was struck down.
  • Prince Umayado (廐戸皇子)A son of Emperor Yōmei. In the battle with Moriya he made images of the Four Heavenly Kings and made a vow.
  • 迹見首赤梼 * (except that 赤梼 is read Ichihi according to the reading note in the original)An attendant (舎人). He cut down Nakatomi no Katsumi and shot Moriya down. The reading note in the original says that "赤梼" is read "伊知毘".
  • Nakatomi no Katsumi no Muraji (中臣勝海連)Together with Moriya he opposed taking refuge in the Three Treasures, and was later cut down by 迹見赤梼*.
  • 捕鳥部萬*An attendant of Moriya. Cornered, he took his own life, and was buried together with his white dog.
  • 東漢直駒*On Umako's orders he killed Emperor Sushun, and was later killed by Umako over the matter of 河上娘*.
  • The nun Zenshin (善信阿尼)The nun Zenshin. She went to Paekche to study the rules of the precepts, returned in the 3rd year, and lived at 桜井寺*.
  • 鞍部多須奈*Son of Shiba Tatto. He offered to take holy orders for Emperor Yōmei's sake, later did take holy orders, and took the name 徳齊法師*.

In the Kojiki

Kojiki, Part 3 — section on Emperor Yōmei (橘豐日命*)

The Kojiki gives Emperor Yōmei's palace as the 池辺宮* and records that he ruled the realm for three years. The Nihon Shoki says that he lived at the 池辺雙槻宮* palace in 磐余* and died in the 4th month of the 2nd year; its listing of Prince Umayado and others among his children corresponds to the "上宮之厩戶豐聰耳命" of the Kojiki.

坐池邊宮。治天下參歲
Kojiki, Part 3 — end of the section on Emperor Yōmei

The Kojiki places Emperor Yōmei's tomb at 石寸掖上* and records that it was later moved to the 科長中陵*. The Nihon Shoki records only that he was buried in the 磐余池上陵* tomb.

御陵在石寸掖上。後遷科長中陵也
Kojiki, Part 3 — section on Emperor Sushun (長谷部若雀天皇*)

For Emperor Sushun, the Kojiki records only the 倉椅柴垣宮* palace, a reign of four years, and a tomb at 倉椅岡上*. For the attack on Moriya, the construction of Hōkō-ji and the killing of the emperor, no corresponding entries could be confirmed in the Kojiki.
