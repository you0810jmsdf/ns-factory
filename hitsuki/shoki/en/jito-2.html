Empress Jitō — The Fujiwara Palace and Her Abdication

持統天皇の藤原宮と譲位

Gathers the records from the 5th year of Empress Jitō to her abdication in the 11th year. A remonstrance over an excursion to Ise, the move to the Fujiwara Palace, repeated excursions to Yoshino, and records of Buddhism and of religious rites follow one another, and in the eighth month of the 11th year she yields the throne to the crown prince, where the Nihon Shoki ends.

Key points to remember

  1. Main textIn the second month of the 6th year, when the empress announced that she would make an excursion to Ise in the third month, the Middle Counselor (中納言) 三輪朝臣高市麻呂* remonstrated that it would interfere with farm work. In the third month he remonstrated again, taking off his cap of rank and offering it up to the court, but the empress did not listen and went to Ise.
  2. Main textIn the tenth month of the 5th year the 新益京 capital* was ritually pacified, and in the fifth month of the 6th year the palace site at Fujiwara was ritually pacified, and the new palace was announced at four places: Ise, 大倭*, Sumiyoshi and Kii. In the twelfth month of the 8th year the empress moved to and took up residence in the Fujiwara Palace.
  3. Main textOn the first day of the eighth month of the 11th year, the empress made her decision in the palace and yielded the imperial throne to the crown prince. This is the last entry in the Nihon Shoki.
  4. Main textIn the eighth month of the 5th year, eighteen clans, including the Ōmiwa (大三輪), Isonokami (石上), Fujiwara (藤原) and Ōtomo (大伴), were made to submit the tomb records (墓記) of their ancestors.
  5. Main textFrom the 5th year to the 11th year, the empress’s excursions to the Yoshino Palace are recorded every year, several times a year.
  6. Main textBuddhist records: in the 6th year monks were sent to Ōsumi and 阿多* to transmit Buddhism, and in the 8th year a hundred copies of the Golden Light Sutra (金光明経) were placed in the provinces to be read every first month. In the 11th year, because of the empress’s illness, the high officials and the hundred officials began making Buddhist images, and in the seventh month an eye-opening ceremony was held at Yakushi-ji.

What the main text says

In the first month of the 5th year, Prince Takechi’s sustenance households were increased to three thousand, and other princes and ministers were also given additional sustenance households. In the second month the empress issued an edict to the high officials telling them to devote themselves to the Buddhist law as in the reign of the previous emperor. In the third month rules were laid down concerning the status of people who had been sold, and in the fourth month concerning lawsuits by slaves. In the sixth month, because long rains had continued since the fourth month, the high officials and the hundred officials were made to abstain from wine and meat and perform repentance (悔過), and sutras were read at the temples. In the eighth month eighteen clans were made to submit their ancestors’ tomb records.

In the ninth month of the 5th year Prince Kawashima (川嶋皇子) died. In the tenth month it was decided that five or more tomb-guardian households (陵戸) should be assigned to the tombs of former emperors, and the 新益京 capital* was ritually pacified. In the eleventh month the Great Thanksgiving (大嘗) was held, and the Head of the Office of Divinities (神祇伯), 中臣朝臣大嶋*, read the words of blessing of the heavenly deities (天神寿詞). In the twelfth month residential land was allotted according to rank, from the Minister of the Right down, and the size of the household.

In the second month of the 6th year, when the empress announced an excursion to Ise, 三輪朝臣高市麻呂* remonstrated that it would interfere with the farming season, and in the third month he remonstrated again, taking off his cap of rank. The empress did not heed him and went to Ise; she gave caps of rank to the divine districts (神郡) she passed through and to the provincial chieftains (国造) of Iga, Ise and Shima, and exempted them from tribute and labor. In the fifth month the palace site at Fujiwara was ritually pacified, and the new palace was announced at the four places. In the intercalary fifth month the Great Deity of Ise (伊勢大神) addressed the empress concerning the treatment of tribute and labor in Ise province, and monks were sent to Ōsumi and 阿多* to transmit Buddhism.

In the first month of the 7th year the common people of the realm were made to wear yellow clothing and slaves black clothing. In the third month people were made to plant mulberry, ramie, pears, chestnuts and turnips as a supplement to the five grains. In the ninth month an unrestricted great assembly (無遮大会) was held in the inner palace for the Kiyomihara Emperor (清御原天皇). In the tenth month imperial princes and those below were made to keep armor, swords, bows and arrows and other equipment according to their rank, and the Sutra of Benevolent Kings (仁王経) was expounded in the provinces. In the eleventh month monks were made to taste the water of the sweet spring (醴泉) in 益須 district* in Ōmi province.

In the third month of the 8th year officials of the mint (鋳銭司) were appointed, and because many sick people had been cured by the sweet spring of 益須 district*, the district was exempted from tribute and labor. In the fifth month a hundred copies of the Golden Light Sutra were sent to the provinces to be read every first month. In the twelfth month the empress moved to and took up residence in the Fujiwara Palace, and the hundred officials paid their respects at court.

In the third month of the 9th year the Silla prince 金良琳* came and reported on the affairs of his country, and envoys were sent to 多禰* to search out the places where the barbarians (蛮) lived. In the fifth month the empress watched the Hayato wrestle beneath the western zelkova tree. In the fourth month of the 10th year, 物部薬 and 壬生諸石 (readings unconfirmed), who had suffered long in the land of Tang, were given rank, rice land and other rewards. In the seventh month the 後皇子尊* died. In the eighth month 多臣品治* was given rank in recognition of his merit in following the emperor from the very beginning and in holding the barrier firmly. In the tenth month the Minister of the Right 丹比真人* was given a palanquin and a staff in consideration of his retirement from office.

In the second month of the 11th year officials such as the Grand Tutor of the Crown Prince (東宮大傅) and the Master of the Crown Prince’s Household (春宮大夫) were appointed, and in the third month an unrestricted great assembly was held at the Crown Prince’s palace (春宮). In the sixth month, because of the empress’s illness, sutras were read at the temples of the capital and the Home Provinces, and the high officials and the hundred officials began making Buddhist images. In the seventh month an eye-opening ceremony was held at Yakushi-ji. On the first day of the eighth month the empress made her decision in the palace and yielded the throne to the crown prince.

A plain retelling of what the main text of the Nihon Shoki says (no published translation has been used).

In the original text

農作之節，車駕未可以動

Words from the remonstrance of 三輪高市麻呂*. It means: in the season of farm work, the imperial carriage must not move.

遷居藤原宮

The empress moved to and took up residence in the Fujiwara Palace.

天皇定策禁中，禪天皇位於皇太子

The empress made her decision in the palace and yielded the throne to the crown prince. The last sentence of the Nihon Shoki.

上進其祖等墓記

Eighteen clans were made to submit the tomb records of their ancestors.
